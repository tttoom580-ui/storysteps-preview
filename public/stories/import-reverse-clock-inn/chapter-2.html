沼澤上的黃昏

Twilight Over the Marsh

逆鐘旅店 · 第 2 / 25 章 · 5000–6000 · 英中對照

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

The fourteenth strike never came. Yuna reached the bell chamber to find its rope cut and Miro crouching beneath the wooden frame. He had escaped his guards through a coal chute, but a rash of red welts covered his neck. "The bell's dust is infectious," he said, wrapping his scarf over his mouth. "Not because it carries a disease. The dust makes people remember what was taken from them, and panic spreads faster than sickness." Yuna could not tell whether that was a scientific account or the frightened belief of a novice investigator. She helped him out through a broken shutter. Beyond the city wall, twilight had stained the marsh violet. "If you want your brother," Miro said, "you must abide by three rules: don't give anyone your full name, don't drink from the western channels, and don't believe a person who claims never to forget." It was an odd introduction to the search, but Yuna accepted it.

第十四聲終究沒響起。優娜抵達鐘室時，發現鐘繩已被割斷，米羅正蹲在木架下。他沿煤炭滑道逃離守衛，脖子上卻冒出一片紅疹。「鐘灰會傳染，」他說，一面用圍巾掩住口鼻，「不是它帶有疾病，而是灰塵會讓人想起被奪走的記憶，恐慌比病菌傳得更快。」優娜無法判斷，這是科學解釋，還是新手調查者驚慌時的猜想。她扶他從破損的百葉窗爬出去。城牆外的沼澤已被暮色染成紫色。「想找妳弟弟，」米羅說，「就得遵守三項規則：別告訴任何人妳的全名、別喝西邊水道的水、也別相信自稱從不遺忘的人。」這作為尋人之旅的開場相當奇怪，但優娜還是接受了。

They followed a shabby stone road until they reached a ferry lodge. At the entrance, an alligator lay motionless beside a basket of fish. Yuna stopped. A woman with a deep bass voice waved from the porch. "He's stuffed," she said. "The living ones stay in the water." Her hospitality was abrupt but genuine. She poured a liter of warm water into a kettle and offered them oat cakes with plum syrup. The woman's name was Brin, and she spoke in the colloquial dialect of the marsh settlements. Some of her phrases were so commonplace here that Miro understood them immediately, though Yuna needed explanations. Brin kept a bottle of whiskey behind the counter, untouched since her husband had died. "People come here to forget," she said. "I kept this place open because sometimes they need to remember first."

兩人沿著一條破舊石道前行，終於來到渡船客舍。入口旁，一隻鱷魚趴在魚籃邊一動也不動。優娜停住腳步。一名嗓音低沉的女子從門廊揮手。「那是標本，」她說，「活的都待在水裡。」她的招呼直接得有些突兀，卻出自真心。她往水壺裡倒了一公升溫水，端出燕麥餅與李子糖漿。女子名叫布琳，說的是沼澤聚落的口語方言。有些片語在當地尋常得很，米羅一聽就懂，優娜卻需要人解釋。布琳在櫃檯後留著一瓶威士忌，自丈夫過世以來從未打開。「人們來這裡是為了忘記，」她說，「但有時候，他們得先記起來。我才一直把店開著。」

Brin led them to a cellar where old electrical wires ran to an abandoned recording machine. "The city claims memory transfers are commonplace medical procedures," she said. "They are not. A donor's memory must be separated from fear and language with extraordinary care." She inserted a fuse and turned a crank. The machine began to hum, then tilted on its stand because one leg was shorter than the others. On the screen appeared a boy with Ren's face, older than the picture Yuna remembered. "This recording will expire when the charge dies," Brin warned. "Ask quickly." The boy in the image looked directly at them. He could not hear their voices, but he spoke as if replying to a question. "Yuna, if you're watching, I did not leave you. Someone used my name to keep another patient alive." He held up a drawing of a bell with no clapper.

布琳領他們下到地下室，廢棄錄影機旁接滿舊電線。「城裡說記憶移植是司空見慣的醫療手術，」她說，「根本不是。要把捐贈者的回憶從恐懼與語言裡分離，需要極度小心。」她裝上一枚保險絲，轉動搖柄。機器開始嗡嗡響，卻因為一支腳太短而歪斜。螢幕上出現一名男孩，長著任恩的臉，卻比優娜記憶中的照片年長。「電力一斷，這段影像就會失效，」布琳警告，「動作快。」影像裡的男孩直視著他們。他聽不到他們的聲音，卻像正回答問題般開口。「優娜，如果妳看到這個，我不是拋下妳離開。有人借用我的名字，保住另一名病患的命。」他舉起一張沒有鐘舌的鐘的圖畫。

Miro leaned toward the screen. "I can affirm that this message was recorded after the alleged death," he said, pointing to a scar on Ren's forehead that was absent from the older documents. Yuna wanted to take the machine apart and carry every piece home. Brin stopped her with a practical question: what would they do when the city sent soldiers to retrieve it? The lodge was built on soft ground; even heavy footsteps could make its foundations tilt. Miro suggested burning the evidence to keep it from Corven. Yuna refused. "We cannot reconcile what happened by destroying the only account he left," she said. The refusal surprised her. Earlier that day she might have chosen anything that seemed likely to bring her brother back. Now she was beginning to understand that saving one person by erasing another was precisely the cruelty they needed to confront.

米羅俯身靠近螢幕。「我可以證實，這段訊息錄製於所謂死亡日期之後，」他說，指著任恩額頭上的疤痕；那道疤不見於舊文件。優娜恨不得把機器拆開，一塊一塊帶回家。布琳用一個現實問題攔住她：當城市派士兵來回收時，他們要怎麼辦？客舍建在鬆軟的土地上，連沉重的腳步都可能讓地基傾斜。米羅提議燒掉證據，免得落入柯文之手。優娜拒絕了。「毀掉他留下的唯一說法，不能讓我們與過去和解。」她說。這項拒絕連自己都感到意外。今天稍早，為了找回弟弟，她也許願意做任何事。如今她開始懂得，藉由抹去另一人來拯救某個人，正是他們必須對抗的殘酷。

Outside, a narrow spotlight swept across the lodge roof before a boatman sounded a warning on a bass drum. Soldiers were approaching across the eastern causeway. Brin extinguished the lantern and revealed a loose floorboard beneath her table. It covered a narrow passage leading to the water. "Take the recording spool," she told Yuna. "My grandmother built that machine. She knew the council could never be trusted with a person's whole life." Miro hesitated at the opening, remembering his rules. Yuna went first, holding the cub above the damp stones. A cold current stirred beneath her boots. Behind them, Brin remained at the counter to greet the soldiers with stale cakes and careful lies. As Yuna crawled into the marsh, the spool pressed against her chest. She was carrying a voice she loved, and a terrible question: whose life had been purchased with her brother's name?

屋外，一道狹窄探照燈掃過客舍屋頂，隨後一名船夫敲響低沉的大鼓示警。士兵正沿東側堤道接近。布琳吹熄油燈，掀起桌下鬆動的地板。下面是一條通往水邊的窄道。「把錄音捲軸帶走，」她對優娜說，「這機器是我祖母造的。她知道議會永遠不值得託付一個人完整的一生。」米羅想到自己訂下的規矩，在洞口猶豫片刻。優娜率先爬進去，把狐狸幼崽高舉在潮濕石塊之上。冰冷的水流在靴底下湧動。身後，布琳留在櫃檯，用走味的糕餅和精心安排的謊言招呼士兵。優娜爬進沼澤時，錄音捲軸緊貼著胸口。她帶著一個深愛的聲音，以及一個可怕的問題：究竟是誰的性命，靠著弟弟的姓名換來？
