雨街的小店

The Store on Rain Street

Rain Street smelled of hot bread when I opened Mother's store the next morning. Our sign had lost its first letter during the last storm. We could afford either a new sign or a new part for the sewing machine, so the machine had won. Mother stood behind the counter, counting small silver coins into a cup. She stopped when I entered. "We will manage," she said before I asked anything. That was her favorite sentence when she did not know how we would manage at all.

隔天早上，我打開媽媽店門時，雨街飄著熱麵包的味道。上次風雨吹掉了招牌的第一個字。我們的錢只夠換招牌，或買縫紉機的新零件，最後是機器贏了。媽媽站在櫃檯後，把零碎銀幣一枚枚數進杯子。我一進門，她就停手了。「我們會撐過去的。」我還沒開口，她先這麼說。每當她也不知道該怎麼撐過去時，最愛說的就是這句。

I put yesterday's photographs on the counter. Mother did not touch them. Her eyes stayed on the blue mark. "Where did you find that?" she asked. I explained, slowly enough that she could stop me. When I mentioned Adrian's name, she pressed her lips together. She remembered his father, who had once visited our store in a company car. My father had come home that evening without his work bag. Mother said they argued in the kitchen, but she had never told me about what. "I was trying to keep you out of it," she said.

我把昨天的照片放在櫃檯上。媽媽沒有碰，眼睛始終盯著藍記號。「妳在哪找到的？」她問。我慢慢說明，讓她隨時可以打斷。提到沈予川的名字時，她抿緊嘴唇。她記得他的父親，那人曾坐公司車來我們店裡。當天晚上，爸爸回家沒有帶工作袋。媽媽說，他們在廚房吵過架，卻從未告訴我爭執的原因。「我只是想讓妳離那些事遠一點。」她說。

"I am already in it," I answered. "Someone put his mark on something they kept for five years." She opened a drawer beneath the money cup. Inside lay an old black-and-white photograph of Father outside a factory gate. On its back he had written, Ask Owen about the second delivery. I knew an Owen who fixed machines on our street, but he was too young to have worked there. Mother nodded. "His father drove for the factory. The son still has some of his things." We had never asked because asking might have hurt.

「我已經在裡面了，」我回答，「有人把他的記號留在一件保存了五年的東西上。」她拉開錢杯底下的抽屜，取出一張舊黑白照片：爸爸站在工廠門外。背面寫著：問歐文第二次送貨的事。我認識街上修機器的歐文，可他太年輕，不可能在那裡上過班。媽媽點頭。「在工廠開車的是他爸爸，兒子還留著一些遺物。」我們一直沒問，因為問起來也許會讓人難過。

Adrian arrived at eleven carrying no flowers, no gifts, and no second person to speak for him. He stood behind a schoolteacher buying a cloth bag and waited while Mother found change. I noticed his shoes first: simple brown ones today, already wet. When his turn came, he asked to see the rent notice. Mother laid it on the counter. He read it twice. "The company owns the building," he said. "But the notice came from the group managing the street. I need to find out who asked them to send it."

沈予川十一點到了，沒帶花、沒帶禮物，也沒帶替他說話的人。他排在一位買布袋的老師後面，等媽媽找零。我先注意到他的鞋：今天換成樸素的棕鞋，已經濕了。輪到他時，他要求看漲租通知。媽媽攤在櫃檯上，他讀了兩遍。「建物是公司的，」他說，「但通知來自管理街區的團隊。我得查清楚，是誰叫他們寄的。」

"Can you stop it?" Mother asked. He looked at me before answering. "I can pause the increase for every store while we check it. I should not give only your store a special price." I had been ready to dislike his answer. Instead, I found myself breathing more easily. A favor for us would leave the baker and the bookseller outside our door. Mother made him tea in our chipped cup. He took it carefully, as if the broken edge mattered as much as the golden cups in his office.

「你能阻止嗎？」媽媽問。他先看我，才回答：「查清楚以前，我可以暫停所有店家的租金調漲。我不該只給妳們一家特價。」我原本準備討厭他的答案，結果卻不自覺鬆了口氣。若只給我們人情，門外的麵包店和書店又該怎麼辦？媽媽用缺角的杯子泡茶給他。他小心接過，彷彿那個缺口和辦公室裡的金杯一樣值得在意。

Before he left, I asked about Owen. Adrian's hand stopped on the door. "Owen Park?" he said. I nodded. He took out his phone and showed me an old staff photograph. A man beside my father had Owen's eyes. Behind them stood a woman wearing silver glasses. Nina. I looked from the photograph to Adrian. "Did you know she worked there?" He shook his head. Through the window, across the street, our repair shop's light suddenly went out. Someone had placed a white envelope beneath its locked door.

他離開前，我問起歐文。他扶著門的手停住了。「朴歐文？」我點頭。他拿出手機，給我看一張舊員工合照。爸爸身旁那個男人有著歐文一樣的眼睛，而兩人背後站著一名戴銀框眼鏡的女人。妮娜。我看了看照片，又看他。「你知道她在那裡工作過嗎？」他搖頭。窗外，對街修理店的燈突然熄了。有人把白信封塞到了鎖著的門底下。
