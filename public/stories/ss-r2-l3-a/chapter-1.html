沒有指針的鐘

The Bell Without a Hand

這座圖書館，禁止替別人作答 · 第 1 / 25 章 · 2000–3000 · 英中對照

Mira Chen reached the library shortly after sunset, carrying the last letter from her younger sister, June. The building stood between a closed bakery and an empty square. Its clock had no hands, but a bell sounded as soon as Mira touched the door. Inside, a sign stated three rules. A reader must answer only questions registered under that reader’s own name. A reader may withdraw an unanswered question before the final bell. Every accepted answer must be supported by evidence. Mira copied all three rules before she stepped across the yellow line on the floor.

米拉・陳在日落後抵達圖書館，帶著妹妹小君留下的最後一封信。建築夾在關門的麵包店和空廣場之間。鐘面沒有指針，可是米拉一碰門，鐘就響了。裡面的告示寫著三條規則：讀者只能回答登記在自己名下的問題；讀者可在最後一聲鐘響前撤回尚未回答的問題；每一個被接受的答案都必須有證據支持。米拉先抄下全部規則，才跨過地板上的黃線。

The desk clerk, Mr. Hale, asked whether she had come to answer a question. Mira said she had come to find a person. Hale pointed at a register: JUNE CHEN, QUESTION 041, ANSWER PENDING. June’s signature looked correct, except that its final letter leaned in the wrong direction. Mira remembered teaching her sister how to sign her name. She noticed a thin red thread trapped under the page and decided not to pull it yet. Somebody had left that thread as a signal.

櫃檯職員黑爾先生問她是否來回答問題。米拉說她是來找人。他指著登記簿：陳小君，問題041，等待回答。簽名看似正確，只有最後一個字母傾斜的方向錯了。米拉記得自己教妹妹簽名的模樣。她發現書頁下壓著一條細紅線，決定先不拉動。那條線顯然是有人留下的暗號。

Mira pulled open the first drawer. A label said it was acceptable to ask for help. Under it lay a coconut, a broken stool, and a child's drawing. "Lost property," Hale said. But the objects were wet. Water began to drip onto Mira's shoes, although the ceiling was dry. She touched the drawing and heard a cruel voice mock a child. Another voice whispered about envy. Mira lifted her hand. The voices stopped. She wanted to explore the drawer, but she would not gamble with June's last letter. She put it safely inside her coat. A second drawer smelled of hay and tobacco. A card showed twin brothers outside a barn. Laundry hung behind them. One brother had written a sincere account of a storm. The other had crossed out a sentence. Mira tried to connect the crossed-out words to the drawing. Then she heard a whistle. It came from neither brother. Hale took one glance at her and reached for the drawer. "I advise you to leave those alone." Mira held it open with her foot. On the next card, a father rested his chin on his hands beside a bottle. Someone had changed the dose written on its label. An ordinary mistake could cause extreme harm. These were not just stories. Someone had changed them, and people outside these walls might still be living with the results. Mira noted the card numbers. When she looked back at the first drawer, the coconut had split. Inside was a thin red thread.

米拉拉開第一個抽屜。標籤寫著：尋求幫助是可以接受的。下面放著椰子、壞板凳和一張孩子的畫。「失物，」黑爾說。但物品都是濕的。天花板明明乾燥，水卻開始滴到米拉的鞋上。她碰了一下畫，便聽見殘酷的聲音嘲笑一個孩子，另一個聲音低聲說著嫉妒。她一縮手，聲音就停了。她想探索抽屜，卻不願拿妹妹最後的信冒險，於是先把信收進外套。第二個抽屜有乾草與菸草的氣味。卡片上是一對站在穀倉外的雙胞胎，身後晾著洗好的衣物。一人誠懇地記下風暴經過，另一人劃掉了一句話。米拉試著把劃掉的字與畫連起來，忽然聽見口哨。那不是任何一個兄弟吹的。黑爾瞥了她一眼，伸手要關抽屜。「我勸妳別碰。」米拉用腳抵住。下一張卡上，父親用手托著下巴，旁邊放著藥瓶，有人改了標籤上的劑量。一個平常的錯誤，也可能造成極大的傷害。這些不只是故事。有人改寫了它們，而牆外的人也許還在承受後果。米拉記下卡片編號，再回頭時，第一個抽屜裡的椰子已經裂了，裡面露出一條細紅線。

When Mira was a child, June had asked questions until their parents laughed with exhaustion. Why did the moon seem to follow a train? Why did some shops close while others stayed open? Their father never told them that asking was rude. He taught them to look for a reason, and to admit when they could not find one. After he died, that habit became a way for the sisters to remain close. Now Mira stood in a building that pretended to honor questions while making the people who asked them disappear. She had to be careful not to become another person who chose for June. A rescuer could also take away someone's voice by deciding in advance what that person needed. Mira opened a clean notebook. On its first page she wrote, “What do I know?” On the second she wrote, “What am I only guessing?” She left the third page blank for June's own account. The room was quiet, but it did not feel empty. It felt full of words that had been kept waiting.

米拉小時候，小君總是問到父母哭笑不得。為什麼月亮像在追著火車？為什麼有些店關門，有些店還營業？爸爸從沒說提問無禮。他教姐妹尋找理由，也教她們承認找不到答案的時候。爸爸過世後，這個習慣成為姐妹維持親近的方式。現在米拉站在一棟假裝尊重問題，卻讓提問者消失的建築裡。她必須小心，別成為另一個替小君做決定的人。救人的人也可能先替別人決定需要什麼，進而奪走對方的聲音。米拉翻開乾淨筆記本，第一頁寫：「我確定知道什麼？」第二頁寫：「哪些只是我的猜測？」第三頁留白，準備給小君自己的說法。房間安靜，但一點也不空曠，反而像塞滿了等候太久的話語。

Mira wrote her own question carefully: “Who filed Question 041?” Hale offered to answer for her, smiling as though it were a kind service. She remembered the first rule and declined. Somewhere behind a closed shelf, somebody gave a short whistle. It was the tune June had used when she wanted Mira to follow her. Mira did not know whether she had found her sister or walked into the same trap. She only knew she would keep ownership of her question.

米拉仔細寫下自己的問題：「誰登記了問題041？」黑爾微笑著表示可以替她回答，彷彿那是好意。她想起第一條規則，拒絕了。封閉的書架後有人吹出短促的口哨，是小君從前叫姊姊跟上時用的曲調。米拉不知道自己是否找到了妹妹，或是走進同一個陷阱。但她知道，自己的問題一定要由自己作主。
