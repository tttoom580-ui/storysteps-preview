誰偷走了記憶

Who Stole the Memory?

第十三站台 · 第 3 / 25 章 · 5000–6000 · 英中對照

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

The stairs opened into a deserted shopping arcade. Its only working store was a pharmacy with an exhausted cashier asleep behind the counter. A sign offered a free checkup to anyone who couldn't remember how they arrived. Shen Man refused to let anyone sign the registration card until she examined the fine print. “The clinic reserves the right to use your memory for disposal,” she read. He Chuan said the phrase was nonsense. Zhou Yan said it was a warning. Jianxia noticed a school photograph behind the counter. She and Zhou Yan stood in the same row, teenagers wearing uniforms, though she had never attended his school. In the center stood a smiling boy whose face had been cut away. Jianxia reached for the photograph, and the cashier opened his eyes.

樓梯通向廢棄商店街，唯一營業的是間藥局，疲倦的收銀員趴在櫃台睡覺。牌子說失去抵達記憶者可免費健檢。沈漫不讓任何人簽名，先讀小字：「診所保留將您的記憶用於銷毀的權利。」賀川說荒唐，周硯說那是警告。見夏發現櫃台後有張校園合照，少年時的她和周硯站在同一排，穿著制服，可她從沒讀過他的學校。中央有名微笑男孩，臉卻被剪掉。她剛碰照片，收銀員便睜開眼睛。

“Madam, please step away,” the cashier told the elderly woman, who had begun to weep. He claimed that she had visited the clinic every month for a lifelong condition. “My daughter was here,” she insisted. “You erased her.” The man said such accusations were a disgrace to the company. Jianxia expected Shen Man to argue, but the medical student simply took the woman's pulse and asked when she last felt certain of her own name. The answer was yesterday. Or fifty years ago. The station had made even time uncertain. Behind them an advertisement offered memory enrichment through approved treatments, its bright colors strangely reminiscent of a childhood television show. A melancholy silence followed when she tried to recall her daughter. The advertisement claimed its treatments would enrich the lives of every patient.

「女士，請退後。」收銀員對正在哭泣的老婦人說。他聲稱她為了終身病症每月都來看診。「我女兒來過這裡，」老婦人堅持，「你們刪掉她了。」男子說這種指控有辱公司名聲。見夏以為沈漫會爭吵，但她只替老婦人量脈搏，問她上次確信自己名字是在何時。答案可能是昨天，也可能是五十年前。車站連時間都搞亂了。身後的廣告推銷經核准的記憶增強療程，鮮豔色彩莫名讓見夏想起童年電視節目。 她試著回想女兒時，一陣憂鬱的沉默籠罩眾人。 廣告聲稱療程將充實每位病人的生活。

A metal shutter crashed down at the entrance, and the noise was loud enough to deafen them for an instant. He Chuan ducked behind a curb-shaped display of medicine boxes. Zhou Yan examined a noticeboard where employees had posted lengthy complaints about unusual patients. One note described a family trying to emigrate from a town that existed nowhere on any map. Another mentioned two siblings who could see tomorrow's train. In the corner, someone had scribbled DISSENT IS A SYMPTOM. Shen Man said no medical condition worked like that. “Here,” Jianxia said, pointing at the photograph. “We all appear together, but nobody remembers this day. What if the memories weren't lost? What if someone collected them?”

金屬鐵門猛地落下，巨響讓所有人短暫失聰。賀川躲到形狀像路緣的藥盒展示台後，周硯查看員工留言板，上面寫滿關於怪病人的冗長抱怨。有張紙提到一家人想從不存在於地圖的小鎮移民；另一張則記載兩名能看見明天列車的兄妹。角落有人寫下「異議就是症狀」。沈漫說醫學根本不是這回事。「看這裡，」見夏指著合照，「我們都在同一天出現，卻沒有人記得。也許記憶不是遺失，而是被收集？」

The cashier offered to return their memories in exchange for permission to preach the clinic's message outside the station. He spoke with the confidence of a salesman, though the sadness in his eyes was unmistakable. “You don't believe your own service,” Jianxia said. He smiled, acknowledging her scrutiny. Then he showed a little plastic bin labeled FORGOTTEN. Inside were five cards, each stamped with a different name. When Jianxia turned hers over, it carried a sentence she remembered writing as a child: I will never leave him here again. The suspense became unbearable. Who was him? Her brother? Zhou Yan? The boy whose face had been removed from the photograph?

收銀員表示，只要允許他們到站外宣揚診所的主張，他就歸還記憶。他說話像自信的推銷員，眼神卻藏不住悲傷。「你自己都不信這個服務，」見夏說。他露出笑容，承認她的審視。隨後拿出標著「遺忘」的小塑膠箱，裡面有五張姓名卡。見夏翻開自己的，背面寫著她確實在童年寫過的一句話：「我不會再把他留在這裡。」懸疑幾乎壓得她喘不過氣。「他」究竟是哥哥、周硯，還是照片裡被剪掉臉的男孩？

He Chuan discovered that the pharmacy carried the trademark of a research foundation dissolved years ago. Its director had once been accused of creating artificial rivalry among test subjects. “If we fight each other, we won't challenge the place itself,” the reporter said. They agreed not to trade one another's memories for access to the exit. The cashier, suddenly moved, produced an old recording from beneath the counter. A young girl's voice said, “When the seventh door opens, follow the person who remembers kindness.” Jianxia recognized the voice as her own. Zhou Yan gripped the table as if he were trying to keep himself upright. “You heard that before,” she said. He nodded without looking at her.

賀川發現藥局印著一間多年前解散研究基金會的商標。該機構負責人曾被指控刻意製造受試者的敵對競爭。「我們只要互相鬥爭，就不會去反抗這個地方，」賀川說。大家決定不拿彼此記憶交換出口。收銀員忽然動容，從櫃台下拿出舊錄音，女孩的聲音說：「第七扇門打開時，跟著記得善良的人。」見夏認出那是自己小時候的聲音。周硯抓住桌緣，像在努力撐住身體。「你以前聽過？」她問。他不看她，只點頭。

The wall behind the pharmacy slid aside, revealing a hallway lined with doors. The cashier's voice became soft. “You may leave. But not every memory is permissible in the outside world.” A door flashed open to a sunlit courtyard, almost beautiful enough to trust. Jianxia hesitated. She understood why people surrendered memories willingly: pain could make forgetting feel like rescue. Yet every choice made without the truth was another kind of captivity. She placed the school photograph in her coat instead of surrendering it. As the group stepped through, the elderly woman squeezed her hand. “I remembered one thing,” she whispered. “My daughter isn't waiting at the end. She's one of the people who built this place.”

藥局後牆滑開，露出排滿門的長廊。收銀員輕聲說：「你們可以走。但不是所有記憶都被允許帶到外面。」一扇門打開，露出陽光庭院，美得幾乎值得相信。見夏猶豫了。她終於明白人為何甘願交出記憶：痛苦太深時，遺忘就像救援。然而沒有真相的選擇，也是另一種囚禁。她把校園合照放進大衣，沒有交出。眾人跨過門檻時，老婦人握住她的手。「我想起一件事，」她低聲說，「我女兒不是在終點等我，她是建造這裡的人之一。」
