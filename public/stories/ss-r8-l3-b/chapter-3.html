橋下的聲音

Voices Under the Bridge

把夜路畫成星圖 · 第 3 / 25 章 · 2000–3000 · 英中對照

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

On Saturday, Nora took the sketch club downtown to speak with the people who knew the old bridge. Eli invited the repair volunteers, but warned them not to promise repairs they had not been authorized to make. They brought a blank sheet instead of a finished proposal. The first person they met was a street cleaner preparing for an early shift. She showed them a dry passage that remained open when the market gates closed. Her directions were simple, but the city's map did not include them. Nora asked for permission to write them down. The cleaner nodded and asked that her name not appear in the public version.

星期六，知禾帶速寫社進市中心，訪問熟悉老橋的人。莊岳邀來修理社志工，卻提醒大家別承諾未獲授權的修理工作。他們帶來的是白紙，而非已完成的提案。第一位遇見的是準備上早班的清潔員。她告訴眾人，市場關門後仍有一條乾燥通道可以走。她的指示很簡單，但市府地圖完全沒有標示。知禾詢問是否能記錄下來；清潔員點頭，只希望公開版別出現她的姓名。

Under a bamboo shelter, residents gathered around a bundle of old photographs. A chip in the stone showed where a market cart had struck the wall long ago. The photographs gave the bridge its background, while a local dealer explained who now owned the nearby stalls. People began to chat. One wanted to deepen the story with family memories; another asked for a civil discussion rather than an argument. Nora proposed a democratic method: each speaker would have time, but no one would be forced to tell a story. Their group decided to dine together afterward at a place that sold doughnut rings with tea. It was a small leap from competition toward listening.

竹棚底下，居民圍著一捆老照片。石牆上的一塊缺口，是多年以前市場推車撞出來的。照片提供橋梁的歷史背景，一名當地商販則說明附近攤位如今由誰持有。大家開始聊天，有人想加入家族回憶，讓故事更深入；也有人希望以文明的方式討論，而不是吵架。知禾提出民主的做法：每個人都有發言時間，但誰都不必被迫講自己的經歷。眾人決定訪談後一起去吃甜甜圈配茶。從競爭走向傾聽，看似只是一小步，意義卻不小。

A mighty argument rose when the group reached the riverside. A council poll had counted property owners but had not asked workers who crossed the bridge at night. One shopkeeper insisted that poverty was somebody else's problem. A pigeon flew from the railing as a delivery rider reacted angrily. Nora asked them to stop for the sake of the people whose journeys they were studying. They needed substance, not louder voices. Eli put the untested crossing on a separate sheet marked “not recommended.” He did not erase it. They would need to explain why the official plan had ignored it and who could authorize a safer alternative.

到了河岸，一場激烈的爭論爆發。市議會民調只計入屋主，沒有詢問夜間過橋的工作者。一位店主堅稱貧困是別人的問題，送貨騎士憤怒回應，欄杆上的鴿子驚飛。知禾請他們為了正在研究的通行者先停下來。大家需要有內容的資訊，而不是更大的聲音。莊岳把尚未測試的過橋方式另列一張紙，標上「不建議使用」。他沒有把它刪除；他們得釐清官方計畫為何忽略這裡，以及誰有權核准更安全的替代方案。

At dusk Nora stood under the bridge and looked toward the apartment windows. Every square of light meant someone was returning from work, preparing supper, or waiting for a family member. She remembered believing that an artist's job was to make a place look beautiful. Now she wondered whether a drawing could also make hidden labor visible. Eli approached with two cups of hot tea. He left one on the bench rather than handing it to her without asking. They reviewed their notes in quiet agreement: tomorrow they would check every route again, this time with people who actually used it.

黃昏時，知禾站在橋下望向公寓的窗戶。每一格燈光，都代表有人下班回家、準備晚飯，或等家人歸來。她以前以為藝術家的工作就是把地方畫得美麗，如今卻想，一張畫是否也能讓隱形的勞動被看見。莊岳端著兩杯熱茶過來，把其中一杯放在長椅上，而不是不問就塞給她。他們安靜地核對筆記，取得共識：明天要再走一遍每條路，這次由真正使用那些路線的人帶路。

The disagreement at the riverside did not end with the meeting. Nora received messages from residents who believed the team had already taken sides. She asked a volunteer to help create a record of questions rather than answer the messages in anger. When a man wrote that she should simply react to complaints more quickly, she acknowledged his frustration but asked which crossing he meant. He sent a photograph, and the team learned that two streets shared the same informal name. Without that follow-up, they would have repaired their report about the wrong corner. Nora wrote a new rule in her notebook: never let a familiar name replace an exact location.

河岸的爭論沒有因會議結束而消失。知禾收到居民訊息，有人認為團隊早已選邊站。她請志工幫忙整理問題紀錄，而不是氣沖沖回覆。男子要求她更快回應抱怨，她先承認對方的挫折，再問具體是哪一個路口。他傳來照片，團隊才發現兩條街都被居民用同一個俗名稱呼。若沒有追問，報告很可能會改錯地方。知禾在筆記本寫下新規則：不能讓熟悉的名稱取代精確位置。

They ended the day by drawing the bridge from opposite banks. Nora's picture emphasized the people waiting beneath the lights. Eli's diagram showed entrances, railings, and places where water collected. When the two sheets were placed together, both became more useful. Nora asked whether he felt strange working beside an artist who changed her mind so often. He admitted that he had expected her to care more about appearances than measurements. She replied that she had expected him to care only about bolts and metal. Both laughed at how little they had known. The bridge stood between them, no longer a border, but still a real structure requiring care.

他們在兩岸分別畫橋，結束這一天。知禾的畫強調燈下等待的人群；莊岳的圖則標出入口、欄杆與積水位置。把兩張紙放在一起，兩者都變得更有用。知禾問他，和一個常常改變主意的藝術家工作會不會很奇怪。他坦承原先以為她在乎外表甚於測量；她則說自己原以為他只在乎螺栓和金屬。兩人笑起來，才發現彼此所知原來那麼少。橋仍在兩人之間，不再是界線，卻仍是需要維護的真實建物。
