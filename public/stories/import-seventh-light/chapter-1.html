The Photograph with Seven Lights

第七盞燈 · 第 1 / 25 章 · 3000–4000 · 英中對照

Lia Chen arrived over Morrow Island at dawn, strapped into a small rescue aircraft. The pilot said the storm was too fierce for landing and offered a parachute instead, as if that were a reasonable choice for a photographer who considered climbing stairs a workout. Far below lay a cottage, a broken pier, and six lighthouse lamps. Yet the visual reference sent to Lia by an unknown witness showed seven. She had come to give a presentation about coastal diversity, but now she possessed a question much more urgent than the conference. The island council had called the missing light a harmless legend. Someone had written VIOLATION across the back of the photograph in red ink. Lia held the picture against the window, trying to convey its strange geometry to the pilot.

黎雅・陳在黎明島上空迎接清晨，身上繫著小型救援飛機的安全帶。機師說風暴太猛烈，無法降落，竟提議她改用降落傘，彷彿一個把爬樓梯當健身的攝影師會覺得這很合理。下方有小屋、毀損的碼頭與六盞燈塔燈，可是匿名證人寄給她的照片卻拍到七盞。她原本是來做海岸生物多樣性簡報的，如今卻多了一個更急迫的問題。島議會把第七盞燈說成無害傳說，照片背面卻有人用紅字寫著「違規」。黎雅把照片貼在舷窗上，試圖向機師說清楚那些奇怪的幾何線條。

The pilot, a pessimistic man named Jori, refused to flatter her courage. “I have met a genius who jumped from a plane,” he said, “but I have never met a genius who enjoyed landing in the sea.” He found a narrow strip of beach behind the headland and put the aircraft down with a tremendous jolt. A cushion slid from the passenger seat and struck Lia's camera bag. She gave a startled giggle, then thanked him with the solemn gratitude usually reserved for a second life. The old woman waiting beside the runway did not laugh. Her name was Rosa, and she said that a fierce argument had divided the island the night before. “Do not ask who built the seventh light,” Rosa advised. “Ask who keeps moving the sixth.”

機師喬里天性悲觀，完全不打算奉承黎雅的勇氣。他說：「我見過跳機的天才，卻沒見過喜歡落海的天才。」最後他找到岬角後一段狹窄沙灘，以劇烈震動把飛機降下來。座椅的軟墊滑落，撞上黎雅的相機袋。她嚇得輕笑一聲，隨即以劫後餘生般的認真向他道謝。跑道旁等候的老婦人卻笑不出來。她叫蘿莎，說前夜一場激烈爭執已撕裂全島。「別問第七盞燈是誰蓋的，」蘿莎說，「要問誰一直在移動第六盞。」

Rosa guided Lia to the hilltop cottage where a retired keeper had once lived. Its windows faced the harbor, but a new metal fence hid the eastern slope. An official notice claimed the barrier was necessary for public defense. On its reverse someone had drawn a tiny spiritual symbol: a circle around a single wave. Lia photographed both sides before the guard could stop her. At lunch, island children told her that the seventh light appeared only when someone was about to disappear. They disagreed over whether it was a warning or a promise. Lia listened without correcting them. She had learned that a community's stories could preserve facts long after an institution had dismissed them as nonsense. And every child pointed to the same place above the sea.

蘿莎帶黎雅上山，來到退休守燈人住過的小屋。窗外面向港口，但新建的金屬圍欄遮住東坡。官方公告聲稱那是公共防護設施；背面卻有人畫著一個帶有宗教意味的符號：圓圈裡只有一道海浪。黎雅趕在守衛阻止前拍下正反兩面。午餐時，島上的孩子告訴她，第七盞燈只會在有人即將消失時出現。他們爭論那是警告還是承諾。黎雅沒有急著糾正。她知道，一個社群的傳說可能保存著早已被機構斥為荒謬的事實。而每個孩子都指向海上的同一個位置。

In the afternoon she met Tomas Reyes, the island's mechanic and volunteer rescue diver. He recognized the photograph immediately. His father had kept the eastern lighthouse until the company Helix Tide bought the cliffs. Two months afterward, he vanished during a routine inspection. Tomas had searched every channel and found neither a body nor the missing boat. The company called the disappearance a private matter; the island police described it as an accident without publishing their evidence. “You want a witness,” Tomas said, studying Lia's camera. “I want an answer.” He warned that the island's most persuasive people could make a lie sound charitable. Then he offered to show her a place no visitor had been permitted to see.

下午她認識了托瑪斯・雷耶斯，島上的機械師，也是志願救援潛水員。他立刻認出照片：父親曾看守東側燈塔，直到海潮螺旋公司買下那片峭壁。兩個月後，父親在例行巡查時失蹤。托瑪斯搜遍所有水道，既沒找到遺體，也沒找到小船。公司稱這是私人事務，警方則未公開證據就判為意外。「妳想找證人，」托瑪斯看著她的相機說，「我想找答案。」他提醒黎雅，島上最能言善道的人有本事把謊話說得像慈善。接著，他提議帶她去一處禁止訪客進入的地方。

That night, the conference organizers sent Lia a message asking her not to mention the photograph. They offered her a better hotel room, a larger audience, and a generous fee if she would simply discuss colorful birds and charming fishing customs. Lia read the message twice. Her editor had often told her that restraint was not the same as surrender, and that an accusation required proof. She agreed with him. Rather than post the photograph immediately, she placed three copies in separate envelopes, recorded Rosa's words, and charged every battery she owned. Outside, the six official lights swept the black water. A seventh flash appeared between two waves and vanished. This time the camera caught it, and the faint silhouette beside it looked unmistakably human.

入夜後，大會主辦方傳訊請黎雅不要提那張照片。只要她願意談談彩色海鳥與可愛漁俗，就有更好的房間、更大的觀眾群與優渥酬勞。黎雅讀了兩次。她的編輯常說，克制不等於投降，指控需要證據；她也同意。因此她沒有立即公開照片，而是分別封存三份副本，錄下蘿莎的說法，並充飽所有電池。窗外六盞官方燈光掃過黑海。忽然，第七道光在兩道浪之間閃現，又瞬間消失。這次相機拍到了，而且光旁淡淡的人影清晰得令人心驚。
