錯誤的入口

The Wrong Entrance

寄給明天的兩張明信片 · 第 3 / 25 章 · 1000–2000 · 英中對照

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

On a cloudy Monday, the Slow Post team tried its first delivery. A classmate offered to operate the old campus scooter, while another student, a runner on the school team, carried a bag of supplies on foot. An had a bowl of cereal before sunrise and almost forgot her jacket. She reached the entrance of the student center just as Chen waved to her. He had marked three possible paths on a map. Their attempt was simple: they would ask whether each receiver wished to accept a postcard before giving it to them. They would not tell anyone who had written the card.

多雲的星期一，慢遞小組嘗試第一次送件。一名同學願意騎校園的舊機車，另一位田徑隊跑者則徒步背著用品。安天還沒亮就吃了碗穀片，差點忘記帶外套。她到學生中心入口時，澄正朝她揮手。他在地圖上標出三條可行路線。這次嘗試很簡單：送出明信片前先問收件人願不願意接受；不會透露寄件人身分。

At the first address, a woman with silver earrings frowned. “Someone told me this was a game where students could cheat and read private mail,” she said. An wanted to defend the team with forceful words. Chen gave a small nod and let her speak first. She explained the general rules and showed the sealed envelope without opening it. The woman could continue the conversation or end it. After a moment she asked how the school was linked to the project. An told her it was a class activity, not a government service, and it had no right to demand anything.

到了第一個地址，一位戴著銀耳環的女士皺起眉頭。「有人說這是學生可以作弊偷看私信的遊戲，」她說。安想用強硬的語氣為團隊辯護。澄輕輕點頭，讓她先說。她解釋一般規則，只展示封好的信封，沒有拆開。女士可以繼續對話，也可以結束。片刻後，她問學校和計畫有何關聯。安說明這只是課堂活動，不是政府服務，因此無權要求任何人配合。

The woman touched the single blue earring on her left ear and asked why they had come in person. An explained that the postcard was old and they had no safe online link to confirm her choice. They could not force a reply. The woman looked at the rain and said she had moved three times since the date on the envelope. “I don't want my old classmates to know where I live now,” she said. An promised not to share the address. The cause of the confusion, she realized, was not merely a rumor; people had real reasons to protect their lives.

那位女士摸著左耳的單只藍色耳環，問他們為何親自來。安解釋，明信片年代久遠，他們沒有安全的網路連結可確認她的選擇，也不能強迫她回覆。女士望著雨說，自信封上的日期以來已搬過三次家。「我不想讓以前的同學知道我現在住哪裡，」她說。安承諾不分享地址。她領悟混亂的原因不只是謠言，人們確實有理由保護自己的生活。

The next stop lay near a public garden. A volunteer believed a liberal attitude meant opening every card to help people connect. Chen disagreed. “Being open-minded does not mean taking someone else's choice away.” An liked that answer. They found the correct receiver, but he was away at work. Rather than leave the card on a door, they wrote a notice and took it back. The scooter driver was disappointed. He had hoped for a final delivery before noon. Yet nobody complained when An pointed out that a safe return was also useful progress.

下一站在公共花園附近。一名志工認為開放的態度，就是打開每張卡幫助人們建立聯繫。澄不同意。「思想開明，不代表可以拿走別人的選擇權。」安很喜歡這個回答。他們找到正確的收件地址，但對方正在上班。兩人沒有把卡片留在門外，而是留下通知，把信帶回。騎車的同學有些失望，他原本希望中午前完成最後一件投遞。不過安指出安全退回同樣是一種進展時，沒有人反對。

At lunch, a visiting professor asked what caused the rumors. An could not identify one person, and she refused to guess. She suggested a public sheet describing exactly what volunteers could and could not do. The professor liked the idea and said their small project might teach more than a big lecture. By afternoon, the woman with the earrings came back. She had decided to receive her letter. Inside, without anyone else watching, she read a few lines from her younger self. Her eyes filled with tears. She thanked An but kept the words private.

午餐時，一位客座教授問謠言是怎麼產生的。安無法指認特定的人，也拒絕猜測。她提議公開一份文件，清楚寫出志工可以做什麼、不能做什麼。教授很贊同，說這個小計畫或許比大型講座更能教會學生。下午，戴耳環的女士回來了。她決定收下那封信。她在沒有人偷看的情況下讀了年輕時的自己寫下的幾行字，眼眶泛紅。她向安道謝，但保留了信中的私密內容。

Back at the center, the runner checked the supply bag and found it nearly empty. They had used the last blank notice at the garden gate. Chen suggested a paper system with fewer trips and a small emergency supply kept at the library. An wrote a reminder for the team, then returned to the list of addresses. Instead of drawing a straight line between sender and receiver, she drew a space in the middle marked “choice.” The drawing was simple enough for new volunteers to understand. She pinned it to the board, where everyone could see why a delivery might stop.

回到中心，跑者檢查用品袋，發現幾乎空了。他們在花園大門用掉最後一張空白通知。澄建議減少往返次數，並在圖書館存一小份緊急用品。安寫下提醒，再回頭看地址清單。她沒有直接把寄件人和收件人連成一直線，而是在中間畫一格「選擇」。這張圖簡單得讓新志工也能理解。她把它釘上看板，讓所有人看見為何投遞有時必須停下。

An wrote the result in the log as “delivered by consent.” She did not call it a victory over the rumors; she knew trust would need time. Before going home, she asked Chen if he had ever sent a letter to himself. “Once,” he said, “but I do not know whether I want to open it.” She could have pressed him for the story. Instead, she asked if he wanted a bowl of noodles after work. He agreed. They walked toward the food street without hurrying, discussing which entrances would be easier for people who used wheelchairs.

安在紀錄本寫下「取得同意後送達」。她沒有把這說成打敗謠言的勝利；她知道信任需要時間。回家前，她問澄是否曾寫信給未來的自己。「有一次，」他說，「但我不確定還想不想打開。」她本來可以追問，卻改問他下班後要不要一起吃麵。他答應了。兩人不急不徐地走向小吃街，途中還討論哪個入口對輪椅使用者更方便。
