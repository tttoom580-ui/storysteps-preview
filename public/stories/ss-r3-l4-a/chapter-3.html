兩名觀測者

Two Observers

On the third morning, Zhiwei climbed toward the mountainous edge of the city with Luo An and a retired teacher named Wen. The official map showed a new barrier across the eastern road, although buses still passed there the previous evening. Wen, who taught the history of ideas, called himself a poor philosopher and an even worse walker. He carried a dry twig to mark each stopping point. “Don't mistake my slow legs for a mild opinion,” he warned. “When someone closes a road, the first question is who gains from the closure.”

第三天早晨，知微與羅安、退休教師溫先生一起往城市多山的邊緣走去。官方地圖顯示東邊道路多了一道障礙，但前一天傍晚仍有公車通過。溫先生教過思想史，自稱是個差勁的哲學家、也是更糟糕的步行者。他拿著乾樹枝標記每個停靠點。「別把我走得慢誤認為意見溫和，」他警告，「有人封路時，第一個問題是誰會因此受益。」

The road rose past an empty school. At its entrance, they saw a warning painted so recently that the surface still shone. A guard said a brutal surge had struck the place at dawn. He spoke without looking at the wall. Zhiwei asked which two observers had signed the report. He could name only one. That might be an accidental omission, she said, not necessarily proof of fraud. Luo An watched the guard blink rapidly when Wen mentioned a second witness. They made no accusation. Instead, they asked permission to examine the public observation board beside the school gate.

道路上坡，經過一所空蕩蕩的學校。入口處的警告漆得很新，表面仍泛著光。守衛說，清晨曾有一股猛烈的回響潮襲擊此地。他說話時沒有看牆面。知微詢問哪兩名觀測者在報告上簽名；守衛只說得出一人。她表示，這也許只是意外遺漏，不必然代表造假。溫先生提到第二位證人時，羅安注意到守衛眨眼得很快。他們沒有指控任何人，而是請求檢查校門旁的公開觀測板。

The board contained four measurements, each written in a different hand. Two described the same vibration, but their times were eleven minutes apart. According to the network rules, both observations had to fall within ten minutes of one another. Zhiwei began to evaluate the records. The first showed a small tremor; the second reported a grave danger. Their composition was different too: one contained an address and direction, while the other used only a dramatic adjective. “This is weak intelligence,” she said. “We need to see the original clocks before drawing a conclusion.”

板子上有四筆測量，筆跡各不相同。其中兩筆描述同一次震動，但時間相差十一分鐘。依警報網路的規定，兩筆觀測時間必須相距不超過十分鐘。知微開始評估紀錄。第一筆寫的是小型震動，第二筆卻宣稱有嚴重危險。兩份紀錄的組成也不同：一份有地址和方向，另一份只有誇張的形容詞。「這樣的情報很薄弱，」她說，「下結論前還要看看原始時鐘。」

Behind the school, an abandoned practice hall stood beside a water tank. A low pulse made the air thicken. Luo An pulled the others behind a concrete post, then covered her ears. Wen began to choke on dust stirred from the ground. Zhiwei offered him water and guided him downwind. She had no skill that could fight the wave, but she could count its rhythm. The first tremor came at a regular interval; the warning board recorded a second tremor at a time when nothing had passed. If the clock was correct, that second entry described an event that had never happened.

學校後方，一座廢棄訓練館立在水塔旁。低沉的脈動使空氣彷彿變厚。羅安把兩人拉到混凝土柱後，接著摀住耳朵。溫先生被地面揚起的灰塵嗆得直咳。知微給他水，扶他到下風處。她沒有任何能與波動對抗的本事，卻能計算節奏。第一次震動以固定間隔出現；觀測板上的第二次震動，卻被記在根本沒有波動經過的時刻。若時鐘正確，第二筆記錄描述的事件就從未發生。

Wen expected Zhiwei to feel triumphant. Instead, she looked worried. “The city has a tendency to believe that a fighter's rank is the same as honesty,” she said. “If I say the guard lied, his friends may call it revenge against stronger people. If I say nothing, the next warning could send children into danger.” Wen answered that reform often began with the uncomfortable duty of proving an ordinary fact. He did not urge her to take a dramatic stand. He asked her to record the actual time, the place, and the names of those who had been present.

溫先生以為知微會感到得意，她卻一臉憂慮。「這座城市總傾向把武者段位當成誠實的證明，」她說，「如果我說守衛說謊，他的朋友可能會說我想報復強者；如果我保持沉默，下次警報也許會把孩子送進危險。」溫先生回答，改革常從證明一件普通事實這種不舒服的責任開始。他沒有催她做出激烈表態，只請她記下真正的時間、地點和在場者姓名。

An older school caretaker came out carrying a clock with a broken face. He had kept it after the school closed, hoping someone would fix it. Behind the glass, its inner gear showed the same official inspection mark as the wall timetable in the opera house. The caretaker explained that officials had chosen to neglect the damaged clock for years. He expressed gratitude when Zhiwei offered to take the clock to a registered technician. “I didn't want to offend the officers,” he said, “but they told me to set it twelve minutes forward before the new warning system was tested.” Luo An wrote down his words without telling him what to believe.

一位年長的校舍管理員抱著面盤破裂的時鐘走出來。學校關閉後他一直留著它，希望有人能修好。玻璃下方，內部齒輪帶著和歌劇院時刻表相同的官方檢驗記號。管理員說，多年來官員選擇忽視這座壞鐘。知微答應把時鐘送交合格技師時，他表達了感謝。「我不想冒犯那些官員，」他說，「但他們要我在新警報系統測試前，把鐘調快十二分鐘。」羅安把他的話記下來，沒有暗示他應該相信什麼。

They returned to the city before sunset. Three separate witnesses now agreed on where the road had been open, but the timing puzzle was not solved. It was possible that someone had altered a clock for a harmless test and forgotten to restore it. It was also possible that inaccurate clocks were helping the ministry make safe streets look unsafe. Zhiwei placed the caretaker's statement beside the painted timetable and drew a line between the two matching inspection marks. Her first real progress was small: not an enemy defeated, but a question precise enough for another person to test.

他們在日落前返回城裡。如今已有三名獨立證人對道路曾經暢通的地點提出一致說法，但時間上的謎團仍未解開。也許有人為無害的測試調過時鐘，卻忘記調回；也可能不準的時鐘正在幫防衛部把安全街道描述成危險地帶。知微把管理員的證詞放到油漆時刻表旁，在兩個相同的檢驗記號之間畫線。她首次取得的真正進展很小：不是打敗敵人，而是提出了一個精確到別人也能驗證的問題。
