# 《裂痕也能盛住光》
*The Light Inside the Cracks*

**代碼：** 3B  **級距：** 2000–3000 詞  **題材：** 山城陶藝工坊、女性創作自主、成年慢熱戀愛。

**主要角色：** 林安（28歲，陶藝師）、鍾維（31歲，窯爐技師）、美怡（社區組織者）、巧巧（陶藝助手）。

## 故事簡介

林安離開代工工廠，接手阿姨留下的山城陶藝工坊。她希望用自己的名字創作，卻先遇上窯爐安全、釉料用途、居民故事授權與經營成本等難題。鍾維願意提供技術協助，但不代替她決定作品方向；美怡與巧巧也讓工坊逐漸從一人的夢想成為能共同承擔責任的場所。林安最終學會：裂痕可以成為學習的證據，卻不能被當作掩藏危險的藉口。感情與創作同樣需要明確的選擇與界線。

## CHAPTER 01｜A Bowl with a Crack｜有裂痕的碗
### 正文與翻譯

**EN**
Lin An carried a berry drink into the small pottery studio on the hill. She was twenty-eight and had spent six years making cups for other people's brands. Today she had come to start work under her own name. On the table lay a bowl with a thin crack, a broken heater, and a deed showing that the studio belonged to her late aunt. The room was not fancy. Its wooden shelves shook when a bus passed. Yet An felt more hopeful here than in the bright factory where she had recently decided to quit.

**繁中**
二十八歲的林安帶著一杯莓果飲料走進山坡上的小陶藝工坊。她六年來都替別人的品牌製作杯子，今天終於要用自己的名字開始工作。桌上放著一只帶有細裂痕的碗、一台壞掉的暖爐，以及證明工坊屬於已故阿姨的產權契據。這個房間並不華麗，公車經過時木架還會搖晃。然而，比起她最近決定離職的明亮工廠，這裡更讓她充滿希望。

**EN**
Outside stood a painted hut once used to sell clay toys. A faded poster advertised a traveling circus, with a fairy, a magician, and a child who could make a plate spin. An remembered coming here at seven and believing that every broken thing could become a miracle. The poster also showed a jet flying above the mountains, an impossible picture for a local circus. Her aunt had laughed at it and played jazz while children shaped cups. Now An understood the joke: imagination could travel farther than any small business, but a useful bowl still needed a solid bottom.

**繁中**
屋外有間彩繪小木屋，過去用來販賣陶土玩具。褪色海報宣傳巡迴馬戲團，上面有仙子、魔術師，還有一個能讓盤子旋轉的孩子。林安記得七歲時來過這裡，相信每件破掉的東西都能變成奇蹟。海報還畫了一架飛過山巒的噴射機，對地方馬戲團來說是不可能的畫面。阿姨曾笑著播放爵士樂，讓孩子們捏杯子。如今林安才懂：想像可以走得比小生意更遠，但能使用的碗仍需要穩固的底部。

**EN**
A tall technician arrived carrying a tool case. Zhong Wei, thirty-one, had agreed to inspect the old kiln before An started firing clay. He found a loose screw beside the control panel and asked her not to touch it. A green lamp on the machine was supposed to signal a safe temperature, but it could not prove that every sensor worked. “We can't tame heat by being confident,” Wei said. “We have to measure it.” His answer disappointed An, who had planned to finish various samples before the weekend. Still, she wrote down his warning instead of arguing.

**繁中**
一位高個子的技師提著工具箱到來。三十一歲的鍾維答應在林安開始燒陶前檢查舊窯。他在控制面板旁發現鬆動的螺絲，請她不要碰觸。機器上的綠燈原本應該發出溫度安全的訊號，卻無法證明每個感測器都正常。「我們不能只憑自信馴服熱度，」鍾維說，「必須測量。」林安原本打算週末前完成各式樣品，因此有些失望。但她還是把警告記下來，沒有爭辯。

**EN**
At noon a local council member came to discuss the hill's development. He spoke of the political value of new shops and asked whether An could promise a quick opening. He offered a pint of tea from a paper bottle, then pointed to the cracked bowl. “Don't let an ugly piece rob you of customers,” he advised. An knew the piece could never be sold as food-safe. But she also knew a damaged bowl might teach visitors how clay moved under pressure. She asked for written conditions instead of an easy promise. The man left without giving a date.

**繁中**
中午，一名地方議員前來討論山坡發展。他談到新店面的政治價值，問林安能否保證迅速開幕。他從紙瓶倒出一品脫茶，又指著裂碗說：「別讓難看的作品搶走你的顧客。」林安知道這只碗絕不能當作食品容器出售，但它或許能教人了解陶土受壓時如何變化。她要求對方提出書面條件，而不是輕易承諾。那人沒留下日期便走了。

**EN**
Before the council member arrived, An had found a box of letters from former students. One writer remembered a winter when the heater failed and children wore coats while painting. Another remembered a grandfather who brought extra food for anyone without lunch money. An could have used the letters to make the studio seem noble. Instead, she checked whether their writers wanted them shared. Two letters carried names and addresses; she placed them in a private folder. She selected only one note from a person who had given clear permission. It spoke of learning to make an uneven bowl and discovering that an object could be loved without being useful. An reread that sentence slowly. She wanted a business that could pay its workers and continue teaching, not an exhibition that survived by taking other people's memories. On the noticeboard she wrote a question for future visitors: “What would you like to make, and what would you rather keep for yourself?” The question did not promise success, but it offered a fair beginning.

**繁中**
議員到來之前，林安找到一盒昔日學員的信。有人記得冬天暖爐壞掉，孩子們穿著外套畫畫；也有人記得一位祖父總帶額外食物，分給沒有午餐錢的人。林安本可以利用這些信把工坊塑造成美好的地方，但她先確認作者是否願意公開。兩封信上有姓名與地址，她將它們放進私人資料夾，只選出一則已取得明確同意的便條。便條說，一名孩子做出不平整的碗，卻學會物品即使不實用，也能受到珍惜。林安反覆讀著。她想經營能付給員工薪資、能持續教學的工坊，而不是靠取走他人記憶維生的展覽。她在公告板寫下問題：「你想製作什麼，又有哪些東西想留給自己？」這個問題不保證成功，卻是一個公平的起點。


**EN**
That evening An put the cracked bowl in the front window, not on the sale shelf. Beneath it she wrote, “For learning, not for food.” Wei returned to disconnect the kiln until its safety test could be completed. He did not ask to manage her studio; he offered measurements, and she decided what to do with them. An turned off the lights with her hands still smelling of clay. Her first day had produced no perfect cup. It had produced something harder: a boundary between what she wanted, what she could safely offer, and what she would not pretend to know.

**繁中**
傍晚，林安把裂碗放在前窗展示，而非商品架上，並寫下「供學習使用，不可盛裝食物」。鍾維再度來到工坊，暫時切斷窯爐電源，等待安全測試完成。他沒有要求管理她的工坊，只提供測量資料，由她決定如何運用。林安關燈時，雙手還留著陶土氣味。第一天沒有做出完美的杯子，卻建立了更難得的界線：她想要什麼、能安全提供什麼，以及哪些事絕不會裝懂。

## CHAPTER 02｜The Price of a Promise｜承諾的價錢
### 正文與翻譯

**EN**
An spread her budget across a table in the town library. The studio had been closed for a decade, and the repair costs seemed larger every time she counted them. Professor Wu, who taught local geography, had offered to help her understand the neighborhood rather than lend her money. He arrived wearing a neat necktie and carrying a map marked with bus stops. The nearest stop looked close, but its path climbed a steep hill. A passenger with heavy bags would have to rest twice. An had imagined visitors arriving as easily as she did on her bicycle.

**繁中**
林安在鎮立圖書館的桌上攤開預算。工坊已關閉十年，每次重新計算，維修費似乎都變得更高。教授地方地理的吳教授願意幫她了解社區，而不是直接借錢。他繫著整齊的領帶，帶來一張標出公車站的地圖。最近的站牌看來很近，路卻要爬上陡坡。提重物的乘客可能必須休息兩次。林安原本想像每個人都能像她騎單車一樣輕鬆到達。

**EN**
An had met Wu at a charity bowling event the previous semester. He was expressive when he spoke about old streets but careful with numbers. “A public workshop cannot be a hungry beast swallowing support,” he said with a smile. He did not want to frighten her. He wanted her to show the cost of materials, the electricity bill, the emergency kit, and a safe entrance. When An admitted that her first plan mixed paid classes with free visits, Wu asked her to separate them. She began to regret how quickly she had called the project simple.

**繁中**
林安上學期在慈善保齡球活動認識吳教授。他談起老街時表情豐富，面對數字則格外謹慎。「公共工坊不能變成吞掉所有補助的飢餓野獸。」他笑著說。他不是要嚇她，而是要她列出材料費、電費、急救包和安全入口的成本。林安承認第一份計畫把付費課與免費參觀混在一起，吳教授請她分開計算。她開始後悔太快把計畫稱作簡單。

**EN**
At the committee meeting, a man who was clearly drunk tried to interrupt. The chair asked him to sit down and offered water rather than arguing. Another voter, a retired teacher, wanted every cost visible on the public noticeboard. A wealthy shop owner suggested charging a high entrance fee; An feared such a policy would reward only the greedy. “The point is to let people make something with their hands,” she said, “not to prove they can afford a ticket.” She proposed a free monthly open table and paid classes with a clear limit on places.

**繁中**
委員會開會時，一名明顯喝醉的男子不斷打斷發言。主席請他坐下，並遞水給他，沒有爭吵。另一位投票委員是退休教師，要求所有費用都公布在公告板上。一位富有的店主建議收取高額入場費；林安擔心這樣只會讓貪心的人得利。「重點是讓大家親手製作，」她說，「不是證明誰付得起門票。」她提出每月免費開放的工作桌，以及名額和收費都清楚的課程。

**EN**
Wei attended as a technical adviser, not as An's representative. He carried a kit of measuring tools and a strip of lace that had caught on an old shelf, evidence that the passage was too narrow. “You may apply for a limited permit to open the display room,” he explained. “That will not permit firing the kiln.” A committee member asked if the safety risk made the whole idea impossible. Wei answered that the risk could be measured and reduced, but not wished away. An appreciated his honesty, even when it cost her an early opening.

**繁中**
鍾維以技術顧問身分參加，並非代表林安發言。他帶來一套測量工具，還有一條勾在舊架子上的蕾絲，證明通道太窄。「妳可以申請有限度開放展示室的許可，」他解釋，「但那不等於准許使用窯爐。」有位委員問安全風險是否讓整個計畫不可能實現。鍾維回答，風險可以測量、降低，卻不能靠願望消失。即使因此無法提早開張，林安仍欣賞他的誠實。

**EN**
When the committee took a break, An sat beside the entrance with her notebook. She had counted the cost of clay and heating but forgotten the hours required to answer questions and clean shared tools. Wu suggested adding those hours to her budget. “Work does not become free just because you enjoy it,” he said. An drew a line beneath her total and added wages for staff, cleaning materials, and insurance. A volunteer asked whether she might charge less for people who were unemployed. An answered that a sliding fee could be considered if the missing income was covered openly rather than pushed onto unpaid workers. She also changed the visitors' map. The steep route was no longer marked as the only way up; the map now showed the bus connection and a place to rest. Her plan remained imperfect, but it had begun to describe a real place with real people and costs.

**繁中**
委員會休息時，林安坐在入口旁打開筆記本。她計算過陶土與加熱成本，卻忘了回答問題和清潔共用工具所花的時間。吳教授建議把這些時數也列入預算。「喜歡一份工作，不代表那份勞動就免費。」他說。林安在總額下畫線，加入員工薪資、清潔材料與保險。志工問，能否讓失業者少付一點。林安回答，可以考慮彈性收費，但差額要公開補足，不能轉嫁給無薪工作者。她也修正訪客地圖，不再把陡坡標成唯一入口，而是加上公車轉乘與休息點。計畫仍不完美，卻開始描述一個有真實居民與成本的地方。


**EN**
By evening, An had drawn three columns: repairs now, classes later, and dreams that could wait. The budget no longer looked beautiful, but it had become useful. Wu pointed at the map and suggested inviting residents who had never climbed the hill. An sent messages asking them what would make a visit possible. She had been eager to open a studio; now she was learning to build an invitation that other people could actually accept. When she left, the empty form for the permit sat in her bag beside a little clay spoon. Both reminded her that care began with details.

**繁中**
傍晚，林安畫了三欄：「現在維修」、「以後開課」與「可以等待的夢想」。預算不再漂亮，卻終於實用。吳教授指著地圖，建議邀請從未爬上這座山坡的居民。林安傳訊詢問大家，要怎樣才能方便到訪。她原本只急著開工坊，如今開始學著提出別人真正能接受的邀請。離開時，申請許可的空白表格和一把小陶匙都躺在包裡，提醒她關心往往始於細節。

## CHAPTER 03｜The First Story Tiles｜第一批故事陶片
