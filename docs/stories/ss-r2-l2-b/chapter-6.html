On the eve of the next broadcast, the student media building still had water marks from the storm. A small crowd stood along the sidewalk, watching workers repair the roof. Xia wore a yellow raincoat and carried a list of damaged equipment. Ethan asked whether the university would accept a student plan to fix what they could safely handle. An official from the facilities office agreed to examine their plan, but he warned them not to touch damaged electrical lines. As they walked toward the studio, Xia said the new rule felt fair. She had learned that a careful limit could protect a team without stealing its freedom. Ethan looked relieved that she understood.

下一次直播前夕，學生媒體大樓仍留著風雨造成的水痕。一小群人站在人行道邊，看工人修屋頂。夏穿著黃色雨衣，拿著受損設備清單。以森問學校會不會接受學生提出的安全修復計畫。設施辦公室的一名主管同意檢查，但警告他們不可碰損壞的電線。兩人走向工作室時，夏說新規則很公平。她學會了，謹慎的限制能保護團隊，而不必奪走自由。以森聽見她這麼說，似乎鬆了口氣。

Their guest was a musician named Mrs. Lee, who had used the old room to record songs twenty years earlier. She brought a short piece of poetry written by her friend during a difficult time. The words spoke about a soul that could find its way home after a storm. Xia wanted the audience to hear the song without turning it into a sad story about one person's pain. Mrs. Lee said she was sensitive to that problem too. “Music helped me,” she explained, “but it was the people who listened who kept me going.” Ethan set the microphone at her favorite height, and she sang without a camera pointed at her face.

來賓是音樂家李女士。二十年前，她曾在這間舊工作室錄歌。她帶來朋友在困難時期寫的一首短詩，詩裡說一顆靈魂能在風雨後找到回家的路。夏希望觀眾聽見歌曲，不想把它變成消費個人痛苦的悲傷故事。李女士說自己也很在意這個問題。「音樂幫過我，」她解釋，「但讓我繼續走下去的，是那些願意聽的人。」以森把麥克風調到她最喜歡的高度，她便在鏡頭不拍攝臉孔的情況下唱了起來。

After the song, Mrs. Lee showed them an old poster from a western city where she had lived. It showed a giraffe painted on a wall beside a war memorial. Xia thought it was an odd picture, but Mrs. Lee explained that local children had drawn animals to make the place feel less frightening. “The poster reminds me that a difficult history can still lead to care,” she said. Ethan asked whether they could display it. She gave permission, and Xia added a clear term to their guest agreement: any personal photograph would be shown only with the owner's approval. This was a lesson she wanted every future student host to remember.

唱完後，李女士拿出一張她曾居住的西部城市舊海報。上面畫著一隻長頸鹿，站在戰爭紀念碑旁的牆上。夏覺得畫面奇怪，李女士卻解釋，當地孩子畫動物，是希望那個地方不再那麼嚇人。「這張海報提醒我，困難的歷史仍能帶來關懷，」她說。以森問能不能展示，她同意了。夏在來賓同意書新增清楚條款：私人照片必須經擁有者同意才能播出。這是她希望每一位未來學生主持人都記住的事。

The broadcast drew fewer views than their food program. Some students called it slow. Others wrote that they had never heard a song from someone their grandparents' age. Xia read every comment and felt a little sad about the low number. Ethan reminded her that a viewer was a person, not only a figure on a screen. They thanked Mrs. Lee and offered to send her the recording before posting it. She listened, smiled, and requested one small cut where she had forgotten a word. For the first time, the two hosts made a change because their guest wanted it, not because they feared losing viewers.

這場直播的觀看數比餐食節目少。有些學生說節奏慢，另一些人則留言，他們從未聽過祖父母年紀的人唱歌。夏讀了每則留言，因為數字偏低而有點難過。以森提醒她，觀眾是人，不只是螢幕上的數字。他們向李女士道謝，並提議發布前先給她聽錄音。她聽完微笑，要求剪掉一處忘詞的片段。這是兩位主持人第一次因為來賓的意願而修改，而不是害怕失去觀眾。

After the program, Mrs. Lee asked whether she could help teach students to record sound. She said the basics were simple, but listening required practice. Xia suggested a free Saturday class, and Ethan offered to prepare the room. They placed a note on the studio door asking students to register without paying. Within an hour, eight names appeared. One student wrote that she had never dared speak into a microphone. Xia remembered the first time she had felt brave enough to question a teacher. She hoped the new class could give other students that same small, important moment.

節目後，李女士問能不能協助教學生錄音。她說基本技術很簡單，但聆聽需要練習。夏提議星期六辦免費課程，以森願意準備場地。他們在工作室門口貼通知，讓學生免費報名。一小時內就有八個名字。一名學生寫道，自己從不敢對著麥克風說話。夏想起第一次敢向老師提問的時刻，希望新課程也能給其他學生同樣微小卻重要的經驗。

That evening Professor Lin visited the room and said the university might allow the students to manage it next term if they could write a strong safety policy. Xia felt excitement rise in her chest. A student-run studio was what she had wanted from the beginning, though she now understood how much work it would require. Ethan said he would help, even if they did not win the contest. “I thought you only liked winning,” Xia teased. “I do,” he answered. “But perhaps this is a different kind of win.” She laughed, then wrote his words in their shared notebook so neither would forget the promise.

當晚林教授來到工作室，說如果學生能寫出完善的安全規定，學校下學期也許會讓學生自行管理這裡。夏胸口湧起興奮。學生自主的工作室一直是她的願望，雖然現在她明白要付出多少工作。以森說即使沒得獎，他也會幫忙。「我以為你只喜歡贏，」夏取笑他。「我的確喜歡，」他回答，「但也許這是另一種勝利。」她笑著把他的話寫進共用筆記本，讓兩人都不會忘記這個承諾。
