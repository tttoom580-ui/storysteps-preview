故事的價碼

The Price of a Story

死對頭成了我的雙人直播搭檔 · 第 11 / 25 章 · 1000–2000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

The written offer from the sponsor arrived on Tuesday. It promised a fee for each video that included the sponsor's name, but it also gave the company the right to change the film before it was shown. Xia stared at that sentence. Ethan raised one eyebrow and said the price was too high, even if the money looked good. Their disagreement was not about money alone. He wanted to study every possible choice; she wanted to refuse at once. Professor Lin advised them to invite a skilled media author to speak about the issue. The guest, Mr. Bai, had helped students make short films for years and knew how much control could hide in a narrow line of writing.

星期二，贊助商送來書面提案。公司承諾每支放入品牌名稱的影片都能獲得一筆費用，但也要求保有上映前修改影片的權利。夏盯著那句話。以森挑起一邊眉毛，說就算金額好看，代價也太大。他們的分歧不只在金錢。他想仔細研究所有選項，她則想立刻拒絕。林教授建議請一位熟練的媒體作者談這件事。來賓白先生多年來協助學生拍短片，知道一行狹窄文字能藏進多少控制權。

Mr. Bai arrived with a puzzle made from small wooden blocks. The picture was a forest, with a crow on a branch and a guard beside a gate. Each piece was only a few centimeters wide. “A story can be changed by moving one little piece,” he said. “You need to know which pieces you are willing to move.” He explained that a good agreement should include a clear rule about who could edit the final program. Xia asked whether all sponsors were rude or dangerous. Mr. Bai shook his head. “No,” he said. “A fair partner asks before changing someone's work. A poor partner does not.”

白先生帶來一幅木塊拼圖，畫面是森林、枝上的烏鴉，以及門旁的守衛。每塊拼圖片只有幾公分寬。「一個故事只要移動一小塊，就可能改變，」他說。「你必須知道自己願意移動哪些部分。」他解釋，好的協議應明確規定誰能編輯最終節目。夏問是不是所有贊助商都粗魯或危險。白先生搖頭。「不是，」他說，「公平的夥伴會在更動別人的作品前詢問；不好的夥伴不會。」

In the afternoon, the team visited a small wedding hall to film an example of how pictures could tell different stories. A waiter showed them the room and explained that a priest would lead a ceremony there on Saturday. One photograph showed a happy family on a bright stage. Another showed the same people waiting in a dark hall. “Neither photograph is false,” said Mr. Bai, “but if you use only one, viewers may imagine a very different day.” The lesson touched Xia. She realized that protecting a guest's relationship with the audience was more important than making every moment exciting. Ethan added that facts could be arranged fairly or unfairly without anyone inventing a single word.

下午，團隊前往小婚禮會場，拍攝照片如何講述不同故事的示例。服務生帶他們參觀，說星期六將有神職人員主持典禮。一張照片中，幸福的一家人站在明亮舞台；另一張則是同樣的人在昏暗大廳等待。「兩張都不假，」白先生說，「但只用一張，觀眾就可能想像出截然不同的一天。」這堂課觸動了夏。她明白，維護來賓和觀眾的關係，比把每個瞬間弄得刺激更重要。以森補充，即使沒有捏造任何一句話，事實仍可能被公平或不公平地排列。

The author gave one more example. He drew a line one centimeter long, then placed a second mark beside it. “Neither a photograph nor a spoken sentence shows the whole world,” he said. “And neither should pretend to.” Xia asked whether a short film could ever be complete. He replied that a film needed clear limits, not every fact in existence. Ethan wrote the idea in their notes and added that they should say when information was missing. For once Xia did not argue about careful wording. She knew it could protect the people whose stories they had promised to tell.

作者又舉了一個例子。他畫出一條一公分長的線，再在旁邊加上一個記號。「照片和一句話都不能呈現整個世界，」他說，「也不該假裝能做到。」夏問短片是否可能完整。白先生回答，影片需要清楚界線，而不是包下世上一切事實。以森把想法記下，補充當資料缺失時就該說清楚。這次夏沒有為謹慎措辭而爭辯。她知道這能保護自己承諾要講述故事的人。

That evening Xia and Ethan sent the sponsor three questions: who would own their recordings, who could edit them, and whether guests could withdraw private parts. The company answered the first two questions but ignored the third. Xia felt annoyed, though not surprised. Ethan proposed a new contract that protected their editorial choices. “They may refuse it,” he said. “Then we can look for other support.” Xia watched him write with care. She remembered calling him her enemy when they first met. Now she could hardly imagine working through this problem without him. But she did not tell him so; she wanted to understand her own changing feelings first.

當晚，夏和以森向贊助商提出三個問題：錄影屬於誰、誰能編輯，以及來賓是否能撤回私人片段。公司回答了前兩題，卻忽略第三題。夏感到煩躁，但並不意外。以森提議另外寫一份保護編輯自主的合約。「他們可能拒絕，」他說，「那我們再找其他支持。」夏看著他仔細地寫。她想起初次見面時曾稱他為敵人。現在她幾乎無法想像沒有他該怎麼處理這個問題。但她沒有說出口；她想先弄清自己改變中的感情。

The sponsor replied the next morning with a short refusal. The team would receive no money for the edited film. Xia's face went pale as she counted what they could still afford. Ethan put the puzzle on the table, leaving its center piece open. “There is a missing piece,” he said. “The old donation records may tell us whether the studio has another source of support.” He was right. The money problem and the lost letter box were not separate stories anymore. Xia decided their next report would explain why a student room needed more than a sponsor's promise. It would need rules that could survive a change in leadership.

隔天早晨，贊助商簡短回覆拒絕。團隊將拿不到這支影片的贊助款。夏算著剩下的預算，臉色發白。以森把拼圖放在桌上，留下中央缺口。「我們還少了一塊，」他說。「舊捐款紀錄也許能告訴我們工作室是否有其他支持來源。」他說得對。金錢問題和遺失的信件箱已不再是兩個分開的故事。夏決定下一集說明，學生空間為何不能只靠贊助商的承諾，而需要能在領導者更換後仍然有效的規則。
