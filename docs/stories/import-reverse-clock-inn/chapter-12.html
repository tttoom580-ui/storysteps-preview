人口普查的空白

The Missing Census

逆鐘旅店 · 第 12 / 25 章 · 5000–6000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

The reformers convened a public hearing in a fish market near the harbor. An attendant brought chairs from a café, while an oyster seller offered warm bread batter fried in small pans. The generous meal was lavish by local standards, but nobody felt festive. Tomas had recovered a census listing thousands of residents as transferred to unnamed institutions. Their addresses no longer appeared in public registers. A chemist named Daro arrived carrying samples of the bell dust. He had studied a fever outbreak years earlier and remembered how officials had withheld laboratory data when they feared public anger. "I am skeptical of rumors," he said, "but this material deserves analysis." He explained that the dust contained a mild chemical marker, not a mystical substance. It could not directly erase memory, but it might interfere with the implanted patterns described in the clinic records.

改革者在港口附近的魚市場召開公開聽證會。工作人員從咖啡館搬來椅子，賣牡蠣的小販則用平底鍋煎麵糊，提供熱食。這頓飯在當地算得上豐盛，卻沒人有慶祝心情。托瑪斯復原了一份人口普查，數千名居民被列為轉送未具名機構，原地址也不再出現在公開戶籍。化學家達羅帶著鐘灰樣本來到。他研究過多年前的熱病爆發，記得官員因害怕民怨而扣住實驗室資料。「我對傳言很懷疑，」他說，「但這種物質值得分析。」他解釋鐘灰含有溫和的化學標記，不是神秘物質。它本身無法直接抹去記憶，卻可能干擾病歷中提及的植入模式。

Yuna described the double register and asked the crowd to distinguish medical consent from administrative permission. The clinic logo appeared on every disputed form, yet the signatures were often those of guardians rather than patients. A martial guard stood by the door, listening. When a patient began to groan with distress, a nurse guided him to a quiet corner and dispensed water. Yuna waited until he was ready to continue. She did not want the hearing to become another place where vulnerable people were ordered to perform suffering. The marketplace grew quiet enough for a sparrow's chirp to be heard above the roof. Hale spoke about the invaluable role of consent and the danger of granting officials unlimited emergency powers. Her speech drew support, but some families still feared losing treatment if the government felt insulted. The hearing had made the problem visible without solving it.

優娜解釋雙重登記簿，請在場的人區分醫療同意與行政許可。每份有爭議的表格上都有診所標誌，簽名卻常是監護人的，不是病患自己的。門邊站著一名軍方守衛，默默聽著。一位病患因痛苦而呻吟時，護理師帶他到安靜角落，給他水喝。優娜等他準備好才繼續，不願讓聽證會變成另一個命令脆弱的人表演痛苦的場所。市場安靜到屋頂上麻雀的啁啾都聽得見。海爾談論同意權的無可取代，以及賦予官員無限緊急權力的危險。演說贏得支持，但有些家庭仍害怕政府受辱後會撤回治療。聽證會讓問題浮出水面，卻還沒解決問題。

The city's loudspeaker suddenly announced that every patient who subscribed to the reformers' medical registry would lose access to public clinics. An official claimed the registry might populate the system with false names. Tomas challenged him to identify a single fabricated entry, but the official refused to answer. The refusal was a setback for the hearing, because frightened people began to leave. Yuna understood their fear: some had nowhere else to receive care. She asked the nurse to read aloud the names of hospitals willing to provide independent treatment, with the consent of the families involved. Several doctors raised their hands to offer help. They could not replace the whole system overnight, but they could begin building an alternative network. Hale called the threats an iceberg whose visible tip concealed much deeper control over food, travel, and employment.

城市擴音器忽然宣布，凡訂閱改革者醫療登記服務的病患，都將失去公立診所就醫資格。官員宣稱這份登記可能讓系統充斥假姓名。托瑪斯挑戰他指出任一筆造假紀錄，官員卻拒絕回答。這對聽證會是一大挫折，害怕的人群開始離開。優娜理解，因為有些人除了公立診所別無去處。她請護理師在取得相關家庭同意後，唸出願意提供獨立治療的醫院名單。幾名醫師舉手表示協助。這無法一夜取代整套制度，至少能開始建立替代網絡。海爾說這些威脅像冰山，露在水面上的只是尖端，水面下還有對糧食、旅行與工作的更深控制。

By evening, the hearing had moved into a warehouse to avoid the rain. A young mother asked why the reformers were collecting the same information that the council had abused. Yuna answered that her question was precisely why every participant needed to know what would be recorded, who could see it, and when it could be removed. Tomas demonstrated a system that stored statements under individual control rather than in a central office. Patients could choose whether to share their names, and could withdraw permission later. It was a small technical design with enormous political consequences. Yuna saw people returning to the chairs, one family at a time. They did not believe that trust had been restored. They believed the reformers were willing to make trust something that could be checked. Outside, the harbor lights appeared through rain, and the public hearing continued. A physician admitted that the census office had tried to withhold treatment vouchers from unregistered residents. He brought both the old order and the revised rule to the hearing. Yuna asked him to explain why the change mattered to someone waiting in a clinic doorway. 'Because a child should not have to prove which memories are hers before receiving a bandage,' he answered. Several witnesses who had remained silent raised their hands. For once, a legal discussion gave people a practical reason to hope.

傍晚為躲雨，聽證會移進倉庫。一名年輕母親問，改革者為什麼也要蒐集議會曾拿來傷害人民的資料。優娜回答，正因如此，每個人都必須知道哪些資訊會被記錄、誰能查看、何時能移除。托瑪斯展示一套把證詞保存在個人控制之下，而非中央辦公室的系統。病患可以自行決定要不要分享姓名，之後也能撤回同意。這是個小小的技術設計，政治後果卻十分巨大。優娜看見家庭一戶戶回到椅子上。他們並非相信信任已經恢復，而是相信改革者願意讓信任成為可以被檢驗的事。外頭，港口燈光穿過雨幕，公開聽證會繼續進行。 一名醫師承認，人口普查局曾試圖扣住未登記居民的醫療券。他把舊命令與修訂條文都帶到聽證會。優娜請他說明，這改變對站在診所門口等待的人有何意義。「因為孩子不應先證明哪些記憶屬於自己，才能領到一片繃帶。」他回答。好幾名原本沉默的證人舉起手。這次法律辯論終於讓人有了具體的希望理由。
