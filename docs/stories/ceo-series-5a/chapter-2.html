標了價的緋聞

A Rumor with a Price Tag

By noon, an absurd story had spread across the city: the chairman was supposedly engaged to the unknown designer he had defended. The rumor brought adverse attention to Yitang's shop, where customers asked for photographs instead of clothes. A journalist named Lin Yue knocked at the door, asking whether she could report the studio's actual history. Yitang showed her the architecture of the old factory building and the first patterns pinned to its walls. They had nothing to do with a billionaire's romance. “I study journalism,” Lin said, “and I would rather publish the woman who works here than the woman people have invented.” Yitang opened the door wider.

中午前，荒謬的消息傳遍全城：據說明曜董事長已和昨夜受他維護的無名設計師訂婚。負面關注湧進以棠的小店，顧客只想拍照，不問衣服。記者林玥登門，想報導工作室的真實歷史。以棠帶她看舊工廠的建築、釘在牆上的第一批版型，那些都與富豪戀情無關。林玥說：「我學新聞，不想寫大眾虛構的女人，我想寫真正做事的你。」以棠於是把門打開得更大。

Meanwhile, Zhiyan faced a board whose ideology treated public affection as a marketing instrument. A donor offered to fund a new fashion program if the couple appeared together in an advertisement. The price was excessive: the sponsor would control every photograph and every answer. Zhiyan refused without consulting Yitang, because refusing to sell another person's story required no permission. But when an editor suggested that he publicly deny ever meeting her, he paused. Such a denial could rip apart her credibility after the gala. He sent Yitang a direct question instead: “Would a joint correction help or harm you?” She was surprised that a man accustomed to instant decisions was willing to wait.

同一時間，知衍面對的董事會奉行一套觀念：公眾情感只是行銷工具。一名捐助者願意資助新時尚計畫，條件是兩人合拍廣告；代價過高，贊助方將控制每張照片和每個回答。知衍不必問以棠便拒絕，因為拒絕出售他人的故事本來就不需要許可。可當編輯建議他公開否認與她見過面，他停下來想了想：這樣可能撕裂她在晚宴後建立的可信度。他直接問以棠：「共同澄清對你是幫助還是傷害？」習慣迅速決策的人竟願意等待，讓她有點意外。

They met at a small restaurant with no photographers. Yitang ordered salmon while Zhiyan drank tea. He admitted that he felt like an outsider in the world of fabric and independent design. She admitted that financial language sounded like a foreign language to her. The waiter offered to massage her sore wrist with a warm cloth, and she politely declined. Zhiyan did not reach across the table to touch her. Lin Yue later explained that a series of online stories had been generated from the same anonymous account, with slight variation in each version. The journalist could highlight that pattern, but identifying the author would require a separate investigation.

兩人在沒有攝影記者的小餐館碰面。以棠點了鮭魚，知衍喝茶。他坦承自己在布料與獨立設計的世界裡像個外人；她也承認財務語言對自己如同外語。服務生拿熱毛巾問要不要替她揉揉痠痛的手腕，她婉拒了；知衍也沒有伸手碰她。林玥後來說，一連串網路文章都來自同一個匿名帳號，每篇只有些微差異。記者能指出這個模式，但要確認作者，還需要另一場調查。

At the end of lunch, Zhiyan asked what Yitang was truly seeking. “Not prosecution,” she said. “Not revenge. I want my shop to persist without borrowing anyone's name.” She described a quest to establish a design residency for young women and indigenous craft workers, each keeping ownership of their patterns. Zhiyan listened instead of immediately offering money. The silence felt strange until he asked what kind of partnership would render the project sustainable. She had an answer: independent accounts, transparent selection, and the right to leave. He wrote each condition in his notebook, then returned the pen to her side of the table.

午餐尾聲，知衍問以棠到底想得到什麼。「不是起訴，也不是報復。我想讓自己的工作室不借任何人的名字也能生存。」她說起想成立的駐村設計計畫，讓年輕女性與原住工藝工作者都能保有圖樣所有權。知衍沒有立刻開價，而是問怎樣的合作才能讓計畫持續。她的答案是獨立帳戶、透明遴選，以及隨時退出的權利。他把條件逐一記下，再把筆放回她那一側的桌面。

In the evening, Lin Yue sent a draft of her article. It did not turn the chairman into a villain or Yitang into a fairy-tale prize. It described the way an unsolicited rumor could change the cost of renting a studio, hiring workers, and obtaining insurance. A photograph showed Yitang at her cutting table. She read the piece twice and asked for a single correction: she was not an apprentice waiting to be discovered, but the founder who had already completed seven commissions. Lin accepted. The article would not settle every rumor, yet it gave readers a more honest place to begin.

晚上，林玥傳來報導初稿。文章沒有把董事長寫成惡人，也沒有把以棠寫成童話獎品，而是描述未經同意的緋聞如何改變租店、雇工與保險的成本。照片中的以棠站在裁衣桌前。她讀了兩遍，只要求改正一點：她不是等人發掘的學徒，而是已完成七份委託的創辦人。林玥接受修改。這篇文章無法平息所有流言，至少能讓讀者從比較真實的地方開始。

A new message arrived after midnight, asking for a decision before morning. She read it twice, then left it unanswered until she had enough information. He had the resources to make a call that would change the whole schedule, yet he asked her what would happen to the people whose names were not printed on the invitation. For once, the most powerful person in the building was the one willing to wait.

午夜過後，一則新訊息要求她天亮前決定。她讀了兩遍，仍等到資訊足夠才答覆。他有能力一通電話改變所有時程，卻先問起那些名字沒有印在邀請函上的人會如何受影響。那一刻，整棟樓裡最有權力的人，反而是願意等待的那個人。

The following morning gave them no simple victory. A clerk still wanted another signature, and a rumor still crossed the city faster than a correction. She made copies of the relevant pages, signed only the portion she understood, and kept her own original. When he offered a car, she chose to walk the first block. He walked beside her without asking whether the choice was sensible. It was simply hers.

隔天早上沒有輕鬆的勝利。職員仍要求另一個簽名，謠言傳遍城市的速度仍比澄清快。她複印相關頁面，只簽下自己理解的部分，並保留正本。他提議派車，她選擇先走一個街口。他沒有質問這個決定是否明智，只是陪她走。那是她自己的選擇。
