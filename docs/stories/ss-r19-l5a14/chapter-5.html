Mara arrived at the quarantine window carrying a photograph of her father as he had looked before the voyage. She was sixty-two; Captain Arsen appeared barely forty. Neither had prepared for the reversal. He remembered teaching a little girl to tie a rope. Mara remembered burying her mother and waiting twenty years before accepting that the ship would never return. "You look like him," she said, "but you don't remember the life I lived." Arsen tried to apologize, then stopped. No apology could restore those missing decades. Lu Sha offered them a supervised conversation without pressure to embrace. Mara placed the photograph against the glass and asked about a birthday trip to the sea. Arsen described a lighthouse that had already been demolished. Mara sat down slowly. It was a real memory, but not the one she needed him to have.
