A Table for Everyone

凌晨四點，咖啡還是熱的 · 第 2 / 25 章 · 1000–2000 · 英中對照

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

On her second night, Yun found a chain across the small table by the window. Ren explained that the daytime staff used that corner to display cakes, but no one had moved the chain for the night guests. An older woman who worked at the hospital stood beside it, holding a paper cup and looking tired. “Is this seat for members only?” she asked. Yun did not know the answer. There was a membership card near the register, but its rules were old. Ren lifted the chain and offered her the chair. “Tonight, you can sit,” he said. “Tomorrow, we will find out why that sign is there.”

第二晚，芸發現窗邊小桌被一條鏈子攔住。任說白天店員用那個角落展示蛋糕，但沒人替夜間客人把鏈子移開。一名在醫院工作的年長女士站在旁邊，手裡拿著紙杯，滿臉疲憊。「這裡只有會員能坐嗎？」她問。芸不知道答案。收銀機旁有會員卡，規則卻是很久以前訂的。任移開鏈子，請她坐下。「今晚您可以坐，」他說。「明天我們會弄清楚那塊牌子為什麼在這裡。」

Mrs. Lin told them the membership plan had once helped pay for a new oven. Now the money from it was small, and the rules confused people. Yun proposed a trial: keep one table open for anyone who needed a short rest, even if they bought nothing. Ren worried about a possible loss. A free seat might be a lovely idea, but if paying customers had nowhere to sit, the whole café would suffer. They agreed to count the guests for five nights and then respond to the facts. “A fair plan should work in real life,” Ren said. Yun liked that he did not cancel her idea before testing it.

林姨告訴他們，會員制度以前是為了籌錢買新烤箱。如今會員收入不多，規則卻讓客人弄不清楚。芸提議試辦：留一張桌子給需要短暫休息的人，即使他們沒買東西也可以坐。任擔心可能造成損失。免費座位是美好的想法，但如果付費客人沒有地方坐，整間店就會受影響。他們決定連續五晚記錄人數，再根據事實作回應。「公平的計畫也要能實際運作，」任說。芸喜歡他沒有在試驗前就否定自己的主意。

The first test was almost ruined by a chess club that arrived after its final match. Six adults wanted the table at once. One student called it a super idea, while another declared that sharing with strangers would be the worst part of the evening. Yun offered them two smaller tables and a plate of pear slices. Ren placed a guide to the new seating rule on the counter. The students began a quiet game, and the hospital worker read beside them. There was room for both groups. The beauty of the scene was not in perfect order; it was in people making small spaces for one another.

第一晚的測試差點被一群棋社學生打亂。他們剛結束決賽，六名成年人同時想坐那張桌子。一人說這主意超棒，另一人卻說和陌生人共桌會是今晚最糟的事。芸替他們安排兩張小桌，端上一盤梨子片。任在櫃檯放上新座位規則的說明。學生們開始安靜地下棋，醫院工作者則坐在旁邊讀書。兩群人都有了位置。這幅畫面的美不在於整齊，而在於人願意替彼此留出一點空間。

During a break, a teacher named Ms. Wu left a message for the café. Her evening class sometimes ended after the last bus, and she wondered whether her students could wait inside. She also offered to lend a small speaker for announcements on rainy nights. Yun was pleased, but Ren pointed out that a loud speaker could trouble people who needed peace. They tested the volume and chose a soft bell instead. “We are not trying to become a station,” he said. “We want people to hear useful things without losing the quiet.” Yun realized she had not thought about guests who disliked noise.

休息時，吳老師替咖啡店留了訊息。她的晚間課程有時比末班車還晚結束，想問學生能不能來這裡等待。她也願意借一個小喇叭，讓店裡在雨夜廣播消息。芸很開心，任卻提醒，大聲的喇叭會打擾需要安靜的人。他們測試音量，最後改選柔和的鈴聲。「我們不是要開車站，」他說。「我們只想讓人聽見有用的事，同時保住安靜。」芸發現自己忘了考慮不喜歡噪音的客人。

On the fourth night, the count showed an unexpected pattern. The free table brought several customers who later bought soup or tea, but it also attracted people who needed nothing except time. Ren wrote the current numbers on a sheet without calling anyone selfish. “Some people have money, some do not,” he told Yun. “That tells us nothing about their character.” Yun added another column for the length of each visit. She wanted a rule that respected every guest's self, not a system that punished people for being poor. When they compared notes, their different ideas had begun to support each other.

第四晚，統計出現了意外的情況。免費桌帶來幾位之後購買湯或茶的客人，也吸引一些只需要一點時間、什麼都沒買的人。任把目前的數字寫在紙上，沒有把任何人稱作自私。「有些人有錢，有些人沒有，」他對芸說。「這不能說明他們的人品。」芸加上一欄記錄停留時間。她希望規則尊重每一個人的自我，而不是因為貧窮懲罰人。兩人比對筆記時，開始發現不同的想法能互相支撐。

The fifth night brought a real challenge. A man asked to sleep across three chairs while other guests stood waiting. Yun spoke gently but firmly: the café was a place to rest for a while, not a place where one person could take the whole room. The man looked angry and walked outside. Ren followed with a cup of warm water and told him about a nearby shelter that stayed open all night. When he returned, Yun expected an argument. Instead, Ren said, “You held the rule, and I offered another choice. We needed both.” She smiled, surprised by how much the words mattered.

第五晚遇上了真正的考驗。一名男子想橫躺三張椅子睡覺，其他客人只能站著等。芸語氣溫和卻堅定：咖啡店可以短暫休息，但不能讓一個人佔去整個空間。男子生氣地走出門。任帶著溫水追出去，告訴他附近有一處整晚開放的收容中心。回來時，芸以為會挨罵，任卻說：「妳守住規則，我提供另一個選擇。我們需要兩種做法。」她笑了，意外發現這些話對自己很重要。

Their hunt for a fair seating rule had shown a quiet kind of growth. It was not the growth of a rich business or a famous club. It was the growth of trust between people who might never meet again. Yun asked Ren to keep the trial sheet where the next shift could find it. A rule that belonged to everyone, she thought, should not live only inside the heads of two workers.

他們尋找公平座位規則的過程，讓芸看見一種安靜的成長。那不是富有企業或知名社團的成長，而是可能再也不會相遇的人之間逐漸建立的信任。芸請任把試辦記錄放在下一班也找得到的地方。她心想，屬於所有人的規則，不該只存在兩名員工的腦袋裡。

At the end of the trial, Mrs. Lin read their record and removed the old membership sign. She approved one shared table with a clear time limit when the shop was full. Neither worker claimed the plan was perfect. Ren knew the numbers would change in winter; Yun knew some guests would still feel shy about asking for a seat. But the table was open, and it was being used. She drew a small pear beside the new rule, while Ren wrote the hours in his careful hand. They did not call it a victory. The room was simply a little kinder than it had been before.

試辦結束，林姨讀完紀錄，拆下舊會員牌。她同意設一張共享桌，客滿時則訂明確的使用時限。兩人都沒有說這個方案完美。任知道冬季的人數會改變；芸也知道仍有客人不好意思要求座位。但桌子已經開放，而且有人使用。她在新規則旁畫上一顆小梨子，任則工整地寫下時段。他們沒有稱它為勝利，只是這個空間比從前稍微溫柔了一些。
