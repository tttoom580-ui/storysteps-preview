那盞不熄的燈

The Light That Stays On

凌晨四點，咖啡還是熱的 · 第 1 / 25 章 · 1000–2000 · 英中對照

At eleven forty on a wet Monday night, Yun stood outside the Blue Window café with a suitcase and a paper bag of old baking tools. She was twenty-four, old enough to know that a new job would not solve every problem, but young enough to hope it might open a door. The sign promised hot drinks until four in the morning. Inside, a man with a short beard was cleaning the counter. His name was Ren, and he was twenty-seven. He looked at Yun's wet shoes, then at the clock. “You are early,” he said. “That is usually my part.”

星期一晚上十一點四十分，雨還在下，二十四歲的芸提著行李箱和裝滿舊烘焙工具的紙袋，站在「藍窗」咖啡店外。她已經知道新工作不能解決所有問題，卻仍願意相信它能替自己打開一道門。招牌寫著凌晨四點前都有熱飲。店內，一名留著短鬍子的男子正在擦拭櫃檯。他叫任，二十七歲。他先看了看芸濕透的鞋，又看了看時鐘。「妳來早了，」他說。「這通常是我的習慣。」

The owner, Mrs. Lin, placed a plain apron in Yun's hands. “We sell coffee, but we also sell a safe place to sit,” she explained. “Our guests include nurses, drivers, and people who have nowhere quiet to wait.” Yun liked the feeling of the warm room. On one wall hung a tiny drawing of a watermelon; below it was a note from a child thanking the shop for giving his mother water after a long shift. Mrs. Lin said the shop had a lively personality by day and a softer one at night. Yun wondered which side she might belong to.

店主林姨把一件素色圍裙交到芸手上。「我們賣咖啡，也提供一個能安心坐下的地方，」她解釋。「客人有護理師、司機，還有找不到安靜地方等候的人。」芸喜歡這間溫暖小店的感覺。牆上掛著一幅小小的西瓜畫，下面有個孩子留下的字條，感謝店家在媽媽長班結束後給她水喝。林姨說，白天的店很熱鬧，夜裡卻有柔和的另一面。芸心想，不知道自己會屬於哪一面。

Before the first customer arrived, Ren showed her the storeroom. They had to weigh the coffee beans each evening and record every sale, including any tax, in a brown book. He asked her to divide the closing work with him instead of doing everything alone. “I can make the drinks,” she offered. “You can handle the money.” Ren shook his head. “We both learn both jobs. If one of us becomes ill, the other must know what to do.” Yun had worked in kitchens where people protected their own tasks like secrets. This simple rule surprised her more than she expected.

第一位客人來之前，任帶她看儲藏室。他們每天晚上都得秤咖啡豆，把每筆銷售和相關稅額記進一本棕色帳冊。他請芸和他分配打烊工作，而不是一個人包辦。「我可以調飲料，」芸提議。「你來管錢。」任搖頭。「兩個人都得學兩份工作。萬一有人生病，另一個也要知道怎麼做。」芸以前待過的廚房裡，大家把工作當成不能分享的秘密。這條簡單規定反而讓她意外。

At midnight, Mr. Gao the gardener came in carrying a broken pot. A pale leaf stuck out of the soil. He said someone had used a strong chemical near the public garden, and now several plants were dying. “Could you do me a favor?” he asked. “Keep this pot here until morning. I need to speak with the town office.” Ren checked the temperature near the kitchen window and found a cool place for it. Yun set a rubber mat under the pot so that water would not damage the wooden shelf. The little plant seemed strangely important in the quiet café.

午夜時，園丁高先生抱著一個破花盆進門。泥土裡伸出一片蒼白的葉子。他說有人在公共花園附近使用強烈化學藥劑，幾株植物正在枯死。「能幫我一個忙嗎？」他問。「花盆可不可以放到早上？我得去鎮公所問問。」任確認廚房窗邊的溫度，替它找了個涼爽的位置。芸在花盆下面墊了橡膠墊，以免水弄壞木架。安靜的咖啡店裡，這株小植物突然顯得很重要。

A taxi driver entered wearing pajamas under his long coat. He ordered tea and opened a newspaper. Its headline used the word crime for a small argument about parking spaces. Yun frowned at such a large word for a small event. “People say strong things when they are tired,” the driver told her. Ren brought him tea without asking about his clothes. Later Yun asked how he could relate so easily to strangers. “I do not always understand them,” Ren answered. “I only try not to make their night harder.” She wrote that sentence beside her first recipe notes.

一名計程車司機走進來，長外套底下還穿著睡衣。他點了茶，攤開報紙。頭條把一場停車位的小爭執稱作「犯罪事件」。芸皺起眉，覺得這樣的小事不該用那麼大的字眼。「人累的時候，說話就容易變重，」司機對她說。任送上茶，沒追問他的穿著。稍後芸問他怎麼能這麼容易和陌生人相處。「我不一定懂他們，」任回答。「我只是盡量不讓他們今晚更難過。」她把這句話寫在第一頁食譜筆記旁。

At two thirty, Mrs. Lin returned with a thin folder. She said the landlord would review the café's night hours in one month. If the shop could not prove that the late shift was useful and safe, it might have to close after ten. Yun had taken this job to save money for a baking course; losing it so soon would hurt. Ren's eyes moved toward the bright street outside. He admitted that the night guests had no other nearby place to rest. “We cannot decide tonight,” Mrs. Lin said. “First, learn how this room works. Then tell me what you see.”

兩點半，林姨拿著一個薄資料夾回來。她說房東會在一個月後重新審查咖啡店的夜間營業時段。如果店裡不能證明深夜營業有用而且安全，往後可能得在十點關門。芸是為了存烘焙課學費才來上班的，這麼快失去工作會很難受。任望向街上仍亮著的燈，坦白說夜班客人附近沒有別的地方可休息。「我們今晚不用決定，」林姨說。「先學會這個地方怎麼運作，再告訴我你們看見了什麼。」

Near dawn, Yun washed the last cup and looked at the gardener's leaf. She had come expecting a normal kitchen job. Instead, she had found a room that held other people's worries without asking them to leave those worries outside. Ren handed her a piece of toast and waited while she tasted it. It was too dark on one side. She laughed, told him how to turn the heat down, and watched him write her advice on the board. The first shift had not made them friends. Yet she felt she could speak honestly here, and that was a useful beginning.

天快亮時，芸洗好最後一只杯子，看了一眼園丁留下的葉子。她原以為這只是一份普通的廚房工作，卻走進了一間容得下別人煩惱、不要求人把煩惱留在門外的小店。任遞給她一片烤吐司，等她嚐過。吐司有一面烤得太黑。她笑了，教他把火力調低，看著他把建議記在板子上。第一次夜班還沒讓他們成為朋友，但她覺得自己能在這裡說真話。這就是一個有用的開始。
