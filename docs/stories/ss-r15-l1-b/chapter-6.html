老店裡的舊規則

The Old Shop Rule

鈴果村的百變遊戲日 · 第 6 / 25 章 · 1000以下 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

Milo went with Nela to the oldest shop in Bellberry. Its owner, Grandfather Rowan, sat behind a deep wooden desk. His arm was wrapped in a warm cloth because he had carried boxes all morning. “I may have something convenient for you,” he said. He took an envelope from the desk and showed them a drawing of a game his family had played long ago. Players moved stones without touching the muddy ground. The drawing was funny, but its history was real. Grandfather Rowan said the old rule was simple: nobody was out until everyone had a turn.

米洛和妮拉來到鈴果村最老的店。店主羅文爺爺坐在深色木桌後面。他一早搬了許多箱子，手臂上包著保暖的布。「我可能有個方便你們使用的東西，」他說。他從桌裡拿出信封，展示家族很久以前玩過的一種遊戲圖。玩家得移動石頭，卻不能碰到泥地。圖畫有點好笑，卻是真實的歷史。羅文爺爺說，舊規則其實很簡單：在每個人輪過之前，沒有人會被淘汰。

A newspaper boy came into the shop with news of the game project. He asked whether this would be the largest day in village history. “We do not know,” Nela answered. “However, we do know that the seats and paths must be safe.” At that moment, two fish sellers arrived with muddy boots and stood by the door. Grandfather Rowan asked them to speak quietly while a young visitor rested. Nela was surprised by how naturally he welcomed different people into one small room. His shop already had something the children hoped to make: a place with room for more than one kind of person.

送報紙的男孩帶來遊戲計畫的新聞，問這是否會是村史上規模最大的一天。「我們還不知道，」妮拉答，「不過我們知道座位和路線必須安全。」這時兩個賣魚的人穿著沾泥的靴子進店，站在門邊。羅文爺爺請他們輕聲說話，因為有位年幼的客人正在休息。妮拉驚訝地發現，爺爺很自然地歡迎不同的人進入同一個小空間。他的小店已經擁有孩子們希望創造的東西：容得下不同人的地方。

Outside the old shop, the children found a small patch of mud. Milo wanted to make a mark in it to show where the wooden disks should go, but Rowan asked him to use paper instead. Mud would dry and change shape. The marks might disappear before the game began. Nela tried placing disks on a board and saw that it was easier to carry, clean, and check. Rowan smiled. “You have kept the old idea without keeping the old problem,” he said. They thanked him for the lesson, then promised to bring the finished game back so he could try it himself.

老店外面有一小塊泥地。米洛想在泥上畫記號，顯示木圓片應該放哪裡，但羅文請他改用紙。泥會乾掉並改變形狀，遊戲開始前記號可能就消失。妮拉試著把木片放到板上，發現這樣更容易搬運、清理和檢查。羅文笑了。「你們保留舊點子，卻沒有留下舊問題，」他說。孩子們謝謝他的教導，並答應把完成的遊戲帶回來，讓他親自試試。

Rowan asked Milo to show the old wooden disks to a child who had never seen that game before. Milo began to explain too quickly, then noticed the child looking confused. He stopped and demonstrated one move without speaking. The child tried it and understood. Nela wrote down that rule cards should include a picture as well as words. Rowan said it was useful to have different ways of teaching, because people did not all learn alike. The children promised to test the instructions on someone new before calling their game ready.

羅文請米洛把舊木圓片拿給從沒玩過那種遊戲的孩子看。米洛一開始解釋得太快，注意到孩子聽不懂後便停下來，不說話先示範一步。孩子跟著試，立刻明白了。妮拉記下規則卡應該同時有圖畫和文字。羅文說，不同的教法很有用，因為每個人學習的方式都不一樣。孩子們答應，稱遊戲已準備好之前，一定要先讓陌生的新玩家試過說明。

Later, the group sat around a small table to talk about Grandfather Rowan’s wooden disks. Nela asked every helper to give one clear example instead of simply calling the work good or bad. They remembered that the oldest design made more sense when a new child could try it. Milo wrote the example on a clean card and put a small question mark beside the parts they still did not know. They talked about what would happen if a stranger arrived without knowing any of their plans. A person might be shy, tired, or ready to ask a question. Nela said that a friendly game needed a clear answer, not a long speech. The group agreed to make picture rules beside the old drawing. Before moving on, they read the card once more and gave each other time to ask for a change.

稍後，小隊圍著小桌子討論羅文爺爺的木圓片。妮拉請每位助手提出一個清楚例子，而不只是說作品好或不好。他們記得：讓第一次玩的孩子實際操作後，舊設計才更容易理解。米洛把例子寫在乾淨卡片上，還在尚未確定的地方畫個小問號。大家討論，如果陌生訪客什麼計畫都不知道就走進來，會發生什麼事。那個人可能害羞、疲倦，或正準備提問。妮拉說，友善的遊戲需要清楚的回答，而不是長篇演說。小隊同意在舊圖畫旁加上圖解規則。接著大家再次閱讀卡片，也給彼此時間提出修改意見。

The children drew the old game on clean paper and asked whether they could find safe stones in the river. Rowan shook his head. The river was rising, and nobody should walk into deep water for a game. He offered wooden disks from a broken seat instead. Nela took them only after checking their edges. “Do we need to do everything ourselves?” Milo asked. “No,” Rowan answered. “Knowing whom to ask is part of doing good work.” They left the shop with a bag of disks and a stronger rule: a game should not make a player risk harm just to earn a turn.

孩子們把舊遊戲畫在乾淨的紙上，問能不能到河裡找安全的石頭。羅文搖頭。河水正在上漲，沒有人該為了遊戲踏進深水。他改拿出壞椅子拆下的木圓片。妮拉先檢查邊緣，才把圓片收下。「我們是不是每件事都得自己做？」米洛問。「不是，」羅文回答，「知道該請誰幫忙，也是把事情做好的能力。」他們帶著一袋木片離開商店，也訂出更堅定的規則：不能為了得到一次機會，就讓玩家冒險受傷。
