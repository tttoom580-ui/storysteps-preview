沒有雨的葬禮

The Funeral Without Rain

神明集體罷工，人類開始選舉新神 · 第 1 / 25 章 · 5000–6000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

The first sign that the gods were on strike was the queen’s dry coffin. Twelve thousand people waited in the rain court while priests lifted their arms toward an empty sky. The storm god had promised rain at every royal funeral for four centuries. Today the silver clouds remained motionless. Liora, chief clerk of the election archive, did not pray. She watched the bells. Eleven shook in the wind; the twelfth stayed stationary. When the priest tried to abbreviate the official prayer, a little boy in the front row shouted that the god was listening but refusing to answer. The whole crowd heard him.

眾神罷工的第一個徵兆，是女王的棺木上沒有一滴雨。一萬兩千人等在祈雨庭院，祭司向空蕩的天空舉起雙臂。四百年來，每逢王室葬禮，暴風神都承諾降雨。今天銀雲卻紋絲不動。選務檔案室主任莉歐拉沒有祈禱，只注意鐘聲。十一口鐘在風中晃動，只有第十二口靜止不動。祭司試著縮短正式禱文時，前排的小男孩喊道：神明有在聽，只是不願回答。所有人都聽見了。

The boy’s mother clasped him to her bosom. An orderly from the infirmary came to lead them away, but Liora asked the woman to stay. In her hand the mother held an invoice printed on virgin parchment, untouched by the rain that should have fallen. It charged her dead daughter for a miracle performed yesterday. Liora read the name twice. The child had been buried nine years earlier. Under payment, the form listed one year of memory. No law in the kingdom made such a charge applicable to the dead. Yet the state seal was genuine, and beneath it stood a warrant authorizing collection.

男孩的母親把他緊緊抱在胸前。醫務所的勤務員準備帶母子離開，莉歐拉卻請那位婦人留下。母親手中拿著印在全新羊皮紙上的帳單，本該落下的雨一滴也未沾濕紙面。帳單竟向她已死的女兒收取昨天奇蹟的費用。莉歐拉把名字讀了兩遍。那孩子九年前就下葬了。付款欄寫著「一年的記憶」。王國沒有法律允許向死者收這樣的費用，但國家印璽是真的，下方甚至有授權催收的令狀。

Outside the court, volunteers were broiling fish over coals for the mourners. A widow with a limp stood beside the clothing stall, where donated tunics, shawls, and a single bra hung on a rope. Everyone had brought something because the temple kitchens were closed. Liora looked beyond them to the old maritime anchor mounted at the junction of three streets. The anchor was supposed to keep the city’s miracle network grounded. If it had failed, the cost of every prayer might have gone somewhere no priest could see. But a comparative reading of the bronze meters showed a different possibility: nothing was broken. The network had been deliberately disconnected.

庭院外，志工在炭火上炙烤魚，分給參加葬禮的人。一名跛腳寡婦站在衣物攤旁，捐贈的上衣、披肩與一件胸罩掛在繩上。神殿廚房關閉後，人人都帶來能分享的東西。莉歐拉望向三條街交會處，舊海事船錨立在那裡。據說它負責穩定全城奇蹟網路。如果船錨故障，祈禱的代價可能被轉往祭司看不見的地方。然而，兩只銅錶的比較讀數顯示另一種可能：網路沒有壞，而是遭人主動切斷。

A bank teller came forward with a payment register. A gangster employed by a temple contractor had offered him a bribe to delete the names of families who could not pay their memory debts. The teller had refused; now he was afraid that speaking would put his children in danger. Liora told him she would not force him to narrate anything before a safe hearing could be arranged. His eyelid shook as he looked toward the guards. “The account is not only for miracles,” he whispered. “It includes election expenses.” That was the first time Liora understood why the royal council wanted a new god before winter.

銀行出納帶著付款紀錄走上前。一名受神殿承包商雇用的黑幫分子曾向他行賄，要求刪除付不起記憶債務的家庭姓名。出納拒絕了，如今卻害怕開口會危及孩子。莉歐拉告訴他，在安排安全聽證前不會強迫他敘述任何事。他望向守衛時眼皮發抖。「這個帳戶不只支付奇蹟，」他低聲說，「還包括選舉費用。」莉歐拉第一次明白，王室議會為何急著在冬天之前選出新神。

The funeral chamber opened again. Chancellor Varos entered carrying a ballot box, smiling as though the absence of rain were part of the ceremony. He declared that a new god would make the kingdom flourish and that all candidates must accept unconditional service. Liora asked where voters could register a refusal. Varos’s smile tightened. “The choice,” he replied, “is which god will protect you, not whether you will be protected.” Beside her, Nemi, a former temple singer, murmured that gods had once been allowed to resign. Liora had never heard that part of the hymn. She asked Nemi to sing it, but Nemi said the missing verse had been banned.

葬禮廳再次打開。瓦洛斯宰相抱著投票箱走進來，笑得彷彿沒有下雨也是儀式的一部分。他宣稱新神會使王國繁榮，而且每位候選者都必須接受無條件服務。莉歐拉問選民在哪裡登記反對。瓦洛斯的笑容僵住。「選項是由哪位神保護你，」他回答，「不是要不要受到保護。」一旁的前神殿歌者妮米輕聲說，神明過去其實可以辭職。莉歐拉從沒在聖歌裡聽過這件事。她請妮米唱出來，妮米卻說缺失的那一段早已遭禁。

When the votes were laid out, Liora noticed that the names of several candidates had already been printed on the paper before nominations opened. The time available to investigate was finite: Varos intended to close registration that very evening. She slid the dead child’s invoice between two pages of the election register and demanded access to the original account book. For a moment she expected the guards to arrest her. Instead, the twelfth bell rang once, without a hand touching it. Everyone turned toward the sound. Under the ballot box, in lettering older than the kingdom itself, a sentence appeared: WE HAVE NOT ABANDONED YOU. WE HAVE REFUSED TO STEAL FROM YOU.

莉歐拉攤開選票時，發現提名尚未開始，紙上便已印好幾名候選人的名字。她能調查的時間很有限：瓦洛斯打算當晚就關閉登記。她把死去孩子的帳單夾進選舉紀錄，要求調閱原始帳簿。那一刻她以為守衛會逮捕自己。沒想到第十二口鐘在無人碰觸下響了一聲。所有人轉頭望去。投票箱底下浮出比王國更古老的文字：「我們沒有拋棄你們。我們拒絕再從你們身上偷取代價。」
