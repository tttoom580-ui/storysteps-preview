A Letter from the Rebels

兩個不能飛升的人，接下了送信任務 · 第 6 / 25 章 · 4000–5000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

The next town's agenda concerned a rebellion that had ended twelve years earlier. Its young people learned the story as a rigid piece of local theology: the rebels had challenged heaven, heaven had punished them, and good citizens should not ask further questions. A municipal teacher named Fen challenged that lesson. She kept a notebook of stories, from heroic songs to ordinary accounts of farming, and insisted that history did not belong to a single genre. Wen arrived during a school debate and heard a boy compare the old conflict to the final inning of a baseball game. The metaphor made some parents laugh; others called it disrespectful. Fen welcomed the comparison but asked for the facts behind it. “A story can help us understand an event,” she said, “yet an easy story can also hide people who were never given a chance to speak.” Yu noticed a faded map on her wall that marked a bell station north of town.

下一座小鎮正在討論十二年前結束的一場叛亂。年輕人把那段歷史當成僵硬的地方教義來學：叛軍挑戰天命，天命懲罰了他們，良民不該再追問。市立學校教師阿芬卻質疑這種教法。她收集各種故事，從英雄歌謠到尋常農事記錄，堅持歷史不只屬於一種文類。李聞抵達時，學校正舉行辯論。一名男孩把舊衝突比成棒球賽最後一局，有些家長笑了，有些罵他不敬。阿芬歡迎比喻，卻要求他拿出支持比喻的事實。「故事能幫助理解事件，」她說，「但太容易的故事也會藏起那些從未得到發言機會的人。」沈嶼注意到牆上褪色地圖，標著小鎮北方的一座鐘驛。

The teacher received a letter from a former rebel who had spent years repairing water channels beyond the border. He warned her to beware of a new official history that described all villagers as willing supporters of the war. Some had simply tried to shield their neighbors. The letter included a drawing of an organism living in the mountain springs, a harmless worm that became toxic only when a certain mineral mixed with the water. The details sounded strange, but Fen recognized them from local science lessons. She brought a jar of vinegar to demonstrate how easily two ordinary materials could produce an unexpected reaction. “A reaction is not a verdict,” she reminded the class. Yu wanted to check the spring immediately. Wen suggested that they first ask why the letter had taken twelve years to arrive. The envelope carried a fresh postmark despite its old handwriting. Someone had kept it, then deliberately put it back into circulation.

阿芬收到一封前叛軍寄來的信。寄件者多年來在邊界外修繕水道，信中提醒她提防新版官方史書：書裡把所有村民都寫成自願參戰的人，事實上有些人只想保護鄰居。信還畫了一種住在山泉裡的生物；那是無害小蟲，只有特定礦物混入水中時才會產生毒性。內容聽來奇怪，阿芬卻認出這與當地自然課所教的知識相符。她拿來一罐醋，示範兩種普通物質如何產生意外反應。「反應不是判決，」她提醒學生。沈嶼想立刻查泉水，李聞則建議先問，為什麼這封信拖了十二年才送到。信封雖有舊字跡，郵戳卻是新的。有人收藏它多年，然後有意重新寄出。

At the town hall a clerk identified the sender as a former nominee for a federal council that had never been created. The council's plan had been to share control of the mountain springs among districts, but the war overtook it. A stone monument showed only a skeleton of the original proposal, with names removed and dates rearranged. Yu became furious at the missing records. Wen reached for his sleeve, afraid that anger would carry him into another conflict. Yu pulled away. “You don't have to guard every rib in my body,” he snapped. Wen answered quietly, “And you don't have to make every question into a fight.” The words hurt because both were partly true. Later, in the empty classroom, Yu apologized for pulling away. Wen said that he could accept an angry answer; he only needed to know Yu would not risk their letters for the sake of being right. They agreed to separate questions from accusations before approaching officials again.

在鎮公所，一名書記認出寄件者曾被提名為聯邦議會議員，但那議會根本沒有成立。原方案打算讓各地區共享山泉管理權，戰爭卻搶先摧毀計畫。石碑上只剩原提案的骨架，名字被刪、日期被重排。沈嶼對消失的紀錄憤怒不已。李聞伸手抓他袖子，怕怒氣把他捲入另一場衝突。沈嶼抽開手：「我身上每一根肋骨都不需要你護著。」李聞輕聲回答：「你也不必把每個問題都變成吵架。」這話刺痛兩人，因為各自都有幾分道理。稍後在空教室裡，沈嶼為抽開手道歉。李聞說，他可以接受生氣的回答，只需要知道沈嶼不會為了證明自己對就拿信件冒險。兩人約定，下次面對官員前要把問題與指控分開。

Their last delivery that day was to an elderly woman who had lost her brother during the rebellion. When she read his surviving letter, she began to sob. The pages did not explain why he had vanished, but they proved that he had tried to send her news. Wen felt grief cling to his own coat, as though her sorrow had found a place beside his. The woman gave no public testimony; she asked only that her brother's name be entered correctly in the local memorial book. Fen promised to help her. Afterward Yu and Wen walked along the silent schoolyard, past a painted scene of warriors facing an enormous sky. “I believed the old story because it gave everything a simple ending,” Yu said. Wen looked toward the mountain, where the missing station should have stood. “Perhaps letters are different,” he answered. “A letter means the story is still going somewhere.” For the first time, Yu reached for Wen's hand and did not let go at once.

當天最後一封信送給一位在叛亂中失去兄長的老婦。她讀到兄長留下的信，忍不住啜泣。紙頁不能解釋兄長為何失蹤，卻證明他曾努力送出消息。李聞感到哀傷黏上自己的外衣，彷彿她的悲痛也在他身旁找到位置。老婦不願公開作證，只請求在地方紀念冊上正確寫下兄長名字。阿芬答應幫忙。之後兩人走過安靜的校園，旁邊牆畫上武士面對巨大天空的場景。「我相信舊故事，是因為它讓一切有個簡單結局。」沈嶼說。李聞望著理應矗立舊驛站的山頭：「但信不一樣。信表示故事還在前往某個地方。」沈嶼第一次主動握住他的手，沒有立刻鬆開。

Fen also warned the couriers not to let the desire for a quick answer overtake their responsibility to the people named in the records. Yu wrote her advice in the margin of his map. He had once measured success by how fast he moved. Now he was learning when to stop.

阿芬也提醒信差，不要讓急著找答案的欲望超越對紀錄中人們的責任。沈嶼把這話寫在地圖邊緣。他過去用行進速度衡量成功，如今正在學習何時該停下。
