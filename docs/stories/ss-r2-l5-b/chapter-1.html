不肯上天的信

The Letters That Refused Heaven

On the morning the heavens closed, Li Wen stood outside a chapel built into the eastern wall of Greybridge. A cluster of failed ascension candidates waited beside him, all pretending to feel certainty about what came next. Wen was twenty-seven, old enough to understand that a second chance might never arrive. He had climbed the Stair of Clouds seven days earlier and turned back when a child stumbled on the final step. The judges called his action an impulse, not a sacrifice. Now his former supervisor offered compensation in silver if he would sign a statement that disclosed nothing about the child. Wen refused the transaction. An immense bell rang inside the chapel, and its sound seemed to wither before reaching the street. Someone in the crowd whispered that the sound had once carried messages between mountain temples. The city had kept that heritage even after the system failed. Wen wondered whether a broken tradition could still serve the living.

天門關閉的早晨，李聞站在灰橋城東牆內的一座小禮拜堂外。一群飛升失敗者聚在他身旁，人人都假裝自己確信接下來的路該怎麼走。二十七歲的李聞明白，第二次機會也許永遠不會來。七天前，他登上雲梯，卻在最後一階看見一名孩子跌倒，於是轉身去扶。審判者稱那只是衝動，並非犧牲。如今昔日的監督官拿出銀兩作為補償，要他簽署一份對孩子隻字不提的聲明。李聞拒絕了這筆交易。禮拜堂裡響起巨大的鐘聲，聲音卻彷彿還沒傳到街上就枯萎了。人群中有人低語，從前這鐘聲曾在山寺間傳遞訊息。即使傳訊系統失效，城市仍保存這份遺產。李聞心想，破損的傳統是否仍能為活人所用。

Inside the chapel, a public forum had become a desperate job market. A metropolitan official spoke about the lost generator beneath the mountain, while displaced families asked who would keep the lamps burning. Their old habitat, a wet valley beyond the eastern ridge, had dried into dust when the sky routes shut down. At a table near the door, Wen met Shen Yu, a thirty-year-old mapmaker with one pale scar across his hand. Yu had also failed to ascend. He was not envious of those who had passed; he was angry that they had left no address. On the table lay a bundle of letters bound with blue cord. A handwritten provision on the cover explained three rules: a courier must never break the seal, each transfer must be entered in a witness ledger, and a bell stone could play a sender's voice only with the sender's permission. There was an optional fourth rule, written in pencil: do not travel alone. Yu pointed at it. “That one,” he said, “is the only rule I added myself.”

禮拜堂內的公開論壇已變成絕望的求職市場。來自都城的官員談著山下失去作用的靈力發生器，流離失所的家庭則只想知道誰能讓燈火繼續亮著。他們原本居住在東方山脊外的濕潤谷地；天空驛道關閉後，那裡乾成了塵土。李聞在門邊的一張桌旁遇見三十歲的繪圖師沈嶼，他手上有一道淡淡的疤。沈嶼同樣沒有飛升。他並不嫉妒成功者，只氣他們離開時連地址都沒有留下。桌上有一束藍繩綑著的信，封面手寫了三條規定：信差不得拆封；每一次轉交都必須記入見證簿；鐘石只有在寄件者同意時，才能播放寄件者的聲音。鉛筆還加了可選的第四條：不要獨自上路。沈嶼指著那一行說：「這一條，是我唯一自己加的。」

Wen asked why anyone would trust two men who had failed the highest examination in the world. Yu gave a small, unexpected smile. “Because the winners are missing.” He showed Wen a route drawn through abandoned gardens and mountain villages, not the official road that led straight to the capital. His insight was practical: letters were lighter than food, but they could tell scattered families where food had been stored. One letter came from a mother who wanted to tell her son that she was alive. Another was addressed simply to the Keeper of the First Post, a person neither man knew. An old woman brought a tiny packet of seeds to accompany a message, then withdrew it when Yu explained that they could carry only what the ledger recorded. “You can keep your seeds,” Wen told her. “We can bring the promise instead.” She placed her thumbprint in the book. Outside, the remaining trees shook in a wind that carried no rain.

李聞問，世上最高考試的兩個失敗者，憑什麼要人相信。沈嶼意外地微微一笑：「因為贏的人都不見了。」他拿出一張穿過荒廢花園和山村的地圖，而不是直通都城的官道。他的洞察很務實：信比食物輕，卻能讓離散家人知道糧食存在哪裡。一封信來自想向兒子報平安的母親；另一封只寫著「初郵站守站人收」，兩人都不認識這個收件者。一位老婦帶來一小包種子，想連同訊息送走，但沈嶼說，他們只能帶見證簿登記過的物品，老婦便收回種子。「種子妳留著，」李聞說，「我們替妳帶去承諾。」老婦在簿上按下指印。門外僅存的樹木在無雨的風中搖晃。

Before sunset, Wen followed Yu into the old bell tower. The stone inside was cold and almost silent. Yu explained that it was not a magical shortcut: it stored an authorized voice, but it could not move a letter or prove the truth of what the voice said. Wen liked that limitation. It made the work honest. The clerk read out their first destination, Reed Village, and handed them twenty-six ordinary letters and one sealed black envelope. There would be no grand parade, no medal, no guarantee of success. Wen did not ask for one. On the steps, he nearly stumbled again, this time over a loose stone. Yu caught his sleeve without laughing. For a moment neither moved. Then Wen lifted the canvas mail bag onto his shoulder. “If the sky will not carry our words,” he said, “we will carry them on the ground.” Yu opened the gate, and they stepped onto a road that had been removed from every official map.

日落前，李聞跟著沈嶼進入舊鐘樓。裡面的鐘石冰冷，幾乎不再作聲。沈嶼解釋，它不是能抄近路的法寶：只能儲存獲授權的聲音，不能搬運信件，也不能證明錄下的話就是真實的。李聞喜歡這種限制，因為它讓工作保持誠實。書記念出第一個目的地「蘆葦村」，交給他們二十六封普通信和一封黑色密封信。不會有盛大遊行，沒有獎章，也沒有成功保證。李聞沒有要求任何保證。下階梯時，他又差點被鬆動的石頭絆倒；沈嶼伸手抓住他的袖子，沒有取笑。兩人短暫停在原地，接著李聞把帆布郵袋背上肩。「天空若不肯替我們帶話，」他說，「我們就走在地上把話送到。」沈嶼推開門，他們踏上一條已從所有官圖刪除的道路。
