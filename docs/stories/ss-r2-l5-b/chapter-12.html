水晶競技場

The Crystal Arena

兩個不能飛升的人，接下了送信任務 · 第 12 / 25 章 · 4000–5000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

The next station had become an arena for public demonstrations. Travelers paid to watch a celebrated cultivator move objects through a crystal ring, and a celebrity merchant sold pieces of supposedly sacred stone at the door. Wen and Yu came to deliver Ren's letter to an apprentice named Nian, but they found her arranging chairs for a legal session. She had filed a lawsuit against the merchant after discovering fraud in his certificates. The man claimed that his crystals could reverse illness and replace ordinary nutrition; he also promised that they were the ultimate protection against the failed sky network. Nian had tested several pieces and found them random stones from a local quarry. The court had agreed to examine the dispute, yet a ban on public demonstrations made the hearing difficult to attend. Wen knew that the dispute was not theirs to decide. He asked Nian whether she still wanted the letter and waited while she cleared a space for it on her desk.

下一站變成公開示範的競技場。旅人付錢看一位知名修士用水晶環移動物品，門口則有名人商人販售所謂聖石。李聞與沈嶼要把任師傅的信送給學徒阿念，卻發現她正在布置法律聽證的座椅。她發現商人證書造假後，已提出訴訟。那人宣稱水晶能逆轉疾病、取代正常營養，甚至能提供抵禦失效天空網路的終極保護。阿念檢驗數塊，發現只是當地採石場的普通石頭。法庭答應審理，但禁止公開示範的規定使民眾難以參與。李聞知道這場爭議不由他們裁決。他問阿念是否仍要收信，等她騰出桌面才把信交過去。

Nian read the letter and smiled at Ren's familiar handwriting. He had offered no advice on the lawsuit, only a reminder that her care with tools had once saved a stranger. She showed Yu her portfolio of repairs and explained how the merchant had used her drawings without permission. At first she had become depressed, believing that no one would trust her work again. Then she recorded the evidence, sought independent testing, and returned to the workshop. “My intent is not to ruin him,” she said. “I want customers to know what they are buying.” Wen admired the distinction. In the public corridor, a guard tried to shove a group of elderly witnesses away from the entrance. Nian stepped between them and pointed to the notice allowing testimony. The guard backed off. A woman carrying a rack of wooden stools offered seats to those who had been standing. Ordinary practical kindness, Wen thought, often changed the atmosphere of a room before any judge spoke.

阿念讀信時，看見任師傅熟悉的字跡便笑了。他沒對訴訟下指導棋，只提醒她過去細心修工具曾救過一個陌生人。她給沈嶼看自己的維修作品集，解釋商人如何未經許可使用她的圖紙。起初她非常沮喪，以為再也沒人相信自己的作品。後來她記下證據、尋求獨立檢驗，又回到工坊。「我不是要毀了他，」她說，「我想讓顧客知道自己買的是什麼。」李聞佩服這種區分。公共走廊上，一名守衛想把幾位年長證人推離入口。阿念擋在中間，指著允許作證的公告，守衛便退開。一位婦人推來擺滿木凳的架子，讓久站的人坐下。李聞心想，平凡而實際的善意，往往能在法官開口前就改變房間氣氛。

The hearing lasted two hours. The merchant's lawyer argued that words such as “healing” could have many meanings, but the certificates had listed specific claims. The judge examined the tests and asked why buyers had been told to abandon medical prescriptions. The merchant could not explain. Outside, a child tried to poke the crystal ring, and Yu gently guided his hand away from its sharp edge. Wen remained in the public gallery rather than joining the argument. He had learned that helping did not always mean speaking. By the end of the session, the judge ordered clearer labels and a refund process pending further review. It was not a final judgment about every crystal, only a specific decision based on evidence. Nian accepted it with relief. Then she showed Yu an old set of travel permits written in plural forms for several couriers traveling together. One belonged to the First Post, and it carried the same three-circle stamp.

聽證持續兩小時。商人的律師辯稱「療癒」等詞有多種解釋，但證書上明明寫了具體效果。法官檢視檢驗結果，追問為何顧客被勸停止使用醫療處方，商人答不出。走廊外一名小孩想戳水晶環，沈嶼輕輕引開他的手，避開尖銳邊緣。李聞留在旁聽席，不參與爭論。他已學會，幫忙不一定代表要開口。聽證結束時，法官下令改善標示，並在進一步審查期間安排退款程序。那不是對所有水晶的最終判決，只是依現有證據作出的具體決定。阿念鬆口氣地接受。接著她拿出幾張以複數形式填寫的舊通行證，供多位信差同行，其中一張屬於初郵站，蓋著相同的三圓印記。

Nian offered the two men a room in the residential quarter behind the arena. They shared a modest meal and talked about the strange distance between a public claim and the person who had to live with its consequences. Yu wondered whether the First Post had been a single person, a position, or an entire group. Nian thought the permits suggested several workers acting under one title. “A title is easier to protect than a person,” she said, “and easier to use for fraud.” Wen placed the permits' reference numbers beside their earlier evidence. A series of apparently separate failures now formed a pattern: removed stations, hidden records, and messages delayed for years. Yet he refused to select a culprit at random. When they left the arena the following day, the merchant's bright signs were still hanging, and the work of correcting public trust had only begun. Wen tightened the mail bag and followed Yu toward the reservoir road.

阿念讓兩人住進競技場後的住宅區。他們吃了頓簡單晚餐，談論公開宣稱與必須承受後果的人之間，那種奇特距離。沈嶼猜想初郵站究竟是一個人、一種職位，還是一整群人。阿念認為通行證顯示多名工作者共用一個稱號。「頭銜比人容易被保護，」她說，「也更容易被拿來造假。」李聞把通行證編號與先前證據放在一起。原本看似互不相干的失敗漸漸成形：驛站被撤、紀錄遭藏、訊息延誤多年。但他拒絕隨機指認罪魁禍首。隔天離開競技場時，商人鮮豔招牌仍掛著，重建公共信任的工作才剛開始。李聞拉緊郵袋，跟沈嶼走向水庫道路。
