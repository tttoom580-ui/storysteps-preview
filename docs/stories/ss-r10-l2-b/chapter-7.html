A Necklace and a Name

把市場的聲音，留到明天 · 第 7 / 25 章 · 1000–2000 · 英中對照

The next recording came with a necklace. Mrs. Tan, a retired shopkeeper, said that its small stone had belonged to her mother. She did not want anyone to call it a royal treasure; the family had bought it at a simple stall. May asked for its actual origin, and Mrs. Tan gave the address of a shop that no longer existed. Evan wanted an expert to examine the metal, but Mrs. Tan reminded him that the story was about memory, not price. He gave a nod and put the necklace back in its soft bag before turning on the recorder.

下一份錄音與一條項鍊有關。退休店主譚姨說，項鍊上的小石頭原屬母親。她不希望別人把它稱作王室寶物；家人是在普通攤位買來的。慧詢問真正的來源，譚姨說出一家早已消失的店址。安澤想找專家檢查金屬，但譚姨提醒他，故事重點是記憶而非價格。他點點頭，把項鍊放回軟袋，才開啟錄音。

During the interview, a listener from the next stall arrived to complain about a rude sound he had heard at midnight. It was a man calling another seller a prisoner of the market. May checked the time and found that the phrase came from a stage play, not a real attack. The actor had played a naughty servant who feared his own boss. Without that role, the joke sounded cruel. Evan made a note: if a scene needed five pages of explanation, perhaps it did not belong in a short public display. They should sort stories by meaning as well as sound.

訪談時，隔壁攤位的一位聽眾來抱怨，他在午夜聽到一段粗魯的聲音：有人把另一位攤商稱為市場的囚犯。慧查了時間，發現那句話出自舞臺劇，不是真正的攻擊。演員當時扮演害怕老闆的淘氣僕人。離開那個角色，笑話就顯得殘酷。安澤記下：若一段表演需要五頁說明，或許就不適合短短的公共展示。他們應按照意義與聲音整理故事。

Mrs. Tan offered them toast and tea. On her shelf stood a row of china cups, a book of folk songs, and an old camera with a thick lens. She spoke about the pleasant noise of preparing breakfast when her children were young. “None of them live near me now,” she said, “but they call every week.” May asked whether the whole family might hear the recording before release. Mrs. Tan agreed with relief. Evan realized that careful questions were becoming easier for him. He no longer heard a pause as a failure; he heard a chance for someone to choose.

譚姨請他們吃吐司、喝茶。架子上排著瓷杯、民歌書，以及裝有厚鏡頭的舊相機。她說起孩子小時候準備早餐的熱鬧聲音。「如今孩子們都不住附近，」她說，「但每週都會打電話。」慧問全家人能否在公開前先聽過錄音。譚姨鬆了口氣，答應了。安澤發現自己愈來愈懂得提問。他不再把停頓當成失敗，而是聽見讓對方自己選擇的機會。

A music student later asked them to add a stronger beat under the interview. Evan almost agreed out of habit. May suggested asking Mrs. Tan first. She refused because the sound reminded her of a busy train station, not the quiet home she remembered. Evan apologized and removed it. “Your advice saves my work from becoming too clever,” he told May. She raised an eyebrow. “I thought you hated being corrected.” “I do,” he said with a smile, “but I dislike being wrong even more.” May laughed, and the old room seemed warmer around them.

稍晚，一位音樂系學生提議在訪談下方加強節拍。安澤差點習慣性答應，慧提醒他先問譚姨。譚姨拒絕了，因為那聲音使她想到忙碌車站，而非記憶中的寧靜家園。安澤道歉後把節拍移除。「妳的建議讓我的作品不會聰明過頭，」他對慧說。慧挑起眉毛：「我以為你討厭被糾正。」「是討厭，」他笑著說，「但我更討厭自己犯錯。」慧笑了，老房間似乎也跟著溫暖起來。

Mrs. Tan asked to see the permission form once more before signing it. May read each line aloud and paused when the woman raised a finger. The form gave the team permission to play one approved version at the market, but nothing beyond that. It did not allow a company to sell the audio or a student to put it on a personal page. Evan added the date, showed Mrs. Tan the exact file name, and offered a phone number for future requests. She wrote a short note in the blank space: “Please tell my children before making changes.” May promised to contact them, not to assume what they would say. When Mrs. Tan finally signed, she thanked the students for giving her time. Evan felt that this single signature carried more trust than a room full of applause.

譚姨簽字前又要求看一次授權表格。慧逐行念給她聽，女士抬手時便停下。表格只允許團隊在市場播放一個核准版本，不包含其他用途。它不准公司販售音檔，也不准學生貼到私人頁面。安澤填上日期，向譚姨展示確切檔名，並留下未來聯絡電話。她在空格寫下：「修改前請先通知我的孩子。」慧答應會聯絡孩子，而不會預設他們的回答。譚姨最後簽字時，謝謝學生願意給她時間。安澤覺得，這一個簽名承載的信任，比滿屋掌聲還多。

Before leaving, Mrs. Tan asked them to return for her birthday dinner. May said she had to check her class schedule, and Evan said he would help carry tables if needed. Neither wanted to use a kind invitation as a reason to own more of the woman's life. Outside, Evan asked whether May might like to hear a concert with him someday, away from the project. She said maybe, but not during the coming festival. He accepted that answer without asking why. They walked to the bus stop talking about the necklace, and their silence afterward was easy.

離開前，譚姨邀他們參加生日晚餐。慧說要先看課表，安澤說若有需要，他可以幫忙搬桌子。他們都不想因為善意邀請就覺得有權進一步介入她的生活。走到外面，安澤問慧是否有一天願意在工作之外與他一起聽音樂會。慧說也許可以，但不是這次節慶期間。他沒有追問原因，接受了答案。兩人一邊聊項鍊一邊走向公車站，之後的沉默也很自在。
