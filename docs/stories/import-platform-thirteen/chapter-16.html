Reading the Rules Backward

第十三站台 · 第 16 / 25 章 · 5000–6000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

The restricted passage took them into a former hospital ward that had been remodeled as an examination hall. Each contestant received a sheet of rules and a cup of bitter coffee filled with caffeine. A sign warned that the rules would accumulate with every mistake. A tired nurse offered detergent for cleaning their hands and advised them to accustom themselves to the routine. Jianxia refused to drink anything. Across the room, a small robot dressed like a goalkeeper guarded a red doorway. “It's a test of endurance,” Zhou Yan said. “But not the sort measured by how long we can stay awake.” He had begun to believe the station's commands should be read from the opposite direction.

限制通道通向改造成考場的舊醫院病房，每名參賽者拿到一張規則和一杯苦咖啡，裡面咖啡因濃得驚人。告示警告每犯一次錯就會累加新規則。疲倦護士提供清潔手用洗滌劑，勸他們習慣例行程序。見夏拒喝任何東西。房內一台守門員造型的小機器人守著紅門。「這是耐力測試，」周硯說，「但不是考誰能保持清醒多久。」他開始相信車站指令應該從反方向閱讀。

The first rule said not to trust an affectionate gesture from a stranger. The second forbade anyone from questioning an official record. The third insisted that the hospital must modernize by removing all outdated patient histories. Shen Man pointed out that these rules, taken literally, would erase every form of care. “Reverse them,” she suggested. “Trust kindness when you can test it. Question records. Preserve histories.” He Chuan studied the examination clock, which displayed diagrams of astronomy instead of numbers. The passing constellations were mapped to patients whose names had been replaced by symbols. One symbol resembled a radish, absurd among the stars. Yet its location matched the laboratory in the old plantation photograph.

第一條規則禁止信任陌生人的親切舉動；第二條禁止質疑官方紀錄；第三條主張醫院現代化，移除過時病史。沈漫指出照字面遵守會抹去所有照護可能。「倒過來讀，」她提議，「在可驗證時相信善意，質疑紀錄，保存病史。」賀川研究考場時鐘，數字變成天文星圖，移動星座對應姓名被符號替代的病人。一個符號像蘿蔔，在星星間滑稽至極，但它的位置竟對應舊種植園照片裡的實驗室。

Jianxia found two patient charts, one marked ORIGINAL and one marked CLONE. They described the same boy but disagreed about the day he had been injured. She remembered seeing two versions of Zhou Yan in the laboratory. “These aren't copies of people,” she said, realizing something important. “They're copies of outcomes. The system tries to preserve whichever version supports its own story.” Zhou Yan took a moment to gauge her expression. “Then my memories are partly subjective,” he said. “But the pain was real.” Shen Man sympathized with him. She knew from medicine that a record might be wrong without the patient's suffering being imaginary. For the first time, Zhou Yan looked almost relieved. That realization unsettled her more than the sight of the two identical men.

見夏找到兩份病歷，標著「原版」和「複製體」，描述同一男孩，受傷日期卻不同。她想起實驗室裡兩個周硯。「它複製的不是人，」她恍然大悟，「而是結果。系統保留最能支持自己故事的版本。」周硯觀察她表情片刻：「那我的記憶有一部分是主觀的，但痛是真的。」沈漫表示理解；醫學紀錄錯誤不代表病人的痛苦是虛構。周硯第一次像真正鬆了一口氣。 這份領悟比看見兩個一模一樣的男人更讓她不安。

At the end of the hall, a nurse asked them to vote on a definitive version of the past. She claimed uncertainty would make them unfit for release. Jianxia replied that uncertainty was sometimes the most responsible answer. The nurse became irritated. She offered to rewrite their memories so all four would agree perfectly, promising a life free from disputes. He Chuan laughed. “That sounds less like harmony than forced silence.” The nurse said such an arrangement resembled socialism, as though one political label could justify eliminating individual choice. Jianxia refused the false comparison. “No system gets to decide what we feel merely by naming an ideology,” she said. The nurse's face flickered and the screen reset.

走廊盡頭護士要求他們投票決定「唯一確定的過去」，聲稱容忍不確定就不配釋放。見夏回答，不確定有時才是最負責的答案。護士不耐，表示願替四人改寫記憶，讓大家永遠一致、沒有爭執。賀川笑道：「這不像和諧，比較像強迫沉默。」護士把這種安排粗暴地比作社會主義，彷彿一個政治標籤就能替剝奪選擇辯護。見夏拒絕這種錯誤類比：「不能光靠替意識形態命名，就決定我們該感覺什麼。」護士的臉閃動，螢幕重置。

Zhou Yan rearranged the warning sheets on the examination desk. Read backward, the rules formed a sequence of questions: Who was helped? Which record was hidden? What happened before the bell? They answered them with details gathered from the previous stations. With each answer, the lights in the ward changed from red to white. The robot goalkeeper stepped aside. A door opened into a maintenance shaft, where handwritten messages covered the walls. One said the system could not distinguish obedience from willing cooperation. Another explained that a single act of care could disrupt its predictions. “Your brother mapped all this,” Zhou Yan said. Jianxia touched the faded writing. She recognized the slant of the letters.

周硯把警告紙逆向排列，規則變成一串問題：「誰得到幫助？哪份紀錄被藏起？鐘響前發生什麼？」他們用先前關卡細節回答，每答對一題，病房燈光由紅轉白。機器守門員讓開，門後是維修井，牆上滿是手寫留言。一句說系統無法分辨服從和自願合作，另一句說單一善行能破壞預測。「妳哥哥畫出了這一切，」周硯說。見夏摸著褪色筆跡，認出熟悉的傾斜筆畫。

At the shaft's end, a recording played a boy's voice repeating a warning to his younger sister. Jianxia had heard it before, but this time it continued: “When the rules become too many to remember, remember the people. The machine can predict fear more easily than love.” She felt weary to the bone, yet the words gave her strength. A hidden monitor showed the station's true plan: to sort survivors into categories and keep whichever minds were easiest to control. Her brother had not merely tried to escape it; he had left a method for disrupting it. Jianxia copied the instructions into her notebook. Then a message appeared in red: INFORMANT DETECTED IN YOUR GROUP.

維修井末端播放哥哥對妹妹的警告，這次多了後半段：「當規則多到記不住，就記得人。機器比起愛，更容易預測恐懼。」見夏疲憊到骨子裡，卻重新有力量。隱藏螢幕顯示車站真正計畫：把倖存者分類，留下最容易控制的心智。哥哥不只是逃亡，還留下干擾系統的方法。見夏把指示抄進筆記本，紅色訊息突然跳出：「偵測到隊伍裡有告密者。」
