不可回頭規則

The Rule Against Turning Back

The carriage smelled of burnt sugar and old rain. A bass note vibrated through the floor while an advertisement offered miraculous hospitality at a hotel that had burned down twenty years earlier. When the train stopped, a sign announced PLATFORM 13 and a second notice appeared in deliberately colloquial language: KEEP WALKING. DON'T LOOK BACK. A shabby curtain divided the exit passage from the tracks. Jianxia tried to lift it, but Zhou Yan caught her sleeve. Behind the curtain came a familiar voice saying, “Little sister, it's me.” Her brother had always called her that. Yet he had disappeared at sea, not in a subway station. She stood motionless until the voice began to imitate the sound of his laughter, syllable by syllable.

車廂裡瀰漫焦糖與陳雨味，低沉的低音震動地板。廣告推銷一家二十年前早已燒毀的旅館，承諾神奇的款待。列車停下時，站牌顯示第十三站台。另一張公告刻意用口語寫著：「繼續前進。不要回頭。」破舊布簾隔開出口走道與軌道。見夏伸手想掀，周硯卻抓住她的袖子。簾後傳來熟悉的聲音：「妹妹，是我。」哥哥向來這樣叫她，但他是在海上失蹤，不是在地鐵站。她一動不動，直到那聲音開始逐音節模仿哥哥的笑。

Shen Man asked whether they were supposed to abide by every rule or only those displayed in red. Zhou Yan pointed to a stamp at the bottom: VALID UNTIL THE NEXT BELL. “It will expire in nine minutes,” he said. He Chuan wanted to test it immediately. “A rash decision is not an experiment,” Shen Man warned. The elderly woman disagreed; she heard her daughter crying from the other side of the curtain. Jianxia tried to reconcile the two urgent facts: a rule might save them, and the rule itself might be lying. The light above them suddenly tilted as if the entire platform were a moving ship. In the dark window, an alligator floated through black water, perfectly silent.

沈漫問他們究竟得遵守所有規則，還是只守紅字的。周硯指出底部印章：「僅於下次鐘響前有效。」「九分鐘後就過期，」他說。賀川想立刻測試，沈漫提醒莽撞行事不叫實驗。老婦人卻不贊同，她聽見女兒在簾後哭。見夏試著調和兩個急迫事實：規則可能救命，也可能本身就撒謊。頭頂的燈忽然傾斜，整座月台像船一樣搖晃。漆黑車窗裡，一隻鱷魚悄悄漂過黑水。

The platform's first doorway opened onto a commonplace waiting room with plastic chairs and a water cooler. Someone had placed a full liter bottle beside five paper cups. “A trap,” He Chuan muttered. “Or an invitation,” said Jianxia. A painted spotlight on the ceiling followed whichever passenger spoke. Each time it crossed the room, a fuse in an electrical box spat blue sparks. Jianxia noticed that the bottled water was ordinary, but the cups had names written underneath them. There were six names, including hers and one she did not recognize: RETURNED. The old woman's name was absent. “The room already knows who won't reach the exit,” Jianxia whispered. Nobody drank.

第一扇門通向一間普通得詭異的候車室，擺著塑膠椅和飲水機。旁邊放了一公升瓶裝水和五個紙杯。「陷阱，」賀川說。「也可能是邀請，」見夏回應。天花板上的聚光燈圖案竟會追著說話的人移動，每掃過一次，電箱裡的保險絲便迸出藍火。水看來普通，紙杯底卻寫了人名。共有六個名字，其中有她，也有一個不認識的：「歸來者」。老婦人的名字不在其中。「房間早知道誰到不了出口，」見夏低聲說。沒有人喝水。

A recording began to affirm that every guest would receive a comfortable room. Then it described the hotel guests who had once shared an infectious laugh while a fire spread through locked hallways. Shen Man examined the map and found the emergency stairs missing. “It uses kindness to make us stay,” she said. At twilight, the recording continued, the guests had been offered syrup for their coughs and whiskey for their fear. The building collapsed before morning. Zhou Yan found the hotel registry under a desk. All the names were crossed out, except for a novice employee who had been assigned to welcome guests. His photograph was identical to the janitor's badge in the station. The realization turned Jianxia's stomach colder than the air.

廣播開始保證每位客人都能得到舒適房間，接著又描述昔日旅館住客如何在上鎖走廊裡笑得極具感染力，火勢卻同時蔓延。沈漫檢查地圖，發現緊急樓梯不見了。「它用善意把人留下，」她說。錄音繼續述說黃昏時客人曾獲得止咳糖漿與壓驚威士忌，建築卻在黎明前倒塌。周硯從桌下找出旅館名冊，所有姓名都被劃掉，只有一名負責接待的新手員工還在。照片與車站清潔工的胸牌一模一樣。見夏頓時感到比空氣更冷的寒意。

A low bell sounded. The curtain swelled toward them, as though dozens of people were breathing behind it. He Chuan lifted his camera, and the painted spotlight snapped onto him. Jianxia remembered the six paper cups. “There are six positions,” she said. “We have five people. The rule doesn't forbid looking behind us. It forbids turning around while moving forward.” Zhou Yan took the nearest chair and rotated it to face the curtain. He sat down. “Then we look without taking another step.” The others followed, moving only their chairs, not their bodies. Through the glass they saw their own reflections standing behind them, patiently waiting to borrow their places.

低沉鐘聲響起，布簾向他們鼓起，彷彿後面有幾十個人同時呼吸。賀川舉起相機，天花板畫出的聚光燈立刻對準他。見夏想起六個紙杯。「這裡有六個位置，」她說，「我們卻只有五人。規則禁止的不是看後方，而是往前走時轉身。」周硯搬來椅子，將椅背轉向布簾後坐下。「那就停住，再看。」其他人照做，只轉動椅子，不讓身體向後踏步。玻璃裡，他們看見自己的倒影站在身後，耐心等著取代主人。

The reflections smiled, but one remained unsmiling: the elderly woman's. She was not looking at a daughter. She was staring at the blank paper cup. Jianxia placed that cup on the sixth chair. “Your name isn't missing,” she told the woman gently. “It was taken. You can choose it again.” The woman wrote her name in shaky letters. The spotlight flickered; the curtain dropped; the corridor beyond opened onto stairs. As they climbed, Jianxia heard her brother's voice again, no longer pleading. “Clever girl,” it said from somewhere above. “You have learned that a rule is never the same thing as the truth.” Zhou Yan caught the final words and went pale. He knew that voice too.

倒影都笑了，唯獨老婦人的倒影沒有。她看著的不是女兒，而是空白紙杯。見夏把杯子放上第六張椅子。「妳的名字不是不見，」她輕聲說，「是被拿走了，妳還可以重新選擇。」老婦人顫抖著寫下名字。聚光燈閃爍，布簾垂落，後面露出通往樓梯的走道。眾人上樓時，見夏再次聽到哥哥的聲音，這次不再哀求。「聰明的女孩，」聲音從上方傳來，「妳知道了，規則從來不等於真相。」周硯聽見最後一句，臉色瞬間蒼白。原來他也認得那個聲音。
