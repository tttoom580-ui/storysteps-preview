鏡廳裡的乘客

Passengers in the Hall of Mirrors

第十三站台 · 第 9 / 25 章 · 5000–6000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

The mirror hall stretched across an impossible peninsula of polished floors and dark water. Every reflection moved a heartbeat ahead of its owner. Jianxia watched her double turn her head before she decided to do so. A brochure promised an exhibition about the revival of childhood joy, narrated by the original founder's stepchild. The program contained photographs of children wearing jade bracelets and paper crowns. “They used people as displays,” Shen Man said. A mirror briefly showed her treating a patient whose face she did not recognize. Its image was so vivid that she touched the glass. Her reflection touched her first, and the glass became cold enough to hurt.

鏡廳像建在暗水半島上，拋光地板無邊無際。每個倒影都比本人早一個心跳行動。見夏還沒決定轉頭，鏡中她已先轉。宣傳冊承諾展示童年喜悅的復甦，由創辦人的繼子女擔任旁白。節目單有戴玉手鐲和紙王冠的孩子照片。「他們把人當展品，」沈漫說。某面鏡子短暫映出她替陌生臉孔病人治療，畫面真實得讓她伸手碰玻璃。倒影卻先碰到她，玻璃冰得刺痛。

A recorded narrator invited them to visit whichever memory made them most joyous. He Chuan saw himself receiving an award for investigative journalism. Jianxia saw her brother bringing home a trout from a mountain stream. Then he turned into a stranger, and she realized that the scene was built from fragments rather than truth. The mirror tried to administer comfort like medicine, measuring her response and adjusting the picture. Her discomfort was not only fear. She felt angry that even grief had become an object to manipulate. She detached the corner of a framed photograph and found a small serial number beneath it, proof that the apparent memory had been manufactured.

錄音旁白邀請他們挑選最喜悅的記憶。賀川看見自己獲得調查報導獎，見夏看見哥哥從山溪帶回一條鱒魚；但哥哥一轉身就變成陌生人，讓她明白畫面是碎片拼貼。鏡子像開藥般施予安慰，不斷測量反應、調整影像。她的不舒服不只恐懼，還有哀傷被操弄的憤怒。她拆開相框一角，找到印在下面的序號，證明眼前所謂記憶是製造出來的。

At the center stood a classroom where a teacher conducted a literacy test. One child was allowed to read while the others were ordered to cram meaningless sentences until they forgot their names. A boy developed acne along his jaw from stress. Another had a bruise beside one nostril. Jianxia watched the teacher select only the children who recited the exact answer, while the rest waited in silent captivity. “Selective learning,” Zhou Yan said, “with obedience confused for intelligence.” The classroom changed into a hospital, and the teacher became a nurse. Still the same children waited, now under fluorescent lights, for someone to decide which of them deserved care.

中央教室裡，老師正在進行讀寫能力測驗。只有一個孩子能閱讀，其餘被迫死背無意義句子，背到忘記名字。某男孩因壓力滿臉青春痘，另一個鼻孔旁有瘀傷。老師只挑能背出標準答案的人，其餘孩子在沉默囚禁中等待。「選擇性教育，」周硯說，「把服從誤當智慧。」教室忽然變成醫院，老師成了護士；仍是同一批孩子，在日光燈下等待別人決定誰值得救。

A bright poster announced a forthcoming festival to celebrate the project's success. The station had apparently planned a public exhibition even while the children were still trapped. He Chuan found a list of sponsors hidden behind the poster. Beside every name was a notation describing the donor's willingness to support further testing. One contribution had come from the family that employed the janitor. Another came from a politician who later vanished. The most potent clue was a signature at the bottom: Jianxia's mother. She stared at it, trying to deny the familiar shape of each stroke. Zhou Yan did not offer an explanation. He merely stood beside her while she struggled to breathe.

鮮豔海報宣布即將舉辦慶祝專案成功的節慶。孩子們還困住時，車站竟已策畫公開展示。賀川在海報後找到贊助名冊，每個名字旁記錄資助者是否願繼續試驗。某筆贊助來自雇用清潔工的家族，另一筆來自後來失蹤的政客。最具衝擊力的線索在底部簽名：林見夏的母親。她盯著熟悉的筆畫，努力否認。周硯沒有解釋，只靜靜站在她身旁，陪她撐過難以呼吸的一刻。

The mirrors began to illuminate one another, creating a chain of reflections that traveled faster than the people themselves. Jianxia noticed that every projected image responded to her desire to know the past, but none showed what happened after the children escaped. “We're allowed to see the experiment,” she said, “not its ending.” She picked up a broken piece of jade from a display and held it where two mirrors faced each other. The reflections multiplied until a hidden passage appeared between them. “We walk toward the part that isn't being shown,” she told the others. He Chuan laughed nervously. “You make terrible directions sound persuasive.” Yet he followed her.

鏡子互相照亮，反射影像連成一條比真人更快的光鏈。見夏發現所有投影都順應她想知道過去的欲望，卻沒有一面展示孩子逃出之後的事。「它准我們看實驗，」她說，「卻不讓我們看結果。」她拿起展示台上破碎的玉片，放在兩面鏡子相對處，倒影不斷增殖，中央竟顯出隱藏走道。「我們往沒被展示的地方走，」她對大家說。賀川緊張地笑：「妳總能把可怕的指路方式講得很有說服力。」仍跟了上去。

At the passage exit, a small child stood beneath a paper lantern. She asked Jianxia why her mother had signed the document. “I don't know,” Jianxia answered. For once she refused to invent certainty. The girl nodded and opened a door marked RETURN. Behind it was a red corridor crowded with figures whose faces had been blurred. Each figure held a photograph taken moments before a disappearance. Jianxia caught sight of her brother, older than in the school picture, carrying a folder across the corridor. “Wait!” she shouted. He turned toward her, but the girl reached up and covered Jianxia's eyes. “That isn't the past,” she whispered. “That's what the station wants you to chase.”

走道出口站著一名提紙燈籠的女孩，問見夏母親為何簽文件。「我不知道。」見夏第一次拒絕編造確定答案。女孩點頭，打開寫著「返回」的門。後面是紅色走廊，擠滿面孔模糊的人影，每個人都拿著失蹤前一刻的照片。見夏瞥見哥哥，他比校園照片裡年長，正抱著文件夾穿過走廊。「等等！」她喊。哥哥轉頭，女孩卻伸手遮住見夏雙眼。「那不是過去，」女孩低語，「是車站希望妳追逐的東西。」
