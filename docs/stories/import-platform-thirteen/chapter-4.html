倒數的廣播

Countdown on the Speakers

第十三站台 · 第 4 / 25 章 · 5000–6000 · 英中對照

The courtyard looked like a museum garden: elegant stone paths, an artificial vineyard, and a fountain arranged for perfect aesthetic symmetry. Yet a rusty loudspeaker crackled every ten minutes. “At zero,” it announced, “one visitor will be removed.” Jianxia counted nine minutes remaining. Beside the fountain stood a statue of a disciple kneeling before a blind teacher. At its feet were a shaver, a paper badge, and a sealed warranty promising that all objects remained safe under proper use. He Chuan laughed at the promise, then stopped when the marble teacher began to radiate warmth. “We need the schedule,” Jianxia said. “And we need to know whether the countdown means death or simply departure.”

庭院像博物館花園：優雅石徑、人工葡萄園、對稱得過分美麗的噴泉。偏偏生鏽的廣播器每十分鐘便響一次：「倒數歸零時，一名訪客將被移除。」見夏算出還剩九分鐘。噴泉旁有座雕像，弟子跪在失明老師面前；腳下放著刮鬍刀、紙徽章和保證「正確使用下所有物品安全」的保固書。賀川笑到一半，發現大理石教師開始散發熱氣，笑容便消失。「我們需要時間表，」見夏說，「還要確定倒數代表死亡，還是單純離開。」

The first announcement described a terrible blaze that had destroyed the foundation's laboratories decades before. Survivors had developed an addiction to replaying their lost relatives' voices. An archaeological researcher had discovered wires beneath the garden, perhaps evidence of an earlier settlement. Shen Man estimated the wires' approximate age from their insulation, but Zhou Yan saw that they were newer than the stone. “They built a museum around machinery,” he said. The lesson seemed implicit: history could be arranged like scenery. Jianxia remembered the pharmacy's photograph and wondered how many of her memories belonged to someone else's exhibition. Above them, the clock made one complete rotation in the wrong direction. Shen Man had studied archaeology as an elective and knew that genuine artifacts rarely fit a designer’s perfect arrangement.

第一則廣播描述幾十年前摧毀基金會實驗室的烈火。倖存者沉迷於重播亡者聲音，像患上成癮症。一位考古學研究者曾發現花園地底電線，推測這裡有更早的人類遺址。沈漫依絕緣層估算約略年代，周硯卻看出電線比石頭新。「他們是把博物館蓋在機器上，」他說。隱含的教訓似乎是：歷史能像佈景一樣任意安排。見夏想起藥局合照，懷疑多少記憶只是別人展覽的一部分。頭頂時鐘反方向轉完了一整圈。 沈漫曾選修考古學，知道真正遺物很少像設計師佈置得那麼完美。

A hidden door opened beneath the statue. Inside stood a polished desk with a microphone and a sign: THE PERSON WITH THE BADGE SHALL PRESIDE. He Chuan snatched the badge, eager for the prestige of leading a group. The microphone immediately amplified a man's voice ordering them to surrender their tickets. Jianxia pulled He Chuan away before he could obey. “The badge does not make you the owner of the command,” she said. “It makes you the target.” A shadow moved behind the desk, drawing a veil across the glass. Zhou Yan noticed that the recorded voice repeated exactly, even the cough between sentences. Someone wasn't speaking live. Someone had programmed authority to sound human.

雕像下方打開暗門，裡面是光亮的辦公桌、麥克風，以及「持徽章者主持」的標示。賀川搶過徽章，急著體驗領導者的威望。麥克風立刻放大男子聲音，命令大家交票。見夏把賀川拉開，阻止他服從。「徽章不代表你擁有命令，」她說，「只代表你成了靶子。」桌後影子移動，一層面紗般的布遮住玻璃。周硯發現錄音每次重播都完全相同，連句間咳嗽都一致。不是活人在發號施令，是有人把權威編成了程式。

The second countdown began, and the room's lights turned red. A strange little fuss erupted when a mechanical bird flew from the fountain carrying a document marked PRIVATIZE THE STATION. The paper claimed the research foundation had sold its memory technology to investors. Shen Man asked who would be accountable for the stolen lives. “Nobody,” Zhou Yan answered bitterly. “That's why they're allowed to call it progress.” Jianxia resisted the urge to argue about politics. She opened the broken clock case and found a rotating disk with thirteen slots. Eleven were filled. One held a strand of her hair; another held her brother's handwriting. The warning was no longer abstract.

第二輪倒數開始，燈光轉紅。噴泉裡飛出機械鳥，攜著寫有「將車站私有化」的文件，造成一陣騷動。紙上宣稱研究基金會已把記憶技術賣給投資者。沈漫問誰該為被偷的人生負責。「沒人，」周硯苦澀地說，「所以他們才能叫它進步。」見夏強忍政治辯論的衝動，打開壞掉的時鐘，裡面有十三格旋轉盤，十一格已滿；一格放著她的髮絲，另一格竟是哥哥的筆跡。警告不再抽象。

They searched the garden while the voice counted down. A stray cat squeezed through the hedge and vanished beneath a metal plate. Beneath it Jianxia found a switch labeled ENLIGHTENMENT, as if a single button could reveal the meaning of a life. The mechanism would stop only when someone chose to make the announcement quiet. She remembered how the speaker always said “one visitor will be removed,” never “one visitor will die.” With three seconds left, Jianxia pulled the microphone cable from the desk and connected it to the clock. She spoke one clear word: “Silence.” The red lamps shut off. The clock stopped. No one vanished. A door opened beside the vineyard, and a child's applause rang from beyond it. But enlightenment, Jianxia thought, could not be delivered like a purchased product.

廣播倒數時，眾人搜遍花園。一隻流浪貓鑽過樹籬，消失在金屬板下。見夏掀開板子，找到標著「啟蒙」的開關，彷彿按鍵就能揭露人生意義。機器只有在有人選擇讓廣播安靜時才會停。她想起廣播始終說「訪客被移除」，從未說「訪客死亡」。倒數剩三秒，她拔下桌上麥克風線，接進時鐘，清楚說：「安靜。」紅燈全熄，時鐘停了，沒有人消失。葡萄園旁開了一扇門，裡面傳來孩子的鼓掌聲。 見夏想，啟蒙並不是能像商品一樣交付的東西。

Their relief lasted only until Zhou Yan unfolded the final page of the warranty. Its smallest clause stated that the system did not cover damage caused by awakening. “The garden was a demonstration,” he said. “It rewarded us for refusing to treat an instruction as fate.” Jianxia touched the badge and watched its printed number fade. Behind the hedge, the abandoned statue began to resemble the janitor. She wondered whether the old man was a prisoner, an employee, or a warning from the future. As they entered the next hallway, the loudspeaker whispered a new sentence, almost tenderly: “You may have won the minute, Lin Jianxia, but you have not yet remembered why it belongs to you.”

他們的喜悅只持續到周硯翻開保固書末頁。最小字條款寫著：本系統不負擔覺醒造成的損害。「花園只是示範，」他說，「它獎勵不把指令當命運的人。」見夏摸著徽章，看印上的編號逐漸褪去。樹籬後被遺棄的雕像慢慢變得像老清潔工。她不知道對方是囚犯、員工，還是來自未來的警告。眾人走入下一條走廊，廣播近乎溫柔地低語：「妳贏得了這一分鐘，林見夏，但妳還沒想起它原本為何屬於妳。」
