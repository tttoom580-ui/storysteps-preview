清晨的第一班車

The First Train at Dawn

第十三站台 · 第 25 / 25 章 · 5000–6000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

The first real train of the morning arrived with a diesel engine that coughed beneath the station roof. Jianxia sat on a bench, her head resting against Zhou Yan's shoulder, almost ready to doze. The world smelled of breakfast rather than burnt wires. Nearby, Shen Man helped a child with asthma use an inhaler while He Chuan collected the names of returning passengers. A printed notice announced bilateral cooperation between the city hospital and a research council to rehabilitate survivors. Its official language was stiff, but Jianxia felt a little goodwill in the volunteers who brought blankets and hot tea. For the first time in many nights, nobody was being asked to pay for a seat.

清晨第一班真正的列車進站，柴油引擎在站棚下發出咳嗽般聲音。見夏坐在長椅上，頭靠著周硯肩，幾乎睡著。空氣是早餐味，不再是燒焦電線。附近沈漫幫氣喘孩子使用吸入器，賀川收集歸來者名字。公告宣布市立醫院與研究委員會雙邊合作，協助倖存者復健。官話依舊生硬，但送毛毯和熱茶的志工讓見夏感到善意。許多個夜晚以來，終於沒有人要求乘客為座位付代價。

A renowned doctor arrived carrying disposable masks and a medical kit. She asked the group whether they had seen her missing counterpart, a physician who had worked at the old foundation. Jianxia showed her mother's photograph. The doctor studied it for a long moment, then said she had known the woman as a colleague who refused to abandon patients after a disaster. “She couldn't save everyone,” the doctor said, “but she kept searching for ways to change the system.” Jianxia cried quietly, relieved to hear a memory of her mother that was neither an accusation nor a polished defense. Zhou Yan held her hand without trying to divert her feelings.

知名醫師帶著一次性口罩與醫療箱抵達，詢問他們是否見過失蹤的對應合作醫師，那人曾在舊基金會工作。見夏拿出母親照片，醫師凝視良久，說她認識照片中人：一名災難後不肯放棄病人的同事。「她沒能救下每個人，」醫師說，「但始終在找改變系統的方法。」見夏靜靜哭了，終於聽到關於母親的記憶，既不是控訴，也不是美化辯護。周硯握住她的手，沒有試圖轉移她的感受。

At a temporary help desk, a young lad returned a manuscript filled with passengers' handwritten accounts. He Chuan had gathered them to prevent another official history from erasing inconvenient people. Among the pages was a letter from the elderly woman: she had found her daughter alive, though older than she remembered. Another told of a couple who had met in the station and were now engaged; the man laughed when a volunteer mistakenly called him the woman's fiancé before he had proposed. The confusion made Jianxia smile. A life beyond the station would contain ordinary misunderstandings again, and she was strangely grateful for them.

臨時服務台上，年輕男孩送來一本由乘客手寫經歷組成的原稿。賀川想保存這些故事，避免另一份官方歷史抹去不方便的人。裡面有老婦人的信，她找到了活著的女兒，雖然比記憶中年長；還有一對在站台相遇的情侶，如今訂了婚，男方笑說志工在求婚前就誤叫他「未婚夫」。這些小誤會讓見夏微笑。站外生活終於又會有普通的誤會，她對此竟非常感激。

The railway authority offered to replace the old trains with a hybrid fleet and to investigate every missing transport record. Activists demanded that the findings be made public rather than buried in technical reports. Jianxia supported them, though she knew the investigation would take years. Some people expressed outrage that the foundation had hidden so much; others feared that disclosure would revive old grief. Both feelings deserved room. The paradox of returning to reality, Jianxia realized, was that escape did not erase harm. It gave people the chance to face that harm with others beside them. She wrote the thought on the lining of her notebook before she could forget it. The new program aimed at the unification of scattered testimony without flattening the differences among survivors.

鐵路單位宣布將更新成混合動力車隊，並調查每筆失蹤運輸紀錄。倡議者要求公開結果，不要埋在技術報告裡。見夏支持，雖知道調查可能多年。有人憤怒基金會掩蓋太多，另一些擔心揭露會喚醒舊傷；兩種感受都值得被容納。見夏明白回到現實的悖論：逃離不能消除傷害，只是讓人有機會與同伴一起面對。她趁未忘記，把想法寫在筆記本內襯。 新計畫希望整合分散證詞，同時不抹平倖存者之間的差異。

Just before departure, Jianxia found a note tucked underneath her seat. It contained a single photograph of a seaside villa, not the false paradise shown by the machine but a modest house where her family had once spent a summer. On the back her brother had written: If you reach morning, don't spend your whole life looking backward. She read the words twice, then gave the note to Zhou Yan. He was silent for a moment. “Your brother trusted you to make your own future,” he said. Jianxia nodded. She wanted to mourn the people they had lost, help the living, finish her studies, and learn how to be happy without calling it betrayal.

發車前，見夏在座位底下找到紙條，裡面只有一張海邊別墅照片。那不是機器展示的完美天堂，而是他們一家曾度夏的樸素小屋。背面是哥哥字跡：「如果妳抵達清晨，別用一生只顧回頭。」她讀了兩次，才交給周硯。他沉默片刻：「妳哥哥信任妳能創造自己的未來。」見夏點頭。她想哀悼失去的人，幫助活著的人，完成學業，也學著快樂而不以為那是背叛。

The doors opened. Shen Man waved from the platform, already planning a hospital program for survivors. He Chuan lifted his camera and promised not to publish a word without the subject's consent. The old woman stood beside her daughter, smiling through tears. Jianxia turned to Zhou Yan. “Would you like to get breakfast?” she asked. He looked at the platform clock, which now showed an ordinary hour. “Only if we can choose the restaurant.” She laughed. They stepped into the carriage together, neither leading nor following. Through the window, a sign marked Platform 13 flickered once and went dark. The city moved toward morning, and for once the road ahead belonged to no machine.

車門打開，沈漫在月台揮手，已開始規畫倖存者醫療計畫。賀川舉起相機，承諾未經當事人同意不公開任何故事。老婦人站在女兒旁，含淚微笑。見夏轉向周硯：「要不要去吃早餐？」他看了一眼顯示正常時間的站鐘：「可以，只要餐廳由我們選。」她笑了。兩人一起上車，沒有人領先或跟隨。窗外「第十三站台」牌子短暫閃了一次，便暗了。城市走向早晨，未來的路終於不再屬於任何機器。
