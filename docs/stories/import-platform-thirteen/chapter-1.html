末班車邀請函

Invitation to the Last Train

第十三站台 · 第 1 / 25 章 · 5000–6000 · 英中對照

At 12:43 a.m., Lin Jianxia was standing beneath a broken departure board when a black ticket appeared between her fingers. She had not bought it. She tried to loosen her clasp, but the ticket seemed to hold her hand instead. Platform 13 was printed in silver beneath the words ONE WAY. No such platform existed in the university district. When a distant bell rang, the pigeons outside the station suddenly rose in a noisy cloud, and a sound like horses at a gallop passed through the sealed tunnel. Jianxia thought of leaving. Then the final message from her missing older brother lit her phone: If a train calls your name, don't answer. A woman's voice spoke from the darkness. “Lin Jianxia. Please board.”

凌晨十二點四十三分，林見夏站在故障的發車看板下，手指間突然多了一張黑色車票。她根本沒買票，試著鬆開緊握的手，車票卻像反過來抓住她。票上以銀字印著「第十三站台」和「單程」。大學區根本沒有這個月台。遠處鐘聲響起，站外的鴿子猛然飛起，封閉的隧道裡傳來萬馬奔騰般的聲響。她正想離開，手機亮出失蹤哥哥留下的最後訊息：「如果列車叫你的名字，不要回答。」黑暗裡卻傳來女人的聲音：「林見夏，請上車。」

An old janitor pushed a mop from the service corridor. “You are late,” he said without surprise. His badge identified him as TRUSTEE NUMBER FOUR, though a trustee ought to manage property, not sweep empty stations. He showed her a directive stamped with an impossible date: All passengers must convene before the thirteenth bell. “What happens afterward?” Jianxia asked. “The rules will be read.” The janitor pointed to a photograph of a small lion cub in the ticket window. It was absurdly cheerful beside a warning about missing passengers. “Why the animal?” she asked. “A reminder that even a cub can inherit the hunger of a king.” He rolled his mop onward, leaving a thin wet trail that ended where the floor was perfectly dry.

一名老清潔工推著拖把從勤務走廊出現。「妳遲到了。」他的語氣毫不意外。胸牌寫著「第四號受託管理人」，可管理財產的人怎麼會在空蕩的車站拖地？他展示一份蓋著不可能日期的指令：「所有乘客必須於第十三聲鐘響前集合。」見夏問之後會怎樣，他只說會宣讀規則。售票窗貼著一張幼獅的照片，笑容般的表情和失蹤警告格格不入。「為什麼貼這個？」她問。「提醒妳，幼獅也可能繼承國王的飢餓。」清潔工推著拖把離開，留下水痕，卻在完全乾燥的地板前突然斷掉。

A train slid toward her without the slightest mechanical noise. Its windows showed different places: a mountain dwelling beneath snow, an operating room filled with dental instruments, and a classroom where children sat at their desks with their faces turned toward the wall. In another window, a dictator in a military coat waved to a crowd that had no eyes. None of these scenes moved with the train. They remained stationary, as if the carriages had been built around frozen moments. Jianxia's fear began to heighten when she noticed that the classroom window contained her own old schoolbag. A boy inside raised his head. For an instant he looked like her brother. Then someone stepped between them.

一列車滑進月台，卻沒有半點機械聲。車窗裡不是座位，而是彼此不同的地方：雪山下的小屋、擺滿牙科器械的手術室，以及孩子們面牆坐著的教室。另一扇窗中，軍裝獨裁者對著沒有眼睛的人群揮手。列車移動，那些場景卻始終靜止，彷彿車廂圍著凝固的時間建成。見夏的恐懼越來越強，因為她看見教室裡放著自己小時候的書包。一名男孩抬起頭，有一瞬間像極了哥哥。接著，有人擋在他們之間。

“Don't look at a window for longer than thirteen seconds,” the stranger said. He was tall, carrying a worn notebook, and looked roughly Jianxia's age. “I'm Zhou Yan.” She demanded to know if that name was another trap. “Probably,” he replied. “But it's the only one I have.” Beside the door hung the station's motto: YOU REAP WHAT YOU REMEMBER. Underneath, a notice explained that the train could transplant a passenger's memories into another person's mind. The document called this an act of unconditional hospitality. Jianxia knew enough about official language to suspect that hospitality was merely a polite synonym for imprisonment. Zhou Yan seemed to reach the same conclusion without needing to read the small print.

「不要盯著車窗超過十三秒。」陌生男生開口。他身形修長，抱著一本舊筆記本，看來跟見夏年紀相仿。「我叫周硯。」見夏質問這名字是不是另一個陷阱。「大概吧，」他說，「但我只剩這一個名字了。」門邊掛著車站格言：「你將收割你所記得的一切。」下方公告聲稱列車能把乘客記憶移植到別人腦中，並稱此為「無條件的款待」。見夏一看就知道，官方文書裡的款待，多半是囚禁的委婉說法。周硯似乎不必讀完小字就得出同樣結論。

More passengers arrived. A medical student named Shen Man carried a first-aid case. A former reporter, He Chuan, carried a camera with its lens cap missing. There was also an elderly woman who spoke about traveling as a pilgrim to visit her daughter. “Is there a precedent for refusing?” He asked the janitor. “Nobody has ever declined,” the man answered. “That isn't the same as nobody being allowed to,” Jianxia said. She heard her own voice tremble but held her ground. The janitor smiled without opening his mouth. Behind them, the departure board changed from NO SERVICE to ALL SEATS RESERVED. Jianxia counted five people on the platform and six shadows on the wall. The sixth shadow was hers, except it wore her brother's jacket.

更多乘客陸續出現。醫學生沈漫提著急救箱，前記者賀川抱著鏡頭蓋不見的相機，還有一位說自己要像朝聖者般去見女兒的老婦人。賀川問清潔工：「以前有人拒絕搭車嗎？」對方說從來沒有人拒絕。「從沒拒絕，不代表不准拒絕，」見夏回應。她聽見自己聲音發抖，卻沒有退縮。清潔工嘴巴不動地笑了。發車看板由「停駛」變為「座位全部預訂」。月台上明明只有五個人，牆上卻有六道影子；第六道是她的輪廓，身上卻穿著哥哥的外套。

The doors opened, and a low voice invited them inside. Jianxia wanted to utter one last question, but the bell rang again and swallowed her words. Zhou Yan stood at the threshold without urging her forward. “You can stay,” he said. “I'm not promising it will be safer.” No one had made that distinction all night. She took one breath and stepped aboard, choosing the seat nearest the emergency handle. The old woman thanked her for making room. Shen Man checked everyone's pulse, perhaps because it was the only normal thing left to do. The train departed, and the station disappeared behind a curtain of white light. Across from Jianxia, an empty seat slowly developed the shape of someone sitting in it. The announcement began: “Welcome back, passenger thirteen.”

車門打開，低沉的聲音邀請眾人上車。見夏原本還想問最後一個問題，鐘聲卻吞掉她的話。周硯站在門邊，沒有催促。「妳可以留下，」他說，「但我不能保證那樣更安全。」整晚只有他說清這個差別。見夏深呼吸，上車後選了靠近緊急拉柄的位置。老婦人謝她讓位，沈漫替大家量脈搏，或許只是想做一件還算正常的事。列車啟動，車站消失在白光後。她對面的空椅子慢慢凹陷，像有人正在坐下。廣播響起：「歡迎歸來，第十三號乘客。」
