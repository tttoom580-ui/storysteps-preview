紅雨校園

Campus Under Red Rain

第十三站台 · 第 14 / 25 章 · 5000–6000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

Red rain fell over the schoolyard, staining the empty courtyard without wetting the people inside it. Jianxia recognized the building from her childhood photographs. A familiar freeway roared behind the classroom block, although the road had been demolished years earlier. On a gate hung a charitable foundation's banner promising free education for every inhabitant of the district. Below it, a smaller notice described the school's modernization program, funded by an anonymous endowment. “This was never just a school,” Shen Man said. “It was a place to select children.” The words seemed to mar the beautiful campus more deeply than the unnatural rain. Jianxia stood very still, waiting for a memory she didn't trust to return. Jianxia had to brace herself against the gate as a sudden gust pushed the rain sideways.

紅雨落在校園，染紅空蕩中庭，卻不會沾濕裡面的人。見夏認出這是童年照片裡的建築。教室後方傳來熟悉的高速公路聲，儘管那條路早已拆除。校門懸著慈善基金會標語，承諾為區內每位居民提供免費教育。下面的小公告寫著學校現代化方案，由匿名捐贈基金支付。「這裡從來不只是學校，」沈漫說，「而是挑選孩子的地方。」這句話比詭異紅雨更深地毀壞校園的美感。見夏站著等候不可信的記憶歸來。 強風把紅雨吹斜，見夏不得不撐住校門才站得穩。

In the first classroom they found an old adaptation of a fairy tale about a reptile princess. The students had been instructed to mimic the creature's movements while repeating the same line: A good child never asks why. He Chuan called the exercise a freak performance. Zhou Yan found another sheet, explaining that the lesson was meant to measure a child's willingness to obey customary authority. The script ended with the princess surrendering her own name for the king's pleasure. Jianxia tore the page in half. “If that's the moral, I refuse it.” Her anger seemed to lighten the room; the red rain outside faded for a second, revealing a bright blue sky.

第一間教室裡有齣改編童話，主角是爬蟲公主。學生被要求模仿爬蟲動作，反覆說：「好孩子不問為什麼。」賀川說這根本是怪異表演。周硯找到另一張說明，表示課程用來測量兒童服從傳統權威的意願。劇本最後公主為討國王歡心，交出自己的名字。見夏撕掉紙張。「如果這就是寓意，我拒絕。」怒意彷彿讓房間明亮了一點；外頭紅雨退去一瞬，露出湛藍天空。

A classroom clock displayed a succession of dates instead of hours. One date matched the laboratory fire; another matched Jianxia's childhood fall. As they moved through the building, old announcements played mournful songs over the speakers. Shen Man found a reading room full of student diaries, each describing long periods of solitude. Some children had written about missing parents, others about prizes promised by the school. One diary contained a careful drawing of two boys carrying a little girl. Jianxia traced their outlines with one finger. “My brother wasn't the only one who came for me,” she whispered. Zhou Yan looked away, unable to bear the recognition in her eyes.

教室時鐘顯示連串日期，不是時刻，其中一個對應實驗室火災，另一個對應見夏童年墜落事件。走廊廣播播放哀傷歌曲。沈漫找到擺滿學生日記的閱覽室，每本都描述漫長孤獨。有孩子寫想念父母，有人寫學校答應給獎品。某本日記畫了兩個男孩抬著小女孩。見夏指尖沿著輪廓滑過。「原來不是只有哥哥來救我。」周硯轉開頭，承受不了她眼裡的認出。

The school principal appeared in a reflective mask and introduced himself as His Majesty, the guardian of proper behavior. He promised refreshments to anyone who completed the test without protest. A machine hissed, releasing a puff of harmless-looking powder, and the class immediately fell silent. Jianxia held her breath. “You're not their king,” she said. “You were hired to teach them.” The principal claimed that strictness was kindness, but the children's diaries contradicted him. Shen Man read one aloud: I am afraid of the bell because it means we will forget someone. The mask flickered. Underneath was not a human face but an empty wooden frame. He offered sweet tea to refresh them after the ordeal, but no one accepted it.

校長戴著會反光的面具現身，自稱「陛下」，是良好行為守護者。他承諾不抗議地完成測試就有茶點。機器噴出一口看似無害的粉末，全班立即安靜。見夏屏住呼吸。「你不是他們的王，」她說，「你只是受聘的老師。」校長說嚴厲即仁慈，但孩子的日記反駁他。沈漫讀出一句：「我害怕鐘聲，因為每次響都會忘記一個人。」面具閃爍，底下不是臉，而是空心木框。 他說可以用甜茶讓大家在考驗後恢復精神，卻沒有人接受。

Behind the principal's office they discovered a sealed room containing photographs of students with numbers written over their names. The numbering system was a crude adaptation of an earlier medical register. Zhou Yan remembered walking this corridor with another boy, older than himself, who had taught him where to hide during an emergency. “Your brother,” he said to Jianxia. “He protected all of us, even after he knew he couldn't leave.” She struggled to take in the words. The floor trembled as if the school were about to collapse. He Chuan called for them to hurry, but Jianxia insisted on copying every name. “We won't leave these children anonymous twice,” she said.

校長室後方的密室放著學生照片，姓名被號碼蓋過；編號制度拙劣改自早期醫療登記。周硯記起曾和另一名年長男孩走過這條走廊，對方教他緊急時躲在哪裡。「是妳哥哥，」他對見夏說，「知道自己無法離開後，仍保護所有人。」見夏努力消化這些話。地板震動，學校似乎即將倒塌。賀川催大家走，見夏卻堅持抄下每個名字。「不能讓這些孩子第二次成為無名者。」

As the red rain grew heavier, the diary pages began to float toward the classroom door. They formed a bridge across a deep crack in the floor. Jianxia knew the station was offering a path, perhaps the first honest one it had shown them. She and Zhou Yan crossed last. On the far side stood a small girl holding an umbrella. “You promised to return,” the child said. Jianxia wanted to ask who she was, but the girl's face changed into her own. “Please don't forget again.” Then the school vanished in a rush of red water. When Jianxia opened her eyes, she was holding the drawing of the two boys, now marked on the back with her brother's name.

紅雨變大時，日記紙頁浮向教室門，竟在地板裂縫上形成紙橋。見夏明白車站正在提供路，可能是它第一次誠實告訴他們的路。她和周硯最後走過。對面有個撐傘小女孩。「妳答應會回來，」孩子說。見夏還想問她是誰，女孩的臉卻變成她自己。「這次不要再忘記。」學校被紅水沖走。見夏再睜眼，手裡仍握著兩名男孩的畫，背面已標上哥哥的名字。
