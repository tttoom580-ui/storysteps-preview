The Floor of Illusions

第十三站台 · 第 11 / 25 章 · 5000–6000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

The train stopped between two stations, but its doors opened onto a hotel corridor marked FLOOR ZERO. A draught carried the smell of burned wood through the fireproof doors. Above the elevator, a digital thermometer displayed 451 degrees Fahrenheit. “That should be impossible,” Shen Man said. Even her bodily response seemed delayed; she could see sweat forming on her reflection before feeling warm. An advertisement promised acclaim to anyone who successfully completed a survey of the building's geographical features. The hotel had no clear address, no windows, and no consistent number of rooms. Jianxia suspected that its ambiguity was deliberate. Every route seemed to ask the same question: would they trust a measurement more than their senses?

列車停在兩站之間，門外卻是標著「零樓」的旅館走廊。防火門間灌進焦木味的冷風，電梯上方溫度計顯示華氏四百五十一度。「這不可能，」沈漫說。連身體反應都像延遲：鏡中已冒汗，她才感到熱。廣告保證完整調查建築地理特徵者可獲得讚譽。但旅館沒地址、沒窗，房間數也一直變。見夏認為這種模糊性是刻意安排。每條路都在問同一件事：他們會比起自己的感官更相信數字嗎？

In the lobby, a mermaid sculpture held a broken utensil where a seashell should have been. Nearby stood an unattended buffet of yogurt and miscellaneous snacks. He Chuan, desperate to relieve the tension, called it the world's worst company outing. An old computer showed a program written by a hacker who had been hired to test the station's defenses. The display offered to liberate one captive memory if they entered the password. He Chuan typed the name of the original foundation, then his own. Both were rejected. Zhou Yan studied the source code and said the password wasn't a word. “It's an occurrence,” he explained. “A moment someone chose to act differently from the system's prediction.”

大廳裡的人魚雕像手持破餐具，取代原本該有的貝殼。旁邊的無人自助餐供應優格和雜七雜八點心。賀川為了緩解緊張，說這是史上最糟的公司出遊。舊電腦裡存著一名受雇測試車站防護的駭客程式，輸入密碼就能解放一段被囚禁記憶。賀川先打基金會名稱，再打自己的，都遭拒。周硯研究程式後說密碼不是字，而是一個事件：「某人做出系統預測之外選擇的瞬間。」

They searched the rooms for such an event. In one, Shen Man found herself back in a hospital, listening to a doctor explain her first medical error. In another, He Chuan faced the family harmed by his reporting. Jianxia entered a childhood kitchen and saw her mother seated with her back turned. She wanted to ask whether the signature on the sponsorship list had been forged. But each time she approached, the room revolved, replacing the kitchen with a courtroom where she sat in the accused chair. “Don't let the room decide the question for you,” Zhou Yan said from the doorway. “You are allowed to ask your own.” Jianxia steadied herself and demanded to know where the missing children had gone.

他們分頭找這種事件。某房裡沈漫回到醫院，重聽醫師解釋她第一次醫療疏失；另一房中賀川面對被他報導傷害的家庭。見夏走進童年廚房，看見母親背對自己坐著。她想問贊助名冊上的簽名是否偽造，但每當靠近，房間便旋轉，廚房變成法庭，她坐在被告席。「別讓房間替妳決定問題，」周硯在門口說，「妳可以問自己的。」她站穩，直接問那些失蹤孩子去了哪裡。

The courtroom emptied. A voice answered with cruel amusement: “A layman would not understand.” Jianxia clenched her fists. “Then explain it without hiding behind special language.” The voice changed into her brother's, telling her to stop searching. She closed her eyes and remembered him teaching her to repair a bicycle chain, patiently repeating each step until she could do it herself. That was not how he spoke when he wanted her to give up. “You're not him,” she said. The walls shuddered. She had rejected a convenient story, and the computer in the lobby emitted a chime. On its screen appeared a date and a filename: THE FIRST UNSCRIPTED MERCY. The real villain, she realized, was the voice that insisted she had no right to question it.

法庭一下清空。聲音帶著殘忍笑意：「外行人不會懂。」見夏握緊拳頭：「那就別躲在專業術語後面，說明白。」聲音變成哥哥，要她停止追查。她閉眼，想起哥哥曾耐心教她修腳踏車鏈條，一遍遍重複直到她會。真正的哥哥不會用這種方式叫她放棄。「你不是他。」牆壁震動。她拒絕了一個方便的故事，大廳電腦傳出提示音，螢幕跳出日期與檔名：「第一次未依劇本發生的憐憫」。 她意識到真正的反派，是那個聲稱她無權質疑的聲音。

The file showed a young boy inside a laboratory, holding a frightened girl while an alarm sounded. The boy's face was blurred, but Jianxia recognized the shape of the train key chain in his hand. “You were there,” she told Zhou Yan. He watched with a grief that seemed older than his years. “I've been trying to remember whether I saved you,” he said, “or whether I was the reason you needed saving.” She wanted to reassure him, yet certainty would have been dishonest. They would need to persevere through whatever the station still concealed. Together they copied the file onto He Chuan's camera, an object the system consistently underestimated.

檔案顯示實驗室裡的小男孩抱著受驚女孩，警報正在響。男孩面孔模糊，見夏卻認出他手上的火車鑰匙圈。「你當時在場，」她對周硯說。他眼中的悲傷像比年齡還古老。「我一直想知道，」他說，「我究竟救了妳，還是我讓妳陷入必須被救的處境。」她很想安慰，卻知道沒有根據的確定是不誠實的。他們得繼續堅持，直到車站隱藏之事曝光。兩人把檔案複製進賀川相機，那是系統一直低估的物件。

When they returned to the elevator, its numbers had disappeared. The screen asked them to ponder one final choice: an easy room filled with familiar people, or a difficult staircase with no promise of safety. Jianxia looked back at her friends. “We already know which one is pretending to be kind.” She stepped onto the stairs, and the others followed. Below them, the mermaid statue began to sing in a human voice. Above, a door opened onto the platform of a train that could not possibly fit inside a hotel. The loudspeaker welcomed them to Floor Minus One, then added quietly, “The next passenger you meet may remember you better than you remember yourself.”

返回電梯時，樓層數字全不見。螢幕要求他們思考最後選擇：充滿熟悉親人的舒適房間，或不保證安全的艱難樓梯。見夏看向朋友：「我們已經知道哪邊在假裝仁慈。」她走上樓梯，其他人跟著。下方的人魚雕像用人聲唱起歌；上方的門則通向一座根本不可能容納在旅館裡的火車月台。廣播歡迎他們來到負一樓，然後低語：「下一個乘客可能比你更記得你自己。」
