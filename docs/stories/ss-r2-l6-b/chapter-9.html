記得那場雨的房客

The Guest Who Remembered the Rain

退房之前，請把真話說完 · 第 9 / 25 章 · 5000–6000 · 英中對照 · 校訂完成

The morning after the trial, a woman named Mrs. Tan arrived holding a faded travel brochure. She had stayed at the Lantern House during the great flood and wanted to tell her story before the official hearing. She was a retired teacher who had spent decades promoting adult literacy on the peninsula. Iris offered to record her account, with permission, and let her review the transcript. Mrs. Tan smiled. “For years I was treated as an unreliable narrator because I was frightened that night,” she said. “But fear can make a memory incomplete without making every detail false.” Iris turned on the recorder and asked where she had been when the rain began.

試住隔天早晨，一位譚女士拿著褪色的旅遊手冊抵達。她曾在大洪水時住進燈屋旅館，希望能在正式聽證前講述自己的經歷。她是退休教師，數十年來致力於半島上的成人識字教育。以澄詢問是否能錄音，並讓她事後審閱逐字稿。譚女士微笑說：「多年來，我因那晚害怕，就被當成不可靠的敘述者。但害怕可能讓記憶不完整，不代表每個細節都是假的。」以澄打開錄音機，請她從下雨的時刻開始說起。

Mrs. Tan described a crowded ballroom and a child with acne who had been embarrassed to accept a borrowed blanket. The child's stepchild relationship to another guest had complicated the registration, but Yun admitted both without demanding papers. Mrs. Tan remembered a strong smell of jade-green herbal oil and a man whose breath whistled through one nostril after an injury. She could not identify the man, and Iris did not press her. The oil's potent scent might have belonged to anyone. What mattered was the detail of the back door: Mrs. Tan said an older engineer had opened it from the inside after a signal from the street.

譚女士描述人滿為患的宴會廳，以及一名因青春痘而不好意思接受借用毛毯的孩子。那孩子與另一位旅客是繼親關係，登記時較複雜，但雲奶奶沒有要求文件，就讓兩人進來。譚女士記得濃烈的翠綠色草本油味，也記得一名男子受傷後，呼吸會從一側鼻孔發出哨聲。她無法辨認男子，以澄也沒有逼問。氣味強烈的油可能屬於任何人。重要的是後門的細節：譚女士說，一名年長工程師在街上有人發出訊號後，從內側打開了門。

Noah was present for the interview but remained silent until Mrs. Tan invited questions. He asked whether she remembered the engineer's coat or voice. She said his sleeve was wet and he spoke softly, as though trying not to frighten those who had been trapped in the alley. The word captivity made her hesitate; they had not been prisoners, she clarified, but the blocked road had made escape feel impossible. Iris noticed the discomfort and offered a break. They served trout soup and tea, then resumed only when Mrs. Tan was ready. The witness retained control over her own story, even when the investigation desperately needed answers.

柏舟在場旁聽，直到譚女士主動邀請提問才開口。他問她是否記得工程師的大衣或聲音。她說對方袖子濕透，說話輕柔，彷彿不想嚇到困在巷子裡的人。她提到「囚禁感」時停頓了一下，隨即澄清大家並不是犯人，而是道路封閉讓逃離看起來不可能。以澄察覺她不自在，便提議休息。他們端出鱒魚湯與茶，直到譚女士準備好了才繼續。即使調查迫切需要答案，證人仍保有自己故事的主導權。

Later Iris and Jules compared the interview with a forthcoming collection of municipal emergency reports. They did not cram every sentence into a single theory. Jules proposed a selective timeline with columns separating observation, inference, and independent confirmation. In an old photograph, light from the hotel kitchen seemed to illuminate the alley at two in the morning. A small metal plate had been detached from the gate, leaving screw marks that matched Noah's recent images. This was promising but not conclusive. Iris wrote a request for the original photograph and asked who had administered the municipal shelter records. She wanted a chain of custody, not merely an attractive exhibit.

稍後，以澄與朱爾把訪談內容和即將公布的市府緊急事件報告彙編比對。他們沒有把每句話硬塞進單一理論。朱爾建議做一份有選擇的時間軸，以不同欄位分開觀察、推論與獨立證實。舊照片裡，凌晨兩點的旅館廚房燈光似乎照亮了巷道。閘門上有一塊金屬牌被拆走，留下的螺絲痕與柏舟最近拍的照片相符。這很有希望，卻仍非定論。以澄申請取得原始照片，並查詢當年由誰管理市府避難紀錄。她需要的是保管鏈，不是好看的展示。

In the afternoon May arranged a small tea gathering to thank the trial guests. Mrs. Tan said she was delighted by the hotel's revival but worried that an expensive reopening would exclude neighbors who once depended on its shelter. Iris admitted she shared that concern. A joyous celebration would mean little if the property became another private wall along the coast. The women discussed low-cost community meals and a reading corner for children. Mrs. Tan offered books instead of money. Iris accepted them with equal gratitude. It was the first donation she could receive without wondering what hidden obligation might follow.

下午，美姨安排小型茶會，感謝試住房客。譚女士說她很高興旅館走向復興，卻擔心昂貴的重新開幕會排除過去曾仰賴此地避難的鄰居。以澄坦承也有相同顧慮。若旅館變成海岸另一道私人高牆，再歡樂的慶祝也沒有意義。她們談起平價社區餐與兒童閱讀角。譚女士願意捐書，不是捐款。以澄同樣感激地接受了。這是她第一次收下捐贈，卻不必猜測後面是否藏著無形義務。

That evening Noah brought Iris an envelope from his mother. It contained a copy of the warning letter he believed Iris had received seven years ago. The paper carried a delivery stamp, but the recipient signature was missing. His mother had kept it because her husband considered the letter important. Iris felt old anger return, yet she did not detach herself from the conversation. “I believed you had chosen silence,” she told Noah. “I believed you had chosen the developer,” he answered. Neither belief had been tested. They set the envelope beside the flood documents, a reminder that private misunderstandings could grow from the same careless handling of evidence as public ones.

那晚，柏舟帶來母親交給他的信封，裡面是七年前那封警告信的副本。他一直以為以澄收到了。紙上有投遞戳章，卻沒有收件人簽名。母親保存這封信，是因為丈夫認為它很重要。以澄感到舊怒意再次升起，卻沒有抽身離開對話。「我一直以為你選擇了沉默，」她告訴柏舟。「我一直以為妳選擇了開發商，」他回答。兩個認定都未曾被查證。他們把信封放在洪水文件旁，提醒自己：私人誤會也可能像公共事件一樣，源自對證據的草率處理。
