Iris Lin returned to Bluehaven with one suitcase and a clasp that had broken on the train. At thirty-four, she had become skilled at leaving places without looking back. Yet she stood motionless before the Lantern House, the coastal dwelling where her grandmother had raised her, and could not make herself open its door. A painted cub of a sea lion still smiled above the bell. Mr. Ho, the retired janitor, emerged from the garden carrying an oilcan. He said the front lock had been stationary for months, but the back entrance still worked. Iris followed him, listening to the sea strike the rocks beneath the windows.

林以澄帶著一只行李箱回到藍灣，箱扣在火車上壞了。三十四歲的她，早已擅長離開一個地方而不回頭。然而，她站在燈屋旅館前，面對這座祖母撫養她長大的海邊房舍，竟無法鼓起勇氣開門。門鈴上方，一隻彩繪小海獅仍露出笑容。退休的清潔工何伯拿著油壺從花園走出，說前門鎖已經好幾個月不能動，但後門還能開。以澄跟著他走，聽著海浪拍打窗下的礁石。

Inside, three trustees had arranged a meeting without asking whether Iris had arrived safely. They would convene at noon, the letter said, to discuss the estate. A directive from the family trust required the hotel to reopen within ninety days or face sale to a development company. Mr. Qiu, the senior trustee, insisted that this was an established precedent, not a personal judgment. Iris read the deed twice. The condition was real, but the trustees could not sell without a documented inspection and a public notice. She had spent twelve years managing other people's businesses. She knew the difference between a legal deadline and a threat delivered in a legal tone.

屋裡，三名受託人已經安排好會議，甚至沒先問以澄是否平安抵達。信上寫著，他們將在中午召開遺產會議。家族信託的指示要求旅館在九十天內重新營業，否則就面臨出售給開發商。資深受託人邱先生堅稱這是既定先例，並非針對她個人的判斷。以澄將契約讀了兩遍。期限確實存在，但受託人不得在缺乏正式檢查與公開公告的情況下出售。她替別人經營企業已十二年，知道合法期限與披著法律外衣的威脅有何不同。

Aunt May was polishing the reception counter when Iris entered. “Your grandmother was no dictator,” she said, “although she could give orders like one.” May still kept Yun's old dental appointment card beside the telephone, a reminder to care for the person behind every reservation. From outside came the gallop of a horse used by the beach patrol. Iris had forgotten how quickly familiar sounds could heighten grief. “You look tired,” May observed. “That is not a synonym for weakness.” Iris tried to answer, but the sentence ended in an utter silence. May did not demand an explanation. She put water on for tea and left the chair beside her empty.

以澄進門時，美姨正在擦拭接待櫃檯。「妳奶奶不是獨裁者，」她說，「雖然她下命令的口氣有時很像。」美姨仍把雲奶奶的舊牙科預約卡放在電話旁，提醒自己每張訂房單背後都有一個真正的人。戶外傳來沙灘巡邏馬匹疾馳的蹄聲。以澄差點忘記，熟悉的聲音竟能如此迅速地加深悲傷。「妳看起來很累，」美姨說，「但累不等於軟弱。」以澄想回答，話語卻停在徹底的沉默裡。美姨沒有逼她說明，只是燒水泡茶，讓身旁的椅子保持空著。

On the kitchen wall hung the house motto: “Give shelter before asking for a story.” Pilgrims traveling along the old coastal route had once slept here for free during storms. Yun had also planted a little orchard; after the seawall cracked, she tried to transplant its youngest trees farther inland. She never lived long enough to reap their fruit. Iris found a notebook of repairs beside the kitchen clock, each task recorded in careful handwriting. The final page named an engineer: Noah Shen. She closed it at once. Seven years earlier, Noah had sent her a warning about this building and then vanished from her life without explaining what he knew.

廚房牆上掛著旅館的座右銘：「先給人遮風避雨的地方，再問他的故事。」從前沿著海岸古道旅行的朝聖者，遇上風暴時曾免費住在這裡。雲奶奶也種了一片小果園；海堤裂開後，她試著把幼樹移植到內陸，卻沒能活到收成果實的時候。以澄在廚房時鐘旁找到一本維修筆記，每項工作都以工整字跡記錄。最後一頁寫著一位工程師的名字：沈柏舟。她立刻合上筆記。七年前，柏舟曾寄來一封關於這棟建築的警告，之後卻沒有向她解釋自己知道的事，就從她的生活中消失。

Noah arrived before the trustees, carrying a survey case rather than flowers. At thirty-six, he looked older around the eyes but just as reluctant to interrupt. “I was hired to examine the seawall,” he said. “You can choose another engineer.” Iris appreciated the offer, though it did not erase the past. “I need a report without favors,” she replied. “Not unconditional loyalty.” Noah nodded. He had brought copies of the previous surveys, including one Iris had never seen. Its title referred to a public stairway behind the hotel. When she asked about it, he did not pretend to have an easy answer. He said the stairs might be the reason a developer wanted the property so badly.

柏舟比受託人更早抵達，手裡拿的是測量箱而不是鮮花。三十六歲的他，眼角多了些歲月痕跡，卻仍不太願意打斷別人。「有人委託我檢查海堤，」他說，「妳也可以換一位工程師。」以澄感謝他給自己選擇權，但這無法抹去過去。「我需要的是不徇私的報告，」她回答，「不是無條件的忠誠。」柏舟點頭。他帶來舊測量紀錄的副本，其中一份以澄從未看過，標題提到旅館後方的公共階梯。她詢問時，他沒有假裝答案很簡單，只說那些階梯或許正是開發商如此想買下旅館的原因。

By noon, the dining room had become a temporary boardroom. Qiu presented a sale proposal as if the signatures were already inevitable. Iris asked him to record her objection, then requested the complete trust accounts and the original survey documents. Her grandmother had left her a building, a debt, and a question that could not be settled by nostalgia. She would not promise to stay forever, nor would she allow strangers to define departure as failure. After the meeting, she wrote ninety small squares on a calendar. Day one ended with a locked front door and a decision: the Lantern House would receive a fair chance, and so would its people.

到了中午，餐廳成了臨時會議室。邱先生拿出售屋提案，態度彷彿所有人簽字已是必然。以澄要求將她的反對意見正式記錄，接著索取完整信託帳目與原始測量文件。祖母留給她的，是一棟建築、一筆債務，以及不能單靠懷舊解決的疑問。她不會承諾永遠留下，也不允許陌生人把離開定義成失敗。散會後，她在日曆上畫了九十個小方格。第一天結束時，前門仍鎖著，但她做出一個決定：燈屋旅館值得一次公平的機會，旅館裡的人也是。
