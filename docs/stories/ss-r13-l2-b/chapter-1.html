地板上的一條線

A Line on the Floor

留給明天的燈光 · 第 1 / 25 章 · 1000–2000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

The first thing Yu-an saw was a red line across the floor of the Lantern Room. The college had closed the small theater after water reached its electrical wall. A gardener with a gray beard stood near the door, holding a wet leaf from the roof garden. He had found the leak at dawn. Yu-an, twenty-four, was the student stage manager, and she wanted to save the room, but she would not cross the line. "No show is worth a person getting hurt," she told the worried actors.

余安第一眼看見的，是橫在燈籠小劇場地板上的紅線。雨水滲進電線牆後，學院封閉了這座小劇場。一名留著灰鬍子的園丁站在門口，手裡拿著從屋頂花園掉下來的濕葉。他清晨就發現漏水。二十四歲的余安是學生舞臺監督，很想保住劇場，但她沒有跨過紅線。「沒有任何演出值得讓人受傷。」她對焦急的演員說。

Wen arrived from the music building with a rubber mat and a box of cables. He was twenty-six and studied sound design. His personality was calm, almost too plain for a group that enjoyed loud arguments. He checked the temperature of the equipment without touching the wet wall. Someone suggested a quick chemical wash to remove the stains. Wen shook his head. "We do not know what is behind that wall. Cleaning first could hide a serious problem." Yu-an liked his care, though she did not yet know how to relate to his quiet manner.

二十六歲、學聲音設計的文從音樂館趕來，帶著橡膠墊與一箱線材。他個性沉穩，對一群愛大聲爭辯的人而言，甚至顯得太過平淡。他沒有碰濕牆，只測量器材的溫度。有人提議先用化學清潔劑洗掉水痕。文搖頭說：「我們不知道牆後面是什麼。先清洗可能掩蓋嚴重問題。」余安欣賞他的謹慎，卻還不知道如何與這種安靜的人相處。

Wen opened his notebook to a clean page and drew two columns. One held the things they knew: the wall was wet, the power was off, and the door was locked. The other held questions: who could test the wires, how much the repairs might cost, and where the company could work next. "Such a list will not fix a wall," he said, "but it stops guesses from pretending to be facts." Yu-an added one more question: who was responsible for telling the visitors about the change? She volunteered to do it herself. She could already imagine angry messages, but she wanted everyone to hear the same honest answer. The table between them was small; the work suddenly looked possible because it had a clear beginning.

文把筆記本翻到空白頁，畫出兩欄。第一欄是已知事項：牆面潮濕、電源關閉、房門上鎖；另一欄是問題：誰能檢查電線、修繕可能花多少錢、劇團接下來在哪裡工作。「這樣的清單不能修好牆壁，」他說，「卻能避免猜測冒充事實。」余安又加上一問：誰負責告訴觀眾計畫改變？她自願承擔。她已能想像生氣的訊息，但希望每個人得到相同且誠實的答案。兩人中間的桌子很小，工作卻因有了清楚起點而突然顯得可行。

The club's money was tiny. After rent and tax for the printing service, they could not pay for a private inspection. Yu-an asked a favor of the arts department: would it help them find a qualified worker? She also asked everyone to divide the remaining work. Nobody was asked to enter the unsafe room. Outside, a student in pajamas carried a whole watermelon to the café for a welcome party. Its strange brightness made Yu-an laugh when she had expected only bad news.

社團的經費少得可憐。扣除場租與印刷服務的稅金後，已經付不起私人檢查費。余安向藝術系請求協助，詢問能否找到合格的檢查人員，並請大家分配剩下的工作。她不讓任何人進入危險的房間。外頭，一名穿睡衣的學生抱著一整顆西瓜走向咖啡館，準備參加迎新會。那鮮亮的畫面讓原本只等著壞消息的余安忍不住笑了。

At lunch, the group met in the café. A student said the closure must be a crime committed by the school. Yu-an stopped him. "A broken pipe is not proof of a crime. We need facts, not a story that makes us feel brave." She asked Wen to weigh the old portable lights and check whether another room could carry the load. Wen drew a simple plan on a napkin. For the first time that day, Yu-an had a hopeful feeling: perhaps the theater was a team, not a building.

午餐時，大家聚在咖啡館。一名學生說校方關閉劇場一定涉及犯罪。余安阻止他：「水管壞了不等於有人犯罪。我們需要事實，不需要一個讓自己覺得勇敢的故事。」她請文秤量舊式移動燈具，確認另一個房間能否承受重量。文在餐巾紙上畫出簡圖。那一天第一次，余安心裡生出希望：也許劇場是一群人，而不只是一棟建築。

On her way to the department office, Yu-an passed the garden where the leaves had first blown against the roof. The gardener was putting a bucket under a dripping pipe. He told her he had filed two reports and could show their dates to the officer. She thanked him for keeping the records. She also wrote down the time of his next visit so the repair workers would not have to search for him. A theater, she thought, had always depended on people outside the bright circle of its stage. Those people were not invisible workers in the background. They had names, knowledge, and their own jobs, and their time deserved respect even when students were frightened about losing a show.

走向系辦公室時，余安經過花園，那裡的葉子曾被風吹向屋頂。園丁正在滴水管下放水桶。他告訴她，自己已提交兩份報告，也能向管理員出示日期。余安感謝他保留紀錄，又記下他下次巡視時間，讓維修人員不必四處找他。她想，劇場向來仰賴舞臺亮光圈外的人。那些人並非看不見的背景工人；他們有姓名、有知識、也有自己的工作。即使學生害怕失去演出，他們的時間也應受到尊重。

Before sunset, the department agreed to send its building officer the next morning. It was not a promise to reopen the room. Yu-an told the actors exactly that. She left a written notice on the door and moved their scripts into a safe cabinet. Wen offered to carry the last box, then asked whether he could join the production team rather than simply lend his tools. Yu-an smiled. "You can join if you tell me when my plans are unsafe." He answered, "That is an unusual first rule, but I accept." They shook hands beside the red line.

日落前，系上同意隔天派校舍管理員來查看，但這不是重新開放劇場的承諾。余安把這點清楚告訴演員，在門口留下書面通知，並將劇本移到安全櫃。文幫忙搬最後一箱，接著問自己能否正式加入製作團隊，而不只是借出工具。余安笑說：「只要你在我的計畫不安全時願意提醒我，就能加入。」他回答：「這第一條規則真特別，但我接受。」兩人在紅線旁握手。
