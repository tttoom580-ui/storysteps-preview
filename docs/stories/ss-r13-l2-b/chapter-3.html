消失的入口

The Missing Entrance

留給明天的燈光 · 第 3 / 25 章 · 1000–2000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

On a cloudy Monday, a classmate tried to enter the closed theater through a side door. Yu-an stopped him before his attempt became dangerous. He said the public entrance was blocked and he only wanted a picture. "That is exactly the cause of the closure," she answered. "Nobody knows whether the floor is safe." The student nodded and returned a pair of earrings he had found near the door. The earrings belonged to a costume, and their owner was relieved to see them again.

多雲的星期一，一名同學想從側門進入已封閉的劇場。余安在他冒險之前攔住了他。他說正門被擋住，只想拍張照。余安回答：「這正是封閉的原因。我們還不知道地板是否安全。」那名學生點頭，交出在門邊找到的一對耳環。耳環屬於戲服，主人見到失而復得的飾物，終於鬆了口氣。

The college had planned a final performance inside the old room. Now a government safety officer would visit before anyone could operate the lights there. Wen was not pleased with the delay, but he did not want to cheat the rules. At breakfast he ate dry cereal and studied the public inspection form. "The law is not here to force a bad ending," he told Yu-an. "It gives us a way to find out what we can do." She said his very general answer sounded like a teacher's lesson.

學院原本打算在舊場地舉行最終演出，如今必須等政府安全人員檢查，才可能在那裡操作燈光。文對延期不高興，卻不願違規。早餐時，他一邊吃乾麥片，一邊看公開檢查表。他對余安說：「規則不是為了強迫故事有壞結局，而是讓我們知道什麼事情能做。」余安笑說，他這種太概括的回答簡直像老師講課。

Bo spread the new route map across a table and asked two volunteers who had never visited the hall to find the way. One took a wrong turn near the stairs. The other reached the room but missed the quiet entrance. Yu-an did not blame either person. "A map that works only for its maker is not ready," she said. Bo made the arrows larger and placed the first sign farther from the corner. When the volunteers tried again, both arrived without help. Wen watched the second test and gave a slow nod. The change had taken less than an hour, and it prevented confusion that could have followed every guest on the festival night.

博把新路線圖攤在桌上，請兩名沒去過大廳的志工自己找路。一人在樓梯附近轉錯方向；另一人到了房間，卻錯過安靜入口。余安沒有責怪任何人。「只有畫圖的人看得懂的地圖，還不能用。」她說。博把箭頭放大，第一張指示牌也移到更靠前的轉角。志工再走一次，兩人都不靠幫忙就抵達。文看著第二次測試，慢慢點頭。這項改動不到一小時，卻能避免藝術節當晚每位來客都感到迷路。

Their friend Bo arrived on a scooter with a supply of paper signs. Bo was a runner in a local club and knew every path through campus. He offered to link the borrowed classroom to the main hall with clear arrows, so guests could find the show. Yu-an worried about using public walls without permission. Bo laughed. "We can ask first. You are allowed to be careful without turning into a police officer." They took the route to the student office and received permission for removable signs.

朋友博騎機車來，帶著一批紙製指示牌。他是地方跑步社的跑者，熟悉校園每條小路。他願意用清楚的箭頭把借用教室連到主廳，讓觀眾找到演出。余安擔心在公共牆上貼牌沒有得到允許。博笑說：「我們可以先問。謹慎不代表一定要當警察。」他們一起走到學務處，取得使用可拆式標示的許可。

The actors wanted to continue rehearsal before the signs were ready. One student argued for a liberal rule that let anyone change a scene whenever they wished. Another feared the play would have no shape. Yu-an asked them to try both ideas. For ten minutes they moved without any plan. The result was funny but confusing. Then Wen played a simple rhythm, and everyone used it as a common point. The scene became clear without taking away any actor's freedom.

演員想在牌子做好前繼續排練。一人主張寬鬆規則，讓任何人隨時改動場景；另一人擔心整齣戲會失去形狀。余安請他們兩種都試試。前十分鐘，大家毫無計畫地移動，畫面雖滑稽卻很混亂。接著文播放簡單的節奏，所有人都把它當成共同起點。場景終於清楚，演員的自由也沒有被奪走。

The stage had a second map, not for the guests but for the actors. It showed a wide circle around a chair where the old traveler would sit. Yu-an asked the cast to enter one at a time, pause, and listen before moving on. A student complained that the plan was too slow. She invited him to try a faster version, then asked the group what changed. The traveler had no chance to speak when people hurried past. The student admitted that the pause mattered. It was not a punishment for speed; it was room for one character's voice. The cast tried the slower path again and found a better rhythm.

舞臺還有第二張地圖，不是給觀眾，而是給演員。圖上畫著一個寬圓圈，圍住老旅人坐的椅子。余安請演員一人一人進場，停下、聆聽，再繼續移動。有學生抱怨太慢，她便請他示範更快的版本，再問大家差別在哪裡。人們匆匆經過時，旅人根本沒機會說話。那名學生承認停頓很重要。這不是懲罰速度，而是為一個角色的聲音留位置。演員重走較慢的路，找到更好的節奏。

At the end of the day, Yu-an wrote down three things: never use the unsafe entrance, ask before placing a sign, and give actors space within a clear plan. Her notes were less exciting than the costume earrings, but more useful. Wen walked her to the bus stop and asked if she still wanted the Lantern Room back. "Yes," she replied, "but I am starting to want something larger as well. I want the people who come to our show to feel they belong there." He nodded without trying to finish her thought.

一天結束時，余安寫下三件事：不用危險的入口、張貼標誌前先申請、在清楚計畫內給演員空間。筆記不如戲服耳環有趣，卻更實用。文陪她走到公車站，問她是否仍想拿回燈籠小劇場。「想，」她回答，「但我開始想要更大的東西：希望來看戲的人覺得自己也屬於這裡。」文點頭，沒有急著替她把話說完。
