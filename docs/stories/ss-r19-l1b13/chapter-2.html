Eli wrote, I like talking to you even when we disagree, and placed the note on Nadia's desk. Before anyone called the day strange, Nadia had noticed how quiet the college library could become when people were worried. The smallest sounds seemed clear, and the smell of ink and tea made the place feel both familiar and new. Rather than act as if every answer was obvious, Nadia opened the letter notebook and began with what had really happened. Eli did not treat silence as an empty space that needed a clever answer. That made Nadia want to speak in her own words. In the corner of the letter notebook, Nadia found the word “anybody” beside an old drawing and stopped to ask what it meant. “here,” Eli read from the practice page, then tried to connect the word to something they could actually see. A little note used “pond” as its key word; Nadia kept the note because the rest of the line was still useful.

Eli suggested they take turns speaking, because repeating the same fear would not create a new solution. Nadia tried to make it more beautiful, but he asked her to keep it plain. This was the point where a simple wish met the world as it really was. Nadia wanted to help, but wanting did not make every road safe or every rule fair. Eli asked what the other people involved might lose if the wrong choice were made. The answer took longer than either had expected. They agreed not to build the rest of their plan on a fact they had never checked. The group returned to the letter notebook and looked at the first line again. It now seemed to ask a different question. Eli asked who should hear the news next, and whether it belonged to them to share it. They chose the careful answer over the exciting one, though excitement was still in the air. The clock moved on. Nadia felt tired, yet there was a good kind of energy left after honest work. Nobody had to say that the day was perfect. They only had to remember what had worked and what still needed care.
