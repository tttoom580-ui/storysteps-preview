Nadia opened the writing workshop with a new sign: Your voice comes first. Some days begin with a plan; others begin with a question no one had expected. This one gave Nadia both. At the college library, people were coming and going, each carrying a different idea about what was important. The letter notebook made it possible to hold on to a few clear details while the rest of the world kept moving. The word “doll” appeared in an example someone had written earlier, and Eli asked if the example fit their situation. “Let us keep ‘skirt’ in our word notes,” Nadia said. “We can check its meaning again if we need it.” In the corner of the letter notebook, Nadia found the word “week” beside an old drawing and stopped to ask what it meant.

Eli listened without offering an easy answer. That was unusual enough for Nadia to notice. They named the part they understood, then left space for the part that still felt unclear. A first-year student asked for a love letter, and Nadia invited her to write the first sentence herself. Nadia felt a quick wish to fix everything at once. Then the faces nearby made the choice more complicated. It was not only about the honest letter. Other people had time, hopes, and worries of their own. Eli drew a line across the letter notebook: facts on one side, guesses on the other. That simple mark changed how they spoke. Eli turned back to the letter notebook and crossed out a guess that no longer fitted what they knew. Neither called the mistake foolish. Changing your mind after new evidence was part of learning. There was no great speech at the end of the day. Nadia and Eli shared a look that said they had both noticed the change. Nadia put away her pen with a quiet smile. She still loved good sentences, but now she knew a feeling could belong to her even if its words were plain.
