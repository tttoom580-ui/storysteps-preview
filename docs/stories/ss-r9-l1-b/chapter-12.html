四點鐘的車票

The Ticket at Four O'Clock

蒲公英鎮的慢慢巴士 · 第 12 / 25 章 · 1000以下 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

At four o’clock in the afternoon, the town hall held a small open meeting. An actor from the local theater came with a band of young musicians. They wanted to celebrate the first bus test with a song, but Koi explained that this was not the last test. They still needed a safe route and a fair ticket plan. One musician, who wore round glasses, asked whether they could play at the bus stop. “You can play in the park,” Dano said, “but the waiting corner must stay clear.” The group agreed. Koi was glad the young people had come to help, even if their first idea did not quite fit.

下午四點，鎮公所在活動廳舉辦小型公開會議。一位本地劇場演員帶著年輕樂團來了。他們想用一首歌慶祝巴士首次測試，柯伊卻解釋，測試還沒結束。他們仍需要安全路線與公平票價。一位戴圓眼鏡的樂手問可不可以在巴士站演奏。「可以去公園演奏，」丹洛說，「但候車轉角必須保持暢通。」大家答應了。柯伊很高興年輕人想幫忙，即使第一個想法還不太合適。

A woman from the supermarket brought a package of sample paper tickets. Each ticket had a large number and a simple picture. Koi took one and held it up. “I want a ticket that is easy to read,” she said, “or we should find another way.” Dano showed a small box where money could be placed, but he warned that a driver should not count coins while the bus was moving. They discussed paying before the ride and how to help a passenger who was poor. Koi had asked the council for a fund, not for the power to decide by herself who deserved a seat. The difference mattered to her.

超市一位女士帶來一包樣本車票，每張都有大號數字和簡單圖片。柯伊拿起一張。「我想要人人容易讀懂的車票，」她說，「不然就要換一種方法。」丹洛拿出一個小投幣盒，卻提醒大家司機不能一邊開車一邊數硬幣。他們討論上車前付款，以及如何幫助付不起車資的乘客。柯伊向議會申請的是補助基金，不是由她私自判斷誰值得有座位。這項差別對她很重要。

An older shop owner stood in the corner and raised a hard question. “What if the bus does not earn more than it costs?” he asked. “Will it die after one month?” Koi did not laugh at his strong word. She said the trial would collect the number of rides and the reasons people used them. They would look at the whole service, not only ticket money. If it did not help enough people, they would have to change it. If it helped but needed money, the council would have to choose openly whether the town could support it. That was the key decision, and Koi refused to hide it behind a song.

一位年長店主站在角落，提出尖銳的問題。「如果巴士賺的比花的少呢？一個月後是不是就得死掉？」柯伊沒有因為用字激烈而笑他。她說，試行期間會記錄搭乘人次與使用原因，評估整體服務，而不只看票款。如果幫助的人太少，就得調整；如果確實有幫助卻需要經費，議會便須公開決定鎮上能否支持。這才是關鍵決定，柯伊不願用一首歌掩蓋。

When the media reporter arrived, he wanted a clear answer about the price. Koi showed him the draft, with a note saying that the council had not voted yet. He asked her to say what she hoped would happen. “I hope we can play a part in making travel easier,” she answered. “But I cannot promise more than the town has agreed to do.” A musician nearby looked tired and sat on a chair. Koi realized the meeting had gone on too long. She asked for a break and gave people water. A good public meeting needed room to rest as well as room to talk.

媒體記者到場後，想知道票價的確切答案。柯伊拿出草案，旁邊清楚註明議會尚未表決。記者問她希望最後如何。「我希望我們能讓出行變得更容易，」她回答，「但不能承諾超過鎮上已同意的事。」旁邊一位樂手累得坐到椅子上。柯伊這才發現會議持續太久了，便提議休息，給大家倒水。好的公開會議不只要能說話，也要有休息的空間。

After the public meeting, the supermarket worker walked with Koi to the corner where the tickets might be sold. The worker said a small table could fit there, but only if it stayed outside the walking line. They marked the space with two paper cards and watched people pass. A parent with a pushchair went by without stopping. An older man with a wide basket passed next. Neither had to step into the road. The worker said she would ask permission before putting a table there. Koi thanked her for testing an idea before making it real. The town was learning to share its small spaces without taking them away from others.

公開會議後，超市員工陪柯伊走到預定售票的轉角。員工說可以放一張小桌子，但必須在步行線外。她們用兩張紙卡標出範圍，觀察行人通過。推嬰兒車的家長沒有停下就走過，接著一位拿著寬籃子的長者也順利通過，兩人都不必踏上馬路。員工說放桌子前會先申請許可。柯伊謝謝她先測試再實施。這座鎮正在學習如何分享有限空間，而不是佔去他人的通道。

At the close of the meeting, the band did not perform a big show. Instead, one player tapped a short rhythm to mark the next date for questions. The actor held up the draft ticket and asked the room to read it together. People could see the number clearly, but one picture looked too much like a package label. Nika promised to change it. Koi took all the notes home. She knew people wanted the bus to begin, and she did too. Still, she could not hurry through rules that would affect someone's daily life. She marked the next meeting on her calendar, then went to sleep with her shoes beside the door.

會議結束時，樂團沒有舉辦大型表演，只由一位樂手敲出簡短節奏，提醒大家下次提問的日期。演員舉起車票草樣，請全場一起讀。數字清楚，卻有個圖示太像包裹標籤。妮卡答應修改。柯伊把所有筆記帶回家。她知道大家急著巴士上路，她自己也是；但她不能草率通過影響他人日常的規則。她在日曆標上下次會議，然後把鞋子放在門邊，準備睡覺。
