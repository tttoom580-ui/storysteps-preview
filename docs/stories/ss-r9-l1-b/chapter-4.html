安靜的轉角

The Quiet Corner

蒲公英鎮的慢慢巴士 · 第 4 / 25 章 · 1000以下 · 英中對照

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

On the east side of town, a narrow street passed a green garden and a small bath house. Koi went there with Dano to check the road. They found a wooden bowl in the middle of the street. A family of ducks had been using it to feed a cute little dog. “The road is not a garden path,” Dano said gently. He moved the bowl back behind a fence, then looked both ways. The biggest problem was not the bowl. A tall wall blocked the view at the corner. Anybody who tried to cross there could not see a vehicle until it was close. Koi wrote “no bus stop here” in large letters and drew a better place one block away.

鎮子東邊有條狹窄街道，經過綠色花園與小澡堂。柯伊和丹洛去那裡檢查路況。他們在路中央發現一個木碗，是一戶鴨子家庭拿來餵一隻可愛的小狗的。「馬路不是花園小徑，」丹洛溫和地說。他把碗移回圍籬後，左右看了看。真正的大問題並不是木碗，而是一面高牆擋住了轉角的視線。想過馬路的人，要等車子靠得很近才看得見。柯伊用大字寫下「此處不設站」，並在一個街區外畫出更好的位置。

Mrs. Pella, who lived in a small house by the wall, came out when she heard a knock. She offered the visitors water and asked what they were doing. Dano explained that a medium-sized bus needed more room than a bicycle. Mrs. Pella looked surprised. She had thought a bus stop must be right outside every home that asked for one. “Not if that makes the crossing dangerous,” Koi said. “We must choose a place people can reach safely.” Mrs. Pella thought about it, then pointed to a wide piece of ground near a tree. It was farther from her door, but she could see traffic from there. Koi thanked her for the suggestion.

住在牆邊小屋的佩拉太太聽見敲門聲，走了出來。她請訪客喝水，問他們在做什麼。丹洛解釋，中型巴士需要的空間比腳踏車多。佩拉太太很驚訝，原以為提出要求的人家門外都應有站牌。「如果那會讓過馬路變危險，就不行，」柯伊說，「我們必須選大家能安全抵達的地方。」佩拉太太想了想，指向樹旁一塊寬敞的空地。那裡離她家遠一些，卻看得清楚來車。柯伊謝謝她的建議。

A taxicab passed while they were measuring the space. Its driver slowed down and stopped beside the tree. He wanted to know whether the new bus would take his job away. Koi did not pretend to know the answer. “People will still need a taxi for some trips,” she said. “Our bus may be useful for short, shared journeys at set times.” Dano invited the driver to the next meeting. They could talk about roads and times without hiding their different needs. The driver nodded, but did not smile. Koi knew that being friendly did not mean everybody would agree. She wrote his concern on the same page as all the others.

他們測量空間時，一輛計程車經過。司機減速，在樹旁停下，想知道新巴士會不會搶走他的工作。柯伊沒有假裝知道答案。「有些旅程還是需要計程車，」她說，「我們的巴士或許適合固定時間的短程共乘。」丹洛邀請司機參加下次會議，大家可以不隱瞞各自需求，直接討論道路與時間。司機點頭，卻沒有笑。柯伊知道，友善不代表人人都會同意。她把他的擔心記在和其他意見同一頁的紙上。

In the afternoon, the town workers checked the tree corner. They found that a painted waiting line could fit there without taking away the walking path. Koi asked them not to mark the ground until the council had approved the place. She did not want a surprise stop to appear before the road was ready. Dano and Koi returned to the wall and watched a child try to pass with a small cart. The cart turned slowly, but it passed. This was a useful test. Koi crossed out the unsafe corner on her map and drew a clear path from the new waiting place to the crossing with the best view.

下午，鎮公所的工務人員檢查了樹旁轉角。他們發現可以畫候車線，而且不必佔用步道。柯伊請他們先別動手，等議會同意站點再施工。她不希望道路還沒準備好，就突然出現一個新站。丹洛與柯伊回到高牆旁，觀察一個孩子推著小車經過。車轉得慢，卻能通過。這是有用的測試。柯伊在地圖上劃掉危險轉角，並畫出從新候車處到視野最清楚斑馬線的路徑。

Koi and Dano later stood at the new tree stop and tried to imagine a full morning there. A child might arrive with a school bag. Mrs. Pella might come with her shopping cart. A worker might head to the east farm before sunrise. They marked a place for each person to wait without standing in the road. Dano reminded Koi that a stop should still be open for people walking past who were not riding the bus. She watched a woman pass with a sleeping baby and saw that the path remained clear. Only then did she feel ready to put the new place before the council.

後來，柯伊與丹洛站在新的樹旁站點，試想繁忙早晨的情況。孩子可能帶著書包來，佩拉太太可能推購物車，工人可能在日出前前往東邊農場。他們標出各自能安全等待的位置，不必站到馬路上。丹洛提醒她，站點也必須讓不搭巴士的行人順利經過。柯伊看著一位抱著熟睡寶寶的女士走過，步道仍保持暢通。直到此時，她才覺得可以把新位置交給議會討論。

At sunset, Mrs. Pella called them back for a final look. She had placed a chair outside her door and was watching the quiet street. “I was not happy when you said no,” she admitted. “But I can understand the choice now. My grandson should not have to stand where he cannot see a car.” Koi sat with her for a moment. The light turned the garden leaves gold. “The new stop will not fix every trip,” Koi said, “but it can be a start.” Mrs. Pella smiled. For the first time that day, the old wall seemed less like an end to the road and more like a problem the town could work around together.

日落時，佩拉太太叫他們回來再看一眼。她在門外放了椅子，正看著安靜的街道。「你說不行的時候，我其實不太高興，」她承認，「但現在我懂了。我不希望孫子站在看不見來車的地方。」柯伊陪她坐了一會兒。夕陽把花園葉子照成金色。「新站不能解決每個問題，」柯伊說，「但可以先從這裡開始。」佩拉太太笑了。這一天裡，舊牆第一次不再像道路的終點，而像是大家可以一起繞過的難題。
