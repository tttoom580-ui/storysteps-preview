一個座位的價格

The Price of a Seat

蒲公英鎮的慢慢巴士 · 第 8 / 25 章 · 1000以下 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

At noon, Koi went to the town museum to look at old travel plans. A book on the shelf showed a train that once stopped outside Dandelion Town. The train had long gone, but its picture gave her an idea: a clear timetable could help people plan their day. The museum guide told her a story about a queen who traveled with more bags than people. Koi listened politely, then asked about real travel costs. The guide opened a file with prices from past years. “Do not make a boring plan full of numbers no one can understand,” she said. “Show each dollar and tell people what it is for.” Koi wrote that advice down.

中午，柯伊去鎮博物館看以前的交通計畫。書架上有本書，畫著一列曾在蒲公英鎮外停靠的火車。火車早已不再行駛，但圖上的時刻表給她一個點子：清楚的班次表可以幫助人們安排一天。導覽員講了個女王旅行時帶的行李比隨從還多的故事。柯伊禮貌聽完，轉而詢問實際費用。導覽員打開一份舊價格檔案。「別做一張只有數字、沒人看得懂的無聊計畫，」她說，「每一元都要清楚寫出用途。」柯伊記下了這個建議。

Back at the town hall, Dano placed three large cards on the table. Each card showed one way to run the bus for a trial month. The first was too expensive. The second left no money for repairs. The third offered fewer trips but kept enough money for safety checks. Koi did not think a successful service meant a full bus every time. A bus that was too full could leave someone behind, while an almost empty bus might still be helping one person make an important trip. She wanted to ask what else the money could do before choosing a plan.

回到鎮公所，丹洛在桌上放了三張大卡片，分別寫著試行一個月的三種方式。第一種太貴，第二種沒有留下維修費，第三種班次較少，卻能保留安全檢查的預算。柯伊不認為成功的服務一定要每班滿載。太滿的車可能讓人上不了車；幾乎空車的巴士，也可能正好幫一個人完成重要的旅程。她想先知道同樣的錢還能做到什麼，再選方案。

A cook came to the meeting with a fork in his pocket. He had hurried from lunch and had forgotten to put it away. Everyone laughed, and the room became less tense. Then he asked a hard question: if a person could not pay, would the driver leave them at the stop? Koi said they needed a simple rule that treated every passenger with care. Dano suggested a low fare and a small fund for those who needed help. They agreed to ask the council, not make a private promise. The cook put his fork on the table and said he would give part of the money for the first month.

一位廚師帶著叉子來開會。他匆匆吃完午飯，忘了把叉子放回去。大家笑了，氣氛也不再那麼緊張。接著他問一個難題：如果有人付不起車資，司機會把他留在站牌嗎？柯伊說，必須訂出簡單又尊重乘客的規則。丹洛建議低票價，另設少量補助給有需要的人。大家同意先提請議會討論，而不是私下承諾。廚師把叉子放到桌上，說自己願意為第一個月捐出一點費用。

Nika used small paper circles to show how money would roll from ticket sales to fuel and repairs. She gave each child at the meeting a card and asked them to tell her where the coins should go. One child put everything under “driver,” forgetting the vehicle itself. Another put every coin under “fuel,” leaving nothing for the driver. Koi liked the game because the mistake was easy to see and fix. “You do not have to be a queen or a museum guide to ask about money,” she told the children. The real plan belonged to the people who would ride and support the bus, not to one person with a neat file.

妮卡用小紙圈示範票款如何流向燃料與維修費。她發卡片給參加會議的孩子，請他們說說硬幣該放在哪裡。有個孩子把所有錢放進「司機」欄，忘了車子本身也要維護；另一個全放進「燃料」，沒替司機留薪水。柯伊喜歡這個遊戲，因為錯誤很容易看見，也能修正。「不用是女王或博物館導覽員，也能問錢的問題，」她對孩子們說。真正的計畫屬於將搭乘和支持巴士的人，不只屬於一個把檔案整理得很整齊的人。

Before everybody left, Koi asked them to listen to a short story about a dangerous road. A man had once picked the cheapest path to save money, but the path had no safe place to wait. He later had to spend more on another ride. Nobody in the room knew that man; it was only an example. Still, the example helped them see that the lowest ticket price was not the whole answer. Dano wrote “safe first, clear second, fair third” on the board, then crossed it out. “All three matter together,” he said. Koi agreed and drew a circle around the words instead of putting them in an order.

大家離開前，柯伊請他們聽一段關於危險道路的小故事。故事裡，一個人為了省錢選了最便宜的路線，卻發現那裡沒有安全候車處，最後還得多花錢另找交通。房裡沒人認識那個人；這只是個例子，卻讓大家明白最低票價不是全部答案。丹洛在板上寫「安全第一、清楚第二、公平第三」，隨即劃掉。「這三件事要一起做到。」他說。柯伊同意，便畫個圓圈把三件事圈在一起，不再排序。

Before the council vote, Koi went back through each price with Dano. She asked him to tell her if any number was based on a guess. They found one: the paper cost did not include signs that could be read in rain. Nika added a small amount for stronger cards. The final cost rose, but the plan became more honest. Koi put the three choices on the wall, with a clear note beside each one. She could not tell people what to choose. She could tell them what each choice meant. As the room emptied, Dano said he trusted a plan more when it showed its weak places openly.

議會表決前，柯伊和丹洛重新核對每一項價格。她請丹洛指出哪些數字只是猜測。他們找到一項：紙張費沒有算進雨天也看得清楚的站牌。妮卡補上耐用卡片的小筆費用。總成本提高了，計畫卻更誠實。柯伊把三個選項貼在牆上，旁邊附上清楚說明。她無法替居民選擇，卻能告訴大家每項選擇代表什麼。人們散去時，丹洛說，願意公開弱點的計畫，反而更值得信任。
