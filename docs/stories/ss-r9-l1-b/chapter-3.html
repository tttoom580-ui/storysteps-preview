有兩個時鐘的道路

The Road with Two Clocks

蒲公英鎮的慢慢巴士 · 第 3 / 25 章 · 1000以下 · 英中對照

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

Koi and Dano began their road check near the school. A basketball rolled into the road, and three children ran after it. Dano stopped at once and waited until all the children were back behind the gate. “This area needs a slow speed,” he said. Koi wrote the fact in an email to the town hall. She wanted to define a safe stop as a place where people could wait away from moving vehicles. A stop beside a game field did not meet that rule yet. One child brought an eraser and helped her change the map. He drew the waiting place on the school side of the road, where a lamp already stood.

柯伊與丹洛從學校附近開始勘查。一顆籃球滾到馬路上，三個孩子跟著追出去。丹洛立刻停下，等孩子全回到校門內。「這個區域車速得慢。」柯伊把這件事寫成電子郵件寄給鎮公所。她想為「安全站牌」下定義：乘客必須能在車流外等待。球場旁目前還不符合這項規則。一個孩子拿來橡皮擦，幫她修改地圖，把候車處畫在學校這側，正好有一盞現成的路燈。

They continued to the orange farm. A few workers had asked for a ride at six pm, but the road was full of small trucks at that hour. Dano watched the traffic for a quarter of an hour. Koi counted every vehicle in a simple table. There were fewer trucks after the packing work ended. A worker showed them a modern clock by the gate and told them when the busy hour would finish. “We will not leave people at night with no light,” Dano said. He pointed to a plant growing over the old lamp. Koi made another note: ask the farm to cut back the leaves before any bus could stop there.

他們接著到了橘子農場。有幾位工人想在晚上六點搭車，但那時路上全是小貨車。丹洛觀察交通十五分鐘，柯伊則用簡單表格數每一輛車。包裝工作結束後，貨車才明顯減少。一位工人指著門口的現代時鐘，告訴他們忙碌時段何時結束。「不能把乘客留在沒有燈的夜路上。」丹洛說。他指著遮住舊路燈的一株植物。柯伊又記下一項工作：巴士設站以前，要先請農場修剪樹葉。

When they returned to town, Koi compared two kinds of time. One was written on the clock. The other was felt by people who needed to wait, carry things, or walk slowly. She could write 6:15 on a timetable, but if the safe place was a long walk from the farm gate, workers might still miss the ride. Dano suggested walking the path from the field to the proposed stop with an empty fruit box. They did so together. The trip took longer than Koi had guessed. She was happy to have made the test before drawing a final line. An easy mark on paper could hide a hard day in real life.

回到鎮上，柯伊比較了兩種時間。一種寫在時鐘上，另一種則是等待、提東西或慢慢走路的人實際感受到的時間。她可以在時刻表寫上六點十五分，但如果安全候車處離農場大門太遠，工人仍可能趕不上。丹洛建議兩人提著空水果箱，從田地走到預定站牌。他們真的走了一趟，花的時間比柯伊原先猜的久。她慶幸自己在定線前做了測試。地圖上輕輕一筆，可能藏著別人辛苦的一天。

At the town hall, Koi put all the times on one page. She had hoped to begin with ten stops, but the road test showed that only five were ready. The other places needed a safe waiting area or a better time. One council member asked why the list had become shorter. Koi explained that less could be better at the start. A route with five safe stops was more useful than a longer route that made people take risks. Dano added that they still had to check how long it took to turn a bus at the end of the road. Their work was not done, but now the questions were clear.

在鎮公所，柯伊把所有時間整理在同一頁。她原本希望一開始就設十站，路勘後才發現只有五站已準備好。其餘地點需要更安全的候車區，或更合適的時段。一位議員問，名單為何變短了。柯伊解釋，起步時少一點也可能更好。五個安全的站點，比讓乘客冒險的長路線實用。丹洛補充，他們還得測試巴士在道路末端迴轉要花多久。工作尚未結束，但問題已經更明確。

The next morning, a mail carrier came past the school gate while Koi was checking the new drawing. He asked whether the bus would stop directly in front of the little mail office. Koi showed him the line farther down the street. He tested the walk with a heavy bag and said it was possible, but he would need to leave a little earlier. That was useful information, not a reason to change the stop at once. Koi wrote the walking time beside the drawing. She also told the carrier that the town was asking people to report unsafe places, not simply the places they liked best. He promised to look carefully on his rounds.

隔天早晨，柯伊檢查新圖畫時，一位郵務員從校門旁經過。他問巴士是否會直接停在小郵務所門前。柯伊指著街道較遠處的站點。郵務員提著沉重郵袋試走一趟，說走得到，但得提早一些出門。這是有用資訊，卻不表示立刻要改站。柯伊把步行時間寫在圖旁，也告訴郵務員鎮上需要居民回報不安全的地方，而不只是自己最喜歡的位置。郵務員答應在巡路時多留意。

In the evening, Koi returned to the school and watched the children finish their game. The gate was quiet now. The child with the eraser waved and asked when the bus would begin. “Not until the road is ready,” she replied. “Will you help tell the other children where to wait?” He agreed and asked if he could draw a big picture for the wall. Koi said yes, as long as it showed the real waiting place. She saw that a plan was not only made by adults with tables and clocks. It could also grow when a child learned why a small choice kept everyone safe. She walked home with the new drawing in her bag.

傍晚，柯伊又去學校，看孩子們打完球。大門現在很安靜。拿橡皮擦的孩子向她揮手，問巴士何時開始。「等道路準備好才行，」她回答，「你願意幫忙告訴其他孩子在哪裡等車嗎？」孩子答應，還問能不能畫張大圖貼在牆上。柯伊同意，但要畫出真正的候車位置。她看見，一份計畫不只來自拿著表格與時鐘的大人，也會在孩子理解某個小選擇如何保護大家時慢慢長大。她把新圖畫收進包裡，走回家。
