泥地上的座位

A Seat above the Mud

蒲公英鎮的慢慢巴士 · 第 6 / 25 章 · 1000以下 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

After two days of rain, the path to the shop was full of mud. Koi went to look at it with Nika, a mole who drew clear signs. At the roadside, a child was trying to pull a chair from a deep puddle. His arm was covered in brown water. “Do not step any farther,” Nika warned. She used a long stick to move the chair toward dry land, while Koi kept the child back. The little boy was surprised that a place he crossed every day had become so hard to use. Koi wrote in her desk book that a seat beside a bus stop must stay dry. A convenient stop in good weather was not always convenient in rain.

連續下了兩天雨，通往商店的小路滿是泥巴。柯伊與擅長畫清楚標誌的鼴鼠妮卡一起去查看。路邊有個孩子正試著把椅子從深水坑拉出來，手臂沾滿棕色泥水。「別再往前走。」妮卡警告。她用長棍把椅子推向乾地，柯伊則讓孩子保持距離。小男孩很驚訝，原來每天走的地方會突然變得這麼難通行。柯伊在桌上的本子記下：巴士站旁的座位必須保持乾燥。晴天方便的站牌，下雨時未必方便。

Dano arrived carrying an old envelope. Inside was a drawing of the first roads in town, made before the pond was wide. He loved history, but he did not want to use an old plan without checking the ground. “We may find a better place beside the stone wall,” he said. “However, we must ask the people who live there.” Koi and Nika walked to the wall and found a narrow, dry space. A shop worker offered them tea. She said the news of a bus had spread quickly, and many people were already speaking as though the route was decided. Koi asked her to tell customers that the plan was still being tested.

丹洛帶著一只舊信封來了，裡面是早年鎮上道路的圖畫，那時池塘還沒有現在這麼寬。他喜歡歷史，卻不想在沒檢查現況前就照舊圖施工。「石牆旁也許有更好的地方，」他說，「但得先問住在那裡的人。」柯伊與妮卡走到牆邊，發現一塊狹窄但乾燥的空地。商店員工請他們喝茶，說巴士消息傳得很快，許多人已經以為路線定案。柯伊請她轉告顧客：計畫仍在測試中。

On the way back, they saw fish in the pond rise to the surface after the rain. Nika found that funny and stopped to watch. Dano noticed something less funny: the water had come close to the road. He told Koi that a real bus would need more room to turn when the ground was wet. A line on paper could not show a slippery wheel. Koi asked whether they could move the waiting seat to higher land. The shop worker said the shop could welcome passengers under its roof during a storm, if they did not block the door. That was kind, but Koi still wanted a public place for the stop.

回程時，他們看到池塘裡的魚在雨後浮上水面。妮卡覺得有趣，停下來看。丹洛注意到另一件不太好笑的事：水已經靠近馬路。他告訴柯伊，地面濕滑時，真正的巴士需要更大的迴轉空間。紙上的線看不出輪胎打滑。柯伊問能不能把候車座位移到較高的地面。店員說，如果遇到暴風雨，商店願意讓乘客在屋簷下避雨，只要不擋住門口。這是好意，但柯伊仍希望站牌有公共的等待空間。

Later that day, Koi called a town worker who knew about roads. He checked the old drawing and found that a small drain had been covered by a pile of stones. Nobody could simply take the stones away; the worker first had to make sure the wall would stay strong. They agreed to put a warning sign near the puddle. Koi wrote down every step and left space for the worker's report. She did not say the problem was solved because she had found it. Finding was only the first part of doing. Nika painted a large blue arrow pointing toward a dry path that people could use that week.

當天下午，柯伊打電話請熟悉道路的鎮公所員工來看。他查了舊圖，發現有一條小排水溝被石塊蓋住。石塊不能直接移走，員工得先確認牆體仍然穩固。大家同意先在水坑旁放警告標誌。柯伊逐步記下工作，留空給員工的報告。她不會因為找到了問題，就說問題已經解決。發現只是工作的第一步。妮卡畫了一支大藍箭頭，指向本週仍可安全通行的乾路。

The town worker returned after checking the stone wall. He said that moving the stones would be safe only if another part of the drain was opened first. Koi listened, then asked him to speak slowly so she could write down the right order. Nika drew a picture showing the water moving away from the waiting place. They put it on the board with a date for the next inspection. Nobody was asked to step into the mud to see whether the repair worked. They would wait for the worker's report and check from dry ground. Koi was learning to respect the difference between helping and doing a job that needed special training.

鎮公所員工檢查石牆後又來了一趟。他說，必須先打開排水溝的另一段，才能安全搬開石頭。柯伊仔細聽，請他說慢些，讓她能把正確順序寫下來。妮卡畫出水流如何遠離候車區的圖，貼在告示板，附上下一次檢查日期。沒有人被要求走進泥裡試修理是否有效。他們會等工人的報告，再從乾燥地面查看。柯伊逐漸學會分辨，什麼是協助，什麼是需要專門訓練的工作。

That evening, Koi took the new path home and met Ressa at its end. Ressa had come out to see the sign. “Do you still believe a bus will come?” she asked. Koi answered, “Yes, but I believe more strongly that we must do it well.” Ressa reached for the back of the dry seat and sat down. From there she could see both the pond and the road. She said it was the first place on that street where she could rest without feeling in the way. Koi looked at the small seat and smiled. Even before the bus arrived, their questions had begun to make the town easier to cross.

傍晚，柯伊走新路回家，在盡頭遇見瑞莎。瑞莎特地出來看新標誌。「你還相信巴士會來嗎？」她問。柯伊回答：「相信，而且我更相信一定要把它做好。」瑞莎扶著乾燥座椅的椅背坐下。從這裡，她能同時看到池塘和道路。她說，這是這條街上第一個讓她能休息、又不覺得擋路的位置。柯伊看著小座椅微笑。巴士還沒出現，他們提出的問題卻已開始讓鎮上更容易行走。
