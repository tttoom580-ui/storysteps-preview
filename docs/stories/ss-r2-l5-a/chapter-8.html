The Ethical Line

實習結束前，我要拿回自己的名字 · 第 8 / 25 章 · 4000–5000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

Mara called a meeting at a rented venue near the library. She asked everyone to state one ethical limit before they discussed strategy. Mei promised not to publish personal health details. Omar promised not to reveal confidential access credentials. Alina promised not to pressure residents for interviews. Nadia promised to distinguish verified information from claims. The room felt solemn, as though they were preparing for a dreadful exploration, but the rules brought clarity. “Caution is not a chore,” Mara said. “It is how we protect the people whose stories we use.” A delegation from the district association arrived with public measurements and an undergraduate volunteer who had organized them. The student had impressive fluency in technical language, yet Mei asked her to explain the results in ordinary terms. A reliable public report had to be understood, not merely admired.

瑪拉在圖書館附近租借場地開會，要每個人在討論策略前先說出一條道德界線。梅承諾不公開個人健康資料；歐瑪承諾不洩漏機密登入資訊；艾莉娜承諾不強迫居民受訪；娜迪亞承諾區分已查證資訊與主張。氣氛莊嚴得像要展開可怕探險，規則卻帶來清晰感。「謹慎不是雜務，」瑪拉說，「它能保護我們引用故事的當事人。」地方協會代表團帶來公開測量資料，還有負責整理的一名大學生。她使用技術語言十分流利，梅仍請她用一般人聽得懂的話解釋。可靠的公開報告必須讓人理解，而不只是令人佩服。

The group examined a patent application belonging to a private mapping company. The document described a system that would generate emergency routes using commercial movement data. A premium service would charge extra for detailed accessibility information. Omar pointed out that a company could hold a patent on a method without owning every independent design that used similar ideas. Mei added that the city should not assume one vendor was the sole possible provider. Several volunteer teams could steer a local pilot, although they could not replace a professional safety review. Mara warned against turning a technical dispute into superstition: a glowing screen was not proof that its predictions were right. In the interior of the old library, they tested the model on paper and found a simple inconsistency. Two streets listed as open had been closed for months.

團隊檢視一家私人地圖公司的專利申請，內容描述利用商業移動資料生成緊急路線的系統。其高價服務會額外收取詳細無障礙資訊費用。歐瑪指出，公司可擁有方法的專利，卻不因此擁有使用類似想法的所有獨立設計。梅補充，市府不應假設只有一家供應商。志工團隊可以協助推動地方試辦，但不能取代專業安全審查。瑪拉提醒別把技術爭議變成迷信：發亮的螢幕不代表預測正確。大家在舊圖書館內部用紙本測試模型，發現一項明顯矛盾：兩條標成開放的街道，幾個月前就封閉了。

Mei dedicated the rest of the day to identifying the origin of those street labels. Some came from a public utility map; others had been imported from a commercial data source without a clear date. The difference mattered. One bad label could shed doubt on an entire route, but it did not mean every part of the model was useless. Alina proposed removing the unreliable streets until the contractor supplied evidence. Omar agreed, provided the change was recorded and reviewed. A resident asked why the team was being so careful when an obvious injustice seemed to demand immediate action. Mei understood the frustration. She explained that a report could become a valuable commodity in a political argument, and that careless language could be used against the very residents it was meant to protect.

梅用剩下的時間追查街道標籤來源：有的來自公共設施地圖，有的從商業資料庫匯入，卻沒有明確日期。差別很重要；一個錯誤標籤可能讓整條路線受到懷疑，卻不表示模型全部無用。艾莉娜提議，在承包商提供證據前先排除不可靠路段。歐瑪同意，但要求記錄變更並受審查。一名居民不解，既然不公如此明顯，團隊為何還如此小心。梅理解他的挫折，解釋一份報告可能成為政治爭論中的寶貴商品，而草率用語可能反過來傷害它原本要保護的居民。

A retired librarian offered to dedicate a weekly afternoon to teaching volunteers how to trace public documents. She stressed that a record's date was not necessarily the date an event had happened; it might only show when someone entered information into a system. Mei added an example from the route database and asked the students to identify the difference. One volunteer proudly found an apparent contradiction, then discovered that the files referred to different versions. Instead of laughing, the group discussed what additional evidence would resolve it. Mara watched the exercise with approval. The team was no longer simply protecting its own case. It was practicing a habit of inquiry that might prevent future mistakes, and nobody needed permission from a powerful director to ask a careful question about a public record.

一位退休圖書館員願意每週奉獻一個下午，教志工追查公開文件。她強調文件日期未必等於事件發生日期，也可能只是有人把資訊輸入系統的時間。梅用路線資料庫舉例，請學員辨認差別。一名志工興奮地找到看似矛盾之處，後來才發現兩份檔案指向不同版本。大家沒有嘲笑他，而是討論還需要什麼證據才有答案。瑪拉欣慰地看著。團隊不再只是在保護自己的案件，也正練習一種能避免未來錯誤的提問習慣；而對公開紀錄提出謹慎疑問，本來就不必得到有權主管的允許。

Before they left, Mara asked Mei to write a one-page statement of purpose. It would say what the team knew, what remained uncertain, and what information they would not seek. Mei expected the exercise to slow them down. Instead it made their work easier to defend. She could tell a skeptical official exactly why the original route mattered and exactly which facts still required verification. Nadia offered to publish the statement alongside any future article. Mei agreed on the condition that residents saw the relevant passages first. When she locked the venue door, the sky had cleared. The team had not uncovered a hidden villain in a single afternoon. They had established something more useful: a method that would still be fair when the facts became uncomfortable.

離開前，瑪拉請梅寫一頁目的聲明，說明團隊已知、未知，以及不會索取哪些資訊。梅原以為這會拖慢進度，結果反而讓工作更容易辯護。面對懷疑的官員，她能明確說出原始路線的重要性，也能指出哪些事實尚待查證。娜迪亞提議未來報導時一併刊登聲明。梅同意，但條件是居民先閱讀相關段落。她鎖上場地門時，天空放晴了。團隊並沒有在一個下午揪出隱藏的反派，而是建立一套更有用的方法——即使事實不合心意，仍能保持公平。
