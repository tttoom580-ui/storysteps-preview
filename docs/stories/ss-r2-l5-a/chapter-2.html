The Donor's Dinner

實習結束前，我要拿回自己的名字 · 第 2 / 25 章 · 4000–5000 · 英中對照

Northline held its donor dinner in a renovated station whose architecture celebrated the city's industrial past. A journalist named Nadia Song stood by the entrance, studying a series of photographs from the river district. Mei had come to deliver a projector cable, not to tell a story, but Nadia noticed the marked map in her folder. “This shelter appears in the older version,” she said. “Why does it disappear in the presentation?” Mei explained that the removal could create adverse conditions for residents who could not walk far. She would not claim that Evan had broken a law; that judgment belonged to an investigation and perhaps a prosecution, not to an angry intern. Nadia appreciated the distinction. Journalism, she said, was a quest for verifiable facts, not a contest to invent the loudest accusation. Mei left her the number of Northline's public information desk, not the private names of vulnerable families.

北線在翻修過的車站舉辦捐助晚宴，建築保留了城市工業時代的特色。記者娜迪亞・宋站在入口旁，研究一系列河岸社區的照片。梅只是來送投影機電線，不是來說故事；但娜迪亞注意到她資料夾裡有標記的地圖。「舊版有這個避難站，」她說，「為什麼簡報版沒有？」梅解釋，取消站點可能讓行動不便的居民陷入不利處境。她不會斷言伊凡犯法；那應交由調查、甚至檢控程序判斷，而不是由憤怒的實習生決定。娜迪亞認同這個區別。她說，新聞工作追求可查證的事實，不是比誰的指控最響亮。梅提供的是北線公關窗口電話，而非弱勢家庭的私人姓名。

Inside the hall, a donor praised Evan for avoiding what he called excessive consultation. An architect replied that the area's indigenous riverside history made simple maps unreliable; paths used for generations were not always recorded by modern planners. The two men began an absurd argument about whether local knowledge counted as evidence. Mei saw an opportunity to highlight a practical concern rather than debate ideology. She showed a public photograph of a narrow ramp beside a fish market. A salmon vendor used that ramp each morning, pulling a cart over cracked stones. If it vanished from the route, a delivery worker might have to rip open a row of temporary barriers just to pass. “That's a variation our model must consider,” Mei said. “It isn't proof of wrongdoing, but it is proof that the new route needs another test.” The architect nodded and offered to visit the site with her.

宴會廳裡，一名捐助者稱讚伊凡省掉「過度諮詢」。一位建築師反駁，河岸地區的原住民歷史，使簡化地圖並不可靠；世代使用的通道未必記錄在現代規劃圖上。兩人竟為在地知識算不算證據展開荒謬爭論。梅趁機提出實際問題，而非爭論意識形態。她展示魚市場狹窄坡道的公開照片。賣鮭魚的商販每天清晨推車越過龜裂石板；若這條路線消失，送貨工人可能得撕開臨時圍欄才能通行。「這就是模型必須考慮的變化，」梅說，「它不能證明有人違規，但可以證明新路線需要再測試。」建築師點頭，願意與她到現場看看。

Evan found Mei beside the dessert table and offered an oily compliment. He said that outsiders did not understand the pressure on a director and that employees who persist in asking questions sometimes harm the very people they mean to help. Mei felt the familiar urge to retreat. Then she saw the architect approaching with the journalist. Evan's smile became stiff. Rather than trade insults, Mei requested the official criteria for route changes. Evan promised to render them in a simple report. He also offered a brief shoulder massage, claiming that everyone was stressed; she stepped away and said no. The awkward moment showed her that authority could hide behind friendly gestures as easily as behind threats. Later, Nadia described a series of similar projects where public participation existed on paper but not in practice. She asked to check the records before publishing anything.

伊凡在甜點桌旁找到梅，給了她一句油滑的稱讚。他說外人不懂主管的壓力，不斷追問的員工有時反而會傷害想幫助的人。梅又想退縮，卻看見建築師和記者走近，伊凡的笑容變僵硬了。梅沒有互罵，只索取變更路線的正式標準。伊凡答應用簡明報告呈現。他又說大家壓力都很大，提議替她按摩肩膀；梅退開，明確說不。這個尷尬片刻讓她知道，權力不只會躲在威脅後，也可能藏在親切姿態裡。稍後娜迪亞談起一系列類似專案：居民參與只存在於文件，實務上卻沒有。她表示會先查核紀錄，再決定是否報導。

Before leaving the hall, Mei visited the small exhibit beside the entrance. It displayed the station's original plans beside modern proposals for public access. Several drawings had been corrected over decades, and every correction named the person responsible. An elderly volunteer explained that the building had once survived a flood because someone noticed an apparently minor door that opened the wrong way. Mei thought about her maps and the families who relied on them. The comparison did not establish that Evan had acted dishonestly, but it reinforced her belief that design history should remain visible. If Northline expected the public to trust its routes, it had to show how those routes had been tested and changed. She left with a photograph of the exhibit's public caption and a clearer request for the archive.

離開會堂前，梅參觀入口旁的小展覽，裡面並列車站原始圖與現代公共通行提案。幾十年來許多圖面被修正，每項修正都寫著負責人。一名年長志工說，車站過去曾在洪水中倖存，是因為有人發現一扇看似不起眼、開啟方向卻錯誤的門。梅想到自己的地圖與依賴它的家庭。這段比較不能證明伊凡不誠實，卻強化她認為設計歷史應保持可見的信念。若北線要大眾信任路線，就得展示路線如何測試、如何變更。她拍下公開展示的圖說，帶著更明確的調閱要求離開。

At home, Mei searched public meeting minutes. She discovered that the donor dinner had been advertised as a celebration of a completed community review, although no review results were attached. She wrote down the missing references rather than guessing what they contained. The next day she sent one calm email asking where those records could be inspected. Ruth told her that this was how a useful question often began: not as a declaration that someone was guilty, but as a request that someone explain an inconsistency. Mei still felt like an outsider among senior professionals. Yet the day had taught her that an outsider could sometimes see what insiders had learned to ignore. At midnight an email appeared from the city archive. It contained a date and the name of the person who had received the first map. That person was not Evan.

回家後，梅搜尋公開會議紀錄，發現捐助晚宴宣傳為「社區審查完成」的慶祝活動，卻沒有附上任何審查結果。她記下缺漏的引用資料，不猜測內容。隔天她寄出語氣平穩的電子郵件，詢問能在哪裡查閱紀錄。露絲告訴她，有價值的追問往往就這樣開始：不是先宣稱誰有罪，而是要求有人解釋不一致之處。梅在資深專業人士中仍像外人，但這一天讓她明白，外人有時能看見內部人早已習慣忽略的問題。午夜，市府檔案室寄來一封信，裡面有日期，以及收到第一版地圖的人名。那個人不是伊凡。
