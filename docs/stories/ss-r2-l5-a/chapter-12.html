證人不是武器

A Witness Is Not a Weapon

實習結束前，我要拿回自己的名字 · 第 12 / 25 章 · 4000–5000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

The first anonymous accusation was abnormal in its precision. It claimed that Omar had taken a hostage: not a person, but a confidential database he supposedly refused to release. Another message used the word kidnap to describe his protection of a backup drive. Mara urged the team to confront the allegations through records, not angry replies. The city's security officer confirmed that the drive had stayed in official custody and that Omar had never held it. “A dramatic script can create a sensation,” Mara said, “but it cannot turn a metaphor into a fact.” Mei felt ashamed that the messages had frightened her so deeply. She had begun to expect a betrayal from every colleague. Ruth reminded her that suspicion was not a reliable norm for a working team. They needed to acquaint themselves with each witness's actual responsibilities before deciding what any person could have done.

第一封匿名指控精確得反常。它說歐瑪把一個「人質」扣在手上——其實不是人，而是一份他據稱拒絕交出的機密資料庫。另一封訊息甚至用「綁架」形容他保護備份硬碟。瑪拉勸團隊透過紀錄面對指控，不要憤怒回嘴。市府資安官證實硬碟一直留在官方保管之下，歐瑪從未持有。「戲劇化的劇本能製造轟動，」瑪拉說，「但不能把比喻變成事實。」梅為自己被訊息嚇成這樣感到羞愧；她開始懷疑每位同事都會背叛。露絲提醒她，長期猜疑不是團隊合作的正常方式。必須先熟悉每名證人的真實職責，才判斷對方可能做過什麼。

A public records exhibit opened in the same hall that week. It explored the continental history of labor migration and included photographs of missionaries, prisoners, and families who had escaped discrimination. One section explained how the language of slavery had been used both to document actual suffering and, carelessly, to exaggerate ordinary disputes. Mei stood before the display with a local history observer. She understood why the anonymous sender's theatrical accusations were wrong: words carried histories that should not be borrowed merely to win attention. The observer showed her an old street photograph containing a visible bolt on the gate of a former shelter. The building had changed uses, but its narrow entrance had been a problem for decades. That image was compatible with the new field measurements. Mei asked for permission to cite it, including its source and date.

那週同一座會堂舉辦公開檔案展，探討大陸間勞工移動的歷史，展出傳教士、囚犯，以及逃離歧視家庭的照片。其中一區說明「奴隸制」一詞如何被用來記錄真正的苦難，也如何被人不當借用來誇大一般爭議。梅和地方歷史觀察員站在展板前，明白匿名寄件者戲劇化的指控為何錯誤：詞語帶著歷史，不能為了吸睛就隨意挪用。觀察員給她看一張舊街景照，照片中前避難站大門有明顯門閂。建築用途已變，狹窄入口卻是幾十年的老問題。這照片與新的田野測量相容。梅請求引用許可，並註明來源與日期。

That afternoon, an auditor requested the company's stock procurement records, believing that they might reveal who had paid for the altered maps. The request was too broad for the technical stage of the review. Helen narrowed it to the contract and specific invoices relevant to the printing. A viewer watching the public session online complained that the investigators were hiding information. Mara explained the difference between a viable inquiry and unlimited access to every corporate file. When the session ended, a nervous clerk approached Mei and began to mumble. She said a manager had instructed staff to replace a folder name but she had refused. She did not want to be called a traitor to her employer. Mei assured her that the team would not use her name without consent and suggested that she speak directly to the independent reviewer.

下午，一名稽核員索取公司股票採購紀錄，認為或可查出誰支付修改地圖的費用。對技術審查階段而言，這項要求太廣。海倫把範圍縮小到相關合約與印刷發票。線上觀看公開會議的一名觀眾抱怨調查員在隱瞞資訊。瑪拉解釋可行調查與無限制存取公司所有檔案的差別。會議結束後，一名緊張的書記靠近梅，含糊說有主管指示員工替換資料夾名稱，她拒絕了。她不願被雇主稱為叛徒。梅保證未經同意不會使用她的姓名，建議她直接向獨立審查員說明。

As they left the exhibit, Mei saw a small portrait of a missionary who had documented early emergency shelters in the district. The caption explained that the records were valuable but incomplete: the writer's viewpoint reflected the institutions of the time, and many residents had not been invited to speak. Mei thought about her own project file. It, too, contained voices that were easy to count and others that had been absent. She asked the historian how to describe a source honestly when it was both useful and limited. “Show its origin, name its perspective, and look for independent evidence,” he replied. She copied those three instructions into her notebook. They would help her describe not just old photographs, but also modern digital records whose tidy labels sometimes concealed missing voices.

離開展覽時，梅看見一位傳教士的小肖像，他曾記錄本區早期緊急避難站。圖說提到紀錄有價值但不完整：作者的觀點受當時機構影響，許多居民沒有機會發聲。梅想到自己的專案檔案，同樣有容易計數的聲音，也有缺席的聲音。她問歷史學者，資料來源既有用又有限制時該如何誠實描述。「說明來源、指出觀點、尋找獨立證據。」對方回答。梅把三點抄進筆記本。這些原則不只適用舊照片，也適用那些標籤整齊、卻可能隱藏缺席聲音的現代數位紀錄。

On her way home, Mei reread the threatening messages and then closed them. She had spent too much time trying to imagine the sender's face. Evidence would not arrive through imagination. At the clinic she gave Mara the messages with their original dates and requested secure handling. Then she wrote a statement for the witnesses: nobody would be pressured to join the case; nobody would be promised an outcome; and nobody would lose the right to correct a previous account. The commitments made her feel less powerless. She could not control what anonymous people said, but she could control whether her own investigation treated people with respect. A useful witness was not a weapon to point at an opponent. A witness was a person who deserved both protection and the freedom to tell the truth.

回家路上，梅重讀威脅訊息，然後關上畫面。她花了太多時間猜寄件人的臉，證據不會靠想像出現。她到診所把保有原始日期的訊息交給瑪拉，要求妥善保管。接著她寫下一份證人承諾：沒有人會被迫加入案件；沒有人會被保證某種結果；沒有人會失去更正先前說法的權利。這些承諾讓她不再那麼無力。她無法控制匿名者說什麼，卻能控制自己的調查是否尊重他人。證人不是用來指向對手的武器，而是值得保護，也有自由說出真相的人。
