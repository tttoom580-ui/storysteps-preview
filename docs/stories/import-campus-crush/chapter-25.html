Case Closed, Hearts Open

戀愛嫌疑犯通緝中 · 第 25 / 25 章 · 5000–6000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

Graduation morning arrived amid a storm of camera flashes and nervous laughter. The whole campus seemed filled with anticipation. Eli had finished his coursework and was preparing to leave for his semester-long placement. I had an internship of my own at the university museum, where I would begin as an apprentice educator. The dean praised our class for showing backbone during difficult projects. June had been placed in charge of the ceremony's treasury, an act of institutional bravery that truly stunned me. Clara, naturally, had organized a farewell performance. “Nothing dramatic,” she promised, which terrified everyone who knew her.

畢業典禮早晨，在閃光燈和緊張笑聲之中到來。整座校園充滿期待。以禮完成課業，即將前往另一座城市展開一學期的實習。我也拿到自己的實習，在大學博物館擔任教育學徒。院長稱讚我們在困難專案中展現骨氣。小君負責典禮財務部門，這種制度性的勇氣讓我非常震驚。克拉拉當然也籌辦告別演出。「絕對不戲劇化，」她保證。所有認識她的人都害怕了。

The performance began with a fake detective investigating who had tried to assassinate romance. There was a cardboard corpse beneath a blanket, a rubber lizard as the supposed witness, and a toy crutch labeled EVIDENCE A. Clara played the detective; June played the suspect. I was meant to reveal that love had not been killed at all, merely delayed by a misplaced train ticket. The script was absurd but harmless, and the audience laughed. Eli watched from the front row, his glasses reflecting the stage lights. I saw him smiling and suddenly remembered the exact moment we'd first met in the archive.

告別演出從假偵探調查誰想「暗殺愛情」開始。台上有一具毯子下的紙板屍體、一隻充當證人的橡膠蜥蜴，以及標示「證物 A」的玩具拐杖。克拉拉演偵探，小君演嫌疑犯。我負責揭露愛情根本沒有被殺，只是因弄錯車票而延遲。劇本荒唐但無害，觀眾笑個不停。以禮坐在前排，舞台燈光映在眼鏡上。我看見他微笑，突然記起我們第一次在檔案室相遇的那一刻。

Disaster, however, still had one final appointment with us. The stage microphone made a sharp vibration sound and stopped working. June ran backstage in a frantic search for replacement batteries, while the sound technician calmly changed the cable. Clara demanded to know whether we were being sentenced to detention for technical failure. “We're graduating,” I whispered. “The school can't keep us forever.” At last the microphone worked, and I finished the scene by delivering the crucial vowel in the villain's name with perfect clarity. My old language coach gave me a thumbs-up from the audience. Small victories mattered, even on big days.

但災難顯然還安排了最後一場約會。舞台麥克風突然發出尖銳震動聲，接著不動了。小君慌亂地跑去後台找備用電池，音控卻冷靜換好電線。克拉拉要求知道技術失敗會不會讓我們被留校。「我們都要畢業了，」我小聲說，「學校不能把我們永遠關著。」麥克風終於恢復，我精準發出台詞中反派名字最重要的母音，完成演出。以前的語言教練在觀眾席向我比讚。即使在重要的日子，小勝利也有意義。

After the ceremony, everyone gathered at a nearby hostel that had offered its garden for the farewell picnic. A knowledgeable museum guide, one of my future colleagues, told stories about university life over the past century. She reminded us that people often dwelt too long on embarrassing moments while forgetting the kindness around them. June disagreed. “Some embarrassment deserves a permanent museum,” she said, pointing at the photograph of our cucumber protest. We laughed. A safety officer gently reminded students that stage jokes about violence were not invitations to attempt anything lethal. Clara bowed solemnly and promised to confine future crimes to paper scripts.

典禮後，大家到附近一間提供花園作惜別野餐的青年旅館集合。一位知識豐富的博物館導覽員，也是我未來的同事，分享過去百年的大學生活故事。她說人們常花太多時間細想丟臉時刻，卻忘了身邊的善意。小君不同意。「有些丟臉值得永久陳列在博物館，」她說，指向我們小黃瓜抗議的照片。大家笑了。安全人員友善提醒，舞台暴力玩笑不代表可以在現實嘗試致命行為。克拉拉莊嚴鞠躬，保證未來的犯罪只會留在紙上劇本。

Eli and I walked away from the picnic toward the old library garden. We had visited that spot on our first night together. “I tried to compute how many times we've been embarrassed this year,” he said. “I stopped at forty.” “Only forty?” “I excluded June's incidents.” We stood beside the same bench, now freshly painted. The remainder of our final day felt unexpectedly peaceful. I asked if he was worried about the move. “Yes,” he said. “But I trust us to adapt.” He told me about the successive weekend visits we'd planned, with room for changes when work became demanding.

我和以禮離開野餐，走向舊圖書館花園。我們交往的第一夜曾來過這裡。「我試著計算今年我們丟臉多少次，」他說，「數到四十次就停了。」「只有四十？」「我沒有把小君算進去。」我們站在同一張長椅旁，椅子已經重新上漆。畢業日剩餘的時間意外平靜。我問他會不會擔心搬走。「會，」他回答，「但我相信我們會適應。」他告訴我，我們已規劃接連幾個週末見面，但工作忙時可以調整。

He took a small envelope from his bag, and my heart leaped before I even saw what was inside. It wasn't a ring, a contract, or another timetable. It was the original love letter from our first Culture Night, carefully preserved. On the back he had written a new line: I still want to stand beside you, even when the roads between us change. I read it slowly, fighting tears. “I don't need a perfect promise,” I told him. “Just the real you.” He kissed my forehead. “Then you're getting a slightly anxious historian with excessive stationery.” “Excellent,” I said. “That's my favorite model.”

他從包裡拿出一封小信，我還沒看到內容，心就先跳了一下。裡面不是戒指、合約或另一張行程表，而是第一次文化之夜那封保存完好的情書。背面多了一句：「即使我們之間的道路改變，我仍想站在妳身旁。」我慢慢讀完，努力忍住眼淚。「我不需要完美承諾，」我說，「只要真實的你。」他吻了我的額頭。「那妳會得到一個有點焦慮、文具多得過頭的歷史系學生。」「太好了，」我說，「這就是我最喜歡的款式。」

As the sun dropped behind the library, June and Clara appeared holding a banner. It read CASE CLOSED: LOVE FOUND. They had included a drawing of a mushroom, a paper boat, a giant peacock, and the infamous astronaut octopus. Eli laughed so hard that I thought he might never recover. We posed for one last photograph: four friends, two ridiculous signs, and a future none of us could completely predict. Then he took my hand. Our story hadn't ended with graduation; it had simply reached the page where we would begin writing the next part ourselves. This time, nobody needed a warrant to prove who had stolen whose heart.

夕陽落到圖書館後方時，小君和克拉拉突然舉著布條出現，上面寫著：「結案：愛情已尋獲。」她們還畫了蘑菇、紙船、巨型孔雀，以及那隻惡名昭彰的太空章魚。以禮笑到我以為他無法恢復。我們拍下最後一張合照：四個朋友、兩塊荒謬牌子，以及誰都無法完全預測的未來。接著他握住我的手。我們的故事沒有因畢業而結束，只是翻到了要親手寫下一頁的地方。這一次，不再需要任何搜查令，就能證明是誰偷走了誰的心。
