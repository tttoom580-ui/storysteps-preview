抽獎箱裡的未來

A Lottery for the Future

戀愛嫌疑犯通緝中 · 第 23 / 25 章 · 5000–6000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

Graduation preparations began with a fundraising lottery for the campus reading center. I volunteered to design the tickets, while Eli helped the finance committee condense five pages of rules into one. We wanted the event to be inclusive, affordable, and transparent. The prizes included books, meal vouchers, and a telescope donated by a nearsighted astronomy professor who joked that the stars looked better through it than through his own eyes. June asked if we could offer a lifetime supply of chocolate. Eli pointed out that promising unlimited chocolate would make the organizers financially liable. She accused him of hostility toward happiness.

畢業準備工作從校園閱讀中心募款抽獎開始。我自願設計彩券，以禮協助財務委員會把五頁規則濃縮成一頁。我們希望活動包容、價格合理而且公開透明。獎品包括書、餐券，以及一名近視的天文學教授捐贈的望遠鏡。他開玩笑說，用望遠鏡看星星比用自己的眼睛更清楚。小君問能不能提供終身巧克力。以禮提醒，承諾無限量巧克力可能讓主辦人負擔財務責任。她指控他敵視幸福。

At the opening meeting, a student caretaker from the reading center thanked volunteers for maintaining the children's playroom. She explained that a small cavity in a toy cabinet had been repaired, so the room was safe again. Someone asked whether the event would include a discussion about emigration and families who moved between countries. We added a storytelling corner where students could share memories voluntarily. Eli offered to help prepare questions without pressuring anyone. I admired his gentleness. He wasn't collecting impressive stories for a report. He was making space for people to decide which parts of their lives they wanted to share.

開幕會議上，一名負責照顧閱讀中心遊戲室的學生志工感謝大家維護空間。她解釋玩具櫃上的小洞已經修補，房間現在安全了。有人問活動是否包含移居海外家庭的故事，我們便加了一個自願分享記憶的說故事角落。以禮主動幫忙設計不會造成壓力的問題。我欣賞他的溫柔。他不是為了報告搜集厲害故事，而是創造空間，讓人自己決定願意分享生命中的哪一部分。

Trouble arrived when June discovered a flaw in the ticket printer. Some tickets contained numbers so faint that nobody could read them. She expressed theatrical dismay, then accused the machine of trying to intimidate future winners. Eli calmly stopped distribution to avert confusion. We checked the tickets and used a fresh printer. “The prize fund is safe,” he said. I volunteered to shred the unusable samples, but only after we had recorded them properly. When June saw the pile of paper, she claimed we were producing evidence for a mystery novel. Clara offered to play the detective. Nobody was surprised.

麻煩在小君發現印票機有瑕疵時登場。部分票上的號碼淡到無法辨識。她戲劇性地沮喪起來，接著指控機器正在威嚇未來得獎者。以禮冷靜停止發票，避免混淆。我們檢查所有票券，改用新印表機。我自願在妥善記錄後，把無法使用的樣本撕碎。小君看著紙堆，聲稱我們正替推理小說製造證據。克拉拉表示願意飾演偵探。沒有人感到意外。

During the draw, the audience gathered so tightly that the room began to feel stuffy. A volunteer opened the windows, preventing anyone from feeling they might suffocate. Then donations suddenly surged after a teacher promised to match contributions up to a fixed amount. The total climbed upward, and everyone applauded. Clara drew the winning ticket with solemn ceremony. The prize went to a first-year student who had been too shy to enter the storytelling contest. She laughed in disbelief. June handed over the telescope as if presenting a royal crown, then immediately requested a photograph for her imaginary legal archive.

抽獎時，觀眾擠在一起，房間逐漸悶熱。志工打開窗戶，避免有人覺得快窒息。接著，一位老師承諾在固定額度內加碼捐款，善款突然激增。總額持續向上攀升，大家鼓掌。克拉拉以莊嚴儀式抽出中獎號碼，獎品落在一名原本害羞得不敢參加說故事比賽的新生手上。她難以置信地笑了。小君像頒王冠一樣交出望遠鏡，接著立刻要求拍照存進她幻想的法律檔案室。

After the event, Eli told me a revelation: he'd spoken with the scholarship coordinator and arranged a schedule that allowed regular visits without interfering with the program. He hadn't announced it earlier because he didn't want to promise something uncertain. “Thank you for checking,” I said, “but you don't have to solve every worry alone.” He nodded. We talked about his future work, my own interests, and the kind of life we might build. Not everything would be easy. The job market and the changing workforce were unpredictable. Still, planning together felt much better than silently fearing the next email.

活動後，以禮告訴我一件新發現：他已和獎學金協調員討論過，安排出不影響計畫、又能定期探訪的時程。他先前沒說，是不想承諾不確定的事。「謝謝你去確認，」我說，「但你不必自己解決每個擔憂。」他點頭。我們聊未來工作、我的興趣，以及可能一起建立的生活。不是所有事都會容易。就業市場和變動中的勞動力環境都有不確定性，但一起計畫遠比默默害怕下一封郵件好多了。

As we left the reading center, a child waved at us with a sticker on his cheek. “Why do grown-ups always look so serious?” he asked. “Because they forget to play,” I answered. Eli leaned down and said, “Or because they haven't met the right teammates yet.” The boy considered that, then gave us each a star sticker. I placed mine beside the paper boat Eli had made at the coast. I was still worried about the future, but the feeling was no longer a heavy secret. It was a problem we could discuss, and perhaps sometimes laugh about, with stickers on our faces.

離開閱讀中心時，一個臉上貼著貼紙的孩子向我們揮手。「為什麼大人總那麼嚴肅？」他問。「因為他們忘了玩，」我說。以禮彎下身：「也可能是還沒遇到對的隊友。」孩子想了一下，送我們一人一張星星貼紙。我把自己的貼在海邊那艘紙船旁。未來仍讓我擔心，但它不再是沉重的秘密，而是我們可以討論、甚至偶爾貼著貼紙一起笑的問題。

A volunteer also showed me a candid photograph in which I was pointing at a new pimple on my forehead. For a moment I wanted to hide the picture. Then Eli said I looked happy, and he was right. I kept it. There were plenty of reasons to be careful about photographs, but there was no reason to be ashamed of having an ordinary face.

志工還拿給我看一張側拍照：我正指著額頭新冒出的一顆青春痘。有一瞬間我想把照片藏起來。接著以禮說我看起來很快樂，而他說得對。我留了下來。照片確實有許多該謹慎的理由，但不需要因為一張平凡的臉而羞愧。
