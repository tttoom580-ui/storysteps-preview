志工博覽會的英雄誤會

The Volunteer Fair Hero

戀愛嫌疑犯通緝中 · 第 8 / 25 章 · 5000–6000 · 英中對照 · 校訂完成

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

The annual volunteer fair was supposed to help students discover a vocation. I went because the booths offered free refreshments. Eli went because he believed useful work shouldn't be treated as a competition. June went because somebody had promised free tote bags. The registration team asked everyone to present a qualification or a previous service record. My record consisted of rescuing museum decorations and almost drowning a dormitory. “That makes you unusually experienced,” Clara said. She wore a blonde wig for a theater demonstration and claimed we were all destined for greatness. I suspected she meant free tote bags too.

年度志工博覽會原本要協助學生探索自己的志業。我去是因為攤位供應免費茶點；以禮去是因為他認為有意義的工作不該被當成比賽；小君則是聽說有人送免費帆布袋。報到隊要求每人提出服務資格或過去紀錄。我的紀錄包括拯救博物館佈置，以及差點讓宿舍淹水。「這讓妳的經驗非常特別，」克拉拉說。她戴著金髮假髮做劇場示範，宣稱我們都註定偉大。我懷疑她指的也是免費帆布袋。

At the literacy booth, volunteers described programs that helped adults become more literate. A teacher explained how being able to read medication labels and official forms could change someone's life. Eli asked questions with genuine interest. I was assigned to retrieve a projector from storage. Unfortunately, the equipment room had undergone a clearance sale, and nobody could tell which machine belonged to us. I returned carrying a rice cooker. “I found a device that produces steam,” I announced. “Wrong category,” Eli said. “It looks like a historical artifact from the age of porridge.”

在識字教育攤位，志工介紹協助成人培養讀寫能力的課程。老師解釋，能看懂藥袋標籤和正式表單如何改變一個人的生活。以禮很有興趣地發問。我被派去儲藏室取回投影機，不幸的是，設備室剛進行過清倉拍賣，沒人說得清哪台機器是我們的。我抱著一個電子鍋回來。「我找到會製造蒸氣的裝置，」我宣布。「分類不對，」以禮說，「這比較像粥的歷史文物。」

Across the hall, a student officer playing an army lieutenant in a safety skit mistakenly saluted me as the emergency coordinator. June encouraged the misunderstanding. Within minutes, people were asking me to certify the safety of a wooden bridge over an artificial swamp in the ecology display. I had no right to certify anything. I called the actual supervisor, who inspected the bridge and found a loose plank. Eli helped keep visitors back. “That was heroic,” June whispered. “No,” I replied. “That was knowing when to find someone with the correct job.”

大廳另一頭，一名在安全短劇中扮演陸軍中尉的學生，錯把我當成緊急應變協調員，還向我敬禮。小君竟然鼓勵這場誤會。幾分鐘內，大家已在請我認證生態展人工沼澤上木橋的安全。我根本沒有資格認證任何東西，於是找來真正的管理員。他檢查後發現一塊木板鬆動。以禮幫忙擋住訪客。「真英勇，」小君低聲說。「不是，」我回答，「只是知道何時該找對的人。」

The finance booth demonstrated how donations worked. Money arrived in small amounts, an accumulation of ordinary kindness. The administrator explained which expenses they could deduct and which had to be recorded separately. Even a marginal contribution mattered, she said. I liked that idea better than flashy speeches. Nearby, a volunteer displayed paper lotus flowers made by children from rural schools. Eli bought one for me, and I placed it behind my ear. Clara called it “the romance of the century.” June made a spreadsheet ranking our levels of emotional embarrassment.

財務攤位展示捐款如何運作。錢是一點一滴進來的，是普通善意的累積。行政人員說明哪些費用可以扣除，哪些必須另外記帳。她說，即使貢獻微小也有意義。我比起華麗演講，更喜歡這種想法。旁邊志工展示鄉村學校孩子摺的紙蓮花。以禮買了一朵給我，我把它別在耳邊。克拉拉稱這是「世紀戀情」，小君則做了一張試算表，替我們的害羞程度排名。

By late afternoon, the fair's activities began to overlap. A local provincial history group needed help moving books, while the science club wanted people to exert pressure on a giant lever in a physics demonstration. Eli volunteered at both booths with astonishing efficiency. I brought him a cold beverage and asked whether he ever grew tired. “Of course,” he admitted. “I just feel useful here.” I told him he didn't have to earn my affection by doing tasks. He looked at me quietly. “I know,” he said. “But it's nice that you noticed.”

到傍晚時，不同攤位的活動開始重疊。地方史協會需要人幫忙搬書，科學社則希望志工在物理示範中對巨大槓桿施力。以禮以驚人效率同時支援兩邊。我拿一杯冰飲給他，問他會不會累。「當然會，」他承認，「但在這裡我感覺自己有用。」我告訴他，不必靠完成任務來換取我的喜歡。他靜靜看著我。「我知道，」他說，「不過妳注意到了，真的很好。」

When the fair closed, the organizer thanked us for helping everyone stay safe. June eventually received her tote bag, only to discover that it displayed a gigantic rice cooker. “They used your photograph,” she told me. I examined the image: there I was, solemnly holding the wrong machine like a sacred object. Eli laughed until he had to lean against the wall. On the way home we shared the last packet of crackers. He said volunteering had changed his definition of an excellent date. I said I had learned one thing too: love might not require a certificate, but a partner with a sense of humor certainly helped.

博覽會結束時，主辦人感謝我們協助維持安全。小君終於拿到帆布袋，卻發現上面印著一個巨大的電子鍋。「他們用妳的照片，」她告訴我。我仔細一看，袋上就是我神情莊嚴地抱著錯誤機器，彷彿捧著聖物。以禮笑到必須靠牆。回家路上，我們分吃最後一包餅乾。他說志工活動改變了他對美好約會的定義。我也學到一件事：愛情或許不需要證書，但有一個懂幽默的伴侶肯定有幫助。

The organizer later emailed us a photograph of the children's handmade flowers. I printed it and put it above my desk, beside the crooked picture of Eli and me on our first date. The two images reminded me that usefulness could look wonderfully ordinary: a small donation, a rescued plank, or someone saving you a seat.

主辦人後來寄來孩子們手作花朵的照片。我印出來貼在書桌上方，旁邊是我和以禮第一次約會那張歪歪的照片。兩張圖提醒我，有用的事也能非常平凡：小額捐款、修好的木板，或有人替你留座位。
