聲音訓練與香草誓言

The Vanilla Oath

戀愛嫌疑犯通緝中 · 第 19 / 25 章 · 5000–6000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

Clara enrolled the four of us in a public-speaking workshop because she claimed our romantic lives lacked professional intonation. “Love is not a presentation,” Eli protested. “Precisely,” Clara answered, “and yet you invariably speak like an educational documentary.” June called the workshop an outrageous attack on natural conversation. I went because I wanted to improve my speaking proficiency before graduation. Eli came to support me, carrying a packet of vanilla biscuits. When he offered one, Clara announced that his quiet kindness already deserved an award. Eli looked as though he regretted bringing snacks.

克拉拉替我們四個人報名公開演說工作坊，理由是我們的戀愛生活缺乏專業語調。「愛情不是簡報，」以禮抗議。「正是如此，」克拉拉回答，「但你總是講得像教育紀錄片。」小君稱這是對自然對話的離譜攻擊。我去是因為想在畢業前提升口語熟練程度，以禮則來支持我，還帶了一小包香草餅乾。他遞給我時，克拉拉宣布這種安靜的體貼已值得領獎。以禮看起來後悔帶點心。

Our instructor asked us to infer emotions from changes in tone. Clara performed grief, triumph, and outrage in one sentence. June responded with scorn for the exercise until she realized that everyone was laughing with her, not at her. Eli explained that people could sound cold while actually feeling nervous. I looked at him, surprised by the honesty. During a break he told me that his stepfather had taught him to meditate when he felt overwhelmed. “I used to rehearse every conversation,” he admitted. “Even saying hello?” I asked. “Especially saying hello.” I squeezed his hand and promised not to grade the next one.

老師請我們根據語調變化推論情緒。克拉拉一句話裡演完悲傷、勝利和憤怒。小君先輕蔑地評論練習，直到發現大家是在跟她一起笑，不是在嘲笑她。以禮解釋，有些人聽起來冷淡，其實只是緊張。我意外地看著他。休息時，他說繼父教他感覺不知所措時可以冥想。「我以前會排練每段對話，」他承認。「連說你好也是？」「尤其是說你好。」我握緊他的手，保證下次不打分數。

One activity used a map of an imaginary isle threatened by pollution and thinning ozone protection. Students had to give short speeches about practical environmental solutions. A participant spoke about the experiences of migrant families after storms displaced their homes. We listened seriously. When June took the microphone, she accidentally described the island's chemical odor as “the smell of a thousand cucumbers seeking revenge.” Even the instructor laughed. Eli pointed out that humor could fortify an argument if it was accurate and kind. June accused him of trying to become her editor. He said he would consider the job only with paid leave.

有項練習使用一張假想小島地圖，設定島嶼面臨污染與臭氧保護層變薄的威脅。學生必須提出實際的環境方案。一名參與者談暴風雨迫使人們離家後，遷徙家庭的經歷，我們都認真聽。輪到小君上台，她卻不小心把化學氣味形容成「一千根想復仇的小黃瓜的味道」。連老師都笑了。以禮指出，若幽默準確又善意，能增強論點。小君指控他想當自己的編輯，他說只有有薪休假才考慮。

In the final exercise we were asked to make a personal oath about communication. Clara promised not to turn every apology into a theatrical death scene. June promised not to smuggle snacks into the university's silent study room again. Eli promised to say what he felt without hiding behind careful explanations. I promised not to assume that silence meant rejection. The teacher said an oath needn't be grand; it simply had to be sincere. I noticed Eli looking at me, and the room felt briefly glamorous, as if the ordinary fluorescent lights had become stars.

最後的活動是替溝通立下一項個人誓言。克拉拉答應不再把每次道歉演成戲劇性死亡場面，小君答應不再偷偷帶點心進大學安靜自習室。以禮答應不再用小心翼翼的解釋掩蓋真正的感受。我則答應不把沉默直接解讀成拒絕。老師說誓言不必宏大，只要真誠。我注意到以禮正看著我，房間短暫地變得迷人，彷彿普通日光燈也化成星星。

After class, the theater department displayed costumes for an art-history presentation. One photograph showed a nude classical statue, which prompted a useful discussion about artistic context rather than embarrassment. Another exhibit described how ordinary mortal people, not only kings and heroes, had preserved cultural memories. Eli seemed moved by that idea. “History is full of people whose names we don't know,” he said. “They still mattered.” I wondered whether anyone would remember our ridiculous campus adventures. Then June began striding across the hallway in a paper crown, and I concluded that anonymity might be safer for everyone.

下課後，戲劇系展示藝術史課使用的戲服。其中一張照片是裸體古典雕像，大家討論藝術脈絡，而不是尷尬。另一個展區介紹，保存文化記憶的不只有國王與英雄，也有平凡凡人。以禮顯然很受觸動。「歷史裡很多人連名字都不知道，」他說，「但他們仍然重要。」我想知道有人會不會記得我們荒謬的校園冒險。接著小君戴著紙皇冠大步走過走廊，我得出結論：對所有人而言，默默無名可能比較安全。

That evening Eli and I sat outside the library. He offered me the last biscuit and spoke slowly, without any polished speech. “I love you,” he said. Three ordinary words. I stared at him for a second, then replied, “I love you too.” He smiled with enormous relief. A nearby cat meowed, which June later insisted was official applause from nature. I didn't care. No rehearsal could have improved the moment. It belonged to us exactly as it was: slightly nervous, completely sincere, and sweet enough that I could almost taste the vanilla on my lips.

那晚我和以禮坐在圖書館外，他把最後一片餅乾遞給我，慢慢開口，沒有經過修飾的演說。「我愛妳，」他說。只是三個普通的字。我望了他一秒，回答：「我也愛你。」他露出如釋重負的笑。旁邊一隻貓喵了一聲，小君後來堅稱這是大自然的官方掌聲。我才不管。任何排練都不可能讓那一刻更好。它就是我們的：有一點緊張、完全真誠，而且甜得像嘴唇還留著香草味。

When I got back to the dorm, I wrote those three words in my notebook, then crossed them out and rewrote them more neatly. June glanced over my shoulder and decided not to tease me. That restraint was so unlike her that I almost asked whether she was feeling ill. Instead, she smiled, and I smiled back.

回宿舍後，我把那三個字寫進筆記本，又劃掉重新寫整齊。小君瞄了一眼我的肩後，卻決定不取笑我。她這種克制太不像平常，我差點問她是不是生病了。最後她只是笑笑，我也回以微笑。
