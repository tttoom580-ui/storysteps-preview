第一次約會的高度問題

A Date at the Wrong Altitude

戀愛嫌疑犯通緝中 · 第 2 / 25 章 · 5000–6000 · 英中對照

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

Our first official date began with Eli holding two train tickets and a clipboard. I had imagined coffee, cake, and perhaps one carefully planned kiss. He had arranged a visit to the university's mountain research station, followed by lunch on its rooftop terrace. “The altitude isn't very high,” he promised. I pointed at the tiny cable car hanging above the valley. “That's what people say before documentary narrators announce their disappearance.” Eli made a note. Apparently, even my fear of heights had acquired a place on his schedule.

我和以禮的第一次正式約會，從他拿著兩張車票和一塊寫字板開始。我原本想像的是咖啡、蛋糕，或許還有一個事先規劃好的吻。他卻安排參觀大學的山區研究站，接著到屋頂露臺吃午餐。「海拔沒有很高，」他保證。我指向懸在山谷上方的小小纜車。「這就是紀錄片旁白宣布主角失蹤前會說的話。」以禮認真記了下來。顯然，連我的懼高症都在他的行程表裡有了位置。

The research station was hosting an exhibition developed with an aboriginal community. A curator explained how community members had reviewed the displays, corrected errors, and chosen which stories could be shared. We listened to a recording about the mountain paths that families had traveled for generations. “It's better to ask whose knowledge we're using,” Eli said, “instead of pretending we discovered it.” I liked his quiet respect. Unfortunately, my attempt to compliment him sounded like, “Your ethical standards make you strangely attractive.” Eli gave me a blunt answer: “I find your honesty attractive.” My knees forgot their normal function.

研究站正舉辦一場與原住民族社群共同策劃的展覽。策展人說明族人如何審閱展示、修正錯誤，並決定哪些故事適合公開。我們聽了代代相傳的山徑紀錄。「使用別人的知識，最好先問清楚是誰的，」以禮說，「而不是裝成自己發現的。」我喜歡他安靜又尊重人的態度。不幸的是，我想稱讚他，出口卻變成：「你的倫理標準讓你莫名有吸引力。」以禮非常直率地回答：「我也覺得妳的坦白很有吸引力。」我的膝蓋當場忘記怎麼運作。

On the way to the terrace we approached a pedestrian crossing decorated with ribbons. Volunteers were conducting a rigorous obstacle course for visitors. Contestants had to align wooden arrows with a compass, jump over a low hurdle, and carry a cup without spilling. Eli entered because he believed his precision would win us a free dessert. I entered because I wanted to prove that I possessed two functional feet. His posture was perfect. Mine resembled an alarmed penguin. He completed the course in fifty seconds; I finished in sixty and somehow collected three additional cups.

前往露臺時，我們來到一處掛滿彩帶的行人穿越道。志工正舉辦要求嚴格的障礙活動。參賽者得把木箭頭與指南針對齊，跨過低矮障礙，再端著水杯不灑出來。以禮參賽，是因為他認為自己的精準度能幫我們贏得免費甜點。我參賽，是為了證明自己還有兩隻正常的腳。他的姿勢完美，我卻像受到驚嚇的企鵝。他五十秒就完賽，我花了六十秒，還莫名多拿了三個杯子。

Naturally, trouble did not abound until we reached lunch. Eli had packed sandwiches, but the station's old coffee machine began to discharge steam like a furious dragon. The attendant suggested we discard our hot drinks, and I obeyed with unusual speed. Eli saw my annoyance and tried to make me laugh. “For the record,” he said, “I did not include volcanic equipment in the itinerary.” “Then your itinerary is incomplete.” He leaned closer. “Would you prefer a spontaneous endeavor?” I swallowed. Nobody had warned me that a spreadsheet enthusiast could flirt.

麻煩直到午餐才開始接連出現。以禮準備了三明治，但研究站老舊的咖啡機突然像暴怒的龍一樣噴出蒸氣。工作人員建議大家丟掉熱飲，我以罕見的速度服從。以禮看見我的懊惱，試著逗笑我。「先聲明，」他說，「行程裡沒有火山設備。」「那你的行程不完整。」他靠近些。「妳想試試一項即興的努力嗎？」我吞了口口水。根本沒有人警告我，熱愛試算表的人也會調情。

We were interrupted by June's phone call. She had somehow joined a student uprising against the cafeteria's new cucumber policy. “I'm leading a protest,” she announced. “Are you two finally holding hands?” I said that the university should veto her right to call me. Eli gently took my free hand while I was still speaking. Across the table, a retired volunteer told us that her neighbor, a widow, had met her second husband on this same terrace forty years earlier. “He was late,” she said. “But he brought peaches. Never underestimate fruit.” Eli inspected our sandwiches with visible concern.

小君的電話突然打斷我們。她不知怎麼竟加入了反對學校新小黃瓜政策的學生抗議。「我正在帶頭抗爭，」她宣布，「你們牽手了沒？」我說學校應該否決她打電話給我的權利。就在我講電話的時候，以禮輕輕牽住我的另一隻手。桌對面一位退休志工說，她的寡婦鄰居四十年前就在這座露臺遇見第二任丈夫。「他遲到了，」她說，「但他帶了桃子。永遠不要小看水果。」以禮憂心忡忡地檢查我們的三明治。

The volunteer offered to usher us toward a better view of the valley. Eli proved surprisingly versatile: he could explain the exhibition, carry our bags, and keep holding my hand without dropping anything. I, meanwhile, could barely walk in a straight line. “You know,” he said, “I made this date too complicated.” I squeezed his fingers. “I was hoping you'd be a little less prepared.” He considered that seriously, folded his clipboard, and slipped it into his pocket. “Then let's stay until sunset without checking the time.” The cable car moved silently below us, but suddenly the height no longer frightened me.

那位志工引領我們到更適合看山谷的地方。以禮展現出意想不到的多才多藝：他能講解展覽、提行李，還能一直牽著我的手而不掉任何東西。反觀我，光走直線就有困難。「妳知道嗎，」他說，「我把約會搞得太複雜了。」我握緊他的手。「我本來就希望你能少準備一點。」他認真想了想，摺起寫字板收進口袋。「那我們就不看時間，待到日落吧。」纜車在腳下靜靜移動，但我突然一點也不怕高度了。

When we reached the station gate, Eli finally showed me the last item on his carefully folded itinerary: 'Enjoy being together.' He had written no time beside it. I decided it was the most romantic entry on the entire page, and I promised not to tell June how much that mattered.

到研究站大門時，以禮終於讓我看見摺好的行程表最後一項：「享受相處的時間。」旁邊沒有任何時刻。我認定那是整張表最浪漫的一行，也答應自己不告訴小君它對我有多重要。
