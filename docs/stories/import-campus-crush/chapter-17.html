The Great Love-Letter Boycott

戀愛嫌疑犯通緝中 · 第 17 / 25 章 · 5000–6000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

One morning the campus newspaper announced a student boycott of the university gift shop. The shop had begun selling printed love letters as a supposedly lucrative Valentine's product. “They charge more for the paper than for the words,” June said, outraged. Clara shrieked when she discovered that one card repeated the opening line of a poem she'd written for drama club. Eli approached the problem with his usual analytical care. He compared the cards with published poems and asked the shop manager for an explanation. I wanted to defend every handwritten letter in history, but he reminded me that we should establish facts before accusing anyone.

某天早上，校園報紙報導學生正在抵制大學禮品店。店裡把印製情書當成號稱有利可圖的情人節商品販售。「紙比字還貴，」小君氣憤地說。克拉拉發現其中一張卡片用了她為戲劇社寫的詩句開頭，當場尖叫。以禮用一貫的分析方法處理：比對卡片和出版詩作，並請店長解釋。我原想替歷史上所有手寫情書辯護，但以禮提醒我，指控之前得先確認事實。

The manager apologized and arranged to remove the copied lines. She also promised clearer credit for original writers. The controversy grew because students began debating the proper disclosure of sources in creative work. June wanted an immediate public trial; Eli proposed a fair conversation. A journalism professor discussed how private correspondence differed from material intended for publication. He also warned that a poorly written article could cause real harm even when no physical casualty occurred. Clara agreed not to publish anyone's private messages. I was relieved; my texts to Eli included enough terrible jokes to end my academic reputation.

店長道歉並安排移除被引用的詩句，也答應更清楚標示原作者。爭議擴大後，學生開始討論創作中應如何揭露來源。小君要求立刻舉辦公開審判，以禮則建議公平對話。新聞學教授解釋私人通信和預定公開的作品有何不同，也提醒大家：即使沒有身體傷亡，不負責任的報導仍可能造成真實傷害。克拉拉同意不公開任何人的私人訊息。我鬆了口氣；我傳給以禮的冷笑話足以毀掉學術聲譽。

We joined a workshop about how to cultivate responsible writing habits. The teacher discussed preventive checks for plagiarism and the importance of a clear page layout. I tried to take notes, but dust from the old curtains made me sneeze repeatedly. “Are you all right?” Eli whispered. “I'm fighting my nose,” I said. June offered consolation in the form of a tissue and a threat to sue the curtains. The teacher laughed and opened a window. A mild breeze moved outward through the room, carrying away the dust and, I hoped, the last of my embarrassment.

我們參加一場培養負責任寫作習慣的工作坊。老師談預防抄襲的檢查方法，以及清楚版面配置的重要。我努力記筆記，卻因舊窗簾的灰塵不停打噴嚏。「妳還好嗎？」以禮低聲問。「我在和鼻子搏鬥，」我說。小君遞衛生紙安慰我，還威脅要對窗簾提告。老師笑著打開窗戶。微風吹向室外，帶走灰塵，也希望帶走我最後一點丟臉感。

At lunch, a student activist argued that ordinary love letters should never be sold like factory products. Another student said printed cards were useful for people who struggled to express feelings. Eli helped them see that the two positions weren't enemies. “A store can sell cards,” he said, “without stealing words.” It was a simple conclusion, but the conversation had taken a tiresome hour to reach it. June announced that compromise was an exhausting sport. Clara suggested charging admission next time. I gave Eli a handmade card that read, “Your calmness deserves a trophy, or at least a nap.”

午餐時，一名學生倡議者說普通情書不該像工廠商品一樣販賣。另一名同學說印製卡片對不善表達的人很有用。以禮協助他們理解，兩種立場不必互相敵對。「商店可以賣卡片，」他說，「但不能偷用別人的文字。」結論很簡單，大家卻花了令人厭煩的一小時才達成。小君宣布妥協是耗費體力的運動。克拉拉建議下次收門票。我給以禮一張手做卡片：「你的冷靜值得一座獎盃，或至少睡個午覺。」

That afternoon the club ran a harmless word game. Students had to invent phrases that would kindle affection rather than merely produce empty stimulation. I wrote, “You make silence feel safe.” Eli stared at the sentence longer than I expected. “I thought you'd write something about cucumbers,” he said. “I have depth.” “I know.” For a moment the noisy room disappeared. I understood then that my affection for him was not a passing distraction from studying or a joke that would fade after graduation. It wasn't eternity either, because we couldn't promise the future. It was a choice we kept making, one ordinary day after another.

下午社團辦了無害的文字遊戲。學生必須創造能喚起好感，而不只是空洞刺激的句子。我寫：「你讓沉默也變得安全。」以禮看著那句話，比我預期還久。「我以為妳會寫小黃瓜，」他說。「我也是有深度的。」「我知道。」有一瞬間，吵鬧的房間彷彿消失了。我明白自己對他的感情不是讀書時短暫分心，也不是畢業後就消失的笑話。但它也不是我們能保證的永恆，而是我們每天都持續做出的選擇。

The next day June admitted that she had nearly flunked her legal writing quiz because she spent too much time designing protest posters. Eli helped her make a sensible study plan. I checked the gift shop: the copied cards were gone, replaced by a display celebrating student authors with their permission. On the way out I spotted a card saying, “I choose you again today.” It was not particularly clever, but it sounded true. I purchased one and handed it to Eli without ceremony. He read it, smiled, and asked, “Tomorrow too?” “Tomorrow too,” I said, “unless you buy me radioactive vanilla cake.”

隔天小君承認，她花太多時間設計抗議海報，差點在法律寫作小考不及格。以禮幫她做了一份合理的讀書計畫。我去禮品店檢查，發現引用不當的卡片都撤下了，換成取得學生作者同意的作品展示。離開時，我看到一張卡片寫著：「今天我再次選擇你。」這句話不算特別聰明，卻很真實。我買了一張，直接遞給以禮。他讀完笑著問：「明天也是？」我說：「明天也是，除非你買放射性香草蛋糕給我。」

During the workshop break, June and I had to wrestle with the dispenser in the hallway to get a paper towel. The organizer eventually fixed it and reminded everyone about proper hand hygiene before eating. I returned to find Eli waiting with the workshop materials neatly stacked. He pretended not to notice that I had somehow lost a battle against a towel machine.

工作坊休息時，我和小君得和走廊的擦手紙機搏鬥，才拿得到一張紙巾。主辦人最後修好機器，也提醒大家吃東西前做好手部衛生。我回來時，以禮已把教材疊好在等我。他假裝沒注意到，我竟然輸給一台紙巾機。
