冬季音樂會的孔雀危機

Peacock in the Orchestra

戀愛嫌疑犯通緝中 · 第 11 / 25 章 · 5000–6000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

The winter concert was held in a hall so cold that the thermostat read twelve degrees Celsius. Clara claimed the temperature would induce emotional depth. June responded with a low growl and wrapped herself in a blanket. I had volunteered to help the student pianist, while Eli organized seating for the history department. The decorations included a giant cardboard peacock that the art club had made to represent elegance. Its tail was wider than the doorway. “The concept is beautiful,” Eli said, “but perhaps we should simplify the design.” The art club president looked as if he had insulted a royal family.

冬季音樂會在一間冷到溫度計只有攝氏十二度的禮堂舉行。克拉拉聲稱低溫能引起更深層的情感。小君發出低沉的咆哮，用毛毯把自己裹起來。我自願協助學生鋼琴家，以禮則負責歷史系座位安排。佈景有一隻巨大的紙板孔雀，是美術社為了象徵優雅做的。孔雀尾巴比門還寬。「概念很美，」以禮說，「但或許可以簡化設計。」美術社長的表情彷彿以禮冒犯了皇室。

Everyone had their respective tasks. I was supposed to probe the cause of a rattling sound behind the stage. June had to keep the audience away from cables because too much friction could damage the protective covers. Clara had to remember that a concert wasn't a dramatic trial. She failed that task immediately and announced, “Tonight we shall unify music, history, and destiny!” The pianist blinked at her. “I'd settle for starting on time.” Eli and I exchanged a glance. We were developing the rare ability to communicate entire sentences without speaking them.

大家各有分工。我負責探查舞台後方的異音來源。小君負責避免觀眾踩到電線，以免過度摩擦損壞保護套。克拉拉則必須記住音樂會不是戲劇法庭。她立刻失敗，宣布：「今晚我們要團結音樂、歷史與命運！」鋼琴家眨眨眼。「我只希望準時開始。」以禮和我交換眼神。我們正培養一種稀有能力：不用開口，就能傳遞完整句子。

The professor wanted the stage layout to show perfect symmetry. Unfortunately, the peacock leaned left, and somebody had placed a small mound of fake snow near the right curtain. I tried to balance them by adding two chairs, which made everything worse. “That is a shortsighted solution,” Eli observed gently. “I know. My eyesight is currently focused on not being eaten by a cardboard bird.” We moved the decorations into a simpler arrangement. The peacock remained awkward but upright, a token of our collective determination. From the audience June declared that it looked like a giant feathered potato.

教授希望舞台布置具有完美對稱。不幸的是，孔雀歪向左邊，而有人在右側布幕旁堆了一小堆假雪。我試著放兩張椅子平衡，結果更糟。「這個解決方案有點目光短淺，」以禮溫柔地指出。「我知道。我現在的注意力只剩不要被紙板鳥吃掉。」我們改成更簡單的布置。孔雀依然彆扭但站得直，成了大家決心的象徵。小君從觀眾席大喊，它看起來像巨大的羽毛馬鈴薯。

During rehearsal the narrator described a voyage across a strait, but the sound engineer accidentally played peacock calls instead of ocean waves. It was profoundly ironic: the bird had finally become the star. The pianist struggled to suppress laughter and stopped in the middle of a piece. The professor grew worried about the urgency of opening the doors on schedule. Eli asked for a two-minute pause. He calmly reset the recording and told the musicians to take a breath. His practical kindness did more than any angry speech would have done. I realized that calm was not the absence of pressure; it was the decision not to spread it.

彩排時，旁白正描述船隻穿越海峽，音控卻誤播孔雀叫聲而不是海浪聲。這實在諷刺：那隻鳥終於成了主角。鋼琴家努力壓抑笑意，最後還是在曲子中途停下。教授擔心開門時間迫近。以禮請大家休息兩分鐘，冷靜重設音檔，並請樂手喘口氣。他務實的善意比任何怒斥都有用。我發現所謂冷靜不是沒有壓力，而是選擇不要把壓力傳出去。

The concert itself was lovely. Students performed music from places where their families had lived, explaining the memories each piece carried. It made the stage feel bigger than any single university. I watched Eli from behind the curtain. He was smiling at the pianist, who had finally finished his solo without interruption. Our affection might have looked superficial to people who saw only our jokes, but I knew how much attention he paid to others. When he noticed me watching, he lifted two fingers in our private signal: two minutes until the finale. I answered with a little heart made from both hands.

正式音樂會很美。學生演奏家人曾居住過的地方的音樂，並介紹每首曲子承載的記憶。舞台忽然顯得比任何一所大學都遼闊。我從布幕後看著以禮。他正對終於順利完成獨奏的鋼琴家微笑。對只看見我們開玩笑的人來說，我們的感情可能很膚淺，但我知道他多在意別人。他發現我在看，舉起兩根手指，這是我們的暗號：離終曲還有兩分鐘。我則用雙手比了一顆小愛心。

After the applause, June offered to return the peacock to storage. Nobody believed she could fit it through the door. Eli measured the frame, then suggested tilting the bird sideways. It passed through with an inch to spare. “Your boyfriend is a miracle,” June told me. “He is,” I answered, with more seriousness than she expected. Eli heard me and turned red. Outside, the night air was even colder than the hall. We walked under the campus lights, hands buried together in his coat pocket. I decided there were worse ways to survive winter than sharing warmth with someone who knew how to move a giant peacock.

掌聲結束後，小君主動要把孔雀搬回倉庫，卻沒人相信她能讓它通過門口。以禮量了門框，建議把鳥側過來。果然，剛好只差一吋就卡住。「妳男朋友真是奇蹟，」小君說。「他確實是，」我回答得比她預期認真。以禮聽見，臉紅了。外頭夜色比禮堂更冷。我們走在校園燈光下，把兩人的手一起藏進他的外套口袋。我想，冬天的求生方式有很多種，但和一個會搬巨型孔雀的人分享溫暖，應該不算壞。

The concert program described the communities that inhabit the islands across the strait and how their music had traveled between generations. Eli showed me that passage afterward. He said he'd liked hearing living voices rather than treating history as something sealed in glass. I told him I liked hearing his voice too, especially when he laughed.

音樂會節目冊介紹居住在海峽彼端島嶼的社群，以及音樂如何傳承世代。以禮事後指給我看，說他喜歡聽見活生生的聲音，而不是把歷史當成玻璃櫃裡的物品。我說我也喜歡聽他的聲音，尤其是笑聲。
