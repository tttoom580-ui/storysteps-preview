最後一場文化祭

The Last Culture Night

戀愛嫌疑犯通緝中 · 第 24 / 25 章 · 5000–6000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

Our final Culture Night arrived almost a year after the evening Eli gave me his love letter. This time June had formed a coalition of student clubs and appointed herself chairperson. She had a conscientious assistant who checked every safety notice twice, which was fortunate because June's plans were occasionally disastrous. We wanted a cozy festival rather than an elaborate spectacle. Still, the opening ceremony began with a loud electronic beep, followed by Clara announcing that our university had reached a new latitude of romance. Nobody knew what that meant. Eli turned toward me and smiled with familiar, helpless amusement.

最後一場文化之夜到來，距離以禮送我情書的那個晚上，幾乎整整一年。這次小君組成多社團聯盟，還自封主席。幸好她有位認真盡責的助理，把安全通知檢查兩遍，因為小君的計畫偶爾非常災難性。我們希望節慶舒適溫馨，不必過度華麗。但開幕儀式仍從一聲響亮的電子嗶聲開始，接著克拉拉宣布大學已抵達「浪漫的新緯度」。沒人懂她在說什麼。以禮轉向我，露出熟悉又無奈的笑。

The food club served grilled clams and noodles, which June began to devour before the opening speech ended. The geography group presented maps of the tropics, while history students offered a discussion about the difference between healthy civic pride and extreme patriotic slogans. A gardening display showed a green vine climbing around a wooden arch. We had to formulate clear instructions so visitors wouldn't step on the plants. Eli wrote the signs. I decorated them with tiny hearts. He called the design an acceptable compromise between scholarship and romance. I declared that he was finally learning my academic language.

餐飲社端出烤蛤蜊和麵，小君在開幕致詞結束前就開始狼吞虎嚥。地理社展示熱帶地區的地圖，歷史系學生則討論健康的公民自豪與極端愛國口號有何不同。園藝展區有一條綠色藤蔓攀上木拱門。我們得擬定清楚指示，避免訪客踩到植物。以禮寫告示，我在上面畫小愛心。他說這是學術與浪漫之間可以接受的妥協。我宣布他終於學會了我的學術語言。

Near the exhibition hall, the records office offered students unofficial copies of their academic transcripts. June opened hers and stared in disbelief. “I passed accounting,” she whispered, as though she'd discovered a new planet. Clara congratulated her with a dramatic speech about human civilization. “We have finally civilized our law student,” she announced. June chased her around a table, but everyone was laughing. Eli received his records too, neat and impressive as always. I looked at them, then at him. His academic stature had grown, yet he still looked like the same shy student who once hid a love letter inside a folder.

展覽廳附近，註冊組提供學生非正式成績單。小君打開自己的，難以置信地盯著。「我會計及格了，」她低聲說，像發現新行星。克拉拉用一段文明演進的戲劇演說恭喜她。「我們終於讓法律系學生文明化了，」她宣布。小君追著她繞桌跑，所有人都笑了。以禮也拿到自己的成績紀錄，一如既往整齊又出色。我看著它們，再看向他。他的學術地位提高了，卻仍像那個曾把情書藏在資料夾裡的害羞男生。

At sunset a student band played beside the pond. The sound created tiny ripples on the water whenever someone tapped the floating decoration. I asked Eli whether he remembered our first Culture Night. “Unfortunately, yes,” he said. “You accused me of loving Clara, then invented mushroom history to save my privacy.” “I reckon it was an excellent first date.” “It wasn't technically our first date.” “Your definition is too narrow.” He laughed, and I felt a surprising sense of gratitude for every misunderstanding that had brought us to this conversation. None of it was elegant, but it belonged to us.

日落時，學生樂團在池邊演奏。有人輕碰水上裝飾，水面便泛起細小漣漪。我問以禮還記不記得第一次文化之夜。「很不幸，記得，」他說，「妳指控我喜歡克拉拉，然後發明蘑菇史來保護我的隱私。」「我認為那是很好的第一次約會。」「技術上那不是第一次約會。」「你的定義太狹隘。」他笑了，我忽然對那些把我們帶到今天的誤會感到感激。它們一點也不優雅，卻全屬於我們。

Eli handed me a small envelope. For a second I thought it might contain another confession. Instead, it held two train tickets and a handwritten note: “First visit, after I move. No pressure. We can change the date.” I looked up. “You bought tickets for me?” “Refundable ones,” he clarified. Of course. But the tenderness was real. He wanted a future that included me while leaving room for us to make decisions together. I kissed him just as the band began a bright tune. A student nearby applauded, and June shouted, “NO FURTHER EVIDENCE REQUIRED!”

以禮遞給我一個小信封。有一秒我以為又是告白信，裡面卻有兩張車票和一張手寫字條：「搬走後第一次探訪。不用有壓力，日期可以改。」我抬頭看他。「你幫我買票？」「可以退的，」他特別說明。果然是他。但那份溫柔是真的。他想要包含我的未來，也保留讓我們共同決定的空間。我在樂團奏起明亮曲調時吻了他。附近學生鼓掌，小君大喊：「不需要更多證據了！」

When the festival ended, we turned off the lights together. June checked the budget and declared that the event had remained solvent. Clara bowed to an empty audience. I found an old photograph from our first Culture Night, with Eli standing beside me and the infamous mushroom presentation on the screen behind us. We looked awkward and completely unaware of how much life would change. I slid the picture into his folder, exactly where his letter had once been. “Keep this,” I said. “For the archive?” he asked. “For us.” He closed the folder and held it against his heart.

文化祭結束後，我們一起關燈。小君核對預算，宣布活動沒有破產。克拉拉對空觀眾席鞠躬。我找到第一次文化之夜的舊照片：以禮站在我身邊，後方螢幕是那場惡名昭彰的蘑菇簡報。我們看起來彆扭，完全不知道生活會改變多少。我把照片放進他的資料夾，正是以前情書待過的位置。「留著這個，」我說。「放檔案室？」「留給我們。」他闔上資料夾，把它貼在心口。

The campus newsletter later printed our photograph and summarized the festival's successful safety work. It described the event as modest and well organized, and accordingly thanked the student volunteers rather than naming a single hero. Eli framed that page beside our first disastrous exhibition picture. Together, the two photographs looked like a small history of growing up.

校刊後來刊出我們的照片，並總結文化祭成功的安全工作。文章說活動簡樸、組織得宜，因此感謝所有學生志工，而不是指定單一英雄。以禮把那頁裝框，放在第一次災難展覽照片旁。兩張照片放在一起，像一小段成長史。
