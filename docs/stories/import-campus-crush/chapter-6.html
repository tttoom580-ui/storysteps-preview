校園選美沒有美

The Worst Beauty Contest

戀愛嫌疑犯通緝中 · 第 6 / 25 章 · 5000–6000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

I chose our next date: the university's “Beautiful Campus Day.” I imagined flowers and pleasant music. I did not expect a beauty contest for the oldest statue on campus. The extracurricular committee wanted to beautify the courtyard and hired a local sculptor to judge the results. Clara alleged that the statue of our founder had “tragic eyebrows,” and June designed a fake mustache made of felt. Eli warned us not to damage historical property. Naturally, his warning became the caption under a photograph of June holding the mustache: STRICT HISTORIAN OPPOSES FACIAL HAIR.

這次換我選約會地點：大學的「美麗校園日」。我想像的是花朵和好聽的音樂，沒想到活動竟然包含校內最古老雕像的選美比賽。課外活動委員會打算美化中庭，還請來一位本地雕刻家擔任評審。克拉拉聲稱校園創辦人雕像「眉毛悲劇」，小君則用毛氈做了假鬍子。以禮提醒大家不能破壞古蹟。結果他的警告成了小君拿鬍子照片下的圖說：「嚴肅歷史學家反對臉部毛髮。」

The volunteers were jolly, the weather was perfect, and the gardening team asked us to mow the grass. I had never used a lawn mower. Eli offered instructions that lasted longer than a parliament debate. “Please stop nagging,” I said, laughing. “This machine has only one button.” I pressed it. Nothing happened. June announced that our romance had reached a tragic climax. The groundskeeper arrived, turned on the safety switch, and thanked Eli for insisting on caution. I apologized to him and promised to consult the manual before challenging mechanical civilization again.

志工們歡樂無比，天氣也完美。園藝隊請我們修剪草坪。我從未用過割草機，以禮的操作說明卻比議會辯論還長。「不要一直嘮叨啦，」我笑著說，「這機器只有一個按鈕。」我按下去，什麼都沒發生。小君宣告我們的戀愛已進入悲劇高潮。校園園丁趕來，打開安全開關，還感謝以禮堅持謹慎。我向他道歉，承諾下次挑戰機械文明前會先看說明書。

At lunch, our group visited a stall run by students from the outskirts of town. Their families had prepared a hospitable feast: rice, soup, and ginger tea. The chef added one ounce of herbal extract to each large pot, then patiently explained that more was not always better. I admired the simplicity of the recipe. Eli asked whether we could purchase some for the dorm. “Is this your idea of a date souvenir?” I teased. “No,” he said. “Your idea of a souvenir is usually something that can explode. Tea seemed safer.”

午餐時，我們走到鎮郊學生設立的攤位。他們的家人準備了熱情好客的餐食：飯、湯和薑茶。廚師在每大鍋茶裡加入一盎司草本萃取物，耐心解釋不是加越多就越好。我很欣賞配方的簡單。以禮問能不能買一些回宿舍。「這就是你的約會紀念品？」我取笑他。「不是，」他說，「妳挑的紀念品通常可能爆炸。茶比較安全。」

The afternoon produced two simultaneous emergencies. First, a rainstorm soaked the exhibition posters. Second, June glued the fake mustache to her own upper lip and could not remove it. The gardening booth became our unexpected oasis of calm. We hung wet signs inside and watched June attempt to negotiate with a mirror. Clara suggested a ruthless solution involving scissors. Eli refused to let anyone approach June's face with sharp tools. He found gentle makeup remover instead. “Why are you so good at everything?” June asked. “I'm not,” Eli said. “I have simply practiced reading labels.”

下午同時發生兩件緊急事件。第一，暴雨把展覽海報淋濕了。第二，小君把假鬍子黏在自己上唇，怎麼都拆不下來。園藝攤位意外成了我們平靜的綠洲。我們把濕掉的牌子掛進棚內，旁觀小君試圖和鏡子談判。克拉拉建議用剪刀採取無情的解決方案。以禮不准任何人拿尖銳工具靠近小君的臉。他找到溫和的卸妝液。「你怎麼什麼都會？」小君問。「我沒有，」以禮說，「我只是有練習看標籤。」

As the rain ended, the sculptor thanked everyone and awarded the statue a prize for “surviving student enthusiasm.” Our fundraiser total did not slump despite the chaos. Eli and I walked home together, our shoes making identical squishing sounds. I told him I had wanted a romantic afternoon, not emergency facial-hair treatment. “We can still have one,” he replied. He led me to a bench beside the pond, where the lights reflected on the water. There were no speeches, committees, or machines in sight. “Your date plan was wonderful,” he said. “Even the mustache?” “Especially the part where it wasn't on me.”

雨停後，雕刻家向所有人道謝，並頒給雕像一座「成功挺過學生熱情」獎。即使亂成一團，募款金額也沒有暴跌。我和以禮一起走回宿舍，鞋子發出一模一樣的濕答答聲。我說我原本想要浪漫的下午，不是緊急處理臉部毛髮。「還可以有啊，」他回答。他帶我到池邊長椅坐下，燈光映在水面上。這裡看不到演講、委員會或機器。「妳的約會計畫很好，」他說。「包括鬍子？」「尤其是鬍子沒有黏在我臉上的部分。」

He moved closer, and I thought he might kiss me. From across the pond, June shouted, “WE CAN SEE YOU!” She was still wearing half a mustache. Eli began laughing, and I buried my face against his jacket. He held me there until I was laughing too. That night he sent a message containing a photograph of the bench. Beneath it he'd written, “Best exhibit: you.” I saved the message immediately. I have lost phones, schedules, umbrellas, and even a shoe once, but there are certain ridiculous little things a person learns never to throw away.

他往我靠近，我以為他準備吻我。池塘另一頭卻傳來小君的大叫：「我們都看得見！」她上唇還黏著半片鬍子。以禮笑了，我把臉埋進他的外套，他就這樣抱著我，直到我也笑了。那晚他傳來一張長椅照片，底下寫著：「最佳展品：妳。」我立刻存起來。我弄丟過手機、行程表、雨傘，甚至有一次還少了一隻鞋。但人總會慢慢學懂，有些荒謬的小東西絕對不能丟。

When Eli asked if the date had disappointed me, I told him the opposite. It was the first afternoon when we'd both made mistakes without treating them as disasters. He admitted that my jokes sometimes helped him stop worrying about details. I promised not to nag him about the clipboard, provided he never packed a fake mustache again.

以禮問今天的約會是否讓我失望時，我告訴他正好相反。這是我們第一次都犯了錯，卻沒把它當成災難。他承認我的笑話有時能讓他不再鑽牛角尖。我答應不再嘮叨他的寫字板，只要他以後別再帶假鬍子。
