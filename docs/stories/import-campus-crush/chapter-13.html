Algebra of a Sports Disaster

戀愛嫌疑犯通緝中 · 第 13 / 25 章 · 5000–6000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

The campus sports festival was already underway when I discovered that Eli had enrolled us in the mixed relay race. “I thought this was a chess tournament,” I protested. He showed me the email, which plainly said ATHLETIC FESTIVAL. My ability to ignore capital letters was becoming a concern. June, our most outgoing friend, announced that she would provide commentary. Clara arrived carrying an enormous suitcase of costumes. She explained that every athlete deserved a dramatic entrance. Our coach, a retired sportswoman, said the only requirement was to arrive at the starting line on time. Apparently, even love could not excuse us from basic reading comprehension.

校園運動會已經開始，我才發現以禮替我們報名了男女混合接力。「我以為這是西洋棋比賽，」我抗議。他拿出郵件，上面清清楚楚寫著「體育節」。我忽略大寫字母的能力實在令人擔憂。最外向的小君宣布她要擔任賽評。克拉拉則拖來巨大的行李箱，表示每位運動員都值得戲劇性進場。我們的教練是一位退休女運動員，她說唯一要求就是準時站到起跑線上。看來，即使談戀愛也不能免除基本閱讀能力。

The mathematics club had set up a booth to explain sports statistics using algebra. One volunteer challenged students to find the antonym of “victory.” June answered “Monday.” Eli argued that “defeat” would be more accurate. I told him that Monday could also be a form of defeat. Nearby, volunteers checked their equipment inventory and reminded everyone to conserve water. We had packed our own bottles, but the snack table displayed twelve kinds of salad dressing. “Which one improves speed?” I asked. Eli said none of them. June suggested mustard because it sounded more urgent than yogurt.

數學社設了攤位，用代數解釋運動統計。有位志工問大家「勝利」的反義詞，小君回答「星期一」。以禮說「失敗」比較精確，我提醒他星期一有時本身就是一種失敗。旁邊志工檢查器材庫存，提醒大家節約用水。我們自備水瓶，但點心桌卻放著十二種沙拉醬。「哪一種能讓人跑更快？」我問。以禮說沒有。小君建議芥末醬，因為聽起來比優格醬更有緊迫感。

Our team was outnumbered by stronger-looking competitors, so the coach gave us a tentative strategy: keep moving and pass the baton safely. Clara wanted an elaborate pre-race chant. I tried to restrain her enthusiasm without seeming rude. “A little quieter?” I suggested. “My dignity is fragile.” Eli stepped beside me and said, “We're here for sportsmanship, not perfection.” The advice helped. I had been haunted by memories of falling on the experimental farm, but he reminded me that one embarrassing moment did not have to predict another. I breathed in, bent my knees, and waited for the starting signal.

我們的隊伍在人數和體能上似乎都比對手弱，教練提出暫定策略：保持前進並安全交棒。克拉拉想設計複雜的賽前口號，我試著克制她的熱情，又不想失禮。「小聲一點好嗎？」我說，「我的尊嚴很脆弱。」以禮站到我身旁。「我們是來展現運動家精神，不是追求完美。」這番話很有用。我一直被上次農場跌倒的回憶糾纏，但他提醒我，一次丟臉不一定預告下一次。我深呼吸、彎膝，等待起跑訊號。

The race itself resembled an encyclopedia of possible mistakes. One runner lost a shoe. Another grabbed the wrong baton. June screamed so loudly that the announcer apologized for a microphone problem. My outward expression probably looked calm, but inside I was negotiating with every muscle in my body. When Eli passed me the baton, he whispered, “You've got this.” I ran faster than I expected. We finished fourth, and Clara immediately described it as “a secret moral victory.” June replied that fourth place was still an achievement if nobody required medical assistance. I agreed wholeheartedly.

正式比賽簡直像一本錯誤百科全書：一名跑者掉了鞋，另一名拿錯接力棒。小君尖叫得太大聲，主持人還為麥克風問題道歉。我外表大概很冷靜，內心卻正和全身每條肌肉談判。以禮交棒給我時，低聲說：「妳可以的。」我跑得比自己預期快。我們得到第四名，克拉拉立刻稱之為「隱藏的道德勝利」。小君說，只要沒有人需要醫療協助，第四名就是成就。我完全同意。

At dusk, the coach gave us participation medals and thanked everyone for showing respect. The awards ceremony was simple, with no exaggerated hero stories. Eli and I sat on the grass afterward, watching students pack their tents. He confessed that he had once mistaken achievement for affection. “I thought if I was useful enough, people would stay,” he said. I tried to be discreet, knowing that he didn't share such things easily. “You don't need a medal for me to stay,” I answered. He rested his head on my shoulder. I hummed a half-remembered lullaby, and he pretended not to hear my terrible pitch.

黃昏時，教練頒發參加獎牌，感謝大家互相尊重。頒獎典禮很簡單，沒有誇張的英雄故事。之後我和以禮坐在草地上，看學生們收帳篷。他坦白自己以前把成就誤當成獲得喜愛的方法。「我以為只要夠有用，別人就會留下，」他說。我知道他很少分享這種事，於是小心不張揚。「你不用拿獎牌，我也會留下，」我回答。他把頭靠在我肩上。我哼了一首記不太清的搖籃曲，他假裝沒聽見我可怕的音準。

June later sent our team photograph with the message, “FOURTH PLACE, FIRST-CLASS EMBARRASSMENT.” Eli printed a copy and put it beside his neat collection of university certificates. “Why there?” I asked. “Because it was a good day,” he said. “And because I didn't feel like I had to win.” I looked at the picture. My hair stood up, Clara wore a cape, and June was pointing at somebody off-camera. We appeared nothing like champions. But I could see how hard everyone had tried. Some victories really did not fit on a scoreboard, and this one felt worth remembering.

小君後來傳來團隊照片，訊息寫著：「第四名，頂級丟臉。」以禮把照片印出來，放在一排整齊的大學證書旁。「為什麼放那裡？」我問。「因為那是很棒的一天，」他說，「而且我沒有覺得自己非贏不可。」我看著照片：我頭髮翹起、克拉拉披著斗篷、小君正指向鏡頭外的某人。我們看起來一點都不像冠軍，但每個人的努力都清清楚楚。有些勝利確實無法顯示在計分板上，而這一次值得珍藏。

As we washed the mud from our shoes, I noticed that Eli had shown no indifference toward our teammates, even after the race ended. He remembered who needed water, who had a sore ankle, and who wanted a group photograph. I wondered how many quiet acts of care it took to make a person feel part of a team.

洗掉鞋上的泥巴時，我發現即使比賽結束，以禮對隊友也毫不冷漠。他記得誰需要水、誰腳踝不舒服、誰想拍團體照。我不禁想，一個人究竟要付出多少安靜的關心，才能讓別人感覺自己屬於一個團隊。
