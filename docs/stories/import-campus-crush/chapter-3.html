小黃瓜陰謀論

The Cucumber Conspiracy

戀愛嫌疑犯通緝中 · 第 3 / 25 章 · 5000–6000 · 英中對照

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

By Monday, June had transformed a cafeteria complaint into an advisory committee. She insisted that the school's decision to put cucumber in every sandwich was a conspiracy against happiness. “This is not a war,” Eli said. “Then why,” June demanded, “have we suffered three days of vegetable conquest?” I tried to lessen the tension by suggesting a blind taste test. June agreed at once, which should have warned me that she had already printed campaign posters. One showed a cucumber wearing sunglasses above the words STOP THE GREEN MENACE.

到星期一，小君已經把對學生餐廳的抱怨變成一個顧問委員會。她堅稱學校在每個三明治裡放小黃瓜，是對幸福的陰謀。「這不是戰爭，」以禮說。「那我們為什麼，」小君反問，「已經遭受了三天蔬菜征服？」我提議辦盲測，希望緩和緊張。小君立刻答應；我早該想到，她連宣傳海報都印好了。上頭畫著戴墨鏡的小黃瓜，並寫著：「阻止綠色威脅。」

The event became a charity picnic to support a local orphanage. Clara volunteered to perform; the textile-design students sold reusable napkins made from recycled textile scraps. A nurse from the campus clinic offered accurate information about the autumn vaccine program at a separate health booth. “Wonderful,” said June, “education and snacks.” Unfortunately, somebody mistook the signs. Our sandwich table received a poster explaining immunity, and the health booth displayed a drawing of an angry cucumber. The nurse laughed so hard she had to sit down. Even Eli failed to keep a straight face.

活動後來變成替當地育幼院募款的野餐。克拉拉自願表演，紡織設計系學生則用回收布料製作餐巾義賣。校內診所的護理師也在另一個健康攤位，提供秋季疫苗計畫的正確資訊。「太棒了，」小君說，「知識與點心兼具。」可惜有人把指示牌放錯了。三明治桌上出現解釋免疫力的海報，健康攤位則掛著一幅憤怒小黃瓜插畫。護理師笑到不得不坐下，連以禮都無法維持嚴肅。

At noon a feeble breeze failed to cool the courtyard. The food club began heating snacks, and grease jumped from a pan. Its president started to fume about careless volunteers. Suddenly, a sound like a firecracker exploded from the speaker system. It was merely June testing her microphone at maximum volume. People jumped behind a row of shrubs. “My voice,” she announced proudly, “has surpassed ordinary technology.” Eli quietly disconnected the speaker. I watched his careful hands and thought that having a sensible boyfriend was useful when my friends behaved like badly supervised fireworks.

中午時，一陣微弱的風吹過，根本無法替中庭降溫。餐飲社開始加熱點心，油脂從平底鍋飛濺而出。社長氣得直冒煙，責怪志工太粗心。突然，音響傳來一聲像鞭炮的巨響。原來只是小君把麥克風音量調到最大來測試。大家都跳到一排灌木後面。「我的聲音，」她得意宣布，「已經超越普通科技。」以禮默默切斷音響。我看著他穩定的雙手，覺得當朋友像失控煙火時，擁有一個理智的男朋友實在很有用。

I had been assigned to explain why some people dislike cucumbers. June offered the slang term “green betrayal,” which was not helpful. Eli produced a scientific report about bitter taste receptors. I accidentally read the title as “bitter romance receptors.” “Those,” Clara said, “can certainly plague college students.” The audience laughed. When Eli passed me my notes, I noticed the little blue liner inside his jacket: I'd sewn it there after he tore his pocket on our mountain date. He still wore the repaired jacket, though the thread was crooked and the fabric didn't match.

我負責解釋為何有些人不喜歡小黃瓜。小君提供了「綠色背叛」這個俚語，毫無幫助。以禮拿出關於苦味受器的研究報告，我卻把標題念成「苦澀戀愛受器」。「這種東西，」克拉拉說，「的確常常困擾大學生。」觀眾笑了起來。以禮遞筆記給我時，我看見他外套裡那片藍色內襯，是上次山上約會時他口袋破掉，我幫他縫上的。他居然還穿著那件外套，儘管我的縫線歪七扭八，顏色也完全不搭。

We had planned recreational games after lunch, including cucumber bowling. I became the unwilling champion when my ball knocked down every plastic bottle. June demanded a rematch, claiming my success was evidence of a secret athletic background. “I have no such background,” I said. “Yesterday I tripped over my own backpack.” Eli smiled. “A spectacular victory can emerge from a savage lack of coordination.” It was the most outrageous compliment anyone had ever paid me. I took his hand in front of everyone. June dropped a cucumber and shouted, “I KNEW IT!”

午餐後，我們安排了娛樂性遊戲，包括小黃瓜保齡球。我竟然意外成為冠軍，一球打倒所有塑膠瓶。小君要求重賽，聲稱我有隱藏的運動員背景。「根本沒有，」我說，「昨天我還被自己的背包絆倒。」以禮笑了。「驚人的勝利，有時源自猛烈到不可思議的四肢不協調。」這大概是我收過最荒唐的稱讚。我在所有人面前牽起他的手。小君把小黃瓜掉在地上，大喊：「我就知道！」

At the end, the charity coordinator thanked us. A child from the orphanage had drawn a picture of our event: a table, a heart, and an enormous cucumber floating over the crowd like a moon. “What does it symbolize?” Clara asked solemnly. “Hope?” guessed Eli. “Danger?” suggested June. The child grinned. “Lunch.” That answer seemed wiser than anything we had said all day. On our way home, Eli asked if I wanted to see a movie. “Only if it contains no vegetables,” I replied. He promised nothing, but he bought two tickets anyway.

結束時，慈善活動負責人向大家道謝。有個育幼院孩子畫了一張活動圖：一張桌子、一顆愛心，以及像月亮一樣飄在人群上方的巨大小黃瓜。「它象徵什麼？」克拉拉鄭重地問。「希望？」以禮猜。「危險？」小君說。孩子咧嘴一笑：「午餐。」這個答案比我們整天講的任何話都明智。回家路上，以禮問我要不要看電影。「只要裡面沒有蔬菜，」我回答。他不保證，但還是買了兩張票。

The following morning June submitted her receipts and counted the donations twice. The charity coordinator confirmed that the money had arrived safely. When I told Eli, he smiled and said the best part of our ridiculous campaign was that the children would receive new books. I agreed, although I still refused to eat cucumber sandwiches.

隔天早上，小君交齊收據，還把捐款數了兩次。慈善協調員確認款項已安全送達。我告訴以禮時，他笑著說，這場荒謬活動最棒的部分是孩子們將有新書。我同意，雖然依舊拒絕吃小黃瓜三明治。
