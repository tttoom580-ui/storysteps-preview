The Hall of Common Arts smelled of wet books and cold stone. Ivo brought a lantern; Nera brought an inventory signed by three municipal archivists. They found torn shelves, a blocked door, and boxes labeled obsolete. Behind a plaster wall, the archivists discovered a sealed wooden drawer. Inside lay handwritten copies of the first spell permissions. The documents divided magic into three categories: natural patterns taught freely, public-service spells held in common, and commercial improvements sold by their makers. The last category carried a price. The second did not. Nera wanted to photograph everything, but one archivist demanded they first record the drawer's condition. She agreed. By morning, an official witness log and two independent copies existed. They did not yet have the original charter. They had enough, however, to ask a dangerous question in public: how had a company acquired something the city had promised never to sell?
