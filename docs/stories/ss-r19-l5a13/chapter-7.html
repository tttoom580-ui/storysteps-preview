Professor Halden lived beyond the old botanical gardens, surrounded by notebooks rather than magical conveniences. He had once taught the city's children to read common runes without payment. When Nera showed him the silver seal, he did not act surprised. "We warned them that a license could become a lock," he said. He opened a cabinet containing a list of teachers, builders, and neighborhood associations that had written the original spells together. His name was not at the top. Neither was a company. The list named the Hall of Common Arts as custodian, never owner. Halden admitted he had signed a reform recommendation years earlier, believing commercial upgrades would be separate from public essentials. Nera was angry, but he handed her the original meeting dates and invited her to check his account. His mistake did not erase the evidence; it made verification more necessary.
