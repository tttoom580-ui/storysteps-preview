At eleven fifty-seven, every streetlamp in Veyla blinked at once. Nera Vale was crossing the public square with a batch of unpaid repair forms when the blue letters appeared above her wrist: YOUR MAGIC LICENSE EXPIRES AT MIDNIGHT. RENEW NOW. The notice spread quickly. Across the square, a baker stood motionless, staring at the rim of a pot whose heating charm had gone dark. A woman carrying a sleeping child ran toward the hospital. The city's power circuit was still working; The underground machines hummed. Only the spells were failing. Then a second message arrived. Renewal would cost triple the old price.

十一點五十七分，維拉城所有的街燈同時閃了一下。奈拉・維爾抱著一疊尚未付款的修繕表格穿過中央廣場，手腕上方忽然浮出一行藍字：「你的魔法使用許可將於午夜到期。請立即續約。」通知迅速傳開。廣場另一頭，一名麵包師站著不動，盯著鍋緣；鍋子的加熱咒已經熄滅。一名抱著熟睡孩子的女子朝醫院奔去。城市的電力迴路仍在運作，地底機器仍嗡嗡作響。失效的只有咒語。隨後第二則通知出現：續約費將是原價的三倍。

Nera worked for the city's small nonprofit office that reviewed public contracts. For six years she had asked whether public services were decent. Lately, officials called her inquiry pointless. Her mentor, Professor Halden, had retired after the council adopted a new licensing regime. Under that regime, private companies could charge for the placement of runes on public property. Officials claimed the arrangement would encourage innovation. Its projection promised better lamps, trains, and hospitals. But no one had explained why every license would expire in the same minute, regardless of when it had been purchased.

奈拉在市府一間小型非營利契約審查辦公室工作。六年來，她追問公共服務是否公正。最近，官員說她的調查毫無意義。她的導師霍爾登教授，在議會通過新授權制度後便退休了。依照該制度，私人企業可以向設置在公共設施上的符文收費。官員宣稱這能鼓勵創新。它的預測報告保證街燈、列車與醫院都會改善。但始終沒有人解釋，為什麼所有許可無論何時購買，都會在同一分鐘到期。

At the hospital gate, a repair worker was balanced on the saddle of a maintenance rig, trying to reinforce the old glass tubes around the emergency entrance. Nera recognized Ivo Marr, who had once repaired her mother's heating stove for no charge. “Don't touch the runes,” he called. “The warning is making them dissolve.” At his feet, sparks crawled through a crack in the stone. It would terrify anyone who trusted the hospital charms. Ivo climbed down slowly, one hand against the wall. “My tools still work,” he said, “but the doors are locked by a spell. I can open them without magic, if you give me ten minutes and keep the crowd back.”

醫院大門旁，一名維修工正坐在維護升降架的鞍形座上，試圖加固急診入口周圍的舊玻璃管。奈拉認出那是伊沃・馬爾；他曾免費替她母親修過暖爐。「別碰符文！」他喊道，「警告訊號正在讓它們溶解。」他的腳邊，火花沿著石板的裂縫爬行。任何相信醫院防護咒的人都會害怕。伊沃慢慢爬下來，一手扶著牆。「我的工具還能用，」他說，「但門被咒語鎖住了。給我十分鐘，幫我擋住人群，我不用魔法也能把它打開。」

Nera wanted to tell him to hurry, but she saw the strain in his shaking hands. On the steps, an administrator wearing a silver company badge shouted that the hospital must renew its license before anyone could interfere. “An idiot would break a private seal,” he said. “The company can sue the city.” Ivo turned away from the damaged tubes. Nera did not. She opened the council archive on her public tablet and found the emergency-service clause, a rule that her old mentor had insisted remain in the law. It allowed a temporary physical repair when a licensed system endangered human life. The clause did not restore a spell. It simply protected workers who used ordinary methods while a court reviewed the danger.

奈拉想催他快一點，卻看見他顫抖的雙手所承受的壓力。台階上，一名佩戴銀色公司徽章的管理員大喊，醫院必須先續約，任何人才可以動手。「只有白痴才會破壞私人封印，」他說，「公司可以告整座城市。」伊沃轉身離開損壞的玻璃管，奈拉卻沒有退開。她在公用平板上打開議會檔案，找到緊急服務條款——她那位老導師曾堅持必須保留在法律裡的規定。當授權系統危及人命時，條款允許進行暫時性的實體修復。它不能讓魔咒恢復運作，只能保障工人在法院審查危險之前，依法使用一般方法進行搶修。

“I'm not ordering you to break a seal,” Nera told Ivo. “I'm asking you to open a door with a wrench.” The administrator stepped between them. Nera turned her tablet toward him and began recording. “Your name, please. And the legal reason for refusing access to emergency care.” He looked at the growing crowd, then stepped aside without answering. Ivo pulled a heavy tool from his bag. The first bolt resisted. The second moved. Inside the building, a nurse had found a hand lamp and was guiding patients down the stairs. No one cheered when the door finally opened; they were too busy carrying water, blankets, and frightened children. But the hospital had an entrance again.

「我不是命令你破壞封印，」奈拉對伊沃說，「我是請你用扳手開一扇門。」管理員擋在兩人中間。奈拉把平板轉向他，開始錄影。「請問您的姓名？以及您拒絕病人獲得急診照護的法律依據？」他看了看越聚越多的人群，沒有回答，便退到一旁。伊沃從工具袋中抽出沉重的工具。第一顆螺栓紋絲不動，第二顆終於鬆了。建築裡，一名護理師找到手提燈，正在引導病人走下樓梯。大門終於開啟時，沒有人歡呼；大家忙著搬水、毯子，還有受到驚嚇的孩子。但醫院總算重新有了入口。

The clock struck midnight. Across Veyla, the city fell into a darkness so complete that Nera could hear the river again. She had expected people to scream. Instead, voices called street names, counted children, and told strangers where the steps were. Ivo sat beside her, breathing hard. “This is not a breakthrough,” he said. “A door isn't a city.” “No,” Nera answered. “But it's evidence that the company can't decide every action we take.” She knew the council would have to implement an emergency plan before sunrise. Her office could not do it alone. She needed records, witnesses, and people who could repair things without waiting for permission.

時鐘敲響午夜。整座維拉城陷入徹底的黑暗，安靜得讓奈拉再次聽見河水的聲音。她原以為人們會尖叫。出乎意料的是，大家開始呼喊街道名稱、清點孩子的人數，並指引陌生人注意台階。伊沃在她身旁坐下，喘著氣。「這算不上什麼突破，」他說，「一扇門不等於一座城市。」「沒錯，」奈拉回答，「但至少證明，公司不能決定我們能做的每一件事。」她知道議會必須在天亮前實施緊急計畫，但她的辦公室無法獨力完成。她需要紀錄、證人，以及不用等誰批准就能修好東西的人。

She copied the hospital's warning into an evidence file and compared it with the notices saved by the baker and the woman with the child. All three named the same owner: Cinder House Holdings. Beneath the owner's name was a tiny line of legal text that most people would never notice. Nera enlarged it until the letters filled her screen. The public license referred to an original agreement signed forty-eight years earlier. But Cinder House had been founded only nineteen years ago. A lawful explanation might exist: inheritance or a company purchase. She would not accuse anyone without checking. Still, the date mattered. It was the first fact tonight that the company could not explain by raising its prices.

她把醫院收到的警告複製進證據檔案，再拿它和麵包師、抱孩子女子保存的通知比對。三則通知都寫著同一個權利所有人：燼屋控股。在公司名稱下方，有一行小得幾乎不會有人注意的法律文字。奈拉把它放大到整個螢幕。公共許可引用了一份四十八年前簽署的原始協議。然而燼屋控股直到十九年前才成立。也許有合法解釋，例如繼承或企業收購。她不會在查證前指控任何人。但那個日期非常重要：這是今晚出現的第一項事實，無法單靠調高價格來解釋。

“Who signed the first agreement?” Ivo asked. Nera touched the blank space beside the date. “That's the question.” For the first time that night, the administrator spoke in a low voice instead of shouting. “You should stop looking,” he said. Nera turned. “Why?” The man's eyes moved toward the river, where the old Hall of Common Arts stood in darkness. “Because the first license didn't belong to the company,” he replied. A bell rang inside the empty hall. It was not powered by magic. Someone had entered the building and was pulling the rope by hand. Nera saved the recording. The city had lost its spells, but it had not lost the people willing to ask who owned them.

「第一份協議是誰簽的？」伊沃問。奈拉點了點日期旁的空白處。「這正是我要知道的。」那一夜第一次，管理員不再大喊，而是壓低聲音說話。「妳最好別再查下去。」奈拉轉過身。「為什麼？」男人的目光移向河邊。古老的公共技藝廳沉在黑暗裡。「因為第一張許可證的主人並不是那家公司，」他回答。這時，空蕩的技藝廳裡響起鐘聲。那座鐘沒有魔力驅動，是有人走進大廳，親手拉動鐘繩。奈拉保存了錄影。城市失去了咒語，卻沒有失去願意追問誰擁有咒語的人。

Why could Ivo legally open the hospital door without renewing its magic license?
