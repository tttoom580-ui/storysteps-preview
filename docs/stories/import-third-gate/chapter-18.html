被遺忘者名冊

The Directory of Forgotten Names

南河雙時錄 · 第 18 / 25 章 · 4000–5000 · 英中對照 · 校訂完成

An received a document from a retired lawmaker who had spent years collecting testimony about Nanhe's waterfront. The file contained a directory of families displaced by past construction and an old certificate issued to workers who had maintained the Third Gate. Its most startling entry identified a collective of women as the owners of the original flood maps. The discovery altered the familiar narrative again. Mei had become the public face of the defense, but many people had undertaken the work of preserving knowledge. An decided that her next exhibition would give those people their names back.

安收到一位退休立法者送來的文件。那人多年蒐集南河水岸的證詞，檔案包括因開發被迫遷移的家庭名冊，以及發給第三道水門維護者的舊證書。最驚人的一頁指出，最初的水道圖其實由一個婦女團體共同保管。這又改變人們熟悉的故事：梅是守城的代表，但保存知識的人遠不只她。安決定，下一場展覽要把那些人的名字還給他們。

A newspaper columnist accused the museum of seeking attention through petty disputes about credit. An asked whether he would make the same argument if the missing names belonged to generals. His ego was wounded; he said the public wanted heroes, not a directory. “History is not an exclusive club,” she replied. “People who carried water and copied plans have as much claim to memory as those who gave speeches.” An tried to remain courteous, though the encounter left her shaken. She knew prejudice could operate quietly even among people who thought themselves fair.

一名專欄作家指責博物館為了小小的署名爭議博取注意。安問，若被漏掉的是將軍的名字，他是否還會這樣說。他自尊受傷，堅稱大眾要英雄，不要名冊。「歷史不是私人俱樂部，」安回答，「抬水與抄圖的人，同樣有被記得的權利。」她努力維持禮貌，心裡卻動搖。偏見有時悄悄運作，連自認公正的人也難察覺。

The opposition to the exhibition grew when the museum sought permission to display records from the old cemetery. Some descendants did not want strangers reading their ancestors' suffering. Others opted to share the documents so that the community would not vanish from public memory. An organized a ceremony in which every family could choose what remained private. A speaker recited the names of workers whose efforts had saved the city. No one was required to reveal more than they wished. This compromise felt less dramatic than a courtroom victory, but far more respectful.

博物館想展出古墓園檔案時，反對聲浪又擴大。有些後代不願陌生人閱讀祖先受苦的記錄，另一些則選擇公開，避免社群從記憶裡消失。安安排儀式，讓各家自行決定何者保密。有人朗誦救城工人的名字，沒有人必須透露超過自己願意的內容。這種折衷不像法庭勝利那般戲劇化，卻更尊重人。

Afterward, a restaurant owner served spicy soup in memory of the cooks who had kept Nanhe alive during the siege. He spoke about his grandmother's trauma after losing her home to a modern flood. An realized that historical wounds could echo in families without becoming identical experiences. She insisted on integrity in the display: the museum would state when documents were authentic, when stories were oral traditions, and when a conclusion remained uncertain. The setting was simple, with wooden tables and photographs rather than expensive decoration. Visitors stayed to read longer than the staff expected.

儀式後，一名餐館老闆端出辛辣湯品，紀念圍城時維繫南河生命的廚子。他談起祖母在現代洪水中失去家園後留下的創傷。安明白，歷史的傷能在家庭回響，卻不代表每代經歷完全相同。她堅持展覽的誠信：哪些文件真確、哪些是口述傳統、哪些結論仍不確定，都要清楚標示。現場只有木桌和照片，沒有奢華布置。觀眾反而停留得比館員預期更久。

The last wall of the exhibition had been left empty for names still being verified. A sponsor suggested filling it with a dramatic painting, saying visitors disliked blank space. An refused. “Nowhere in our records does it say that every worker's identity is known,” she explained. “An honest blank is better than a beautiful invention.” Instead she placed a table where visitors could submit family documents for examination, with clear rules about privacy and ownership. On opening day a man brought his grandfather's river notebook, while a woman offered photographs of a lost courtyard. Neither item solved the ancient mystery immediately. But the table filled with careful questions, and An felt that the empty wall had finally found its purpose: it made room for people still missing from the story.

展覽最後一面牆留給尚在核實的人名。一位贊助者建議用戲劇性油畫填滿，說觀眾不愛空白。安拒絕：「我們的紀錄從未說已知道所有工人身分。誠實的空白，勝過漂亮的捏造。」她改放一張桌子，讓訪客提交家族文件查驗，並明訂隱私和所有權。開幕日有人帶來祖父的河岸筆記，也有婦人拿來失落庭院的照片。兩者未立刻解開古謎，但桌前湧現細緻提問。安覺得空牆終於有了用途：替仍被故事遺忘的人留下位置。

Near closing time, An found a note in the visitor book. A teenager wrote that she had always believed history was made by famous rulers, until she read about the women who had carried the old maps through the siege. “I think my grandmother has a place in history too,” the note said. An kept a copy for the museum archive. That night she reviewed the lawmaker's document once more and found a clue to a second, hidden record: a list of people who had operated the gate on the final night. The last name was Mei's, followed by a mark that looked like a warning.

閉館前，安在留言本上看到一段話。一名少女說，她以前總以為歷史由名王名將創造，直到讀見婦女們冒圍城之險攜帶古圖。「我想，我的祖母也有歷史位置。」安把留言存檔。那晚她再讀立法者的文件，找到第二份祕密紀錄的線索：最後一夜操作水門者名單。末尾是梅的名字，後頭還有個像警告的符號。
