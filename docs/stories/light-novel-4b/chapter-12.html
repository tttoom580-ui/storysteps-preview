A Burglar in the Archive Room

1998，被刪除的女主角拒絕退場！ · 第 12 / 25 章 · 3000–4000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

The trouble began with an alert from the night guard: the archive room door had been forced open. Nothing obvious was missing, but a broken bracelet lay beside a storage bin. Chikage asked the guard not to call anyone a burglar until the police had examined the door. Yui wanted to search the whole building. "A desperate search can destroy the evidence we need," Chikage said. She photographed the floor without moving the fragments and wrote down the procedure she had followed. A former agent from the insurer arrived to document the damage. He warned that a careless accusation could become a costly lawsuit. Chikage promised only verified comments.

麻煩從夜間警衛的通報開始：檔案室的門被人硬扳開。表面上沒有少東西，但收納箱旁躺著一條斷掉的手鍊。千景請警衛在警方檢查門鎖前，別急著稱任何人為竊賊。小結想把整棟樓翻個遍。「急著搜查可能毀掉我們需要的證據。」千景說。她先拍攝地板，不移動碎片，並寫下自己遵守的程序。保險公司的前理賠代理人到場記錄損害，提醒她隨意指控可能引來昂貴官司。千景答應只發表已核實的意見。

A woman from the former staff committee recognized the bracelet. It had belonged to a painter famous for drawing graceful hands, a quiet artist who had survived years of financial hardship. She was now dead, and her daughter had inherited some of the studio's papers. The daughter arrived in tears. "I came to find my mother's work," she confessed. "The door was already damaged. I shouldn't have entered without calling." Chikage listened, then asked her to give a statement to the police rather than disappear in shame. The woman's faithful memories did not automatically prove ownership, but they deserved a respectful hearing.

前員工委員會的一名女士認出手鍊。那曾屬於一名以畫出優美手勢聞名的畫師，她安靜地熬過多年經濟困境，如今已不在人世，女兒繼承了部分工房文件。女兒哭著趕來。「我是來找母親作品的。」她坦承。「門本來就壞了，但我不該不打電話就進來。」千景聽完，請她向警方說明，不要因羞愧而躲起來。她忠實的回憶不能自動證明所有權，卻值得被尊重地聽取。

Inside a durable cardboard tube, they found a series of pencil drawings portraying a tribal community from the canceled film. The sketches were beautiful, but the background notes contained stereotypes that the artist had intended to correct. A margin comment read, "Ask the community before finalizing the costumes." Chikage realized that the artist had been trying to prevent harm, not simply decorate a scene. The daughter wanted the work to be seen, but she also feared that an exhibition might repeat the old mistakes. "Then we don't exhibit it until the context is ready," Chikage said. "Careful preservation is not the same as immediate publication."

她們在堅固的紙筒裡找到一系列鉛筆畫，描繪被取消動畫中的某個部族社群。草圖很美，但背景筆記含有刻板印象，而畫師原本就打算修正。頁邊註記寫著：「定稿服裝前先請教社群。」千景明白，那名畫師不只是裝飾場景，而是想避免傷害。女兒希望作品被看見，也怕展覽重演舊錯。「那就等資料脈絡準備好再展出。」千景說。「謹慎保存不等於必須立刻公開。」

The police concluded that someone had damaged the door before the daughter entered, but they had no definite suspect. Chikage disliked uncertainty, yet she refused to invent a villain merely to make a tragic scene more satisfying. She changed the locks, checked the windows, and requested an inspection of the building's old fire equipment. Yui discovered a photograph showing the same doorway occupied by large storage boxes during an earlier era. The blocked exit had been a safety problem long before the current break-in. Chikage wrote a repair request, then posted a notice explaining that the archive would open only with staff present.

警方認為門在女兒進入前就已被破壞，但沒有明確嫌疑人。千景討厭不確定，卻拒絕為了讓悲劇場景更戲劇化而憑空捏造反派。她換了門鎖、檢查窗戶，並安排舊消防設備的檢驗。小結找到一張照片，顯示同一扇門在更早的年代曾被大型收納箱擋住。出口堵塞這個安全問題，遠早於這次闖入。千景寫下修繕申請，並張貼公告，說明檔案室之後只會在工作人員在場時開放。

The daughter asked permission to pursue recognition of her mother's drawings without seeking fame for herself. She could not commit to a public interview yet. Chikage assured her that the archive would use a private contact procedure. Another file recorded how inflation had reduced the real value of the unpaid wages over the years. The figures made the damage harder to dismiss. When a museum adviser arrived, he warned that careless handling could lead to the destruction of delicate originals. Chikage saw a pattern emerge: protecting a person and protecting a painting both began by asking who had the right to touch it.

女兒請求允許自己替母親的畫作追求認可，卻不想因此成名。她目前無法承諾接受公開採訪。千景保證典藏會用私下聯絡程序。另一份文件記載多年物價上漲如何降低欠薪的實際價值，使損失更難被輕忽。博物館顧問來訪時提醒，粗心碰觸可能導致細緻原件遭到毀壞。千景看見一個模式逐漸浮現：保護一個人和保護一張畫，都得先問誰有權碰觸它。

That evening, Akari asked whether a story could have a hero without a monster. Chikage considered the broken bracelet, the lost wages, and the unnecessary fear that had followed one damaged door. "Sometimes the hard thing is not fighting," she said. "It's finding out what happened without turning the nearest frightened person into the enemy." Akari looked disappointed, then thoughtful. She drew a small circle in the painted dust with one boot. "Perhaps," she said, "I could be the heroine who carries everyone's papers safely home." It was not an exciting promise, but Chikage thought it might be the most useful one the girl had made.

那晚燈里問，一個故事可不可以沒有怪物，卻仍然有英雄。千景想著斷掉的手鍊、失蹤的薪水，以及一扇壞門引起的多餘恐懼。「有時困難的不是打仗。」她說。「是找出發生什麼事，又不把眼前最害怕的人當成敵人。」燈里先失望，隨即陷入思考。她用一隻靴子在畫中的灰塵上畫了小圓圈。「也許，」她說，「我可以當個把所有人的文件安全帶回家的女主角。」那不是令人熱血沸騰的承諾，千景卻覺得，可能是燈里說過最有用的一句話。
