Avery had learned that a city could lose its memory one small decision at a time. The danger rarely arrived with a warning; sometimes it came inside a polite email or a neat drawing. Outside, the street continued its ordinary life. Someone opened a shop, someone carried supper home, and a child asked whether the market would still be there next year. 街道仍過著平凡生活。有人開店，有人提晚餐回家，一名孩子問市場明年是否還在。兩人此刻都不知道答案。一名孩子向他道謝時，他露出意外神情，艾薇幾乎笑了。艾薇仔細核對工人筆記裡的小細節，不因它們看似不起眼就忽略。移交協議承諾保護地方收藏。唐女士碰金屬牌前先放下鏟子。他認為毀掉稀有種子是對記憶的罪過。水煮蛋的黃色蛋黃讓唐女士想到第一次收成。

At the window, Julian asked whether Avery thought people could change. She answered that change was not a speech made at the right moment; it was a series of difficult actions that other people could check. He did not reply at once. She found she appreciated that delay more than a clever compliment. When they turned back toward the street, they were still two people with different responsibilities, and both knew it.

在窗邊，景衡問艾薇是否相信人會改變。她說，改變不是在恰當時機說出的一篇演說，而是一連串能被他人檢驗的困難行動。他沒有立刻回答。她發現自己比起漂亮的恭維，更欣賞這份停頓。兩人轉身走向街道時，仍肩負不同責任，彼此都很清楚。

Before they left, Avery checked the route one more time and explained her decision to the people standing beside the gate. She did not want their agreement merely because a famous company president was present. She wanted questions that could be answered with dates, measurements, and names. Julian listened without reaching for his phone. The next step would take time, but this time everyone knew who was responsible for it.

離開前，艾薇又檢查一次路線，向站在門旁的人解釋自己的決定。她不希望大家只是因為知名集團總裁在場就點頭，而是希望每個問題都能以日期、測量與姓名回答。景衡沒有伸手拿手機，只是安靜聽著。下一步需要時間，但這次每個人都知道該由誰負責。
