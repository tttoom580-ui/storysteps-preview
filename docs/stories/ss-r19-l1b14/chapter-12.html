Without the headphones, Nina and Leo worked on a song for the school festival. Before anyone called the day strange, Nina had noticed how quiet the college music room could become when people were worried. The smallest sounds seemed clear, and the smell of old wood and coffee made the place feel both familiar and new. Rather than act as if every answer was obvious, Nina opened the sound journal and began with what had really happened. The word “die” appeared in an example someone had written earlier, and Leo asked if the example fit their situation. “Let us keep ‘o’clock’ in our word notes,” Nina said. “We can check its meaning again if we need it.”

不用耳機的日子，妮娜和里歐一起為學園祭寫歌。在人們說這一天奇怪之前，妮娜就注意到，當大家擔心時，大學音樂教室會變得多麼安靜。細微的聲音變得清楚，舊木頭與咖啡的氣味也讓這地方既熟悉又陌生。妮娜沒有裝作一切都很明白，而是打開聲音日記，從真正發生的事情開始。「die」出現在前人留下的例句裡，里歐問這個例子是否適合現在的情況。「把『o’clock』留在詞彙筆記裡吧，」妮娜說，「有需要時再確認意思。」

Nobody could say yet how the story would end. Nina found this uncomfortable, especially with tomorrow’s ten seconds still waiting for an answer. Leo suggested they take turns speaking, because repeating the same fear would not create a new solution. A little order returned to the conversation, and that made the next difficult step possible. They made mistakes, laughed, and started a line again instead of seeking a perfect future. This was the point where a simple wish met the world as it really was. The answer took longer than either had expected. They agreed not to build the rest of their plan on a fact they had never checked.
