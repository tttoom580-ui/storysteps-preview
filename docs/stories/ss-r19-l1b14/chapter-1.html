明天的十秒聲音

Ten Seconds of Tomorrow

你借我的耳機，正在播放明天的聲音 · 第 1 / 25 章 · 1000以下 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

At nine o'clock, the headphones said, “Stop the music!” Nina dropped her juice on the desk. She was nineteen, in the middle of her first college year, and she had only borrowed the headphones from Leo because her own pair was broken. The room was quiet. There was no music playing, no one shouting, not even a small bug near the open window. Yet the voice had been hers. It had sounded frightened and very close. Nina pulled the headphones away and stared at Leo. “Is this a joke?” she asked. Leo looked at the red light on the side and said, “I hoped they would not turn on.”

晚上九點，耳機突然說：「音樂停下！」妮娜把果汁灑在桌上。她十九歲，正讀大學一年級，只因為自己的耳機壞了才向里歐借這一副。房間很安靜。沒有音樂，沒有人大喊，甚至連開著的窗邊都沒有一隻小蟲。但她剛才聽見的確實是自己的聲音，而且聽起來害怕又靠近。妮娜摘下耳機，盯著里歐。「你在開玩笑嗎？」她問。里歐看著耳機側面的紅燈，說：「我本來希望它不要啟動。」

Leo reached for a small book on the sofa. Inside were notes from his aunt, a woman who worked with sound machines. “They record the future,” he said, and then stopped because even he knew how strange that sounded. He turned the page toward Nina. One line was clear: At nine every night, ten seconds of sound from the place you will stand at noon tomorrow.* There was no picture and no way to choose a different time. “So I will shout tomorrow?” Nina asked. “Maybe,” Leo said. “But sound does not tell you why.” He looked serious, not proud of the trick. A little note used “other” as its key word; Nina kept the note because the rest of the line was still useful.

里歐伸手拿起沙發上的小本子。裡頭是他姑姑留下的筆記，那位女士研究聲音機器。「它會錄下未來，」他說到一半停住，因為連他也知道這聽起來太奇怪。他把頁面轉給妮娜看。一行字寫得很清楚：「每晚九點，播放你明天中午所在位置的十秒聲音。」沒有畫面，也不能選別的時間。「所以我明天會大喊嗎？」妮娜問。「也許，」里歐說，「但聲音不能告訴你為什麼。」他看起來很認真，沒有為惡作劇得意。一張小筆記以「other」為關鍵字；妮娜留下它，因為剩下的句子仍然有用。

Nina looked at the camera on her telephone, then put it down. She wanted to record the ten seconds to prove they were real, but the light had already gone dark. On the wall was a poster for a friendly college picnic in the north garden. It showed a red star, a happy pet dog, and the words OUR FUTURE MUSIC. “This is all too much,” Nina said. “I came here to ask about a song.” Leo moved the headphones into their case. “I can still help with the song,” he offered. “You can also leave now. You do not have to believe me.” That was the first fact she trusted. Nina circled “reach” on a reading sheet, not to win a test but to remember the meaning of the whole sentence.

妮娜看看手機相機，接著放下。她想錄下那十秒證明是真的，可耳機的燈已熄滅。牆上有一張北花園友善野餐的海報，畫著紅星、快樂的寵物狗，寫著「我們的未來音樂」。妮娜說：「這太多了。我只是來問一首歌的事。」里歐把耳機放回盒子。「我還是可以幫你處理那首歌，」他說，「你也可以現在離開，不必相信我。」這是她第一件願意相信的事。妮娜在閱讀單上圈出「reach」，不是為了考高分，而是為了理解整句話。

They tried a simple music exercise. Nina played the middle part of her song on an old piano, and Leo asked her to listen to each note before changing it. He did not use a strange machine or claim to know the perfect ending. A quiet woman from the next room came by to say the sound level was fine. Nina noticed how kind Leo was when he answered her. She had been ready to call him a liar, but now she was not sure. “If I hear tomorrow, can I change it?” she asked. Leo shook his head. “I don't know. I only know the sound comes true.” In the corner of the sound journal, Nina found the word “lie” beside an old drawing and stopped to ask what it meant.

他們開始做簡單的音樂練習。妮娜在舊鋼琴上彈出歌曲中段，里歐請她先聽清楚每個音，再決定怎麼改。里歐沒有使用奇怪機器，也沒有宣稱知道完美結尾。隔壁房間一位安靜的女士走來，說音量沒問題。妮娜注意到里歐回應那人的方式很溫柔。她原本準備叫他騙子，現在卻沒那麼確定。「如果我聽見明天，就能改變它嗎？」她問。里歐搖頭。「我不知道。我只知道聲音會成真。」在聲音日記的角落，妮娜看見一幅舊畫旁寫著「lie」，於是停下來詢問意思。

On the way home, Nina could not stop thinking about the words she had heard. She did not want to use ten seconds to make choices for everyone else. If the voice was truly hers, tomorrow would still belong to her, not to the headphones. At home she wrote three questions in her notebook and placed it by the window: What will happen? Who will be there? What does the sound leave out? Then she drew a tiny star next to the last question. The night seemed very long, but she went to bed instead of calling Leo again and again. “notice,” Leo read from the practice page, then tried to connect the word to something they could actually see.

回家路上，妮娜一直想著聽到的那些字。她不願靠十秒聲音替其他人作決定。如果聲音真的是自己的，明天仍屬於她，而不是耳機。回家後，她在筆記本寫下三個問題，放在窗邊：「會發生什麼？誰會在場？聲音沒說出什麼？」接著她在最後一題旁畫了一顆小星星。夜晚顯得漫長，但她還是上床睡覺，沒有一再打電話給里歐。「notice。」里歐讀出練習頁上的字，接著試著把它和眼前真正看得到的東西連起來。

At noon the next day, Nina stood in the music room with a group of students. One piano key made a hard, ugly noise. A little girl watching a school visit put her hands over her ears. Nina saw that the sound was painful, and she called, “Stop the music!” The ten seconds had come true. But nobody was hurt, and there was no great secret about her life hidden in the cry. She used a pen to write down what had actually happened. Then she sent Leo one message: It was real. You were right about the sound. You were also right that I guessed wrong.*

隔天中午，妮娜和一群學生站在音樂教室。一個琴鍵發出刺耳難聽的聲音。參觀學校的小女孩用手摀住耳朵。妮娜看見聲音讓人不舒服，立刻喊：「音樂停下！」那十秒真的發生了。但沒有人受傷，那聲呼喊也沒有藏著她人生的重大祕密。她拿筆記下真正發生的事，然後傳一則訊息給里歐：「是真的。你對聲音的事說對了。你也說對了，我的猜測是錯的。」
