錯誤時刻的圖書館

The Library of False Hours

The public archive occupied the old foreign embassy, a building that had survived three changes of government. Its librarian, Ms. Song, refused Zhiwei's first request because the time records had been moved into restricted storage. “I need written approval,” she said. Zhiwei felt disappointment, but she did not argue. She returned with the inspection code, the caretaker's statement, and an earnest letter explaining why the records mattered. Ms. Song read the letter twice. “You may examine copies,” she finally said, “but you cannot take the originals into your possession.”

市立檔案館設在舊外國使館內，這棟建築經歷過三次政府更替。圖書館員宋女士拒絕了知微最初的請求，因為時刻紀錄已移至限制借閱區。「我需要書面核准。」她說。知微感到失望，卻沒有爭辯。她帶著檢驗編碼、管理員證詞，以及一封誠懇說明紀錄重要性的信再度前來。宋女士讀了兩遍，終於說：「妳可以看副本，但不能把原件據為己有。」

The first box held children's workbooks and a small book of rhyme. Someone had placed the city's clock inspection lists between its pages. Banu, who served as a volunteer translator at the shelter, joined them to examine the notes in several languages. A cartoon murderer in the workbook was drawn chasing a man around a clock, each step tracing an orbit. “That is probably nonsense,” Luo An said. “Perhaps,” Banu replied. “But the numbers written in the margin aren't part of the lesson. They are equipment serial numbers.”

第一個箱子裝著兒童習作和一本押韻讀物。有人把城市時鐘檢驗清單夾進書頁裡。平日擔任收容所志工翻譯的巴努，也來協助閱讀不同語言的註記。習作裡有個卡通殺人犯追著一名男子繞鐘跑，每一步都畫出一圈軌道。「大概只是胡鬧，」羅安說。「也許，」巴努回答，「但頁邊寫的那些數字不是教材內容，而是設備序號。」

A young researcher named Taro had classified the inspection lists before they were sealed. He explained that the city did not use one master clock. Every district had a local timer, and the timers received continual corrections through public signal towers. The technicians were supposed to record each correction. A person could accomplish much by changing only one entry, because a false timestamp would make two real observations appear unrelated. Zhiwei understood the danger immediately. A street could be declared unsafe without anyone inventing a wave; the evidence needed only to be shifted by a few minutes.

年輕研究員太郎曾在清單封存前替它們分類。他解釋，城市並沒有唯一的主時鐘。各區都有自己的計時器，公共訊號塔會持續修正它們，技師理應記下每次修正。一個人只要改掉一筆紀錄，就可能造成極大影響；錯誤時間戳記會讓兩次真正的觀測看起來毫不相關。知微立刻明白其中的危險：不需要憑空捏造波動，只要挪動幾分鐘的證據，就能把安全街道宣布成危險地區。

Taro placed three clocks on a table. The second hand of one seemed to bounce backward whenever the signal tower sent a correction. “Don't read too much into a single jump,” he said. “A poor connection can do that.” He put special emphasis on repeated patterns. Across five districts, their clock corrections fell at identical intervals, even though the districts had different distances from the tower. The pattern was unusual, but it still lacked a direct connection to Qiao's office. Zhiwei copied the numbers by hand while Luo An checked that nobody had changed the pages during their visit.

太郎把三個時鐘放到桌上。其中一個時鐘每當訊號塔傳來校正，秒針就像往後彈了一下。「別太在意單次跳動，」他說，「連線不好也會發生這種事。」他特別強調重複出現的模式。五個街區的時鐘竟以完全相同的間隔受到校正，儘管它們與訊號塔距離不同。這種模式不尋常，卻仍缺乏直接連到喬辦公室的證據。知微用手抄下數字，羅安則確認他們查閱期間沒人更動頁面。

In the reading room next door, officials were holding a small public meeting. To lighten the mood, a speaker tried to provide amusement with a story about people who trusted maps more than their own eyes. Some listeners laughed; others stared at their shoes. Zhiwei wanted to answer, but Ms. Song placed a warning hand on the table. “If you interrupt, they will remove you before you can examine the last file,” she whispered. Zhiwei decided that remaining passive for one more hour was not the same as giving up her right to speak later.

隔壁閱覽室裡，官員正舉辦一場小型公開會議。為了活絡氣氛，一名講者說起那些寧可相信地圖、也不相信自己眼睛的人的笑話。有些聽眾笑了，另一些低頭盯著鞋子。知微想反駁，宋女士卻把手放在桌上示意她不要動。「妳一打斷，他們就會趕妳出去，最後那份檔案也看不到了。」她低聲說。知微決定暫時保持被動一個小時，並不等於放棄以後發言的權利。

The last file was an urgent repair order for Tower Seven, issued two nights before the first surge. The repair had not been finished. Its final page contained a list of those authorized to adjust district timers. Next to Qiao's office stood an unfamiliar contractor, Meridian Data. Luo An recognized the name from a recent defense exhibition. “They sold the equipment that draws the arrows,” she said. Zhiwei asked Ms. Song for a certified copy, and this time the librarian agreed without hesitation. “If the record is public,” she said, “the public must be able to question it.”

最後一份檔案，是第一次回響潮前兩晚發出的第七訊號塔緊急維修單。維修一直沒有完成。末頁列著獲授權調整各區計時器的人員名單；喬的辦公室旁，還有一家陌生承包商「經線數據」。羅安記得在最近的防衛展覽看過這名字。「他們賣的就是畫出箭頭的設備。」她說。知微請宋女士提供經認證的副本，這次圖書館員毫不猶豫地答應。「如果這是公共紀錄，」她說，「公眾就必須有權質疑它。」

On the steps outside, Taro offered to explain the correction system at an open meeting, provided that someone else checked his calculations. He refused to become a single expert whom everyone had to trust. Zhiwei welcomed the condition. The more people who could repeat the test, the less the case depended on her reputation. They left the former embassy carrying paper, not weapons, and an uncomfortable conclusion: the city's warnings might be accurate about the waves themselves while dangerously wrong about the roads people should take.

走出舊使館時，太郎主動表示願意在公開會議解說時鐘校正系統，條件是必須有人獨立核對他的計算。他不願成為所有人只能盲信的唯一專家。知微很贊同。能重做測試的人越多，事件就越不依賴她個人的名聲。他們帶著紙張而非武器離開舊使館，心裡有個令人不安的結論：城市的警報可能準確預測了波動本身，卻在指引人們走哪條路時犯下致命錯誤。
