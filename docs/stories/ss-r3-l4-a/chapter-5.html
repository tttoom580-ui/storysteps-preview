廣場上的聽證會

The Hearing in the Square

The first public hearing took place in the central square, where a temporary reception desk stood beneath paper decoration left from a spring festival. The Civic Safety Agency invited residents to discuss the outcome of recent evacuations, but the invitation allowed each speaker only one minute. Qiao's supporters arrived early. Some wore shirts showing a legendary witch and wizard, old figures from stories about warriors who protected the city alone. Zhiwei was not frightened by legends. The peaceful scenery of the square did not calm her. What could horrify her was a notice promising to punish anyone who questioned the official map during an emergency.

第一次公開聽證會在中央廣場舉行。臨時接待桌上方，還掛著春季節慶留下的紙製裝飾。市民安全機構邀請居民討論最近幾次疏散的結果，卻只給每位發言者一分鐘。喬的支持者很早就到了，有些人穿著印有傳說女巫和巫師的上衣，那是描述武者獨自守護城市的古老故事。知微不害怕傳說。廣場平靜的風景也無法使她安心。真正可能令她驚恐的，是公告上威脅要處罰緊急情況下質疑官方地圖的任何人。

Luo An asked the crowd to adopt a simple rule for the meeting: no interruptions while a witness spoke. A retired shopkeeper described the behavior of officers who had blocked his street before the surge arrived. An influential merchant replied that a strong central command was fundamental to survival. Both arguments had merit. Zhiwei did not want frightened citizens to take risks merely to prove that officials could be wrong. But she also knew that blind interaction with a faulty system might carry dangers of its own. She asked the moderator whether witnesses would be allowed to show documents.

羅安請群眾接受一項簡單的會議規則：證人發言時不得打斷。一名退休店主描述警員在回響潮抵達前封鎖他那條街的行為。一名有影響力的商人回答，強而有力的中央指揮是生存的根本。雙方論點都有道理。知微不希望驚慌的市民為了證明官員可能犯錯而冒險，但她也知道，盲目配合有缺陷的系統同樣可能造成危害。她詢問主持人，證人是否可以展示文件。

The moderator allowed one photograph. Zhiwei held up the old timetable and the newer warning map. She explained that they used the same inspection code but gave opposite instructions for the north road. A man shouted that the discrepancy was primitive evidence, hardly worthy of a modern hearing. Another accused her of behaving like a robber who stole authority from trained fighters. The words were shameful, but Zhiwei did not answer the insult. “A piece of paper is not proof by itself,” she said. “That is why we have brought its source, a witness, and a method you can repeat.”

主持人允許展示一張照片。知微舉起舊時刻表與新警報地圖，解釋兩者使用相同檢驗編碼，對北方道路卻給出相反指示。一名男子喊道，這種證據太原始，根本不配出現在現代聽證會上。另一人指控她像個強盜，想偷走訓練有素武者的權威。這些話很可恥，但知微沒有回罵。「單張紙本身不能算證據，」她說，「所以我們帶來了來源、證人，以及任何人都能重做的檢驗方法。」

Taro demonstrated the correction records using two ordinary clocks. He showed how changing a timestamp could make one valid observation appear too late to count. The crowd grew quiet. Banu then spoke about the storage boxes removed without an initial inspection. She did not accuse the officers of theft; she asked for the box numbers and destinations. A council representative promised to send the question to the city congress, though she made no guarantee of a quick answer. Zhiwei thanked her and noted the promise in writing. A public record mattered even when the answer was uncertain.

太郎用兩個普通時鐘展示校正紀錄。他說明只要更改時間戳記，就能讓原本有效的一次觀測看起來超過期限。群眾安靜下來。接著巴努說明，儲物箱在沒有初步檢查的情況下被搬走。她沒有指控警員偷竊，只要求公開箱號與去向。一名議會代表承諾把問題送交市議會，但沒有保證很快答覆。知微向她致謝，並把承諾寫下來。即使答案尚不確定，公開紀錄仍然重要。

Qiao approached the microphone. “These people would discourage every officer who acts in a crisis,” he said. “They speak of rules while the city faces terror.” His speech stirred fear. Zhiwei could understand that fear; she had felt it at the opera house. What she could not accept was the claim that fear canceled one's conscience. She asked Qiao to state whether Meridian Data had received permission to edit the timer network. He did not deny it. Instead, he replied that the private company had worked under temporary authority. That was the first public admission of its role.

喬走向麥克風。「這些人會讓每個在危機中行動的官員失去勇氣，」他說，「城市面臨恐懼，他們還只談規則。」他的話激起了群眾的害怕。知微理解那份恐懼，因為她自己在歌劇院也曾經害怕。但她無法接受恐懼就能讓人拋棄良知的說法。她請喬說明，經線數據是否得到修改計時網路的許可。喬沒有否認，只回答私人公司是在臨時權限下工作。這是他第一次公開承認該公司的角色。

Before the hearing ended, the moderator called for written proposals. One group wanted every warning removed until the investigation finished. Another wanted to grant Qiao complete control. Zhiwei opposed both. She proposed a temporary double-check system: fighters would still report waves, but two independent recorders would confirm the time and keep the results public. No one could change an evacuation path without publishing the reason. It would cost staff hours, and some warnings might arrive more slowly. She said those limits aloud instead of hiding them. Public safety required honest choices, not perfect promises.

聽證會結束前，主持人請眾人提交書面提案。一組人希望調查結束前取消所有警報，另一組則想給喬完全的控制權。知微兩者都反對。她提出臨時雙重核對制度：武者仍負責回報波動，但由兩名獨立紀錄員核實時間並公開結果。任何人若要改變疏散路線，都必須公布理由。這會花費人力，有些警報可能變慢。她把限制明白說出來，不加掩飾。公共安全需要誠實的選擇，而不是完美的承諾。

The council accepted the proposal as a five-day trial. It was a small victory, not a final judgment. Luo An would train volunteers to read observation boards, Banu would keep a public copy of the records, and Taro would teach the clock test. Zhiwei looked across the crowded square and remembered the city before the first surge. She had once wanted only to finish her quiet map work. Now she would have to cherish that ordinary skill more fiercely than ever. Someone had used the city's faith in strength to silence questions. She intended to make questions useful again.

議會接受了提案，先試行五天。這是小小的勝利，還不是最終裁決。羅安將訓練志工閱讀觀測板，巴努負責保存公開紀錄副本，太郎則教授時鐘檢驗法。知微望著擁擠的廣場，想起第一次回響潮之前的城市。她原本只想安靜做完地圖工作；現在，她卻得比以往更珍惜那項普通技能。有人利用城市對力量的信仰，讓人們不敢提問。她決心讓問題重新變得有用。
