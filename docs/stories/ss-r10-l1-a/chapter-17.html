一次問一個問題

One Question at a Time

As the trial went on, the center room felt busy. Some people wanted more pictures, while others asked for more time to talk. Nia had to decide what was enough for one lesson. She asked each person to write one question on a card. A driver who had come after a long shift asked whether city air could hide stars. A married couple asked if the moon changed shape because of clouds. Finn wanted to repeat the lamp test. Tomas asked for a new raised map. Nia read the cards and saw that a single hour would not answer them all. She needed to put the questions in a sensible order.

隨著試辦期推進，中心教室變得忙碌。有些人想看更多圖片，有些人想花更多時間討論。妮雅必須決定一堂課能教多少才適當。她請每個人在卡片上寫一個問題。一位剛下長班的司機問，城市的空氣是否會讓星星看不見。一對已婚夫妻問，月亮變形是不是因為雲。芬恩想再做一次燈的實驗，托馬斯則希望有新觸摸地圖。妮雅讀著卡片，發現一小時不可能回答全部。她需要安排合理順序。

Ada made three piles. The first held questions they could test in the room. The second held questions for a clear night outside. The third held questions that needed a book or an expert. "Here is an example," she said, holding up the moon card. "We can use the lamp and ball now." She put the card on the first pile. The driver agreed to wait for a later lesson about air and city lights. Nia did not call one question more important than another. She simply explained what was possible with the time, tools, and people they had. The group seemed calmer once they could see the order.

艾達把卡片分成三堆。第一堆是在教室就能測試的問題，第二堆需要晴朗夜晚的戶外觀察，第三堆則得查書或請教專家。「這是一個例子，」她拿起月亮卡說，「我們現在就能用燈和球來試。」她把卡片放進第一堆。司機同意等後面的課再談空氣與城市燈光。妮雅沒有說哪個問題比較重要，只是解釋以現在的時間、工具和人力，哪些事情做得到。順序清楚後，大家也比較安心。

A strong smell of popcorn came from the hall. The center was preparing a film night in another room. Finn wanted to go there at once, but Nia reminded him that the star class would end soon. "You can make your own choice after the lesson," she said. A little girl asked whether music could help her remember the order of the planets. Ada said a song might help some people but not everyone. Tomas preferred words he could read slowly with his fingers. Nia let students choose a song, a short sentence, or a simple line of raised dots. Different ways of learning could belong in the same room.

走廊傳來濃濃的爆米花味。中心另一個房間正準備電影之夜。芬恩想立刻去，但妮雅提醒他星星課很快就結束。「下課後你可以自己選，」她說。一個小女孩問，音樂能不能幫她記住行星的順序。艾達說，歌曲對某些人有用，卻不一定適合所有人。托馬斯比較喜歡能用手指慢慢讀的文字。妮雅讓學生自己選歌曲、短句，或簡單凸點線。同一間教室裡可以容納不同的學習方法。

During the break, a visitor spoke sharply to Bo for asking the same question again. Bo's face turned red. Nia stepped in. "There is a reason we repeat things," she said. "People learn at different speeds." She asked Bo if he wanted to hear the answer again or test it himself. He chose the lamp. A strong teacher, Nia was learning, did not need to talk the loudest. She needed to make room for quiet answers too. The visitor listened and later said he had been impatient. Bo nodded, then held the model light for a younger child without being asked.

休息時，一位訪客因波又問同一個問題而語氣尖銳。波的臉紅了。妮雅走過來說：「我們重複事情是有原因的，每個人學習速度不同。」她問波想再聽一次答案，還是自己做實驗。波選了小燈。妮雅逐漸明白，好的老師不需要聲音最大，而是要替安靜的回答留空間。那位訪客聽了，後來承認自己太沒耐心。波點點頭，接著主動替年紀更小的孩子拿模型燈。

As the students sorted their cards, Tomas used one finger to find the first raised dot on his map. He said the paper had started to wear out and asked whether another copy was possible. Ada noted the request. From the kitchen came the smell of cooked vegetable soup. Outside, a worker saw smoke rising from a legal cooking area and checked that the air was moving away from the doorway. Nia kept the windows closed during the short test. She used the moment to explain that people could notice the same place in different ways. Some could see the smoke, some could smell it, and some heard the door close. Listening to more than one person often gave the group a clearer picture of what was happening.

學生整理卡片時，托馬斯用一根手指找到地圖上的第一個凸點。他說紙張已經開始磨損，問能不能再做一張。艾達記下需求。廚房傳來蔬菜湯煮好的香味。戶外一名工作人員看見合法烹煮區升起煙，確認煙霧往遠離門口的方向飄。妮雅在短暫測試時保持窗戶關閉。她趁機說明，人可以用不同方式注意同一個地方。有些人看得到煙，有些人聞得到，有些人聽見門關上。多聽幾個人的觀察，常能讓小組更清楚了解發生什麼事。

At home, Nia copied the cards into her lesson book. She left blank spaces beside questions that were not yet answered. She did not want the book to look more complete than the class really was. Ada sent her a note: You do not have to answer everything to have taught something. Nia read it twice. She had spent much of the week afraid of disappointing people. Now she saw that an honest "not yet" could be useful. On Saturday, the group had learned to organize questions, listen to each other, and choose a next step. That work was real, even without a dramatic view of the night sky.

回家後，妮雅把卡片內容抄進課堂簿，在尚未回答的問題旁留白。她不希望簿子看起來比實際課程更完整。艾達傳訊息給她：妳不必回答所有問題，才算真正教會了人。妮雅讀了兩遍。這一週她常擔心讓人失望，現在才看見誠實的「還不知道」也有價值。星期六，全班學會整理問題、互相傾聽、選擇下一步。即使沒有壯觀的夜空，那些也都是真實的學習。
