紙箱上的名字

A Name on the Box

Owen's shop smelled of metal and cold tea. Every tool still lay in its proper place. Only the wooden board from the bottom of the box was gone, leaving a pale square in the dust. Owen knelt beside it without speaking. I asked whether anyone else had a key. "My landlord, my sister, and the man who checks the building," he said. Adrian offered to call his security team. Owen shook his head. "We call the police first. This is my shop." Adrian put his phone away and waited while Owen made the call.

歐文店裡有金屬和冷茶的氣味，所有工具仍在原位。只有箱底木板消失了，在灰塵裡留下一塊淡色方形。他一聲不吭地跪在旁邊。我問誰還有鑰匙。「房東、我妹妹，還有檢查建物的人。」他說。沈予川提議打給公司保全團隊，歐文搖頭。「先報警。這是我的店。」沈予川收起手機，等歐文親自撥號。

The officer asked us not to touch anything else. While we waited outside, Mother brought sandwiches wrapped in paper. She passed one to Adrian without asking whether he wanted it. For a moment, we were four tired people eating on a low wall, not a rich man and the families his company could destroy. Owen remembered something when he opened the sandwich. "Dad put names on everything," he said. "Even boxes nobody would ever see. He said people lose things faster when they believe the things belong to nobody."

警員請我們別再碰其他東西。在外面等候時，媽媽拿來用紙包的三明治，沒問沈予川要不要，也遞了一份給他。有一瞬間，我們只是四個坐在矮牆上吃東西的疲倦人，不是一個富人，和可能被他公司摧毀的家庭。歐文拆開包裝，想起一件事。「爸爸什麼都寫名字，連根本不會有人看的箱子也寫。他說，當人們以為東西不屬於任何人時，東西就更容易不見。」

I looked at the photograph again. Owen's father held a box behind Daniel Lin's shoulder. On its side was a dark square that I had taken for a shadow. I made the picture larger on my phone. The square was writing: RETURN TO BLUE ROOM. "Does your box have those words?" I asked. Owen shook his head. His box said SPARE PARTS. It was not the same box. Someone had stolen the board because they thought it might hide papers, but perhaps they had been looking in the wrong place.

我又看了照片。爸爸林立安肩後，歐文的父親抱著一個箱子。側面有塊我一直以為是影子的黑方形。我在手機上放大，才發現那是字：送回藍房。「你的箱子也寫這些字嗎？」我問。歐文搖頭，他的箱子寫的是「備用零件」。那不是同一個箱子。有人偷走木板，也許以為裡頭能藏文件，但他們或許從一開始就找錯地方。

Nina met us at the factory store room the next morning. She did not seem surprised by the photograph. "I worked in accounts there," she said. "I should have told you yesterday." I asked why she had not. "Because I kept telling myself that being fair now could make up for being quiet then." Her voice was steady, but she held her glasses too tightly. Adrian asked her to stay outside while we looked through the room. She nodded. Whatever she knew, she could not be the only person deciding which papers we were allowed to see.

隔天早上，妮娜在工廠倉庫與我們會合。她看見照片並不意外。「我以前在這裡做會計，昨天就該告訴妳了。」我問她為什麼沒說。「因為我一直說服自己，現在公平一點，就能彌補當年的沉默。」她聲音平穩，卻把眼鏡握得太緊。沈予川請她先在門外等，讓我們自己查看。她點頭。無論她知道什麼，都不能由她獨自決定哪些文件准許我們看。

The blue room was no longer blue. Fresh white paint covered its walls, and new chairs stood where shelves had been. A cleaner told us the old boxes had gone upstairs during the hotel work. We found them behind rolls of carpet. Owen read every label aloud. Most held cloth samples, old cups, or broken lamps. Then he stopped at a small box tied with green string. On its side, in the same careful hand as the photograph, were two names: Daniel Lin and Helen Wu. I had never heard of Helen Wu.

藍房已經不藍了。牆面刷上新白漆，原本架子的位置擺著新椅子。清潔人員說，整修飯店時，舊箱子都搬到樓上。我們在成卷地毯後找到它們。歐文逐一念標籤，大多是布樣、舊杯子或壞燈。接著，他停在一個綁綠繩的小箱前。側面是和照片上一樣工整的筆跡，寫著兩個名字：林立安，以及吳海倫。我從沒聽過吳海倫。

Inside lay one bag, three folded shirts, and a birthday card for a girl turning seven. The card's date was a week before Father's dismissal. Beneath it was a note: Keep this safe until Helen returns with the book. Owen let out a slow breath. Nina stood in the doorway, her face white. "She never came back," she said. I looked at the child's card again. Until that morning, I thought my father had left one daughter waiting for answers. Perhaps another daughter had been waiting just as long.

箱裡有一只袋子、三件摺好的襯衫，還有一張給即將滿七歲女孩的生日卡。日期在爸爸遭解僱前一週。卡片下有便條：海倫帶本子回來以前，請妥善保存。歐文緩緩吐了口氣。妮娜站在門口，臉色蒼白。「她再也沒回來。」她說。我重新看著小女孩的卡片。直到今早，我都以為爸爸只留下了一個等待答案的女兒。也許另一個女兒，已經等了一樣久。
