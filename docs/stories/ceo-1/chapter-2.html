A Chair at His Table

Adrian let go of the bag first. I placed it on Nina's desk, away from my wet coat. "This is five years old," I said. "Why is it in a box for a new hotel?" Nina turned the box toward the light. A white label covered an older yellow one. She lifted one corner carefully. Beneath it, in my father's small writing, were three numbers and a date. Adrian reached for his phone. "Stop," I said. "Before you call anyone, tell me who you plan to call."

沈予川先鬆開袋子。我把它放在妮娜的桌上，避開自己的濕外套。「這是五年前的東西，」我說，「為什麼會放在新飯店的箱子裡？」妮娜把箱子轉向光線，白色標籤蓋著一張更舊的黃標籤。她小心掀起一角，底下是爸爸細小的字跡：三個數字和一個日期。沈予川伸手拿手機。「等等，」我說，「打給任何人以前，先告訴我你打算找誰。」

He looked surprised, then put the phone down. "The man in charge of our old supply room." "Will he know why you are asking?" "He will if I ask directly." Nina moved another chair to her desk. "Then we begin with what we already have." She gave me paper and asked me to draw the blue mark from memory. My father drew a small wave, then a line under it. The mark inside the bag had the wave, but the line was above it. My stomach felt cold.

他露出意外的神情，接著放下手機。「管理舊供應倉的人。」「他會知道你為什麼查問嗎？」「如果直接問，他就會知道。」妮娜搬了一張椅子到桌邊。「那我們先從手上的東西開始。」她給我一張紙，要我憑記憶畫出藍記號。爸爸會先畫一道小波浪，再在下方畫線；袋子裡也有波浪，那條線卻在上面。我的胃一陣發冷。

"He used the upper line for things he had not finished checking," I said. "The lower line meant he had checked them." Adrian sat opposite me. For the first time, we were at the same small table instead of on different sides of his large desk. "Are you certain?" he asked. I wanted to say yes immediately. Instead, I remembered the way my father made me check a measurement twice. "I can show you his old work," I said. "I cannot prove this mark is his just by looking at it."

「上面的線，代表還沒檢查完，」我說，「下面的線才表示已經查過。」沈予川在我對面坐下。我們第一次坐在同一張小桌旁，而不是分據他的大辦公桌兩側。「妳確定？」他問。我原本想立刻點頭，卻記起爸爸總要我把尺寸核對兩次。「我可以拿他以前的作品給你看，」我說，「但不能光看一眼，就證明這一定是他的記號。」

Nina wrote down my exact words. She did not write that my father was innocent. Somehow that made me trust her more. Adrian asked whether he could photograph the bag. I agreed if I received the photographs too. We took pictures of the front, the back, the blue mark, and both labels. Nina added a ruler beside the mark. Then she wrote the time on a clean sheet and placed it beside the bag for one last picture. The simple steps slowed my heart. We were making a record, not making a wish.

妮娜一字不漏地記下我的話，沒有寫成「爸爸是無辜的」。不知為什麼，這反而讓我更信任她。沈予川問能否替袋子拍照；我說可以，但照片也得給我。我們拍了正面、背面、藍記號和兩張標籤。妮娜在記號旁放了一把尺，最後又在乾淨紙上寫下時間，把紙放在袋邊多拍一張。這些簡單步驟讓我的心跳慢下來。我們在留下紀錄，不是在許願。

My phone rang. Mother wanted to know whether we had the hotel order. I looked at the unsigned contract and told her the truth: not yet. She went quiet, then said someone from the street office had visited. Our rent would rise next month unless we joined Bright Harbor's new shop plan. Adrian heard only my side of the call, but his face changed when I repeated the amount. "That is not the price I approved," he said. I held up one hand. Mother was still speaking. She deserved my attention before he received my questions.

手機響了。媽媽問飯店訂單談成了沒有。我看著還沒簽字的合約，照實說：還沒有。她沉默片刻，才說街區辦公室有人來過；除非加入晴港的新商店計畫，否則下個月就要漲租。沈予川只聽得到我這頭的聲音，但我複述金額時，他臉色變了。「我核准的不是這個價錢。」我抬手制止他。媽媽還在說話；我應該先專心聽她說完，再讓他回答我的問題。

When we ended the call, he offered to have a car take me home. I said I could take the bus. "Then may I come to the store tomorrow?" he asked. "During business hours," I answered. "You will wait your turn like any other customer." His mouth moved a little, almost a smile. Nina put the old bag into a clear cover and asked both of us to sign across its edge. Below my father's strange blue mark, two fresh names lay side by side. Neither one was large enough to hide the other.

通話結束後，他提議派車送我回家。我說搭公車就好。「那我明天可以到店裡嗎？」他問。「營業時間來，」我回答，「跟其他客人一樣排隊。」他的嘴角動了一下，幾乎像在笑。妮娜把舊袋子裝進透明保護套，請我們兩人在封口邊緣簽名。爸爸那個古怪的藍記號下方，兩個新寫的名字並列著。沒有哪一個大到能遮住另一個。
