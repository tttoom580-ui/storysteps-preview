The Price of a Favor

Mother wanted to explain everything at once. The words came too quickly: the hospital, the factory, a telephone call Father would not discuss. I made tea and asked her to begin with one thing she had seen herself. She grew quiet. Then she told me about the night Helen visited with a white cover on her hand. Father had placed two bags on the table. One smelled of oil. The other smelled of nothing at all. Helen said they had come from boxes carrying the same number. Father had taken photographs before he made a call.

媽媽想一次解釋所有事，話湧得太快：醫院、工廠、爸爸不肯談的一通電話。我泡了茶，請她先講一件親眼見過的事。她安靜下來，才說起海倫手上包著白布來訪的那晚。爸爸把兩只袋子放在桌上，一只有油味，另一只完全沒味道。海倫說，它們出自標著同一編號的箱子。爸爸先拍照，才打了電話。

I wrote down the difference between what Mother remembered and what she guessed. She watched me draw two lines down the page. "Your father did that too," she said. For years, I had thought loving him meant never questioning the story he left behind. Now I wondered whether I had been listening to his story at all. He had not told me he was innocent. Other people had told me he was guilty. Between those two silences, a great deal of room remained. I closed the book before hope could fill the empty space with answers.

我把媽媽記得的，和她猜的，分開記下。她看我在紙上畫兩條直線。「妳爸爸也這樣。」多年來，我以為愛他就代表不能質疑他留下的故事。現在我懷疑，自己究竟有沒有真正聽過他的故事。他沒告訴我自己無辜；是其他人告訴我他有罪。兩種沉默之間，還有很大的空間。在希望擅自替空白填上答案以前，我先闔起本子。

Victor arrived before Nina. He came through the shop door carrying flowers for Mother and a new copy of our rent notice. The increase had been stopped. "I thought you would like the good news in person," he said. Mother reached for the flowers, then noticed that my hands had not moved. Victor laid them on the counter. "Your father was proud," he told me. "Pride can be expensive. I would hate to watch his daughter pay the same price." I asked whether he had come to threaten us. "To help," he said softly.

維克多比妮娜先到，拿著送給媽媽的花和新版租金通知。調漲已取消。「這個好消息，我想親自送來。」媽媽伸手接花，卻注意到我一動不動，維克多便把花放在櫃檯。「妳父親很驕傲，」他告訴我，「驕傲有時很昂貴。我不願看他女兒付同樣的代價。」我問他是不是來威脅我們。「來幫忙。」他輕聲回答。

His help had a price. Bright Harbor would give our store a five-year order if I signed a letter saying the old samples were private family property and had no connection with the new hotel. We would not have to lie about the bag, he said. We would simply agree not to discuss it. I imagined Mother sleeping without worrying about rent. I imagined buying a machine that did not hurt her hands. Then I read the letter again. The space for my name was wide and empty. I pushed it back without touching the pen.

他的幫忙有價碼。只要我簽信，承認舊樣品屬於私人家族財物、與新飯店無關，晴港就給店裡五年訂單。他說，我們不必對袋子撒謊，只是同意不談它。我想像媽媽不再為房租失眠，想像買一台不會讓她雙手發疼的機器。然後，我再讀一遍那封信。留給我簽名的位置又寬又空。我沒碰筆，把信推回去。

"You are refusing a very generous offer," Victor said. "I am refusing a sentence I cannot check." Mother put the flowers back in his arms. Her voice shook, but she spoke clearly. "Please take these with you." When he left, she sat down behind the counter. "I wanted to say yes," she admitted. I knelt beside her. "So did I." The truth did not make either of us smaller. It simply meant we understood what refusing would cost. We held each other's hands until the shop bell rang again.

「妳拒絕的是很慷慨的提議。」維克多說。「我拒絕的是一句我無法核對的話。」媽媽把花放回他懷裡。她聲音發抖，話卻清楚：「請把這些一起帶走。」他走後，媽媽坐在櫃檯後。「我其實想答應。」她承認。我跪到她身旁。「我也是。」這個事實沒有讓我們任何一個人矮一截，只表示我們明白拒絕要付什麼代價。我們握著彼此的手，直到門鈴再響。

Nina stood at the door with a brown paper folder. I told her what Victor had offered. She closed her eyes for a moment. "Five years ago," she said, "he offered me a better job if I helped finish a report quickly." She opened the folder. Inside were copies of delivery dates, each with her name beside it. "These will not clear your father," she said. "But they will show why I should not have been allowed to say that his work was complete." On the last sheet, a different hand had added tomorrow's hotel opening date.

妮娜站在門前，拿著牛皮紙資料夾。我告訴她維克多的提議。她閉眼片刻。「五年前，」她說，「他也答應給我更好的職位，只要我幫忙快點完成報告。」資料夾裡是送貨日期的副本，每筆旁邊都有她的名字。「它們不能證明妳父親無辜，卻能證明，當時不該讓我宣稱他的工作已經完成。」最後一張紙上，有別人的筆跡，加上了明天的飯店開幕日期。
