她劃掉的那一條

The Line I Crossed Out

The man behind the long desk did not look up when I came in. He was talking on the phone, wearing a dark coat that probably cost more than my mother's whole store. I put my wet bag on the floor. Water ran onto his clean shoes. He looked down at them, then at me. "We agreed to meet at nine," he said. "Your lift stopped at eight fifty," I said. "I took twenty floors of stairs. You are welcome."

長桌後的男人在我進門時沒有抬頭。他穿著可能比媽媽整間店還貴的深色外套講電話。我把濕袋子放在地上，水流到他乾淨的鞋面。他低頭看鞋，再看我。「我們約好九點見面。」「你的電梯八點五十就停了，」我說，「我爬了二十層樓。不客氣。」

His name was Adrian Shen. At thirty-one, he ran Bright Harbor, a company that owned shops all over the city. I was Maya Lin, twenty-six, a designer with one broken computer and a mother who believed every rich customer would pay on time. Last month, Bright Harbor bought our street. Today, Adrian wanted our small store to make bags for his new hotel. If I said no, we might lose the only large order we had.

他叫沈予川，三十一歲，管理著全城都有店面的晴港集團。我叫林蔓，二十六歲，是個只有一台壞電腦的設計師，還有一位相信有錢客人都會準時付款的母親。上個月，晴港買下我們那條街。今天，沈予川要我們的小店替新飯店做袋子。如果拒絕，我們可能會失去唯一的大訂單。

He pushed a contract across the desk. "Read it. My next meeting starts in ten minutes." I opened it. The first pages were full of numbers. The last page had one short line: The company may use all designs forever. I read it twice. Then I took a red pen from my bag and crossed it out. For the first time, Adrian put his phone down. "Nobody changes that contract," he said. "Then today is a new day."

他把合約推過桌面。「讀吧。我十分鐘後有下一場會議。」前幾頁滿是數字。最後一頁有句短話：公司得永久使用所有設計。我讀了兩遍，從包包拿出紅筆劃掉。沈予川第一次放下手機。「沒有人改那份合約。」「那今天是新的一天。」

"You need this order," he said. I hated that he knew. "Yes. You need a design people will remember. We can both need something without one of us owning the other." Beyond his window, rain blurred the city into pale shapes; a dry patch of glass still showed the narrow street below. My hands were still cold from the rain. I held the pen harder so he would not see them shake. He rang a bell. I thought he was calling someone to take me out.

「妳需要這張訂單。」我討厭他知道。「對，你需要讓人記住的設計。我們可以各有所需，不必有一方擁有另一方。」窗外，雨水把城市暈成一片淡淡的影子；玻璃上只有一小塊乾燥處，仍看得見下方狹窄的街道。我的手還被雨凍得冰冷，我握緊筆，不讓他看見發抖。他按鈴，我以為他叫人來帶我出去。

A woman with silver glasses came in. "Nina, can we change this line?" he asked. She read my red mark and smiled. "We should have changed it years ago." I almost laughed. Adrian did not. Nina sat beside me, not behind him, and asked what I wanted instead. I said the store would keep its designs. Bright Harbor could use one design for one year. If they wanted more, they would have to ask again.

戴銀框眼鏡的女人走進來。「妮娜，這條能改嗎？」她讀了我的紅筆記號，笑了。「我們幾年前就該改了。」我差點笑出來，沈予川沒有。妮娜坐在我旁邊，而不是他身後，問我想怎麼改。我說設計屬於小店，晴港可以使用其中一款一年；若要更多，就得再問。

"And your name?" Adrian asked. "It goes on the bag," I said. "The same size as yours." Nina stopped writing. The room grew quiet enough for me to hear water falling from my coat. Then Adrian pulled a sample bag from a box. There was no maker's name on it. He turned it over slowly, as if he had never noticed the empty space. "Put it in," he told Nina. "The same size."

「還有妳的名字？」他問。「要放在袋子上，大小跟你的一樣。」妮娜停筆。房間安靜得能聽見外套滴水。沈予川從箱子取出樣品袋，上面沒有製作者的名字。他慢慢翻過來，好像從沒注意到那塊空白。「加上去，」他告訴妮娜，「一樣大。」

I should have felt pleased. Instead, I saw the little blue mark inside the bag. My father used to draw that mark beside every order he checked. He had died five years ago, after losing his job at a company that supplied Bright Harbor. They said he had sold bad cloth and hidden the truth. I touched the blue mark. Adrian saw my face change. He reached for the bag, but I did not let go.

我本來該高興，卻看見袋子裡的小藍色記號。爸爸以前會在每張檢查過的訂單旁畫那個記號。五年前，他失去晴港供應商的工作後去世。大家說他賣了壞布，還隱瞞真相。我摸著藍記號。沈予川看見我臉色變了，伸手拿袋子，我卻沒有放開。

"Where did you get this?" I asked. He looked at the open contract, then at my name on the first page. Something in his face closed. "Your father was Daniel Lin?" Outside the office, someone called his next meeting. Neither of us moved. I had climbed twenty floors to save a store. Now the richest man I had ever met was holding the other end of my father's last mistake. Or the first proof that it had never been his.

「這是哪裡來的？」我問。他看著攤開的合約，再看首頁的名字，表情忽然收起來。「妳父親是林立安？」門外有人提醒下一場會議，我們都沒動。我爬二十層樓是為了救店，如今眼前最有錢的男人握著父親最後一個錯誤的另一端——或者，證明那從來不是他的錯誤的第一項證據。
