A Visit After Closing

Nina came after closing with no work bag, only a small white box. I thought it might be another old sample. It contained cakes from the bakery across the street. "Your mother likes these," she said. Mother did not take the box. For a long moment, the three of us stood among cloth and unfinished bags, waiting for the person who had come to explain herself to begin. Nina set the cakes on an empty chair. "Helen called me too," she said. "She asked whether you could trust the people now looking into the report."

妮娜在打烊後來了，沒拿工作袋，只帶一個白盒。我以為又是舊樣品，裡頭卻裝著對街麵包店的蛋糕。「妳媽媽喜歡這個。」她說。媽媽沒接。好一陣子，我們三人站在布料和未完成的袋子之間，等那個來解釋自己的人開口。妮娜把蛋糕放在空椅上。「海倫也打給我了。她問，現在查報告的人值不值得妳信任。」

"And what did you tell her?" I asked. "That she should decide for herself." Nina took off her glasses. "I knew the report was incomplete five years ago. I did not know how much was wrong with it. When I asked for the missing pages, Victor told me your father had left without handing them in. I let myself believe that because it allowed me to keep my job." Mother finally spoke. "He came home every evening for a week carrying that empty work bag. You told me there was nothing you could do."

「那妳怎麼說？」「我說，她應該親自判斷。」妮娜摘下眼鏡。「五年前，我知道報告不完整，卻不知道錯得多嚴重。我索取缺頁時，維克多說妳父親離開時沒交。我讓自己相信，因為那樣就保得住工作。」媽媽終於開口。「那整整一週，他每天晚上都提著空工作袋回家。妳告訴我，妳什麼都做不了。」

"There were things I could have done," Nina said. She did not add a reason or ask us to forgive her. That made the room harder to stay in, not easier. I asked why she had stayed at Bright Harbor. She said she wanted to change how the company wrote its contracts. She had changed some. She had also accepted every year of silence as the price of being useful. "I do not know whether staying helped more people than leaving would have," she admitted. "I know it helped me tell myself I was not afraid."

「我有能做的事。」妮娜沒補理由，也沒求原諒。這讓人更難待在屋裡，而不是更輕鬆。我問她為何留下。她想改變公司的合約，也確實改過一些；同時，她接受一年又一年的沉默，當成讓自己有用的代價。「我不知道留下來是不是比離開幫了更多人，」她承認，「但我知道，這樣能讓我告訴自己，我不是因為害怕。」

I wanted to ask a question that would make her hurt the way Mother had hurt. Instead, I asked what she could give Rose now. Nina opened a folder she had left outside the door. Inside were copies of the questions she had sent to Victor. He had replied to none of them directly. Each time, another person had answered in his name. The last message told her not to contact Helen because she was an unreliable worker. "I believed a man who would not answer me over a woman who was asking for help," Nina said.

我想問一句能讓她像媽媽一樣痛的話，最後卻問：現在能給蘿絲什麼？妮娜拿來先前留在門外的資料夾，裡頭是她寄給維克多的問題副本。他一次也沒直接回答，每回都由其他人以他的名義回覆。最後一封要她別聯絡海倫，因為海倫是個不可靠的工人。「我選擇相信一個不肯回答我的男人，卻不相信一個向我求助的女人。」妮娜說。

Mother moved the cakes onto the counter and sat in the chair. She did not open the box. "You can help us check the facts," she told Nina. "That does not mean everything between us is settled." Nina nodded. I called Rose, who asked permission to copy the messages with their dates still attached. When she arrived, we left Mother to close the shop at her own pace. I followed her into the back room. She folded a piece of blue cloth twice, unfolded it, then began again. I stood beside her until her hands became still.

媽媽把蛋糕移到櫃檯，坐上椅子，沒打開盒子。「妳可以協助我們核對事實，」她告訴妮娜，「但不代表我們之間都沒事了。」妮娜點頭。我打給蘿絲，她要求在保留日期的情況下複製訊息，並先徵求同意。她來了以後，我們讓媽媽照自己的步調關店。我跟到後房。她把一塊藍布摺兩次，攤開，又重摺。我站在旁邊，直到她的手不再動。

At eleven, a second call came from the hidden number. This time the younger woman spoke first. "My name is June Wu," she said. "My mother will meet you tomorrow. A public place, no company people." I agreed before looking at Adrian, who had arrived to collect Rose. He heard enough to understand. "You do not have to bring me," he said. June asked one more question: "Is your mother still making those little red bags?" I looked toward the back room. For the first time, the old story contained something somebody had wanted to keep.

十一點，隱藏號碼第二次來電，這次年輕女人先說話。「我叫吳小君。媽媽明天願意見妳。公共場所，不要公司的人。」我先答應，才看向剛來接蘿絲的沈予川。他聽到足夠明白的內容。「妳不必帶我去。」他說。小君又問：「妳媽媽還在做那種小紅袋嗎？」我望向後房。第一次，舊故事裡出現了一件有人想留下的東西。
