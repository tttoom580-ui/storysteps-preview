照片裡的女人

The Woman in the Photo

The woman in the photograph was Helen, but the girl beside her was not me. Mother found the original picture at the back of an old cooking book. The small child had a mark above her left ear, hidden in the copy Nina sent. "Her name was June," Mother said. "She stayed here when Helen had to work late. You gave her your red shoes because they had become too small." I remembered the shoes. I did not remember the child. That difference hurt more than I expected. Someone had belonged in our kitchen, and I had let her disappear from my own story.

照片裡的女人是海倫，女孩卻不是我。媽媽在一本舊食譜後面找到原照；小女孩左耳上方有個記號，在妮娜傳的副本裡看不清。「她叫小君，」媽媽說，「海倫加班時，她會留在這裡。妳把穿不下的紅鞋送給她。」我記得鞋，卻不記得孩子。這個落差比想像中更痛。曾有人是我們廚房裡的一分子，我卻讓她從自己的故事消失了。

We had no current phone number. The one in Mother's address book belonged to a family who had never heard of Helen. Nina knew only an old street name outside the city. Owen offered to search public work records, but Rose warned us not to spread the photograph online. Helen might have reasons for staying away. I put the picture in a clear cover and kept it in my bag. Wanting an answer did not give me the right to turn somebody else's life into a public hunt. I wished Father had told us that before leaving us so many questions.

我們沒有她現在的號碼。媽媽通訊錄裡的電話，屬於一戶從沒聽過海倫的人家。妮娜只知道城外一條舊街名。歐文提議查公開工作紀錄，蘿絲卻提醒別把照片撒到網路上：海倫不出現，也許自有原因。我把照片裝進透明套，收進袋子。想要答案，不代表我有權把別人的人生變成公開搜尋。我真希望爸爸留下這麼多問題前，也曾告訴我們這一點。

Adrian drove us to the old street after work. Ben drove, and Adrian sat in the back beside me with a paper map across his knees. It looked strange to see him lose an argument to a road that had changed its name. At the address stood a small flower shop. Its owner remembered Helen as a woman who helped her mother after an illness. She had moved near a train station three years ago. The owner offered to pass along a letter. "I will not give you her address," she said. I liked her immediately.

下班後，沈予川陪我去舊街，班開車，他坐後座，在膝上攤紙地圖。一條改了名字的路，竟能讓他辯不過，看起來很新鮮。地址那裡是一間小花店。老闆記得海倫，她曾在母親生病後照顧她，三年前搬到車站附近。老闆願意轉交信件。「我不會把她的地址給你們。」她說。我立刻喜歡上她。

I wrote on a sheet from the shop's receipt book. My name, my number, and one sentence: I found the bag you left with my father. I read it three times before adding that she did not have to answer. Adrian waited outside in the cold rather than look over my shoulder. When I joined him, he offered half a warm bun from the baker next door. "They had only one left," he said. He had torn it unevenly, giving me the larger piece. For once, I did not measure whether a kindness might become a debt.

我拿花店收據本的紙寫信，留下姓名、電話和一句話：我找到妳留給我爸爸的袋子。讀了三遍，又補上：妳可以不回覆。沈予川寧可在寒冷的外面等，也沒站在我肩後偷看。我出去時，他遞給我隔壁麵包店買的半個熱麵包。「只剩一個。」他說。他撕得不均，把比較大的那半給我。這一次，我沒有衡量，這份好意會不會變成債。

On the way home, I asked why he had come himself. "You could have sent a person with better shoes and a better map." He watched the streetlights move across the window. "For years, I thought being useful meant asking somebody else to make a problem go away." He paused. "I am not very good at staying with one." I did not tell him he was good now. I simply let our shoulders touch as the car turned. Ben looked at us in the mirror, then returned his eyes carefully to the road.

回家路上，我問他為何親自來。「你可以派一個鞋子更好、地圖也更好的人。」他看著路燈掠過車窗。「很多年，我以為有用，就是找人讓問題消失。」他停了停。「我不太擅長留在問題旁邊。」我沒說他現在已經做得很好。車子轉彎時，我只是讓肩膀碰著他的。班從後照鏡看了看，隨即小心把目光放回路上。

My phone rang as we reached Rain Street. No name appeared. I answered with the bun still warm in my hand. A woman asked me to say my father's full name. I did. She drew a breath that sounded almost like crying. "Tell your mother I am sorry," she said. "And do not bring that bag to the hotel." Before I could ask why, a younger voice spoke behind her: "Mum, let me talk to her." The call ended. On my screen, the number was hidden, but in the quiet car the warning remained very clear.

抵達雨街時，手機響了，沒有顯示姓名。我手裡還握著溫熱麵包，接起電話。女人要我說出爸爸全名。我照做，她吸了一口氣，幾乎像在哭。「替我向妳媽媽說對不起，」她說，「別把那個袋子帶到飯店。」我還沒問原因，她身後傳來更年輕的聲音：「媽，讓我跟她說。」電話掛斷。號碼隱藏著，但在安靜的車裡，那句警告仍十分清楚。
