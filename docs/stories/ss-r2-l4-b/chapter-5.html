Sixty-Three Names

1998，海禾戲院不打烊 · 第 5 / 25 章 · 3000–4000 · 英中對照

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

On the morning of the courtyard exhibition, rain threatened to abandon the town to another gray weekend. Yulan adjusted the tables and checked the small allowance set aside for umbrellas. Her anxiety grew when a volunteer arrived carrying an old canoe paddle as a decoration. The idea was funny but impractical. The only electrical device in the courtyard was a borrowed slide projector, safely placed beneath a roofed passage. Qin insisted on a distinct path for visitors and workers. The daughter of the mechanic enlarged the route map so even people at the gate could read it. When the wind changed and the clouds opened, the weather became more favorable. Yulan tried not to treat that as a sign that everything else would go well.

庭院展當天早上，雨勢像要讓小鎮陷入另一個灰色週末。雨蘭調整桌椅，確認預留給雨傘的小筆補貼。當志工拿著一支舊獨木舟槳當裝飾來時，她更焦慮了。這主意有趣，卻不實際。庭院裡唯一的電器是借來的幻燈機，安全放在有屋頂的走道下。秦姨要求訪客與工作人員有清楚分開的動線。機械師的女兒把路線圖放大，讓大門口的人也能看見。風向轉變、雲層裂開時，天氣變得有利。雨蘭努力不把好天氣誤當作其他事情也會順利的預兆。

The first visitors came for their own reasons. A retired teacher loved old photographs; a fisherman wanted to see his parents on film; two teenagers only wanted cheap noodles. One woman quoted an idiom about old trees having deep roots. Yulan smiled but answered that roots could also damage a wall. They were not building an idol for the past. She wanted an imaginative place where new stories could grow. A small boy asked why a film projector could not simply be carried outside. Qin explained the impact of wind and dust on fragile film. A portable slide machine was safe enough for copies, but the original reels had to remain indoors until the building was repaired.

第一批訪客各有目的。退休老師愛看舊照片；漁夫想在影片裡找父母；兩名少年只想買便宜的麵。有位婦女引用「老樹根深」的俗語。雨蘭笑著回答，樹根有時也會弄壞牆壁。她們不是在供奉過去的偶像，而是希望打造一個能長出新故事、富有想像力的地方。一個小男孩問，為何不乾脆把電影放映機搬出來。秦姨解釋風與灰塵會對脆弱底片造成影響。便攜式幻燈機放映複製照片尚可，原始膠卷卻必須留在室內，直到建築修好。

At noon, Xu Jian, the developer who proposed the parking lot, arrived with an objection. He said the exhibition was a disguised protest against his project. "It is a public information day," Yulan replied, showing him the approved courtyard notice. "You may speak here, but you cannot seize the microphone." Xu pointed toward the empty theater and talked about prosperity: more cars, more shops, more customers. Some neighbors nodded. An orchestra from the local middle school began playing in the square, forcing everyone to pause. Yulan recognized that Xu was not inventing the town's need for jobs. Her task was to offer an honest alternative rather than pretend the problem did not exist.

正午時，提出興建停車場的開發商許健帶著異議而來。他認為這場展覽是偽裝成活動的抗議。「這是公開說明日，」雨蘭拿出核准的庭院公告回答，「你可以在這裡發言，但不能搶走麥克風。」許健指著空蕩戲院，談起繁榮：更多車子、商店和顧客。有些鄰居點頭。鎮中學的管弦樂團恰好在廣場演奏，所有人不得不停下爭辯。雨蘭明白，許健並不是憑空捏造鎮上需要工作的事實。她必須提出誠實可行的替代方案，而不是假裝問題不存在。

An elderly visitor asked Yulan to read the names on a photograph. She stumbled over a rare syllable in one family's name and apologized. The woman pronounced it slowly, then explained that her parents had spoken a different language at home. Yulan asked whether she might write down the correct pronunciation. The woman agreed, but requested that the photograph not be published until her cousins had seen it. Yulan marked the condition on the back of the display copy. In one afternoon she had learned why consent mattered more than applause. Several people signed a petition supporting a fair hearing, yet others refused. She counted both groups honestly. A signature gained through pressure would not help the theater.

一位年長訪客請雨蘭讀出照片上的名字。她在一個家族姓氏的少見音節上唸錯了，連忙道歉。婦人慢慢示範發音，並說父母在家講的是另一種語言。雨蘭問能否記下正確念法。婦人同意，但要求照片在表親看過之前不得出版。雨蘭把條件寫在展示副本背面。一個下午，她學會同意權比掌聲更重要。有人簽署支持公平公聽會的連署，也有人拒絕。她如實計入兩種情況。用壓力換來的簽名，不會真正幫助戲院。

When evening came, Qin counted the ticketless visitors and Mei counted the bowls of noodles. The exhibition had earned only a little money, yet more than sixty residents had left contact information for future events. Wen recorded every purchase and every donation in separate columns. There were no grand speeches at the end, only people folding chairs and carrying tables into dry rooms. Yulan studied the visitors' handwritten comments. Some requested films for children, others evening classes, and several still preferred the parking lot. She copied those objections without softening them. The cinema would survive only if its future made room for neighbors who did not share her memories. That night, the first sixty-three signatures went into a locked box beside the cashbook.

傍晚，秦姨統計沒買票卻來參觀的人數，廖梅則清點賣出的麵。展覽只賺了一點錢，卻有六十多位居民留下聯絡方式，希望參加後續活動。沈文把每筆採購與捐款分欄紀錄。閉幕時沒有盛大的演講，只有大家摺好椅子、把桌子搬進乾燥房間。雨蘭看著訪客親筆意見：有人想看兒童片、有人想上晚間課，也有人仍偏好停車場。她原樣抄下反對意見，沒有替它們修飾。只有讓不分享她記憶的鄰居也能有一席之地，戲院才可能活下來。那晚，第一批六十三個簽名被放進帳本旁的上鎖盒子。

When the last volunteer left, Yulan checked the lock on the donation box twice. She had feared that an unsuccessful event would embarrass her, but the day's honest questions had been more valuable than easy praise. Qin asked whether they should hold another exhibition. "Only after we read the comments," Yulan answered. "People have given us information, not orders." She carried the box to Wen's office, where both of them counted the contents and signed the record. Good habits were beginning to feel ordinary.

最後一位志工走後，雨蘭把捐款箱的鎖檢查兩次。她原本害怕活動不成功會讓自己丟臉，但今天誠實的提問比輕易的稱讚更有價值。秦姨問要不要再辦一次展覽。「先讀完意見再說，」雨蘭回答，「人們給我們的是資訊，不是命令。」她把箱子帶到沈文辦公室，兩人共同清點並簽名。好的習慣開始變得自然。
