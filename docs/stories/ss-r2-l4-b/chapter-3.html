一張座位的成本

The Cost of a Seat

1998，海禾戲院不打烊 · 第 3 / 25 章 · 3000–4000 · 英中對照

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

Shen Wen arrived with a heavy ledger and a small calculator. His first assignment was not to raise money but to separate repairs from dreams. He asked Yulan to concentrate on the numbers while Qin brought tea and biscuits. The notes from last year's snack counter included the price of every raisin bun and even a rough calorie count for schoolchildren's lunches. Wen smiled at the unnecessary detail, then became serious. "Context matters," he said. "A cheap ticket does not mean a cheap building." Yulan wanted to defend the theater, but she could not defend a budget that left out electricity, insurance, or wages. By noon they had found a large gap between memory and cost.

沈文帶著厚帳本和小計算機來了。他的第一項工作不是募款，而是把真正的修繕和美好的想像分開。他請雨蘭專心看數字，秦姨則端來茶與餅乾。去年點心櫃的紀錄細到每個葡萄乾麵包的價格，甚至還有提供學童午餐的大略熱量。沈文對這些多餘的細節笑了一下，隨後正色說：「事情要放在脈絡裡看。便宜的票價不代表便宜的建築。」雨蘭想為戲院辯護，卻無法為漏掉電費、保險與薪資的預算辯護。中午前，他們已發現記憶和成本之間存在巨大落差。

A visitor from the ferry terminal brought a second problem. A touring company offered an incredible price for a summer performance, but demanded that the theater become a luxurious private hall for one month. Yulan felt the offer as an insult. Was the town only useful when wealthy strangers needed a stage? Her mother listened, then pointed out that feeling offended would not pay for a roof. "Find inspiration in their offer," Mei said. "They have customers. You have a room. Perhaps you can propose something neither side has tried." Wen suggested a smaller shared event instead of renting away the entire building. The idea gave Yulan a direction without solving the figures.

渡輪碼頭來的訪客帶來第二個難題。一家巡迴演出公司提出驚人的高價，條件卻是把戲院整整一個月改成豪華私人會館。雨蘭覺得這近乎羞辱：難道小鎮只有在富有外人需要舞台時才有用嗎？母親聽完提醒她，生氣付不了屋頂的錢。「從他們的提議找靈感，」廖梅說，「他們有客人，妳有場地，也許能提出雙方都沒想過的方式。」沈文建議合作辦小型活動，不要把整棟戲院租出去。這讓雨蘭找到方向，卻還沒有解決數字。

At the harbor, an old lifeguard named Dawei described how the swimming club had rebuilt its changing room. They had worked at a steady pace, beginning with the weakest wall and finishing one section before starting another. "There is no intermediate trick," he told Yulan. "You pay attention, or somebody gets hurt." He estimated the maximum number of people who could safely gather in the theater before repairs: none. Qin looked disappointed, but she accepted it. The cinema could host meetings in the courtyard, not indoors. Yulan drew a line through her attractive first-screening date. The decision cost them a week of possible ticket sales; it also kept them from selling tickets for a room they could not yet protect.

港口的老救生員大偉說起游泳社如何重建更衣室。他們採取穩定的步調，先處理最弱的牆，每完成一區才開始下一區。「這裡沒有什麼中間的巧招，」他告訴雨蘭，「你要留心，否則就會有人受傷。」他估計在修好之前，戲院內可以安全聚集的人數上限是零。秦姨失望，卻也接受了。戲院可以在庭院開會，但不能讓人入內。雨蘭把原先令人期待的首映日期劃掉。這個決定讓他們少了一週可能的票房，也避免售出無法保障安全的座位。

Wen calculated what percentage of the budget could come from weekend markets. The number was small, but not meaningless. Yulan proposed a photography display in the courtyard, using copies rather than precious originals. Their first priority would remain the exit stairs and leaking roof. If enough volunteers agreed, they could proceed with a public information day next Sunday. Qin produced a tin of raisins for the workers and announced that she would charge for tea, because unpaid kindness was not a lasting business plan. Everyone laughed, then helped write clear prices on a wooden sign. The sale of drinks would not save the theater, but it could show whether ordinary visitors wanted to return.

沈文計算週末市集大約能負擔預算的百分之多少。數字很小，卻不是毫無意義。雨蘭提議在庭院辦攝影展，展示複製照片而非珍貴原件。第一優先仍是逃生樓梯與漏水屋頂。若有足夠志工，他們下週日便能進行公開說明日。秦姨拿出一罐葡萄乾給工作者，又宣布茶要收費，因為不收報酬的善意無法成為持久的營運計畫。大家笑了，隨後一起在木板上寫清楚價格。茶水收入救不了戲院，卻能讓他們知道一般訪客是否願意再來。

That night, Yulan sat beside her mother's stall and copied the calculations into a notebook anyone could read. The figures were not comforting. The repairs would require paid labor, and no one had promised that people would buy tickets after the work was done. Still, the page felt more hopeful than the vague promises she had made on her first day. Mei read it slowly. "You have written the cost of the building," she said, "but not the cost of your time." Yulan added a modest wage for herself and asked Mei to check the sum again. For years she had given away extra hours at work to prove she was useful. She would not begin a new life by making the same mistake.

當晚，雨蘭坐在母親的麵攤旁，把計算結果抄進一本任何人都看得懂的筆記。數字並不樂觀。修繕需要付薪水的人手，工程完成後也沒有人能保證觀眾會買票。然而這頁帳比她第一天空泛的承諾更讓她安心。廖梅慢慢看完：「妳寫了建築的成本，卻沒有寫妳自己的時間成本。」雨蘭替自己加上合理但不高的薪資，再請母親重算一遍。多年來她常以免費加班證明自己有用。她不願在新生活裡重蹈覆轍。

Mei also reminded Yulan that the people doing the quiet housework behind every meeting needed time to rest. Tables were cleaned, cups were washed, and chairs were carried back without appearing on any colorful poster. Yulan added these tasks to the schedule with names and expected hours. She wanted the volunteers to know that care had a cost. A successful public project should not depend on making the least visible people do the most invisible work.

廖梅也提醒雨蘭，每場會議背後默默做家事般雜務的人，都需要休息。桌子要擦、杯子要洗、椅子要搬回原處，卻不會出現在彩色海報上。雨蘭把這些工作加進班表，註明姓名和預計時數。她希望志工知道照顧也有成本。成功的公共計畫不該依賴最不顯眼的人做最多看不見的工作。
