An Open Ledger

1998，海禾戲院不打烊 · 第 6 / 25 章 · 3000–4000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

Wen put the weekend figures on a blackboard outside the theater. Some neighbors called his approach too conservative. They wanted to borrow a large sum immediately and begin selling tickets, while others feared any debt at all. A business consultant from the county visited without charging a fee, but warned them to treat every future visitor as a consumer with choices. "Good feelings cannot replace clear prices," she said. Yulan agreed to list the goods they could actually offer: courtyard exhibitions, simple classes, refreshments, and eventually screenings after inspection. Qin offered to dye the faded curtain fabric herself. Wen asked her to enclose a receipt for the materials, even if she refused payment for her time.

沈文把週末收支寫在戲院外的黑板上。有些鄰居說他的做法過於保守，希望立刻借一大筆錢開始賣票；另一些人則害怕任何債務。縣裡來了一位不收費的企業顧問，提醒大家要把未來每個訪客都當成有選擇權的消費者。「好感不能取代清楚的價格。」她說。雨蘭同意列出目前真正能提供的商品與服務：庭院展覽、簡單課程、茶點，還有通過安全檢查後才能開始的放映。秦姨提議自己把褪色布幕重新染色。沈文請她附上材料收據，就算她堅持不領工錢也一樣。

A habitual visitor to the neighboring market accused the group of being ignorant about business. "People want comfort and luxury, not damp walls," he said. Instead of answering with rage, Yulan invited him to inspect the courtyard plan. She showed the narrow path marked with linen tape, the rooms they had chosen to isolate, and the safety warnings written in large characters. His anger softened when he learned they were not inviting people inside. "I still prefer a parking lot," he said, "but at least you are not hiding the danger." Yulan thanked him for the visit. It took more grace to listen to criticism than to accept applause from friends.

隔壁市場有位常客指責團隊不懂生意。「人們想要的是舒適和奢華，不是潮濕的牆壁。」他說。雨蘭沒有發怒，而是請他看看庭院活動計畫。她指著麻布帶標出的狹窄動線、刻意隔離的房間，以及用大字寫出的安全警告。得知團隊根本不打算讓人入內後，他的怒氣稍稍消退。「我還是比較喜歡停車場，」他說，「但至少你們沒有隱瞞危險。」雨蘭感謝他前來。聽取批評比接受朋友的掌聲更需要風度。

The science teacher offered a microscope for the next exhibition, hoping children could examine safe samples of cloth fibers. Yulan worried that the special equipment would distract from the theater's problems, but Qin disagreed. "A place survives when people use it," she said. The teacher explained that the study of local crafts could be a meaningful occupation for students during their holidays. At the first volunteer orientation, each participant learned where supplies were kept and which doors must remain closed. A timid boy raised his hand to ask who would pay if something broke. Wen gave an answer everyone could hear: the repair fund covered approved work, while private possessions required separate permission.

科學老師提供顯微鏡，希望下一場展覽能讓孩子安全地觀察布料纖維。雨蘭擔心器材會讓大家忘記戲院真正的問題，秦姨卻不同意。「一個地方能活下來，是因為人們會使用它。」她說。老師指出，研究地方工藝可以成為學童假期裡有意義的活動。第一次志工說明會上，每個人都學會物資放在哪裡，以及哪些門必須關閉。一位害羞的男孩舉手問東西壞掉由誰賠。沈文給出大家都聽得見的答案：核准工作由修繕基金負擔，私人財物則必須另行取得同意。

The town's only electrical shop loaned them a stereo with two working speakers. When a record played, the thin wooden walls seemed to tremble. An old song drifted across the courtyard and drew three shopkeepers from the market. They stayed to ask about class schedules, not to donate. Yulan was disappointed for a moment, then remembered the consultant's warning. A useful institution could not be measured only by how many people were willing to sacrifice for it. She wrote the price of each activity beside its expected cost, and crossed out two plans that would lose money every time. The theater needed responsible choices more than impressive announcements.

鎮上唯一的電器行借出一台雙喇叭還能運作的立體音響。唱片一放，薄木牆彷彿跟著震動。老歌飄過庭院，把市場的三位店主吸引過來。他們留下來問課程時間，卻沒有人捐款。雨蘭一開始有些失望，隨後想起顧問的提醒。一個有用的機構，不能只用人們願意為它犧牲多少來衡量。她把每項活動的價格與預期成本並排寫下，刪掉兩個每辦一次就會虧錢的計畫。戲院需要的是負責任的選擇，不是動聽的宣言。

Before going home, Yulan checked the cashbox with Wen and asked Qin to sign the day's record. The amount was small, but it matched the sales and the expenses. She could hear her mother closing the stall across the square. During her years in Taipei, she had learned to hide mistakes so a supervisor would not become angry. Now she was trying something harder: letting others examine her work before it looked perfect. She placed the signed page in a folder marked OPEN ACCOUNTS. If the theater could teach the town anything, perhaps it should begin with the simple habit of showing where the money went.

回家前，雨蘭與沈文核對收銀盒，並請秦姨在當日紀錄上簽名。金額雖小，卻與銷售和支出相符。廣場另一頭傳來母親收攤的聲音。在台北那些年，她學會了藏起錯誤，以免主管生氣。如今她正嘗試更難的事：在作品尚未完美以前，就讓別人檢查。她把簽署好的資料放進標著「公開帳目」的資料夾。如果戲院能教小鎮一件事，也許應該先從讓錢的去向清清楚楚開始。

The consultant returned a week later and asked whether anyone wanted to invest money for a share of future profits. Yulan explained that the project sought a public lease, not private ownership of the town's property. Residents could donate, lend under clear terms, or buy approved services; they could not purchase the right to silence other neighbors. The consultant nodded and asked for those rules in writing. Wen added them to the financial documents, grateful that an awkward question had arrived before the committee hearing.

一週後，顧問再來，問是否有人想投資以換取未來利潤分成。雨蘭解釋，計畫爭取的是公共租約，不是把鎮有財產變成私有。居民可以捐款、依清楚條件借款，或購買核准的服務，卻不能買到讓其他鄰居閉嘴的權力。顧問點頭，要求把規則寫成文字。沈文將它加入財務文件，很慶幸尷尬的問題能在公聽會前出現。
