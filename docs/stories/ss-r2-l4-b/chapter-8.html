一座城兩張圖

Two Drawings, One Town

1998，海禾戲院不打烊 · 第 8 / 25 章 · 3000–4000 · 英中對照 · 校訂完成

The second exhibition began with an abstract painting from the theater's old program cover. Yulan had been able to acquire it from a former designer who gave written permission for display. On a nearby wall hung a wedding photograph; the bridegroom had stood at the theater door while his family waited for a newsreel. Qin prepared a bulletin explaining that the people shown were civilians, not actors. A local composer offered to play the music he had written for their celebration. Yulan reminded him that a public performance required agreement about his fee. She was learning that memories constituted a collection of separate rights and responsibilities, not a treasure chest she could open whenever she liked.

第二場展覽以舊戲院節目封面的抽象畫開場。雨蘭從當年的設計者手中取得作品，也拿到了書面展示許可。旁邊掛著一張婚禮照：新郎站在戲院門口，全家正在等看新聞短片。秦姨準備告示，說明照片裡是普通市民而不是演員。一位在地作曲家願意演奏當年為這場婚禮寫的音樂。雨蘭提醒他，公開表演必須先談妥報酬。她漸漸明白，回憶不是隨時可以打開的寶箱，而是由許多不同權利與責任組成的收藏。

Xu Jian arrived when the music gave its first cue. He had brought careful drawings and a serious estimate for the proposed parking structure. His assistant tried to dominate the discussion by repeating that the theater had already failed. Yulan asked the crowd to listen first. She admired the enthusiasm of residents who wanted easier access to the market, but she also asked Xu which shops would lose their current entrance during construction. The question revealed a gulf between the drawing and the daily habits of merchants. Xu admitted that the plans were preliminary. "Then ours should be judged as preliminary too," Yulan said. Qin nodded from behind the table.

音樂給出第一個提示時，許健到了。他帶著停車場的詳細圖面與認真估算。助理試著反覆強調戲院早已失敗，企圖主導討論。雨蘭先請大家聽完。她理解希望市場交通方便的居民所展現的熱情，卻也問許健，施工期間哪些商店會失去現有入口。問題讓圖面與商家的日常習慣之間的鴻溝浮現。許健承認計畫仍在初期。「那我們的方案也該被視為初期計畫，」雨蘭說。秦姨在桌後點頭。

The next questions were harder. A woman asked whether the theater's program would represent every gender fairly. A farmer wondered if the courtyard was fertile enough for trees that could provide shade. Another visitor worried that a noisy performance would invade the quiet evenings of nearby homes. The intensity of their concerns surprised Yulan. Someone accused her of jealousy because Xu had more money. She refused to answer an insult with another insult. Instead, she wrote each question on a sheet and promised a written response. A retired, learned professor suggested that the town establish common standards for both proposals before voting on either one.

接下來的問題更難。有位婦女問戲院節目是否能公平呈現不同性別；農民想知道庭院土地夠不夠肥沃，能否種下遮蔭樹；另一位訪客擔心吵鬧的演出侵擾附近住戶的安靜夜晚。這些擔憂的強度令雨蘭意外。有人說她只是嫉妒許健比較有錢。她拒絕以侮辱回敬侮辱，只把每個問題寫在紙上，承諾書面回答。一位學識豐富的退休教授建議，鎮上應先訂出兩案共同遵守的標準，再表決支持哪一個。

That evening Wen borrowed the school's computer room to enter the questions into basic database software. In 1998 the machines were slow, and the printer jammed twice, but the process was more systematic than searching through piles of loose paper. Yulan divided each issue into four columns: question, evidence available, evidence missing, and person responsible. She noticed that many entries had no answer yet. For the first time, that did not frighten her. An empty box was an invitation to investigate, not a reason to invent certainty. She gave every speaker a copy of the relevant notes, including Xu's assistant, so nobody could claim that only supporters were allowed to speak.

當晚，沈文借用學校電腦教室，把問題輸入簡單的資料庫軟體。1998年的機器運作緩慢，印表機還卡紙兩次，但這比翻找一堆散紙更有系統。雨蘭把每項議題分成四欄：問題、已有證據、缺少證據與負責人。她發現好多欄都還空著。這次她第一次沒有因此害怕。空白格代表需要調查，不代表可以憑空編出肯定答案。她把相關紀錄影印給每位發言者，包括許健的助理，免得有人說只有支持者才有機會表達意見。

When the visitors left, Qin placed the old wedding photograph back in its protective envelope. She said the photographer's family had asked for a copy and would pay the printing cost. It was a small, welcome transaction. Yulan looked over the day's records and realized that the town had not chosen between a cinema and a parking lot. It had begun asking what each would actually provide. That was slower than applause and much less exciting than a dramatic speech. Yet it was exactly the kind of work she had wished somebody would do during her years in the city, when plans arrived from above and workers had to live with the results. This time, she intended to remain at the table.

訪客離開後，秦姨把舊婚禮照放回保護套。她說攝影師家屬想要一份複本，也願意支付沖印費。這是樁小而令人欣慰的交易。雨蘭檢查當天紀錄，意識到鎮民尚未在戲院與停車場之間做出選擇；大家只是開始問，每個方案實際能提供什麼。這比掌聲慢，也遠不如激昂演講令人興奮。但她在城市工作時，一直希望有人能做這種事：因為計畫往往由上面交代，最後卻是基層的人承受結果。這一次，她打算一直坐在討論桌旁。

After the computer room closed, Yulan stayed on the steps with Wen for a few minutes. The machine had organized questions neatly, but it had not made the choices easier. She could still hear the arguments from the exhibition. One resident wanted quiet, another wanted music, and a third needed a better delivery route. "Perhaps we have been searching for one perfect answer," she said. Wen shook his head. "A public place needs a method for living with several honest answers." Yulan wrote that down before she forgot it.

電腦教室關門後，雨蘭和沈文在台階上坐了一會兒。機器把問題整理得很整齊，卻沒讓選擇比較容易。她耳邊還有展覽上的爭論：有人想要安靜，有人想要音樂，還有人需要更好的送貨路線。「也許我們一直在找唯一完美答案，」她說。沈文搖頭。「公共場所需要的是讓幾種誠實答案並存的方法。」雨蘭趁忘記前把話寫下。
