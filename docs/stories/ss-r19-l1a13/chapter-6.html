The class agreed to listen before choosing a plan for Sol. Before anyone called the day strange, Mina had noticed how quiet the school hall could become when people were worried. The smallest sounds seemed clear, and the low sound of the hallway clock made the place feel both familiar and new. Rather than act as if every answer was obvious, Mina opened the class notebook and began with what had really happened. Sol was not a broken toy to be fixed without asking. Mina reminded herself of that whenever an adult spoke too quickly. In the corner of the class notebook, Mina found the word “arrive” beside an old drawing and stopped to ask what it meant. “finally,” Sol read from the practice page, then tried to connect the word to something they could actually see. A little note used “sign” as its key word; Mina kept the note because the rest of the line was still useful. Mina circled “weekend” on a reading sheet, not to win a test but to remember the meaning of the whole sentence.

Nobody could say yet how the story would end. Mina found this uncomfortable, especially with the countdown still waiting for an answer. Sol suggested they take turns speaking, because repeating the same fear would not create a new solution. One student wanted to hide him, and another wanted to break into the company system. This was the point where a simple wish met the world as it really was. Mina wanted to help, but wanting did not make every road safe or every rule fair. Sol asked what the other people involved might lose if the wrong choice were made. The answer took longer than either had expected. They agreed not to build the rest of their plan on a fact they had never checked. The word “drive” appeared in an example someone had written earlier, and Sol asked if the example fit their situation. “Let us keep ‘see’ in our word notes,” Mina said. “We can check its meaning again if we need it.” In the corner of the class notebook, Mina found the word “uncle” beside an old drawing and stopped to ask what it meant.

The group returned to the class notebook and looked at the first line again. It now seemed to ask a different question. Mina asked Sol to explain the idea in plain words, as if telling it to someone who had just arrived. When the idea sounded confusing, they changed the plan rather than blaming the listener. Sol chose a legal meeting instead, even though the clock kept falling. The answer brought relief, but it also carried a new responsibility. Mina did not look away from that part. Sol asked who should hear the news next, and whether it belonged to them to share it. They chose the careful answer over the exciting one, though excitement was still in the air. 索爾選擇合法開會，雖然倒數仍不斷減少。答案帶來安心，也帶來新的責任。米娜沒有迴避這一部分。索爾問下一個該讓誰知道消息，以及他們是否有權公開。他們選擇審慎的回答，而非最刺激的那個，雖然空氣中仍充滿興奮。一張小筆記以「box」為關鍵字；米娜留下它，因為剩下的句子仍然有用。米娜在閱讀單上圈出「house」，不是為了考高分，而是為了理解整句話。「terrible」出現在前人留下的例句裡，索爾問這個例子是否適合現在的情況。

The clock moved on. Mina felt tired, yet there was a good kind of energy left after honest work. Nobody had to say that the day was perfect. They only had to remember what had worked and what still needed care.
