Rain had washed away the only fertile field near the station. Several families wanted to cultivate a protected hillside that another group had maintained for years. Yan proposed a land ledger recording work, harvest shares, and soil restoration. The families objected that hunger could not wait for paperwork. Xia agreed and arranged emergency meals while the dispute was heard. The landholders were asked to explain their costs, not to surrender the field on demand. A rotating work agreement preserved part of the crop for those who had kept it alive and opened new plots for newcomers. The first sowing was late and uncertain. No one called it a fairytale of unlimited food. It was a practical compromise made by people who needed to live together through winter.
