Pirates Prefer Proper Receipts

The river pirates welcomed us with an array of stolen umbrellas and a suspiciously elegant dinner. Their captain said the controversy over the heating line had reached every port, and that a corrupt official was selling passage permits to people who already had legal rights. We walked down a narrow corridor into a cabin full of old maps. "Why would a pirate keep records?" I asked. "Because respectable people are very good at losing them," she replied. The ship's cuisine was excellent, which made her argument even more difficult to reject. Pip immediately asked for a second serving and nearly forgot we were on a mission.

河上海盜用一排偷來的雨傘和一頓意外優雅的晚餐迎接我們。船長說，暖氣管線的爭議已傳遍港口，一名貪腐官員正在向本就有合法通行權的人出售通行許可。我們沿狹窄走廊走進堆滿舊地圖的船艙。「海盜為什麼要保存紀錄？」我問。「因為體面人士很擅長把它們弄丟。」船長回答。船上的菜好吃得要命，害她的論點更難反駁。皮普立刻要求再來一份，幾乎忘記我們正在執行任務。

The captain claimed custody of a ledger taken from a royal barge. She asked us to derive the identity of its owner without disconnecting the locks that protected its pages. Kael said our combined expertise in repairs and documents might help. I pointed out that the first rule of our work was indispensable: no correction without evidence and consent. A damp corner smelled as if it might infect the whole cargo with mold. The captain showed us the infrastructure schedule for three river locks, then asked whether the capital had told us about a fourth. It had not. Suddenly the pirates seemed better informed than our official guide.

船長聲稱自己保管一份從王室駁船取來的帳冊。她要求我們找出所有人，卻不能拆掉保護書頁的鎖。凱爾說，我們結合修理與文件的專業能力，或許能幫忙。我指出工作的第一條規則不可或缺：沒有證據與同意就不能修正。潮濕的角落聞起來像黴菌快感染整批貨物。船長拿出三座河閘的基礎設施時程，又問首都是否告訴我們第四座的存在。沒有。海盜突然看起來比官方嚮導更清楚內情。

The captain also showed us a wall of letters from people who had used the river route to escape floods. Some described lost homes; others thanked sailors for a meal or a blanket. The letters could arouse sympathy, she said, but sympathy was not proof that every claim against the harbor office was true. She kept a separate box for verified shipping times and another for questions still unanswered. I admired that distinction. When an angry sailor demanded we publish everything, the captain refused because several writers had asked to remain private. Her rough ship had better information rules than the royal archive.

船長還讓我們看一面信牆，上面都是利用河道逃離洪災的人寫的信。有人描述失去家園，也有人感謝水手提供一餐飯或一條毛毯。她說，這些信會喚起同情，但同情不能證明每項對港務局的指控都是真的。她把已驗證的航運時間放一盒，待查問題放另一盒。我欣賞這個區別。憤怒水手要求全部公開時，船長拒絕，因為有些寫信者要求保密。她粗獷的船竟有比王室檔案館更好的資訊規則。

A coastal league had organized a tournament to decide which crews could carry emergency medicine during floods. The pirates entered under false names, but their sturdy boat won the safety test. The official recipient of the medicine then claimed the winner had no right to deliver it. "Convenient," Kael said, examining the removal order. The stamp was genuine, but the date beneath it was older than the competition itself. My pencil exposed a missing entry in the harbor journal. Rather than arrest anyone on the spot, the captain invited two neutral clerks to compare the originals and speak publicly.

海岸聯盟舉辦了一場競賽，決定哪些船隊能在洪水時運送緊急藥品。海盜用假名報名，但他們堅固的船贏得安全測試。官方指定的藥品收件人卻說優勝者無權配送。「真方便。」凱爾檢查撤銷命令時說。印章是真的，下面日期卻比競賽本身還早。我的鉛筆顯露港口日誌裡遺失的條目。船長沒有當場逮捕誰，而是請兩名中立書記比對原件並公開發言。

At dawn the captain returned the ledger to a public archive and received a receipt that named her boat correctly. "A pirate with paperwork," Pip whispered. "The world is ending." The captain laughed and said keeping records was worthwhile because a future captain might need to prove the same right. Before we left, she pointed us toward the third bell, hidden beneath a water market. She offered to sail us there without charge if we promised to record the river villages as real communities, not obstacles on a merchant's map. This time, agreeing felt simple.

黎明時，船長把帳冊交還公共檔案館，取得一張正確寫出她船名的收據。「有文書作業的海盜，」皮普低聲說，「世界要完了。」船長笑說，保留紀錄值得，因為未來船長可能還得證明相同權利。出發前她告訴我們，第三座鐘藏在水上市場底下。只要我們答應把河岸村落記錄成真正社群，而非商人地圖上的障礙，她願意免費載我們去。這次我答應得很乾脆。

Before the ship departed, Kael asked whether the captain would testify if the fourth lock's map was challenged. She said yes, provided her crew could verify the public copy before it was used. Pip proposed putting the entire ship on the witness stand, sails and all. I told him courtrooms were not built for masts. He looked genuinely disappointed. As we sailed toward the market, I watched the river widen beneath the moon. Every bell connected a thousand ordinary decisions, from who opened a gate to who could take medicine home. Perhaps the greatest spell in this world was the ability to make those decisions visible.

船出發前，凱爾問船長，若第四河閘的地圖受到質疑，她願不願意作證。船長說願意，條件是船員先核對公開副本。皮普提議讓整艘船，包括船帆，一起站上證人席。我提醒他法庭容不下桅杆。他看起來真的很失望。駛向市場時，我看著月下河道逐漸變寬。每座鐘塔連結千百個平凡決定：誰開閘、誰能把藥帶回家。也許這世界最強的魔法，就是讓這些決定被看見。
