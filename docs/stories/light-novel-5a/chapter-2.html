A Reporter Is Not a Side Quest

被召喚後只能改錯字的我，竟要拯救異世界的七座鐘塔？！ · 第 2 / 25 章 · 4000–5000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

Bellhaven's library had absurd architecture: its stairs led upward only when a person admitted being wrong. I disliked the building on principle. The librarian said adverse weather had ruined the western road, so the indigenous cliff villages could not deliver their usual salmon. A donor offered money for a replacement route, but demanded an excessive fee from every family who used it. Dahl told me to highlight the terms in the agreement, not to assume the donor was evil. A contract could be unfair without being imaginary, and my pencil could reveal omissions rather than punish intentions.

鈴港圖書館的建築荒謬至極：只有承認自己犯錯的人，樓梯才肯往上走。我原則上就討厭這棟建築。圖書館員說，惡劣天氣毀了西路，使懸崖原住村落無法運來平時的鮭魚。一名捐款者願意出錢修替代道路，卻要求每戶支付過高費用。達爾叫我標示契約條款，不要先認定捐款者是壞人。契約可以不公平，卻不代表它不存在；我的鉛筆只能揭露遺漏，不能懲罰動機。

"The palace ideology says every citizen shares the burden," announced a newspaper official. "Funny how the palace never carries a fish," replied Tessa, a journalist from the coast. She practiced journalism by interviewing the people who actually walked the route, and she invited me along. To an outsider like me, the cliff path looked impossible, but the villagers crossed it every morning. Their elder offered me a shoulder massage after watching me climb. "Your quest is not to save us without asking," she said. "Your quest is to understand why we said no." That was the most useful advice I had received.

「王宮的意識形態主張每位公民共同負擔。」報社官員宣布。「奇怪，王宮從不扛魚。」海岸記者泰莎回嘴。她以採訪真正走過路的人來實踐新聞工作，也邀我同行。對我這個外人而言，懸崖小路幾乎無法通行，可村民每天早上都會穿越。長老看我爬山，還替我按摩肩膀。「妳的任務不是沒問過就來救我們，」她說，「而是弄懂我們為何拒絕。」這是我得到過最有用的忠告。

Tessa showed me her notebook before we questioned anyone. One page contained statements from merchants who supported the new road fee; another listed families who opposed it. She refused to remove either page, even though her editor had asked for a cleaner story. "A villain is easier to print than a complicated supply route," she said. I understood the temptation. In Tokyo, the smallest typographical error could become a public scandal, while difficult explanations rarely made a headline. Here, however, the consequences included whether people could bring medicine across a cliff. I resolved to ask what each witness had actually seen, not what answer would make me look clever.

詢問任何人之前，泰莎先讓我看她的筆記。一頁是支持新過路費的商人說法，另一頁是反對的家庭名單。即使編輯希望故事更單純，她也拒絕刪掉任何一頁。「惡人比複雜的供應路線更容易上報。」她說。我懂那種誘惑。在東京，小小打字錯誤可能成為公開醜聞，困難的解釋卻很少變成標題。但在這裡，後果關乎人民能不能把藥送過懸崖。我決定只問每位證人真正看見了什麼，不問哪種答案能讓自己顯得聰明。

We found a series of maps pinned beneath an old watchtower. One variation showed a legal footbridge that the new permit had erased. Tessa wanted to rip the permit from its frame, but I made her stop; destroying evidence would render the original wording harder to verify. The elder explained that the bridge had been repaired by their own workers, long before the capital charged admission. A public prosecution against the donor might follow, but it required an independent hearing. For now, we copied the maps, recorded who owned them, and carried the copies back under wax seals.

我們在舊瞭望塔下找到一系列地圖，其中一個版本畫著合法的人行橋，卻被新許可證抹去。泰莎想把許可證從框上撕下來，我攔住她；毀壞證物只會讓原文更難查證。長老說，那座橋早在首都收通行費前，就由村民親手修好了。未來或許會對捐款者提起公訴，但那需要獨立聽證。眼下我們先影印地圖，記下持有人，並用蠟封將副本帶回。

The royal editor tried to persist with his claim that the bridge did not exist. Tessa asked him to stand beside the real wooden crossing while repeating it. He turned red and said nothing. Dahl reminded me that no law changed merely because someone was embarrassed. We submitted the testimony as a public record and asked the cliff villagers to choose two witnesses for the first bell. For the first time since arriving, I understood why a magic system built on consent could be slower than a sword. It could also be harder for a single hero to steal.

王宮編輯仍堅持那座橋不存在。泰莎請他站到真正的木橋旁再說一次，他漲紅了臉，卻一句話也說不出。達爾提醒我，任何法律都不會因為有人尷尬就自動改變。我們把證詞送進公開紀錄，並請懸崖村民自行選出兩位第一鐘塔的見證人。來到這裡以後，我首次明白，依賴同意的魔法制度為何比刀劍慢，也為何更不容易被單一英雄奪走。

Before we left the cliff, the elder invited us to dinner in a house that leaned toward the sea. She served hot fish stew and let the children explain how they kept track of deliveries with colored knots. Their method was simple enough that a visitor could learn it in an hour, and that seemed to be its strength. The capital had built an expensive register that even its own clerks barely understood. I asked permission to copy the knot chart for Dahl. The elder agreed, provided I wrote down who had invented it. On the road home, Tessa promised to print the inventor's name. I liked her more with every step.

離開懸崖前，長老邀我們去一間向海傾斜的房子吃晚飯。她端上熱魚湯，讓孩子說明如何用不同顏色的繩結追蹤配送。方法簡單到訪客一小時就能學會，這似乎正是優點。首都建了一套昂貴名冊，連自家書記都幾乎搞不懂。我徵求許可，想把繩結圖複製給達爾。長老答應，條件是我記下發明者的名字。回程路上，泰莎保證會印出發明者姓名。我每走一步就更喜歡她一點。
