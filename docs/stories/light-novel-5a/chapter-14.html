The Laser Monopoly Is Surprisingly Romantic

被召喚後只能改錯字的我，竟要拯救異世界的七座鐘塔？！ · 第 14 / 25 章 · 4000–5000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

The eastern city celebrated athletics with a festival that included climbing contests, races, and very bad poetry. A local advocate asked us to examine a contract clause granting a single company control over all laser lamps used by the relay bells. A council member called anyone who questioned the monopoly a communist, as if that label could replace an argument. Kael studied the maintenance records and said the lamps were failing because their cooling systems had not evolved with demand. "The company has a right to earn money," he said, "but not to exploit people who cannot turn the lights off."

東方城市以運動祭典慶祝，節目有攀爬競賽、賽跑和糟得出奇的詩朗誦。一名地方倡議者請我們檢查契約條款，其中一條讓單一公司控制轉運鐘使用的所有雷射燈。一名議員把質疑壟斷的人都叫做共產主義者，彷彿貼標籤就能代替論證。凱爾查看維護紀錄，指出燈具故障是因冷卻系統沒有隨需求演進。「公司有賺錢的權利，」他說，「卻不能剝削那些不能關燈的人。」

The supplier argued that a compromise would exceed the city's budget. A customs officer brought a border invoice showing identical equipment sold for much less elsewhere. The official at the hearing gave us a glare and accused us of wanting infinite supplies for free. I was tempted to indulge in a dramatic speech, but the figures mattered more than my feelings. We asked two independent engineers to compare the models and measure the cost of repairs. The results did not prove every accusation. They did show that the supposed emergency price included fees for services never provided.

供應商主張，任何妥協都會超過城市預算。一名海關官員帶來邊境發票，顯示其他地方同樣設備便宜得多。聽證官怒瞪我們，指控我們想免費取得無限物資。我很想發表一場戲劇性演說，但數字比感受重要。我們請兩名獨立工程師比較型號、量測維修成本。結果不能證明每一項指控，卻清楚顯示所謂緊急價格包含從未提供過的服務費。

The factory owner complained that our proposal ignored the evolution of his business, which had begun as a one-person workshop. Kael acknowledged the work that had built the company but explained that history could not justify a permanent exclusive fee. A few residents argued that the palace was partly responsible for allowing the contract to renew without review. The council accepted that point and added a regular public inspection date to the agreement. I had expected a simple confrontation between greedy owners and good citizens. Instead, we were slowly separating useful invention from rules that had become unfair. It took longer, and it was worth doing.

工廠老闆抱怨我們的方案忽視他企業的演進；公司原本只是一人工作坊。凱爾承認他建立企業的努力，卻說歷史不能合理化永久獨家收費。有些居民認為王宮也得負部分責任，因為多年來容許契約不經審查就續期。議會接受這看法，將定期公開檢查日期寫進協議。我原以為只是貪心業主和好市民的簡單對立，結果我們是在慢慢區分有用的發明與已變得不公平的規則。這很花時間，卻值得。

During a break Pip arranged a pickup of replacement lenses and nearly drove the cart into a fountain. "Pathetic," he muttered about his own steering. Kael told him not to mistake a bad turn for a permanent identity, and I thought that was unexpectedly sentimental advice. The council's rhetoric had grown louder, but the technicians were quietly solving the actual problem. We marked the lamp positions, agreed on shared spare parts, and wrote a schedule that every neighborhood could inspect. My third correction mark revealed a hidden renewal clause; the company representative looked annoyed but did not dispute its presence.

休息時皮普安排取回替換鏡片，卻差點把貨車開進噴泉。「真沒用。」他罵自己的駕駛技術。凱爾叫他別把一次轉彎失誤當成永久身分，我覺得這建議意外多愁善感。議會的修辭越來越響亮，但技師們正安靜解決真正的問題。我們標記燈具位置，協議共享備品，並寫出各社區都能檢查的時程。我的第三次標記揭露隱藏的續約條款；公司代表很不高興，卻沒有否認它存在。

At the end of the festival, Kael asked whether I wanted to watch the race from the hill. We sat above the city while the new lamps blinked in their test pattern. "I used to think fixing a machine meant leaving people out of the room," he said. "You are the first person who made me bring them in." I wanted to answer with something clever. Instead I handed him half my sweet rice cake. He accepted it without teasing. For a few quiet minutes there was no villain, no broken rule, and no urgent document between us.

祭典結束時，凱爾問我要不要從山丘看賽跑。我們坐在城市上方，看新的燈具閃出測試圖案。「我以前以為修好機器，就得把人排除在房間外。」他說，「妳是第一個叫我把他們請進來的人。」我想回答一句聰明的話，最後卻只分他半塊甜米糕。他沒有取笑，安靜收下。那幾分鐘，沒有反派、沒有壞規則，也沒有急件橫在我們之間。

After the festival, Kael showed me the workshop where he had learned to repair lamps as a child. The room was small, with a cracked bench and a window overlooking the racecourse. He admitted that his earliest ambition had been to make cities safer, not to earn a royal title. "When did you change your mind?" I asked. He said he was not sure he had changed; he had simply become afraid of admitting failure. I wanted to reach for his hand, but he was still studying the bench. Instead I sat down beside him and let the sound of the evening runners fill the quiet room.

祭典結束後，凱爾帶我看他童年學修燈的工坊。房間很小，長椅有裂痕，窗外能望見跑道。他承認最初的志願是讓城市更安全，而不是得到王室頭銜。「你什麼時候改變想法？」我問。他說自己不確定有沒有改變，只是漸漸害怕承認失敗。我想伸手握他，但他仍低頭看長椅。我便坐在旁邊，讓傍晚跑者的腳步聲填滿安靜房間。
