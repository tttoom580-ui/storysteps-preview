我的專利是一隻紙鶴

My Patent Is a Paper Crane

The water market greeted us with a warning: caution was required around an inventor whose experiments sometimes turned the roofs upside down. My chore was to check the competition rules for clarity, while Pip carried a commodity more precious than gold—a box of dry paper. A student hoped to dedicate her final school project to low-cost warning lamps. The official delegation called her design dreadful because she had no wealthy sponsor. I thought their judgment was deeply unethical, though I made myself ask for evidence before speaking. Her invention folded into a paper crane that glowed when floodwater approached.

水上市場用一張警告標語迎接我們：接近某位發明家必須小心，因為她的實驗有時會把屋頂翻過來。我的雜務是確認競賽規則清楚，皮普則搬運一箱比黃金珍貴的商品——乾燥紙張。一名學生想把畢業作品奉獻給低價的警示燈。官方代表團說她的設計糟糕透頂，只因她沒有有錢的贊助者。我認為這判斷很不道德，但還是逼自己先問證據再開口。她的發明能摺成一隻紙鶴，洪水接近時就會發光。

The student had practiced her presentation for months, yet her fluency disappeared in front of judges. Kael suggested a simple demonstration rather than an expensive speech. We placed the crane beside a bucket and watched a tiny current generate its light. The apprentice had written down every step of her exploration, including the failures. I searched her notes for the patent number she had registered as an undergraduate. The committee had demanded a premium fee not listed in the original application. My pencil uncovered the deleted exemption, and even the sternest judge had to admit the fee did not belong there.

那位學生練習報告好幾個月，可站到評審面前，口語流暢度就消失了。凱爾建議簡單展示，而不是昂貴演說。我們把紙鶴放在水桶旁，看見微弱電流產生光亮。學徒記下探索的每一步，連失敗也不省略。我在筆記中找她以大學生身分申請的專利號碼。委員會要求收取原申請書裡沒有的額外費用。我的鉛筆找出被刪除的豁免條款，連最嚴肅的評審也不得不承認那筆費用不該存在。

The competition venue was a floating hall held steady by chains beneath the water. Its interior walls were covered with portraits of famous inventors, all of them wealthy men. The student stared at the pictures and asked whether she belonged there. I said an ethical judging system should measure the work instead of a family name. Tessa asked each judge to disclose any financial relationship with the suppliers before the trials began. Two agreed immediately; one requested time to consult the rules. The student did not suddenly become fearless, but she stepped onto the stage carrying her little paper crane with both hands.

比賽場地是一座浮在水上的大廳，靠水底鐵鏈保持平穩。內部牆面掛滿知名發明家的肖像，清一色是有錢男性。學生望著照片，問自己是否有資格來。我說，有倫理的評審制度應該衡量作品，而不是家世。泰莎請每位評審在比賽前揭露與供應商的金錢關係。兩人立刻答應，一人要求時間查閱規則。學生沒有突然變得無所畏懼，但她仍雙手捧著小紙鶴走上舞臺。

Inside the market hall, rain began to shed from the roof in silver sheets. A boat captain argued that his vessel was the sole safe way to reach the third bell. Kael offered to steer a smaller boat while the student installed the cranes along the waterline. "Superstition says paper birds dislike storms," Pip declared. "Physics says wet paper is fragile," Kael answered, earning a grateful nod from the inventor. We tested a waterproof coating instead of trusting either a legend or a boast. By evening a chain of small lights marked the safe path, and every stall owner had seen the experiment with their own eyes.

市場大廳裡，雨水像銀色布幕從屋頂傾瀉。一名船長主張只有他的船能安全抵達第三座鐘。凱爾提出駕駛較小的船，讓學生沿水線裝上紙鶴。「迷信說紙鳥討厭暴風。」皮普宣布。「物理說濕紙很脆弱。」凱爾回答，發明者感激地點頭。我們測試防水塗層，而不是相信傳說或吹噓。傍晚，小燈串已標出安全路徑，每名攤主都親眼見證了實驗。

The bell under the market was older than any of our maps. Its keeper agreed to open its records once traders, boat crews, and the nearby island had each selected a witness. The inventor gave me a paper crane as thanks. "If it explodes, return it," she said with a perfectly serious face. I carried it carefully anyway. I was beginning to suspect that a light novel protagonist's best equipment was not a legendary sword, but a friend willing to tell her when an idea was foolish. Kael noticed my smile and, for once, smiled back before asking why.

市場下方的鐘塔比我們所有地圖都古老。守鐘人答應開放紀錄，條件是商販、船員與附近島嶼各自選出見證人。發明者送我一隻紙鶴致謝。「如果爆炸，記得退貨。」她一臉認真地說。我還是小心收下。我開始懷疑，輕小說主角最好的裝備不是傳說之劍，而是願意直說妳的主意很蠢的朋友。凱爾注意到我的笑，難得先回以微笑，才問我為什麼。

After the successful test, the student invited us to her workshop behind the market. Shelves were filled with designs that had failed spectacularly: a spoon that sang at night, an umbrella that attracted rain, and a clock that gave entirely unhelpful advice. I liked the failed models more than the expensive trophies in the hall. Kael helped her write a clear maintenance sheet for the paper crane, including what to do when its battery weakened. Pip attempted to use the singing spoon as a musical instrument. It promptly told him to practice harder. I laughed so loudly that the student looked proud for the first time that day.

測試成功後，學生邀我們去市場後方工坊。架上擺滿失敗得壯觀的設計：半夜會唱歌的湯匙、會吸引雨水的雨傘，以及只會給無用建議的鐘。我比起大廳昂貴獎盃，更喜歡這些失敗模型。凱爾協助她替紙鶴寫明確維護單，包含電池變弱時該做什麼。皮普試著把唱歌湯匙當樂器，它立刻叫他再努力練習。我笑得太大聲，學生當天第一次露出驕傲表情。
