Adult rescuers reached the stream with ropes while Toto held the safe trail clear. The mountain shelter had its own familiar rhythm. Toto knew the sound of footsteps there, yet something about this moment called for a closer look. It was tempting to make a quick story out of a small surprise. Instead, Toto looked for one thing that could be checked and one person who deserved to be heard. There was room in the trail map for questions as well as answers, and today the questions were beginning to matter. The red letter was small beneath his shell. The people who needed it were not small, and Toto remembered that with every step. In the corner of the trail map, Toto found the word “banana” beside an old drawing and stopped to ask what it meant. “lake,” Kes read from the practice page, then tried to connect the word to something they could actually see. A little note used “shout” as its key word; Toto kept the note because the rest of the line was still useful.

The first reaction in the mountain shelter was a mixture of surprise and hope. Toto felt both, and did not pretend otherwise. The goat was frightened, and shouting made him step closer to the water. The trouble was real, even if the danger had not yet become clear to everyone in the mountain shelter. Toto took a step back to let someone with more experience speak. Help did not always mean being the first person to act. That gave Kes time to notice a detail that had been lost in the noise. Kes had helped without taking away the choice, and that made the help easier to accept. For now, they could rest.
