免費書的代價

The Price of a Free Book

借一本到明天 · 第 8 / 25 章 · 1000–2000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

The finance office asked how a free exchange would pay for paper, storage, and carts. Bo suggested charging a small fee for each book. Hsin understood the problem, but worried the rule would push away the readers they most wanted to welcome.

財務室詢問免費換書如何支付紙張、倉儲和推車費用。博建議每本書收取少量費用。欣明白問題，卻擔心收費會排除他們最想歡迎的讀者。

The average cost of a label was small, but hundreds of labels added up. A student club offered a barbecue fundraiser, though Hsin wanted free books to stay free. Kai remembered a childhood library that had never charged children to enter. A pair of chopsticks lay beside Bo's lunch while he worked on the budget. Hsin refused a command from a volunteer who wanted to charge readers without a vote.

每張標籤的平均成本很低，但幾百張加起來就不少。 一個社團提議辦烤肉募款，但欣仍希望免費書保持免費。 凱想起童年去過的一間圖書館，從未向孩子收門票。 博整理預算時，午餐旁放著一雙筷子。 有志工沒經表決就想收費，欣拒絕接受他的命令。

Kai set three possible budgets on the table. The first charged everyone; the second depended entirely on gifts; the third used a voluntary support box and a small university grant. They compared costs rather than arguing about which idea sounded kinder.

凱在桌上放了三種預算。第一種向所有人收費，第二種完全仰賴捐贈，第三種設自願支持箱並申請少量校方補助。他們比較成本，而不是爭論哪個主意聽起來較善良。

The project could disappear if the team spent more money than it had. Kai joked that fate had made them partners after years of disagreement. They moved forward with a six-week budget instead of promising a full year. A gentle reply helped the worried student explain why free books mattered. The cart could not carry another heavy load without stronger wheels.

如果團隊花的錢超過現有資金，計畫就可能消失。 凱笑說，命運讓爭論多年的兩人成為搭檔。 他們用六週預算向前走，而不是承諾整年經費。 溫柔的回覆使焦急的學生說出免費書的重要性。 沒有更堅固的輪子，推車就不能再承受重載。

Hsin asked two students who had never brought books to speak at the meeting. One said the exchange had helped him find course material when money was short. The other needed reading material for her younger sister. Their voices changed the room.

欣邀請兩位從未捐書的學生發言。一位說自己缺錢時在這裡找到課程用書；另一位需要讀物給妹妹。她們的聲音改變了會議氣氛。

At the budget meeting, a student asked whether people who donated money should be allowed to borrow more books. Hsin answered that donations were a gift, not a ticket to better treatment. She suggested thanking donors with a public message, provided they agreed to be named. Kai pointed out that the proposed system would be cheaper to run than printing different cards for different payment levels. The room became quiet. Their values and their budget had finally pointed in the same direction.

預算會議上，一名學生問捐錢的人是否能多借幾本。欣回答捐款是禮物，不是取得特殊待遇的門票。她建議經同意後用公開訊息感謝捐款人。凱指出這種制度比為不同付費等級印製不同卡片還省錢。會議室安靜下來，理念與預算終於指向同一方向。

Hsin asked the group to explain the meaning of 'free' on every public sign. A donation box was one means of support, not a price for reading. The financial reality was less exciting than the original dream. An old story about a servant saving books became popular at the exchange. Kai measured the empty shelf before buying wood for new supports.

欣請團隊在每張公告上說明「免費」的意義。 自願捐款箱是支持的方式之一，不是閱讀的價格。 財務現實沒有最初的夢想那麼令人興奮。 一本描述僕人拯救書籍的舊故事在換書活動很受歡迎。 凱買木材支架前，先量了空書架的尺寸。

The committee chose a six-week trial of the third budget. Donations would not affect how many books a person could take. Bo agreed to publish expenses every Friday, and Kai built a simple sign explaining where each coin went.

委員會決定試辦六週第三種預算。捐不捐款不影響取書數量。博同意每週五公布支出，凱則做了簡單的標示，說明每一枚硬幣的用途。

The source of their small grant was listed openly on the budget page. Bo used a wooden stick to mark the exact length of a shelf board. A smooth stone under the cart wheel had to be removed before anyone moved it. Whatever the vote decided, Hsin wanted to keep the visitors' trust. A reader whom nobody knew spoke about needing free course books.

小額補助的來源公開列在預算頁面。 博用木棒標記書架木板的精確長度。 推車輪下的一塊光滑石頭必須在移動前取走。 無論投票結果如何，欣都想維護訪客的信任。 一名大家都不認識的讀者說自己需要免費課本。

The next morning, Bo placed the voluntary support box near the exit and kept it away from the borrowing desk. There was no notice saying people should pay. Instead, a separate card explained how any money would be spent. Hsin saw a student leave without giving anything and felt glad nobody looked at him. Later, someone placed coins in the box without writing a name. Bo counted the money with another volunteer and entered the amount in the public report. Their plan worked without judging either visitor.

隔天早上，博把自願支持箱放在出口附近，與借閱櫃台分開。沒有標示要求付錢，另一張卡片只解釋款項用途。欣看見一名學生沒捐款就離開，慶幸沒人盯著他看。後來有人不留姓名投入幾枚硬幣。博與另一名志工一起清點，將金額登錄公開報告。這套制度不必評判任何訪客也能運作。

Later Kai confessed that he had once thought a free project was only a pleasant dream. Hsin said dreams still needed numbers. They shared a quiet laugh, pleased that their different ways of thinking had finally made one practical plan.

後來凱承認自己曾以為免費計畫只是美夢。欣說，美夢也需要數字。兩人安靜地笑了，因為不同的思考方式終於合成了一個可行的計畫。
