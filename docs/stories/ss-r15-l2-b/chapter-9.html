沒有地址的信

A Letter Without an Address

借一本到明天 · 第 9 / 25 章 · 1000–2000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

Inside a poetry collection, Hsin found a letter that had never been sent. It thanked a former teacher for keeping the annex open during an especially hard year. There was no full name or address, only a date and the words "For the room that listened."

欣在一本詩集中找到一封從未寄出的信，內容感謝一位舊老師在艱難的一年仍讓閱覽館開放。信上沒有全名與地址，只有日期和「給那間願意傾聽的房間」。

In addition to new books, they displayed public notices from the old annex. A student wrote an article about the program after checking the facts. Each chapter of the old annex's history began with a different group of readers. Children used a crayon to draw their favorite reading places. The daily report counted visits without listing the names of quiet readers.

除了新書，團隊也展示了舊館的公開公告。 一名學生核對事實後寫了篇介紹計畫的文章。 舊館歷史的每個篇章，都從不同讀者群開始。 孩子們用蠟筆畫出最喜歡的閱讀地方。 每日報告統計來訪次數，沒有列出安靜讀者的名字。

Kai wanted to turn the words into a large festival poster. Hsin disagreed. A private letter was not automatically public just because someone had placed it in a book. She suggested contacting the archive officer and asking what could be described without showing the letter.

凱想把字句做成大型節慶海報，欣不同意。私人信件不會只因放在書裡就自動變成公開資料。她提議先聯繫檔案管理員，詢問能描述哪些內容，而不展示原信。

Hsin's main fear was that a private letter would become public entertainment. The historical notices described free evening study hours in the annex. The archive officer explained the legal limits of sharing personal letters. A loose nail in the display board was replaced before visitors arrived. A painting of an empty chair stood beside the public documents.

欣最大的擔憂，是私人信件會成為公開娛樂。 歷史公告記載舊館曾提供免費夜間自習時段。 檔案管理員解釋分享私人信件的法律限制。 訪客到場前，團隊換掉展板上一顆鬆動的釘子。 一幅空椅子的畫作放在公開文件旁。

The officer recognized the date as part of the annex's public history. She found old notices about free evening study hours, but she would not identify the letter's writer. Hsin could now explain the service without turning an unknown person's feelings into a show.

檔案管理員認出日期與閱覽館公開歷史有關，找到舊日夜間免費自習時段的公告，但不會辨識寫信者。欣現在能說明服務歷史，不必把陌生人的心情變成表演。

The archive officer let Hsin examine a public schedule from the year the letter was written. It showed that the annex had once stayed open after most campus offices closed. The schedule also named the staff roles, but it did not say who had written the letter. Hsin copied only the public facts into her notes. She was beginning to understand that an unknown author could still influence a place without becoming the subject of a search.

檔案管理員讓欣查閱寫信那年的公開時刻表。表上顯示舊館曾在大多數校園辦公室關閉後仍開放，也列出員工職務，卻沒有寫誰是寫信人。欣只把公開事實抄進筆記。她開始明白，未知作者仍能影響一個地方，不必因此成為被追查的對象。

Kai asked his partner Hsin whether the drawing was too sad for the exhibition. Bo used paper paste to attach the approved notices to fresh boards. A picture of a puppy brought a smile to a visitor reading serious history. One section of the wall showed events in order without guessing anyone's motives. Sunlight began to shine through the courtyard windows during the opening talk.

凱問搭檔欣，這幅畫對展覽來說是否太悲傷。 博用漿糊把核准展示的公告貼在新展板上。 一張小狗圖片讓正在讀嚴肅歷史的訪客露出微笑。 展牆的一個區塊依時序列出事件，不猜測任何人的動機。 開場談話時，陽光開始透過中庭窗戶照進來。

Bo added the public notices to a new wall display. Kai drew a simple picture of an empty chair and a light left on. He asked Hsin for approval before printing. She smiled at the small change in his habit.

博把公開公告放上新展牆。凱畫了一張空椅子與一盞亮燈的簡圖，印製前先徵詢欣的同意。她因他習慣中的小改變而微笑。

The shopkeeper across the street donated paper but asked for no advertisement. Hsin said a project could succeed without showing every private document it found. Their new system separated historical facts from unconfirmed personal stories. They would not print the letter unless the proper owner gave permission. Kai thought the project might be worse off if it lost its promise of trust.

街對面的店主捐了紙張，沒有要求廣告回報。 欣說，計畫可以成功，不需要展示每份找到的私人文件。 新系統把歷史事實和未經證實的個人故事分開。 除非正當所有者許可，他們不會印出那封信。 凱認為若失去信任的承諾，計畫處境可能變得更差。

Kai offered to draw a wall with an empty chair and a list of the public evening hours. The drawing would tell visitors what the room had offered without pretending to know every person who had needed it. Hsin asked him to leave room for a simple question: 'What kind of place helps you read?' Visitors later added answers about light, quiet, friends, and being welcome. Nobody had to sign. The display became more personal because it did not demand anyone's private history.

凱提議畫一面展牆，放上空椅子與公開的夜間開放時段。圖畫會告訴訪客房間曾提供什麼，而不假裝知道每位使用者的故事。欣請他留下簡單問題：「什麼樣的地方能幫助你閱讀？」訪客後來寫下光線、安靜、朋友和被歡迎等答案，都不需要署名。展覽沒有要求私人歷史，反而更能貼近人心。

That night Hsin told Kai why the letter had moved her: she had once studied alone in a public library when her family could not afford a quiet home. He listened without rushing to give advice. The silence between them felt kind rather than empty.

那晚欣告訴凱，這封信為何讓她動容：家裡無法提供安靜空間時，她曾獨自在公共圖書館讀書。凱沒有急著建議，只是聽著。兩人之間的沉默溫柔而不空洞。
