The First Public Return

失物檔案館：城市遺忘的那一天 · 第 5 / 25 章 · 3000–4000 · 英中對照

Mei decided to adopt a simple agency rule for the growing archive: nobody could sign a claim on behalf of someone else without written permission. She had watched how frightened behavior could be mistaken for agreement. Tao helped decorate the counter with paper stars, while Han removed an old decoration that covered the notice of review rights. Mei wanted people to cherish their possessions, but she cared even more about their power to say no. That distinction seemed fundamental. A beautiful display was useless if visitors did not know what choice they were making.

陳玫決定替逐漸擴大的檔案館採用一項簡單代理規則：沒有書面授權，誰都不能替別人簽署申領單。她已看過人們如何把驚恐的反應誤認成同意。陶陶替櫃檯貼上紙星星，韓叔則拆掉遮住複核權利公告的舊裝飾。陳玫希望大家珍惜物品，但她更在意每個人都有說不的能力。這個差別非常根本。若訪客根本不知道自己在作什麼選擇，再美的展示也沒有意義。

The first public hearing was held in the room once used for a student congress. A respected business owner argued that free transfers would improve trade and make the district influential. Others worried that refusing the game would discourage investment. Mei did not deny those concerns. She presented the actual interaction between three tickets: the piano, the dog's collar, and the bakery door. All had been marked unclaimed while their users were present. The outcome depended not on speed but on whether independent evidence was accepted before midnight. The suggestion that a family could lose its own bell without notice continued to horrify Tao.

第一場公開聽證會在曾舉辦學生代表大會的房間舉行。一位受敬重的企業主主張，自由移轉有利交易，也能使街區更具影響力。有人擔心拒絕遊戲會打擊投資。陳玫沒有否定這些顧慮；她展示鋼琴、狗項圈和麵包店門口三張申領單彼此如何牽連。三件東西都在使用者仍在場時被標成無主。結果取決的不是誰最快，而是午夜前能否接受獨立證據。 想到一家人可能毫無預警地失去自己的鐘，這件事仍令陶陶驚恐。

A girl at the reception desk asked whether a robber could win by stealing an owner's papers. The thought horrified several people. Mei admitted the danger and explained why the archive stored photographs in separate places. Han showed the primitive carbon-copy book his father had used for decades. It lacked the brightness of the new screens, but its pages could not change without leaving marks. A visiting artist brought scenery panels from the old opera room to protect the papers from the draft. They had built a small defense out of ordinary work.

接待桌旁的女孩問，如果強盜偷走主人的文件，是否就能贏得申領。幾個人聽得毛骨悚然。陳玫承認風險，解釋檔案館為何把照片副本存放在不同地點。韓叔拿出父親用了數十年的原始複寫簿。它沒有新螢幕耀眼，卻不能不留痕跡地更改。來訪的藝術家搬來舊歌劇廳的布景板，替文件擋風。他們靠普通工作建立了一道小小防線。

The gray-badged man returned with a claim for the town's brass bell. His accusation was shameful: he said anyone protecting public objects must be a witch or wizard using forbidden tricks. Tao trembled at the word, but Mei made him read the form. It listed no witness and no last use. The bell had sounded every noon for thirty years; dozens of people could describe it independently. The claim was rejected at the first hearing. There was no terror in the crowd this time, only relief followed by difficult questions about what might happen next.

戴灰徽章的男子帶著鎮上銅鐘的申領單回來。他的指控十分可恥：凡保護公共物品的人，必定是施展禁術的女巫或巫師。陶陶聽見這些詞發抖，陳玫卻請他直接讀表格。裡面沒有見證人，也沒有最近使用紀錄。這口鐘三十年來每天中午都會敲響，數十人能獨立描述其聲音。申請在第一次聽證便遭駁回。這次人群不再恐懼，只有鬆一口氣後接踵而來的難題。

An unsigned game notice promised that a faster answer would settle everything. Mei folded it and placed it beside the original source instead of destroying it. Both versions might be needed later. The dispute had revealed an awkward fact: the bell had been used at noon for thirty years and the rival claim had no witness. It also carried a human cost: neighbors might accept the first public victory as proof that every case was safe. Mei told Tao that the archive was not built to win arguments with every visitor. It was built so that an argument could be checked even after the loudest speaker had gone home. The notebook remained open for corrections. For a city beginning to distrust its own memories, that ordinary permission mattered more than another silver badge.

一張沒有簽名的遊戲公告承諾，更快的答案便能解決一切。陳玫把它摺好，放在原始證據旁，沒有銷毀；將來可能需要兩個版本。爭議揭露一項尷尬事實：銅鐘三十年來每日中午使用，而對方沒有見證人。它也帶來人的代價：鄰居可能誤把第一次勝利當成所有案件都安全。陳玫告訴陶陶，檔案館不是為了贏過每個訪客的辯論，而是讓最會說話的人離開之後，爭論仍可被查核。筆記本繼續開放更正。對一座開始懷疑自身記憶的城市而言，這種平凡的許可，比另一枚銀色徽章更重要。

Before anyone could close the file, Mei divided a new page into two columns. One was reserved for things a person could directly check; the other held claims that still required interpretation. In the first column she wrote that the bell had been used at noon for thirty years and the rival claim had no witness. In the second she left a question mark beside the game’s conclusion. Han asked whether the difference was really important. Mei told him that a bright label was a statement, not a witness. Another reader examined her notes without seeing the first account. They agreed on the date and location, then marked the limits of their knowledge. Nothing was erased just to make the page look tidy.

在結案前，陳玫把新紙分成兩欄。一欄記載可直接查驗的事實，另一欄放仍需解釋的主張。第一欄寫著：銅鐘三十年來每日中午使用，而對方沒有見證人；第二欄則在遊戲結論旁留下問號。韓叔問這樣的差別是否真重要。陳玫說，發亮的標籤只是一項說法，不是見證人。另一名讀者沒有先看原始說明，就獨立檢查她的筆記。雙方核對了日期和地點，再標示自己不知道的部分。沒有人只為了讓頁面好看就刪去內容。

After the meeting Mei checked the archive's shelves. They held sixteen claims, three frozen transfers, and one public return. No one could honestly call that a finished reform. Still, the bell belonged to its community that evening, and a written explanation of the decision hung beside it. Mei thought of her mother's warning about approval and consent. Her conscience would not let her turn an important first success into a story of total victory. She wrote a new question in the logbook: who was making the silver tickets, and why did so many of them arrive together?

散會後，陳玫查看檔案架，上面有十六件申領案、三件暫停移轉案，以及一件正式歸還公眾的物品。任何人都不能誠實地稱它為已完成的改革。可是當晚，鐘確實屬於共同使用它的居民，判決理由也張貼在鐘旁。陳玫想起母親對核准與同意的警告。她的良心不允許自己把重要的第一次成功說成全面勝利。她在紀錄簿寫下新問題：銀色領物單是誰製造的？為何如此密集地一起出現？
