一面鏡子的價錢

The Price of a Mirror

不會飛的信使，替群山點燈 · 第 5 / 25 章 · 1000–2000 · 英中對照

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

The village college let Lan use an empty classroom for a workshop. Many students came after soccer practice, still wearing their team sweaters. They wanted to help make a double set of mirror frames, one for daily use and one for repair. The workshop teacher was a kind lady who seldom spoke during meetings, but her hands were skilled. She showed the group a feature on the frame that let them turn the mirror without touching its bright surface. Bo explained its function and asked each student to try it. Lan liked their spirit, yet she knew that a lively afternoon was not the same as a lasting work plan.

村裡的學院借給嵐一間空教室當工坊。許多學生踢完足球後趕來，還穿著隊服毛衣。他們想做雙套鏡架，一套日常使用，一套供修理備用。工坊老師是一位親切的女士，開會時很少說話，手藝卻很好。她示範鏡架上一個可以轉動鏡子、又不必碰亮面的設計特色。博解釋它的功能，請每位學生試用。嵐喜歡大家的熱情，但她也知道，熱鬧的一下午不等於長久可行的工作計畫。

A fellow student brought an expense sheet from the school office. Glass, wood, cloth, and travel all cost money. The total was larger than Lan had expected. A student offered to pay for everything, but Lan would not place the whole burden on one family. She asked the group to make a selection of the parts they truly needed first. The perfect mirror was too costly, and the brightest one broke easily. A safer, cheaper mirror could still carry the signal across the next hill. They cut the list in half and wrote the reasons beside each choice so that any donor could understand it.

一名同學從學校辦公室帶來費用表。玻璃、木頭、布料與交通都要錢，總額比嵐預想得高。有學生願意全部出資，但嵐不想讓單一家庭承擔整個負擔。她請大家先挑選真正必要的零件。最完美的鏡子太昂貴，而最亮的那一種又很容易碎。較安全、便宜的鏡子也能把訊號送過下一座山。他們把清單砍掉一半，還在每項選擇旁寫下理由，讓捐助者都能看懂。

During the next test, one student tried to swallow a laugh when a small screw fell into a cup. Another reached for a tissue to clean the table. The teacher told them to sweep the floor first so no one stepped on metal. She had once worked in a factory and understood safe production. Her lessons were not exciting, but they saved time. A visitor wearing a thick sweater said that a person from the guild had called their work a terrorist threat. The classroom fell silent. Lan asked for the visitor's exact words and refused to repeat the claim as a fact without proof.

下一次測試時，一名學生看見小螺絲掉進杯子，忍著笑。另一人伸手拿面紙清理桌面。老師先叫他們掃地，免得有人踩到金屬。她以前在工廠工作，懂得安全生產。那些課不刺激，卻節省時間。一名穿厚毛衣的訪客說，公會有人把他們的工作說成恐怖主義的威脅。教室瞬間安靜。嵐追問訪客聽到的原話，並拒絕在沒有證據的情況下，把這指控當成事實傳播。

The teacher helped the students upload a public note about the project. It gave the materials, costs, safety rules, and the names of people who had agreed to be listed. The note did not attack the guild. Instead, it invited anyone with a concern to observe a practice from a safe place. Lan also wrote that the signal line did not carry private letters. That was a limit, not a failure. Families could still use trained couriers for long messages. The light line would only send short, agreed warnings and requests. Bo read the note aloud and found no sentence that promised more than the team could do.

老師協助學生上傳一份公開說明，列出材料、成本、安全規則，以及同意公開姓名的參與者。說明沒有攻擊公會，而是邀請有疑慮的人在安全位置觀看演練。嵐還寫明，光訊號線不傳私人長信。這是限制，不是失敗。家庭仍可請受訓信使送長訊息；光線只負責傳送簡短、事先約定的警示與請求。博把說明大聲讀過，確認沒有一句話承諾超出團隊能力的事。

The next morning, a few parents came to inspect the classroom. One parent wanted to know whether the school would lose money if the project failed. Lan showed the expense sheet and explained which materials could be returned or reused. Another parent asked what would happen if a child sent a false signal. She showed the cover and the station-name rule. The parents did not all agree, but their questions improved the plan. When they left, the workshop teacher smiled. “A group is not stronger because everyone says yes,” she told Lan. “It is stronger when someone can ask why and still be welcome.”

隔天早上，幾位家長來教室查看。一名家長問，計畫失敗是否會讓學校賠錢。嵐拿出費用表，說明哪些材料可以退還或重複使用。另一位問，孩子若發出假訊號該怎麼辦。她展示保護罩與站名規則。家長們沒有全部贊同，但問題讓計畫更完善。大家離開後，工坊老師笑著對嵐說：「團隊不會因所有人都說好而更強。真正強的是有人能問為什麼，而且仍然受到歡迎。」

At the end of the week, the first complete frame stood in the college yard. The glass was not perfect, and a careful hand was needed to turn it. However, it worked in strong sunlight. Lan watched a flash travel from one building to another and felt hope rise in her chest. Then she looked at the cost sheet again. A second frame would need money; a third would need volunteers; every frame would need a trained watcher. She could see the expense ahead, but she could also see a way to share it. Instead of asking one rich person to rescue the valley, she would ask many neighbors to build something they could all understand.

週末時，第一座完整鏡架立在學院院子裡。玻璃不算完美，轉動時也得小心，但在強烈陽光下確實能用。嵐看著閃光從一棟樓傳到另一棟樓，胸口升起希望。接著她又看了一眼成本表。第二座鏡架需要錢，第三座需要志工，而每一座都需要受過訓練的守望者。她看得見往後的開支，也看得見共同分擔的方法。她不想請一個富人拯救山谷，而是請許多鄰居一起建造大家都懂得使用的東西。
