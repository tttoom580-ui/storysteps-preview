The ceasefire treaty was written in three languages. It recognized the surrender of Urian's army but said nothing about the peaceful residence of former enemies. Politicians had treated that silence as permanent permission to expel them. Mira asked a language expert to examine the wording. The expert confirmed that the treaty neither granted automatic residence nor banned future applications. Urian accepted the finding. He had hoped for a forgotten clause that could solve everything. Instead, Mira prepared an ordinary legal argument: the current residence law applied to anyone meeting its conditions. A treaty's silence could not invent a restriction that lawmakers had never enacted.
