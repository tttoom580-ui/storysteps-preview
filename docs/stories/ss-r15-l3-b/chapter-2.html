承諾的價錢

The Price of a Promise

裂痕也能盛住光 · 第 2 / 25 章 · 2000–3000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

An spread her budget across a table in the town library. The studio had been closed for a decade, and the repair costs seemed larger every time she counted them. Professor Wu, who taught local geography, had offered to help her understand the neighborhood rather than lend her money. He arrived wearing a neat necktie and carrying a map marked with bus stops. The nearest stop looked close, but its path climbed a steep hill. A passenger with heavy bags would have to rest twice. An had imagined visitors arriving as easily as she did on her bicycle.

林安在鎮立圖書館的桌上攤開預算。工坊已關閉十年，每次重新計算，維修費似乎都變得更高。教授地方地理的吳教授願意幫她了解社區，而不是直接借錢。他繫著整齊的領帶，帶來一張標出公車站的地圖。最近的站牌看來很近，路卻要爬上陡坡。提重物的乘客可能必須休息兩次。林安原本想像每個人都能像她騎單車一樣輕鬆到達。

An had met Wu at a charity bowling event the previous semester. He was expressive when he spoke about old streets but careful with numbers. “A public workshop cannot be a hungry beast swallowing support,” he said with a smile. He did not want to frighten her. He wanted her to show the cost of materials, the electricity bill, the emergency kit, and a safe entrance. When An admitted that her first plan mixed paid classes with free visits, Wu asked her to separate them. She began to regret how quickly she had called the project simple.

林安上學期在慈善保齡球活動認識吳教授。他談起老街時表情豐富，面對數字則格外謹慎。「公共工坊不能變成吞掉所有補助的飢餓野獸。」他笑著說。他不是要嚇她，而是要她列出材料費、電費、急救包和安全入口的成本。林安承認第一份計畫把付費課與免費參觀混在一起，吳教授請她分開計算。她開始後悔太快把計畫稱作簡單。

At the committee meeting, a man who was clearly drunk tried to interrupt. The chair asked him to sit down and offered water rather than arguing. Another voter, a retired teacher, wanted every cost visible on the public noticeboard. A wealthy shop owner suggested charging a high entrance fee; An feared such a policy would reward only the greedy. “The point is to let people make something with their hands,” she said, “not to prove they can afford a ticket.” She proposed a free monthly open table and paid classes with a clear limit on places.

委員會開會時，一名明顯喝醉的男子不斷打斷發言。主席請他坐下，並遞水給他，沒有爭吵。另一位投票委員是退休教師，要求所有費用都公布在公告板上。一位富有的店主建議收取高額入場費；林安擔心這樣只會讓貪心的人得利。「重點是讓大家親手製作，」她說，「不是證明誰付得起門票。」她提出每月免費開放的工作桌，以及名額和收費都清楚的課程。

Wei attended as a technical adviser, not as An's representative. He carried a kit of measuring tools and a strip of lace that had caught on an old shelf, evidence that the passage was too narrow. “You may apply for a limited permit to open the display room,” he explained. “That will not permit firing the kiln.” A committee member asked if the safety risk made the whole idea impossible. Wei answered that the risk could be measured and reduced, but not wished away. An appreciated his honesty, even when it cost her an early opening.

鍾維以技術顧問身分參加，並非代表林安發言。他帶來一套測量工具，還有一條勾在舊架子上的蕾絲，證明通道太窄。「妳可以申請有限度開放展示室的許可，」他解釋，「但那不等於准許使用窯爐。」有位委員問安全風險是否讓整個計畫不可能實現。鍾維回答，風險可以測量、降低，卻不能靠願望消失。即使因此無法提早開張，林安仍欣賞他的誠實。

When the committee took a break, An sat beside the entrance with her notebook. She had counted the cost of clay and heating but forgotten the hours required to answer questions and clean shared tools. Wu suggested adding those hours to her budget. “Work does not become free just because you enjoy it,” he said. An drew a line beneath her total and added wages for staff, cleaning materials, and insurance. A volunteer asked whether she might charge less for people who were unemployed. An answered that a sliding fee could be considered if the missing income was covered openly rather than pushed onto unpaid workers. She also changed the visitors' map. The steep route was no longer marked as the only way up; the map now showed the bus connection and a place to rest. Her plan remained imperfect, but it had begun to describe a real place with real people and costs.

委員會休息時，林安坐在入口旁打開筆記本。她計算過陶土與加熱成本，卻忘了回答問題和清潔共用工具所花的時間。吳教授建議把這些時數也列入預算。「喜歡一份工作，不代表那份勞動就免費。」他說。林安在總額下畫線，加入員工薪資、清潔材料與保險。志工問，能否讓失業者少付一點。林安回答，可以考慮彈性收費，但差額要公開補足，不能轉嫁給無薪工作者。她也修正訪客地圖，不再把陡坡標成唯一入口，而是加上公車轉乘與休息點。計畫仍不完美，卻開始描述一個有真實居民與成本的地方。

By evening, An had drawn three columns: repairs now, classes later, and dreams that could wait. The budget no longer looked beautiful, but it had become useful. Wu pointed at the map and suggested inviting residents who had never climbed the hill. An sent messages asking them what would make a visit possible. She had been eager to open a studio; now she was learning to build an invitation that other people could actually accept. When she left, the empty form for the permit sat in her bag beside a little clay spoon. Both reminded her that care began with details.

傍晚，林安畫了三欄：「現在維修」、「以後開課」與「可以等待的夢想」。預算不再漂亮，卻終於實用。吳教授指著地圖，建議邀請從未爬上這座山坡的居民。林安傳訊詢問大家，要怎樣才能方便到訪。她原本只急著開工坊，如今開始學著提出別人真正能接受的邀請。離開時，申請許可的空白表格和一把小陶匙都躺在包裡，提醒她關心往往始於細節。
