The First Story Tiles

裂痕也能盛住光 · 第 3 / 25 章 · 2000–3000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

An invited neighbors to bring a small object and tell its background before shaping a clay tile. An elderly woman arrived with a bundle of bamboo brushes; a student carried a chipped mug. A downtown dealer offered to buy the old mug, but the student preferred to keep it. “Its chip came from my first day of work,” she said. “Money cannot buy that day again.” An asked everyone to chat in pairs, not to tell the whole room a private history. The simple choice made shy visitors more willing to speak.

林安邀請鄰居帶一件小物，先說說它的背景，再捏一片故事陶片。一位老婦帶來一捆竹刷，學生則帶著缺角的馬克杯。市中心的一名古物商想買那只杯子，學生卻寧可留著。「這個缺角是我第一天上班留下的，」她說，「再多錢也買不回那一天。」林安讓大家兩人一組聊天，而不必對全場公開私事。這個簡單選擇讓害羞的訪客也願意開口。

A civil discussion started when someone asked who could own the stories after they were made into clay. Mei, a community organizer, suggested a democratic process: each maker could keep, lend, or withdraw a tile. They wrote those choices on paper before touching the clay. A baker brought a doughnut for each volunteer, then said the town had grown poorer since a factory closed. The word poverty was not an abstract idea to her; it was a customer asking to pay next week. An listened without turning the baker's experience into a sales story.

有人問：故事做成陶片後，究竟由誰擁有？討論因此變得正式而有禮。社區工作者美怡提議採民主程序：每位創作者都能保留、借出或撤回自己的陶片。大家在碰陶土前，先把這些選擇寫成文件。麵包師傅替志工各帶一個甜甜圈，又說工廠關閉後小鎮愈來愈窮。對她來說，貧窮不是抽象名詞，而是顧客問能不能下週再付款。林安聆聽，卻沒有把她的經歷變成推銷故事。

At lunchtime, the group chose to dine outside beneath a large tree. A pigeon watched them from a wall, and the children laughed when it made a mighty leap toward a fallen crumb. Wu brought an old poll about neighborhood needs. In it, residents had asked for evening workshops, but almost no one had been invited to explain why. Mei proposed a new poll with questions written by residents themselves. “For the sake of fairness,” she said, “we should know whose voices are missing.” An agreed to let the answers change her plan.

午餐時，大家選擇到大樹下用餐。一隻鴿子站在牆上看著人群，突然朝掉落的麵包屑奮力一躍，逗得孩子們大笑。吳教授拿來一份舊的社區需求民調，居民曾希望開晚間課，卻幾乎沒人被邀請說明原因。美怡提議重新調查，讓居民自己寫題目。「為了公平，」她說，「我們得知道誰的聲音缺席了。」林安同意讓答案真正改變計畫。

The clay was a soft substance, but it showed every careless push of a thumb. Wei explained that the pieces needed to dry slowly or their small cracks would deepen. When someone asked whether the kilns were ready, An said no and showed the inspection notice. She expected disappointment. Instead, one girl suggested leaving the tiles unfinished until the team could fire them safely. It was a practical leap from wanting a quick result to wanting a lasting one. An suddenly recognized that a community could help her think, not merely watch her work.

陶土是一種柔軟的物質，卻會留下每一次粗心按壓的痕跡。鍾維解釋，陶片必須慢慢乾燥，否則細裂會加深。有人問窯爐能否使用，林安坦白說還不行，並展示檢查通知。她原本以為大家會失望，沒想到一個女孩提議先保留未燒的陶片，等安全時再燒。這是從急著看到成果，到願意追求長久作品的一大步。林安忽然明白，社區不只是觀眾，也能幫她思考。

After the afternoon class, An asked the group how they might react if one of their stories appeared online without permission. A teenager said she would feel angry even if the photograph looked beautiful. An invited her to explain why. The girl answered that a picture could be passed from person to person long after the original maker had changed her mind. Mei suggested placing a simple notice beside the shared worktable: photographing one's own piece was allowed, but photographing another person's story required agreement. The notice could not control every phone, yet it gave the group a clear standard. An tested it by asking a visitor who had already taken several pictures to review them. He deleted one image after learning its owner had declined public sharing. No one shouted or accused him of bad intentions. The conversation helped the group recognize that respecting a boundary was a practical skill, not a punishment.

下午課程結束後，林安問大家：如果自己的故事未經同意就出現在網路上，會如何反應？一名少女說，即使照片很美，她仍會生氣。林安請她解釋。少女表示，照片可能不斷被轉傳，遠在創作者改變心意之後仍繼續流通。美怡建議在共用工作桌旁張貼簡單說明：可以拍自己的作品，但拍攝別人的故事必須先取得同意。公告無法控制每支手機，卻能建立清楚標準。林安請一名已拍了幾張照片的訪客檢查內容；對方得知其中一件作品不許公開後，主動刪除照片。沒有人叫罵或指控他懷有惡意。這次對話讓大家知道，尊重界線是一種實際技能，不是懲罰。

Before leaving, each participant wrote a short note beside a tile. One note described an ordinary meal, another a family argument, and a third a journey by train. An placed the notes in separate envelopes, with names concealed unless their owners requested credit. Mei warned that a beautiful exhibit could still hurt someone if the artist treated a personal memory as public property. An looked at the small unfinished wall and changed the title of her future show. It would not be “My Town in Clay.” It would be “The Stories We Choose to Share.”

離開前，每位參與者都在陶片旁寫了短短的說明。有的寫一頓普通的飯，有的寫家人爭吵，還有人寫火車旅程。林安把便條分別放進信封，除非作者希望署名，否則不公開姓名。美怡提醒她：即使展覽很美，若藝術家把私人記憶當作公共財產，仍可能傷害人。林安望著尚未完成的小牆，改了未來展覽的名字。它不再叫《我的泥土小鎮》，而是《我們選擇分享的故事》。
