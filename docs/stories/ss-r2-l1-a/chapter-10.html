兩張訂單，一個號碼

Two Orders, One Number

打烊後，這間店由我作主 · 第 10 / 25 章 · 1000以下 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

The morning after the festival, Rina sat between Leo and Mr. Park in the center of the shop. Leo opened the online order page. Rina held a pencil and a simple paper list. One customer had ordered half a box of apples. Another had ordered a net of oranges. But both orders had the same number. "Is that right?" Mr. Park asked. Leo shook his head. "No. The computer gave the same number to two different people." Rina wrote the names on two lines. A wrong number could make one order look like two sales, or two sales look like one.

節慶隔天早上，莉娜坐在店中央，旁邊是里歐與朴先生。里歐打開線上訂單頁。莉娜拿著鉛筆和一張簡單清單。一位顧客訂了半箱蘋果，另一位訂了一網袋橘子，但兩張訂單的編號竟一樣。「這樣對嗎？」朴先生問。里歐搖頭。「不對。電腦把同一個號碼給了兩個不同的人。」莉娜把名字寫在兩行。錯誤編號會讓一筆訂單看似兩筆，也可能讓兩筆看似一筆。

Ben's older brother came to help. He was a teacher and liked to show people an easy way to learn. He took two small cards and put them on the table. "If I hit this card with my hand, do I change the other card?" he asked. "No," Rina said. "Then each sale needs its own number." He moved the cards apart. "This is the same idea as a seat number at a game." Rina liked the example. She had spent a lot of time looking at hard pages, and this small lesson made the problem easier to see.

班的哥哥來幫忙。他是一位老師，喜歡用簡單的方法教人。他拿出兩張小卡片放在桌上。「如果我用手拍這張卡，會改變另一張嗎？」他問。「不會。」莉娜說。「所以每筆交易都需要自己的號碼。」他把卡片分開。「和比賽座位號碼是一樣的道理。」莉娜喜歡這個例子。她花了許多時間看難懂的帳頁，這堂小課讓問題容易看清。

Rina was still worried about the missing money. She asked how many orders had the same number in the past month. Leo could not see the old page without the owner, so Mr. Park opened it for him. They found three pairs. Each pair had one food order and one money entry. "This does not tell us who made the mistake," Rina said. "It tells us what we must check." She looked at the right side of the page and saw a note in Mark's voice: "Use one number for the day." It was not a safe rule.

莉娜仍擔心短少的錢。她問上個月有幾張訂單用了相同編號。沒有店主授權，里歐不能查看舊頁，所以朴先生替他打開。他們找到三組重複編號，每組都有一張食品訂單與一筆金額紀錄。「這還不能說明誰犯錯，」莉娜說，「但告訴我們該查什麼。」她看到頁面右側有馬克留下的備註：「當天使用一個號碼。」那並不是安全的規則。

Mark came in when they were about to finish. "Why are you looking at my old work?" he asked. Mr. Park answered, "Because this is my shop. I need to understand the way we use." Mark said the online program was slow and one number was simple. Rina replied, "Simple is good only when it is also correct." She did not love speaking to him like that, but she wanted to be clear. Mr. Park asked Mark to help fix the old order list. Mark said he had no time. Mr. Park asked him to make time the next day.

他們快做完時，馬克進來了。「你們為什麼查我的舊工作？」他問。朴先生回答：「因為這是我的店，我得了解我們使用的方法。」馬克說線上程式太慢，用一個號碼比較簡單。莉娜答：「簡單只有在正確時才是好事。」她並不喜歡這樣對馬克說話，但希望把事情講清楚。朴先生要求馬克一起修正舊訂單表。馬克說沒時間，朴先生請他隔天挪出時間。

In the afternoon, a customer called. She had paid for apples, but the shop page still showed an empty order. Rina found the paper card from the festival and showed her how to check the number. The woman was blind in one eye, so Rina read the number slowly and clearly. The customer thanked her. Rina did not ask her to solve the shop's problem. She only helped her get the food she had paid for. Then she wrote down the call, so the next worker could see what had happened.

下午，一位顧客打電話來。她付過蘋果的錢，店裡的頁面卻顯示訂單未完成。莉娜找出節慶使用的紙卡，向她說明如何核對編號。女子有一隻眼睛看不見，莉娜便慢慢把號碼唸清楚。顧客向她道謝。莉娜沒有請顧客解決店裡的麻煩，只幫她拿到已付錢的食物，再寫下這通電話，好讓下一位員工知道發生了什麼。

Later, Rina asked the cook how much time she must spend on each food order. The cook said an apple cake took longer than a bowl of ice cream. "We cannot kick the clock and make it move faster," she joked. Rina laughed and wrote down the time beside each item. She wanted to see the true cost of food and work together. A low price was good for customers only if the shop could still pay its workers fairly. That was another number worth checking.

後來，莉娜問廚師每筆餐點要花多少時間。廚師說蘋果蛋糕比一碗冰淇淋花得久。「我們總不能踢時鐘，讓時間走快一點。」她開玩笑。莉娜笑了，在每樣餐點旁記下時間。她想一起看清食物與工作真正的成本。低價格若讓商店無法公平付員工薪水，也不見得對客人好。這是另一個值得查核的數字。

When the shop closed, Rina looked at two orders on the screen. They were different, even if the old page had given them one number. "Every person has a place in this shop," she said to Ben. "Every order should have one too." Ben smiled. "That sounds like a rule you can teach," he said. Rina wrote it in large letters and placed it by the computer. It would not bring old money back by itself. But tomorrow no one would have to guess which order was which. That was a good way forward.

店門關閉時，莉娜看著螢幕上的兩筆訂單。即使舊頁面把它們編成同一號，它們仍是不同的訂單。「每個人在這間店都有自己的位置，」她對班說，「每張訂單也應該如此。」班笑了。「這聽起來像可以教給別人的規則。」莉娜用大字寫下它，貼在電腦旁。這規則無法自己找回舊錢，卻能讓明天的員工不必猜哪張是哪張。那是向前走的好方法。
