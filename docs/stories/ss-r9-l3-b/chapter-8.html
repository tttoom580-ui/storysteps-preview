記憶的許可

Permission to Remember

把海風留在最後一格 · 第 8 / 25 章 · 2000–3000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

The archive office stood above an old baggage room, where the port had once kept a wooden buffalo statue from a visiting exhibition. An antique bullet was also displayed there, locked in glass and labeled as a harmless museum object. Nia had come as a citizen requesting access to public records, not as a collector. The clerk handed her a form and explained the process for each kind of permission. Some documents were public; others contained private names and needed review. A small controller on his desk opened the records cabinet. When he reached across the keys, his shadow covered the form, and Nia asked him to move his hand so she could read every line.

檔案室位在舊行李間樓上。港口以前曾存放外地展覽送來的木雕水牛，裡面還展示一枚上鎖陳列、標為無危險博物館藏品的古董子彈。夏黎是以申請公共紀錄的公民身分來到這裡，而不是收藏家。職員遞給她表格，解釋各類許可的申請程序。有些文件公開，有些包含私人姓名，必須先審查。桌上一個小控制器能打開檔案櫃。職員伸手按鍵時，影子遮住表單，夏黎便請他移開手，讓自己讀清楚每一行。

The clerk was talkative and meant well, but he tried to answer questions Nia had not asked. He described how a wealthy shipping family had donated the archive building. He praised their generosity, then admitted that the city still owed the original owners a report about the material transferred there. Nia asked for the relevant transaction list. “I cannot just grab that file,” he said. “I need my supervisor's approval.” She thanked him rather than yell at him. A public service should not depend on a clerk breaking rules to be helpful. She wrote a formal request and took a numbered receipt, even though she knew that a slow procedure could be frustrating.

職員很健談，也沒有惡意，卻不斷回答夏黎沒問的問題。他描述一個富有航運家族如何捐贈檔案館建築，稱讚對方慷慨，又承認市府還欠原始所有人一份移交報告。夏黎要求查閱相關交易清單。「我不能隨手把檔案抓出來，」職員說，「要主管批准。」她沒有對他大吼，而是道謝。好的公共服務不該仰賴職員違反規則來幫忙。她填好正式申請，領了有編號的收據，儘管知道緩慢的程序很令人挫折。

Outside, Jo was collecting old street sounds for a separate class. She had recently recorded a shopkeeper making crispy rice in a cooker and two workers arguing about litter near the pier. Nia asked whether either person knew about the river gate. Jo said that was not the reason she had recorded them and refused to pass the audio to the film project without asking again. Nia agreed. The fact that information existed did not mean its owner had granted unlimited use. This mattered especially to people who had once experienced sexual harassment at work and had told their stories only to a trusted listener. Nobody should discover that a private account had become a public exhibit.

屋外，小喬正為另一門課蒐集老街聲響。她最近錄下店主用鍋具做酥脆米餅的聲音，也錄了兩名工人爭論碼頭垃圾的對話。夏黎問其中是否有人知道河閘的事。小喬說，當初錄音並不是為了電影計畫，因此沒有再次徵求同意前，她不會交出音檔。夏黎同意。資訊存在，不表示擁有者同意無限制使用。這對曾在工作中遭遇性騷擾、只向可信任的人訴說經歷的當事者尤其重要。沒有人應該突然發現私人陳述變成公共展品。

“Lately, the office has received more requests than we can answer,” the clerk admitted when Nia returned for her receipt. “We owe each applicant a clear date for the next response.” Nia suggested posting that schedule openly. It would not make the review faster, but people could plan around a known delay rather than fear their papers had disappeared.

夏黎回來領收據時，職員承認：「最近我們收到的申請，比能處理的還多。我們欠每位申請人一個清楚的下次回覆日期。」夏黎建議公開時程。這不會加快審查，但人們至少能依已知延遲安排，而不用擔心文件無端消失。

By afternoon the office approved a list of meeting dates, but not the private attendance sheets. Nia found that the earliest gate documents mentioned a public request to clear the channel. Later reports described an expensive harbor project with no clear explanation of its costs. She wrote the two facts separately. A request could be reasonable, while the method chosen to answer it could still cause harm. The clerk offered to send a web link to the public files and a printed index of the sealed records. Nia accepted both. She was learning that an incomplete answer, properly labeled, was more useful than a confident guess.

到了下午，檔案室核准提供會議日期清單，卻沒有提供私人出席簿。夏黎發現最早的閘門文件提到居民要求清理航道，後來的報告則描述一項昂貴、卻未清楚說明支出的港口工程。她把這兩件事分開記錄。提出要求可能很合理，選擇的執行方式卻仍可能造成傷害。職員提供公共檔案的網頁連結和封存紀錄的紙本索引，夏黎兩者都收下。她逐漸理解，清楚標明不完整的答案，比自信的猜測有用。

On the way home, a small child asked why Nia carried so many papers. She answered that they belonged to a story about how the harbor had changed. The child's mother smiled, then asked if the old flood film would show children who had never agreed to be filmed. Nia said the team was working on exactly that question. She did not promise that every face would appear or disappear. They needed to ask people, protect those who preferred privacy, and explain when permission could no longer be obtained. The mother seemed satisfied by the careful answer. Nia realized that ethical work did not always sound impressive. Sometimes it sounded like refusing to make promises too soon.

回家路上，一個小孩問夏黎為什麼抱那麼多紙。她回答，那些紙記錄港口如何改變的故事。孩子的母親微笑後又問，舊洪水電影是否會出現當年根本不曾同意拍攝的孩子。夏黎說，團隊正努力處理這個問題。她沒有保證每張臉都會出現或消失，而是說明他們必須詢問當事人、保護希望保有隱私的人，並解釋無法再取得許可時的做法。母親似乎滿意這個謹慎的答案。夏黎發現，負責任的工作不一定聽起來很厲害；有時它只是拒絕太早承諾。

At night, Eli brought a bag of oranges and the repaired controller for the projector. He asked Nia whether the records office had helped. “Yes, though not in the way I expected,” she said. He listened while she described the different levels of access. Then he made a suggestion: the public screening could include a table showing what they knew, what they did not know, and who had approved each use. It was a practical idea, and Nia thanked him. She also told him she wanted to design the final screen herself. “Of course,” he replied. Their work together was becoming easier because neither treated agreement as ownership of the other's choices.

夜裡，以安帶來一袋橘子和修好的放映機控制器，問檔案室是否有幫上忙。「有，但和我預期的不一樣，」夏黎說。以安聽她解釋不同層級的查閱許可，接著建議公開放映時附上一張表，列出已知、未知，以及每項使用得到誰的批准。這是實用的想法，夏黎向他道謝，也說她想自己設計最後的展示畫面。「當然，」以安回答。兩人的合作越來越順利，因為誰都沒有把彼此的同意當成對對方選擇的所有權。
