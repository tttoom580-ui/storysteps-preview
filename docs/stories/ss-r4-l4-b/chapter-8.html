Who Gets to Speak

1997，青禾市場的第二張招牌 · 第 8 / 25 章 · 3000–4000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

The town committee opened its hearing in a room with twenty chairs and more than fifty visitors. A bulletin on the door said property owners would speak first, followed by registered vendors and then the public. Yingshu asked whether tenants could acquire the same speaking time as owners. The chair said the categories were only a guide, not a judgment of value. "Then let us measure time fairly," she suggested. A young clerk agreed to keep a public record. The room grew quiet when he placed two clocks on the table, one for official speakers and one for everyone else.

鎮公所的委員會在只有二十張椅子、卻來了五十多人的房間開會。門口公告說產權人先發言，接著是登記攤商，最後才是一般民眾。映書問租攤者是否能和所有權人得到相同發言時間。主席說分類只是引導，不代表價值高低。「那就公平計時吧。」她建議。一名年輕職員同意公開記錄，並在桌上擺兩個計時鐘，分別記官員和其他人的發言。

The first speaker was a florist preparing flowers for a bridegroom. She had rented her corner for fourteen years and feared that her lack of formal ownership would make her invisible. A retired composer next described the songs he had heard in the market courtyard. His words carried enthusiasm, but he admitted nostalgia could not replace a safety estimate. The hearing was not a competition in beauty. It was a chance to show what activities constituted daily life, and which of them depended on the market's location. Yingshu thanked each speaker, even those who favored complete rebuilding.

第一位發言的是正替新郎準備花束的花販。她租那個角落十四年，擔心沒有正式產權就變成看不見的人。接著一名退休作曲家談起市場中庭聽過的歌聲。他講得熱情，卻也承認懷舊不能取代安全估價。聽證會不是比誰說得動聽，而是呈現哪些活動構成日常生活，以及它們如何依賴市場的位置。映書感謝每位發言者，包括支持全部重建的人。

A contractor argued that the existing drainage had been invaded by tree roots and that the nearby fertile field would soon become a parking lot. He showed a chart of rainfall intensity and claimed no repairs could succeed. The engineer asked him to identify the calculation behind that conclusion. He could not. The numbers described a real problem, she said, but did not settle whether a new drain, a raised walkway, or relocation was best. A gulf opened between people who thought criticism meant denying danger and those who wanted to compare solutions. Yingshu asked the clerk to record the difference.

承包商說樹根侵入現有排水系統，附近肥沃的田地又即將改成停車場。他拿出雨量強度圖，斷言任何修繕都不會成功。工程師問這個結論的計算依據，他答不出來。她說數據確實描述了真實問題，卻無法單獨決定新排水、抬高走道或搬遷哪個較好。現場分成兩派：有人認為質疑就是否認危險，有人則想比較方案。映書請職員清楚記下分歧。

The next argument concerned gender. Several men assumed that family stalls were controlled by their husbands even when women did the buying, selling, and bookkeeping. Mei-ling stood up. "My late father gave me the keys, not a husband," she said. Her voice did not dominate the room, but it changed the subject. Yingshu explained that the survey had counted work rather than surnames. A civilian committee, she argued, needed evidence about who depended on each stall. She noticed jealousy in one man's expression when the chair praised Mei-ling's records, yet she refused to turn the moment into a personal contest.

下一個爭議與性別有關。幾位男子假定家族攤位由丈夫掌管，即使真正採購、販售、記帳的都是女性。美玲站起來說：「鑰匙是亡父交給我的，不是哪位丈夫給的。」她的聲音沒有壓過全場，卻改變了話題。映書說調查數的是實際工作，不是姓氏。民間參與的委員會，應根據誰依賴攤位的證據來判斷。她注意到一名男子因主席稱讚美玲的紀錄而露出嫉妒，卻不讓會議變成人身競爭。

At the side desk, the clerk entered figures into simple computer software used by the town office. He showed Yingshu how an abstract category such as "small business" could hide different kinds of work. Together they made a systematic list of market activities, with separate lines for food storage, loading, repairs, and customers with limited mobility. The clerk, a learned young man fresh from college, warned that a computer table was only as honest as the questions behind it. Yingshu agreed. A printed list did not become true merely because it looked official.

一旁的書記把數字輸入鎮公所使用的簡易電腦軟體。他讓映書看，像「小型企業」這種抽象分類，可能掩蓋各種不同的工作。兩人把市場活動系統地分列：食物儲存、裝卸、維修、行動不便顧客。這位剛畢業的博學年輕人提醒，電腦表格是否誠實，取決於背後問的是什麼問題。映書同意。資料印得正式，不代表內容就自動成真。

As the afternoon wore on, the chair looked for a cue to end the discussion. Yingshu offered a compromise: post the draft minutes for one week, accept written corrections, and hold a second session on costs. Nobody would be asked to approve a construction plan that day. The chair accepted after consulting the engineer. Outside, the crowd had mixed feelings. Some called the hearing slow. Others were relieved to have spoken at all. Yingshu folded her notes and realized that fairness was not a grand statement. It was the repeated work of making room for another voice.

下午將盡，主席等著結束討論的提示。映書提出折衷方案：會議紀錄草稿公告一週，接受書面更正，另開一場專談成本的會議。當天不要求任何人批准施工計畫。主席和工程師商量後接受。會場外眾人心情複雜：有人嫌程序太慢，有人慶幸終於能說話。映書收起筆記，明白公平不是華麗宣言，而是一次次為別人的聲音留位置。

That night, Xiu-ying asked whether Yingshu was proud of speaking. "Not exactly," she answered. "I am proud that Mei-ling could speak without asking my permission." Her mother laughed and passed her the bowl of rice she had kept warm. Across the street, a wedding procession moved slowly through the lamps, and the florist waved from the front. Life continued around the unfinished dispute. Yingshu copied the date of the next hearing into the family calendar, then wrote beneath it: Nobody's silence is a vote. She wanted the rule visible before anyone counted supporters.

那晚，秀英問映書是否以發言為榮。「不完全是，」她說，「我更高興美玲不用先問我就能說話。」母親笑了，把保溫的飯遞給她。街對面，婚禮隊伍在燈下緩緩經過，花販在前頭招手。爭議未完，生活仍繼續。映書把下次聽證日期抄上家用月曆，下面又寫：沉默不代表贊成。她希望任何人計票前都先看見這句話。
