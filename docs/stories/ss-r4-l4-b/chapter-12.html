不見的收據

The Missing Receipt

1997，青禾市場的第二張招牌 · 第 12 / 25 章 · 3000–4000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

The missing receipt appeared in an unlikely place: a plastic bin behind the old grain shop. A-Zhan found it while sorting papers saved from a flood years earlier. It recorded a payment to a shipping agent for thirty storage shelves, yet the market had received only twelve. The date belonged to another era of town government, before the present redevelopment debate. Yingshu resisted the urge to call it theft. She needed definite evidence about where the remaining shelves had gone. A receipt showed money paid, not goods delivered, and she did not want to make a serious accusation from half a record.

遺失的收據竟出現在舊米店後方的塑膠箱裡。阿湛整理多年前洪水後保存的紙張時找到它。上面記著付給貨運代理人三十組倉儲架的費用，市場卻只收到十二組。日期屬於另一個鎮政時代，比現在的改建爭議早得多。映書忍住不稱它是偷竊。她需要確切證據確認其餘架子去了哪裡。收據只能證明付款，不能證明交貨；她不願憑半份紀錄做嚴重指控。

The old shopkeeper remembered a burglar who had broken into the warehouse during a storm, but he could not say whether any shelves were stolen. Another vendor said the warehouse had been occupied by emergency workers that winter. Both accounts might explain the missing goods, yet neither was a final answer. Yingshu wrote down each comment and asked where supporting documents might be found. Her careful procedure frustrated A-Zhan. "You chase paper while everyone loses money," he said. She answered that a false charge could destroy someone's reputation and make an honest investigation harder to pursue.

老店主記得某次暴風雨裡有竊賊闖入倉庫，卻不確定貨架是否被偷。另一名攤商說那年冬天倉庫曾被救災人員占用。兩種說法都可能解釋貨物去向，卻都不是最後答案。映書記下每一則評論，詢問哪裡能找佐證文件。謹慎程序讓阿湛不耐煩：「大家都在賠錢，妳還追紙本。」她回答，錯誤指控可能毀掉他人名譽，也讓真正調查更難追下去。

At the library, a woman with a silver bracelet offered to help. Her father had managed shipments for the market and kept a durable ledger that survived damp summers. She was faithful to his memory but warned the records were not perfect. The ledger showed the remaining shelves had been transferred to a school shelter during a typhoon. After a tragic landslide farther inland, the shelter had supplied nearby villages for months. Yingshu felt both relief and sorrow. The shelves were not missing because someone had secretly taken them, but their public use had never been properly recorded.

圖書館裡，一位戴銀手鐲的婦人主動幫忙。她父親曾管理市場貨運，留下經得起潮濕夏季的耐用帳冊。她忠於父親的記憶，卻先警告紀錄不完美。帳上顯示其餘架子在颱風期間轉到學校避難所；內陸發生悲慘山崩後，避難所又供應鄰村數月。映書既鬆口氣又難過。架子不是遭人暗中拿走，而是公共使用過程一直沒有正式記錄。

The account raised a larger problem. If public equipment could move without a visible record, how would anyone know what the market actually owned? The town had faced hardship and inflation over the years, and older ledgers mixed prices from different periods. Yingshu asked the accountant to separate historical costs from present replacement values. She also requested a fresh inventory before the planned relocation. Some vendors complained about the extra work. Others, remembering past losses, offered to count shelves, tables, and scales after closing time. The inventory began as a search for twelve objects and emerged as a map of shared responsibilities.

這個發現帶出更大的問題：公共設備若能沒有明確紀錄就移動，誰知道市場真正擁有什麼？多年來小鎮經歷困苦與通貨膨脹，舊帳本混用了不同年代的價格。映書請會計師分開歷史成本與現在重置價值，也要求搬遷前重新盤點。有攤商抱怨增加工作，另一些想起過去損失，願意收攤後清點架子、桌子和秤。起初只是找十二件東西，最後成為一份共同責任地圖。

The schoolteacher brought a local history booklet that described the coast's tribal communities and later trading families. She reminded the group not to use these stories as decorations for a new shopping center. Each community had its own records and the right to decide what could be displayed. Yingshu agreed to ask permission before including photographs in the public exhibit. The town's history was not a collection of objects waiting for someone to claim fame. It belonged to people who still lived with its effects. Respect, she thought, had to be part of redevelopment, not something added at the opening ceremony.

老師帶來一本地方史小冊，介紹海岸原有部落社群與後來的商業家族。她提醒大家，不要把這些故事當成新商場的裝飾。各社群有自己的紀錄，也有權決定哪些能展示。映書同意在公共展覽使用照片前先取得許可。小鎮歷史不是等著別人藉以成名的物品收藏，它屬於仍承受其影響的人。她想，尊重必須是改建的一部分，不能只在開幕那天加上去。

At the end of the week, the town clerk confirmed that the public shelter still held most of the old shelves. A few had suffered complete destruction, but the remaining ones could be recovered. The clerk admitted the transfer should have been documented. Yingshu chose not to turn his admission into public humiliation. She asked for a corrected inventory, a return schedule, and a rule requiring two signatures for future transfers, and a promise to commit to accurate records. Xiu-ying said her daughter was becoming frighteningly serious. "Maybe," Yingshu answered, "but we need a system that works even when everyone means well."

週末，鎮職員確認公共避難所仍保有大部分舊架子，少數已徹底毀損，其餘可取回。職員承認當年的轉移本該留下紀錄。映書沒有把他的承認變成公開羞辱，而是要求更正清冊、歸還時程，以及未來轉移必須兩人簽名、承諾維護準確紀錄的規則。秀英說女兒變得嚴肅得可怕。「也許吧，」映書答，「但即使大家都出於好意，也需要會運作的制度。」

That night, A-Zhan brought home the first returned shelf, cleaned and ready to use. He apologized for accusing his sister of wasting time. She said the worry had been reasonable even if his conclusion had been wrong. Together they carried the shelf into the back room without disturbing their mother's sleeping cat. It was a small ending to a small mystery. The larger question remained: could the town build a market whose records, equipment, and rules were clear enough that the next generation would not have to solve the same puzzle again?

那晚，阿湛把第一組歸還的架子搬回家，已擦洗乾淨，可以再用。他為先前指責姐姐浪費時間道歉。映書說他的擔憂可以理解，只是結論下得太早。兩人把架子搬進後間，沒有吵醒母親睡著的貓。小小謎團終於有了結局，但更大的問題仍在：小鎮能不能建立一座紀錄、設備和規則都夠清楚的市場，讓下一代不必再解同一道謎？
