一張桌子的早晨

One Table, One Morning

1997，青禾市場的第二張招牌 · 第 5 / 25 章 · 3000–4000 · 英中對照

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

The first trial stall stood under a striped awning beside the bus stop. Yingshu had obtained a temporary allowance for a single table, not permission to enlarge the market. Mei-ling offered fish soup in small cups while Samir sold dried fruit. A-Zhan carried a portable scale from their mother's shop. The arrangement was deliberately modest: one day, a limited menu, clear prices, and a device to record each sale on paper. Yingshu felt anxiety rising as the first customers approached. If the event failed, opponents would call it proof that the vendors could not manage any change themselves.

第一個試營運攤設在公車站旁的條紋遮陽棚下。映書爭取到的是一張桌子的臨時使用額度，不是擴大市場的許可。美玲用小杯賣魚湯，沙米爾賣果乾，阿湛從母親店裡搬來便攜式秤。安排刻意簡單：只做一天、菜單有限、價格清楚，每筆交易都記在紙上。第一批客人走近時，映書感到焦慮。如果失敗，反對者肯定會說攤商根本無力自行改變。

An old fisherman stopped by with a canoe tied near the riverbank. He bought a cup of soup and asked if the market would abandon the harbor. Yingshu explained that no site had been chosen. A favorable location for one vendor might be useless to another, so the trial was meant to test customer habits, not settle the whole question. "You talk like a book," he said, then used a local idiom about a crab trying to walk in a straight line. They both laughed. Yingshu wrote down that he came from the harbor side and preferred a morning stall.

一名老漁夫把獨木舟繫在河岸附近，停下買湯，問市場是不是要放棄港口。映書解釋尚未選址；對一攤有利的位置，對另一攤可能毫無用處。試營運只是測顧客習慣，不是替全部問題定案。「妳講話像書本。」漁夫說，還用一個螃蟹硬要直走的在地成語取笑她。兩人都笑了。映書記下他從港口方向來，偏好早晨營業。

Children from the nearby school arrived carrying instruments. Their music teacher had arranged a tiny orchestra to play at the opening, but the official permit allowed no stage. They adjusted the plan: three students stood beside the wall, keeping the passage clear. One boy treated the conductor as his idol and copied every movement of her hands. The performance drew a crowd, and the noise made Yingshu realize that a successful event could create its own safety problem. She asked the students to pause until the walkway emptied. Her objection was about space, not about music.

附近學校的孩子帶著樂器來了。音樂老師原想安排小型管弦樂團開場，但許可沒有舞台使用權。他們調整安排，讓三名學生靠牆演奏，保留通道。一個男孩把指揮當偶像，學她的每個手勢。表演吸引人群，映書才發現成功也可能帶來安全問題。她請學生暫停，等走道清空再演。她反對的是擁擠，不是音樂。

A young mother suggested placing the price list at a child's eye level. Samir added pictures for people who could not read every word. Their imaginative changes had an immediate impact: fewer customers asked the same questions, and the line moved smoothly. Yingshu noticed that one small syllable in the handwritten sign made the name of a spice confusing. She corrected it with a thick black pen. Xiu-ying watched from across the road, pretending to count coins. Her face softened when she saw her daughter giving customers patient explanations instead of arguing with officials.

一位年輕母親建議把價目表貼到孩子也看得見的高度。沙米爾替不識所有文字的人加上圖片。這些有想像力的改動立刻見效：重複詢問變少，隊伍也順了。映書發現手寫招牌上一個音節讓香料名稱容易誤解，便用粗黑筆改正。秀英隔街看著，假裝在數零錢。看見女兒耐心替顧客說明，而非只跟官員爭辯，她的神情柔和下來。

Trouble arrived after lunch. A private developer's representative wanted to seize the table for an unscheduled publicity photograph. His assistant promised that new shops would bring prosperity, then asked everyone to stand behind a company banner. Several vendors began a protest, afraid their participation would be used as approval. Yingshu stepped between them. She showed the permit and said the company could observe like any customer but could not take control of the event. The representative called her difficult. "There is a distinct difference," she answered, "between asking a question and giving consent." He withdrew the banner.

午餐後出了狀況。開發商代表想把桌子拿去拍臨時安排的宣傳照。助理說新店面會帶來繁榮，請大家站到公司布條後。幾名攤商開始抗議，怕自己的參與被當成支持改建。映書站到中間，出示許可，說公司可以像其他顧客一樣觀察，不能接管活動。代表說她難搞。「提問和同意之間，有明確差別。」她回答。代表把布條收了回去。

At closing time, the team compared planned sales with real receipts. They had made a small profit, but the profit would disappear if they included everyone's unpaid hours. Yingshu refused to hide that fact. She listed the necessary adjustments: a safer queue, a better water supply, and paid work for loading. Samir suggested a second test near the ferry, where customers might have different needs. The others agreed, but only after they finished cleaning the pavement. "A test isn't a victory parade," Mei-ling said. "It's a way to find what still needs fixing."

收攤時，大家比較預估營業額與實際收據。帳面上有些小獲利，但若計入所有人的無薪工時，就不算賺錢。映書不願隱瞞，列出需要調整的事：更安全的排隊區、較可靠的用水、以及付費裝卸工作。沙米爾提議在渡船旁再測一次，因為客人可能有不同需求。大家同意，但先一起清理地面。「測試不是勝利遊行，」美玲說，「是找出還有哪裡需要修。」

Late that evening, Xiu-ying placed a second, smaller sign beside the family shop name: Questions welcome. Yingshu traced the letters with a finger. Her mother explained that the original sign had survived many storms because they repaired it whenever the wood cracked. "A market can change, too," she said. Yingshu thought of the fishermen, students, customers, and worried vendors. None had offered a complete rescue. Together they had done something more useful: tested a choice, recorded its limits, and kept the next choice open. For the first time in a week, she slept without dreaming of the red notice.

深夜，秀英在自家店名旁掛上第二塊較小的牌子：「歡迎提問。」映書用手指描著字。母親說原招牌經過許多風雨，因為木頭裂了就修，才一直留到現在。「市場也能改變。」她說。映書想起漁夫、學生、顧客和擔心的攤商。沒有人給出完整救援辦法，他們卻一起做了更有用的事：測試一種選擇、記下限制，並保留下一次選擇的可能。一週以來，她第一次沒有夢見紅色公告。
