A Ledger for Everyone

1997，青禾市場的第二張招牌 · 第 3 / 25 章 · 3000–4000 · 英中對照

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

By Monday, Yingshu had given herself an assignment that sounded simple: ask every vendor what moving would actually cost. She asked A-Zhan to concentrate on transport, Mei-ling on water and ice, and Samir on rented equipment. Each wrote down answers in the speaker's own words so that a personal worry would not be lost in a general category. The work moved at a slow pace. A tailor needed space for sewing machines; a vegetable seller needed shade; an elderly woman needed an intermediate stop between the bus and her stall. The market was not one business but a chain of very different lives.

星期一，映書給自己一項聽起來很簡單的任務：問清每個攤商搬遷究竟要花多少錢。她請阿湛調查運輸，美玲調查水與冰，沙米爾調查租用設備。每個人都用受訪者自己的話記下答案，避免個別憂慮被籠統分類抹平。工作進行得很慢。裁縫需要放縫紉機的空間，菜販需要遮蔭，一位老婦人則需要公車站與攤位之間的中途休息處。市場不是單一生意，而是一連串不同的生活。

At noon, Mei-ling invited them to share lunch beside the ferry landing. The ferry carried fish and passengers from the small islands twice a day. A former lifeguard now worked there selling cold drinks. He explained that emergency exits were useful only if a crowd could reach them, and he offered to walk the proposed route with the team. He did not claim to be an expert in buildings. His experience defending swimmers from dangerous currents, however, made him careful about the distance between a warning and a safe place. "People need room to move," he said. "Not a luxurious sign that points nowhere."

中午，美玲邀大家在渡船碼頭旁吃飯。渡輪每天兩次載著離島的魚貨和乘客。一名曾當過救生員的男子如今在碼頭賣冷飲。他說緊急出口只有在人群能抵達時才有用，並願意陪團隊實地走過新路線。他沒有自稱建築專家，但救援泳客的經驗使他格外注意警告地點與安全地點之間的距離。「人需要真正走得動的空間，」他說，「不是一塊豪華卻指錯方向的標示。」

Back at the stall, Yingshu found Xiu-ying doing housework behind the counter, washing jars while keeping an eye on customers. "You forgot to eat," her mother said, handing her bread with a raisin inside. On the wrapper a bakery had printed the calorie count, a surprisingly modern detail for such a modest snack. Yingshu smiled, then noticed the receipt book beneath the jars. Her mother had counted the market fee as part of household expenses for years. In the context of their family, the shop and the kitchen had never been separate. That made its possible loss harder to measure.

回到攤位時，秀英一面看顧客，一面在櫃檯後清洗罐子，做著像家事又像生意的工作。「妳忘了吃飯。」母親遞來一個裡頭有葡萄乾的麵包。包裝竟印著熱量標示，對這樣平價的點心而言顯得很新潮。映書笑了，隨後看見罐子下面的收據簿。母親多年來把市場攤費算進家用。在她們家的脈絡裡，店與廚房從來不是分開的；因此失去攤位的代價也更難計算。

The first summary produced an incredible result. Of the fifty-three operating stalls, seventeen had no independent storage, and twelve relied on family members who were not paid wages. A percentage of the proposed compensation was tied to declared sales, which would ignore much unpaid labor. Yingshu wrote that nobody should proceed with a final decision until the method of counting was explained. A visiting photographer offered to teach them photography and take pictures of the working spaces. Yingshu accepted, but asked her to seek permission from each person rather than photograph people as though the market were a display.

第一份統計出現驚人的結果：五十三個營業攤中，十七個沒有獨立倉儲，十二個依賴未領薪的家人幫忙。擬議補償的一部分按申報營業額比例計算，卻會忽略許多無償勞動。映書寫下：計算方法未說明前，不該直接做最後決定。一位到訪攝影師願意教他們攝影，並拍攤位工作照片。映書答應了，但要求每拍一人都先徵求同意，而不是把人當作展品。

That evening, a wholesaler accused her of wasting everyone's time. "You have a city education and think you can insult us with questions," he said. The remark stung because Yingshu already felt like a visitor in her childhood home. She took a breath before answering. "Your priority may be a new loading bay. Mei-ling's priority is running water. I am not asking you to agree; I am asking for both needs to be written down." The wholesaler stared at the notebook, then gave her the maximum weight his truck could safely carry through the narrow lane. It was a beginning.

當晚，一名批發商指責她浪費大家時間。「妳受過城市教育，就以為能拿問題羞辱我們？」這句話刺痛映書，因為她本就覺得自己像童年故鄉的外人。她深呼吸後回答：「你最在意裝卸區，美玲最在意供水。我沒有要求你們同意彼此，只想把兩邊需求都記下來。」批發商盯著筆記本，終於說出卡車能安全穿過窄巷的最大載重。至少是個開始。

A schoolteacher who bought vegetables every week brought her students' drawings of the market. Their pictures were colorful and inaccurate: a fish was taller than its seller, and the fruit stalls floated in the air. Yet they offered unexpected inspiration. Children understood the market as a place where people met, not just where money changed hands. Yingshu asked the teacher to explain that the drawings would be shown only with permission. The teacher agreed and suggested a public wall for questions. "No one has to defend every old stone," she said. "We can defend the right to know what happens next."

每週來買菜的老師送來學生畫的市場圖。畫很鮮豔，也很不精確：魚比魚販還高，水果攤浮在空中。但那些畫給了映書意外靈感。孩子眼中的市場不只是一處交易地，也是一個人們相遇的地方。映書請老師先說明，畫作只有獲得同意才會展示。老師答應了，並建議設立公開提問牆。「我們不用替每塊老石頭辯護，」她說，「可以替知道下一步會發生什麼的權利辯護。」

Before going home, Yingshu left the first printed survey at the entrance. It named no private incomes, only shared needs and the number of people who had offered information. Beside it she wrote a rule: any correction could be made in person, and a refusal to answer would never count as support for either side. The rule made the survey slower, but it also gave the group a way to earn trust. A-Zhan worried that they would never finish. Yingshu looked at the fifty-three numbered stalls. "We do not need to finish the argument tonight," she said. "Only the next honest page."

回家前，映書把第一份列印調查張貼在入口。上面沒有私人收入，只列共同需求與提供資料的人數。旁邊她寫下規則：任何人可當面要求更正，拒絕回答不等於支持任何一方。這讓調查變慢，卻也給大家累積信任的方法。阿湛擔心永遠做不完。映書望著五十三個編號攤位說：「我們今晚不需要結束所有爭論，只要完成下一頁誠實的紀錄。」
