紅色公告

The Red Notice

1997，青禾市場的第二張招牌 · 第 1 / 25 章 · 3000–4000 · 英中對照

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

The red notice appeared on the entrance of Qinghe Market before sunrise. Lin Yingshu found it while carrying a cushion for her mother's wooden chair. A fierce wind lifted one corner of the paper, but its meaning was clear: the market would close in ninety days. Behind her, a child began to giggle at a drawing in his notebook of a worker floating down with a parachute beside a crane. Yingshu did not laugh. She had returned from the city to help her mother for one month, not to witness the end of the business that had fed their family for thirty years.

天亮前，青禾市場入口貼出一張紅色公告。林映書替母親搬木椅的坐墊時看見了它。強風掀起紙角，但內容很清楚：市場九十天後將關閉。她身後一個孩子看著自己的筆記本，裡面畫了工人在吊車旁撐降落傘飄下來，忍不住咯咯笑。映書笑不出來。她從城市回來，本來只打算幫母親一個月，沒想過會親眼看著養活全家三十年的生意走到盡頭。

Her mother, Xiu-ying, owned a small stall selling rice, tea, and jars of preserved vegetables. She had no talent for dramatic speeches and asked only whether the notice mentioned compensation. Yingshu read it again. An official presentation was scheduled for Friday, and the notice offered a reference number for inquiries, but no clear list of payments. A visual plan showed the future market as a row of bright shops beside a car park. Nothing on it explained where old vendors would work during construction. In tiny print, a violation of the closure order could result in a fine.

母親秀英經營小攤，賣米、茶葉和醃菜。她不擅長慷慨激昂的演說，只問公告有沒有提到補償。映書重新讀了一遍：週五有官方說明會，也提供詢問編號，卻沒有清楚列出補償方式。彩色示意圖把新市場畫成停車場旁一排明亮店面，完全沒說工程期間老攤商要去哪裡。最下方的小字還警告，違反停業命令可能遭罰。

A fish seller named Mei-ling said the picture was meant to flatter rich visitors. A fruit seller argued that a new building could protect everyone from floods. The market's diversity was visible in its voices: some rented space, some inherited family stalls, and others paid a daily fee. Yingshu refused to treat disagreement as betrayal. "A defense of this place must include the people who want change," she told her mother. Xiu-ying looked pessimistic. "You have been home for three hours," she replied. "Already you are talking like someone who plans to stay."

賣魚的美玲說，圖片只是拿來討好有錢遊客。水果攤老闆卻認為新建築能防洪。市場裡的聲音本來就多元：有人租攤位，有人承繼家業，也有人按日繳費。映書不願把意見不同當作背叛。「要替這地方辯護，也得聽見想改變的人。」她對母親說。秀英神情悲觀：「妳回家才三個小時，就已經像打算留下來的人了。」

Yingshu's younger brother, A-Zhan, came in after his morning workout, carrying a basket of unsold buns. He called his sister a genius because she could read contracts faster than anyone he knew. She reminded him that reading a contract did not mean possessing the authority to change it. On the stall wall hung a photograph of their grandfather's cottage after the great flood. The family had survived by sharing food, not by winning an argument. To Xiu-ying the picture had almost spiritual value; to Yingshu it was also a warning against confusing memories with a practical plan.

弟弟阿湛晨間運動後進來，提著一籃沒賣完的包子。他稱姐姐是天才，說她看契約比誰都快。映書提醒他，看得懂不代表擁有修改契約的權力。攤位牆上掛著祖父的小屋在大水之後的照片。當年全家靠分食度過災害，並不是靠吵贏別人。對秀英來說，那張照片有近乎精神寄託的意義；對映書而言，它也提醒她不能把回憶誤認為可行計畫。

At noon, Yingshu walked the central aisle with a notebook. She counted fifty-three operating stalls, eleven empty ones, two narrow exits, and one public tap that often failed. That was a tremendous amount of information for a single morning, yet the numbers gave her something solid to convey. The fish seller pointed out where children slipped on wet tiles. The fruit seller showed her a wall that had cracked twice. Neither fact supported the easy claim that the old market was perfect. She wrote both down and left space beside each item for an independent witness.

中午，映書拿著筆記本走過中央走道。她數出五十三個營業攤位、十一個空攤、兩個狹窄出口，以及一個常故障的公用水龍頭。這一早的資訊多得驚人，但至少讓她有具體事實可以傳達。魚販指出孩子常在濕地磚上滑倒的位置，水果攤則讓她看裂過兩次的牆。這些都不能支持「舊市場完美無缺」的說法。她照實記下，並在每一項旁留欄位給獨立見證者。

She telephoned the town office from the public booth and asked for the materials behind Friday's presentation. The clerk sounded surprised that someone wanted the measurements rather than a promise. He agreed to provide a printed plan if she collected it before closing time. When Yingshu returned, her mother had placed an extra chair beside the stall. "For people who come to complain?" Yingshu asked. "For people who come to speak," Xiu-ying corrected her. "If you're going to ask questions, first give them somewhere to sit." It was a small act, but it changed the afternoon.

她到公用電話亭打給鎮公所，索取週五說明會的原始資料。職員似乎很意外竟有人要尺寸數據，而不是口頭保證。他答應下班前提供紙本圖。映書回來時，母親已在攤旁多放一張椅子。「給來抱怨的人？」映書問。「給願意說話的人。」秀英糾正她，「妳想問問題，至少先讓人有地方坐。」那只是個小動作，卻改變了整個下午。

By evening, the chair had held a butcher, a retired teacher, a delivery worker, and two customers. Their stories did not form a single demand. Some wanted repairs, some a safe replacement, and one woman simply wanted notice early enough to find another job. Yingshu wrote three questions on a board: What is unsafe? Who pays for change? Who gets to decide? She would not promise to save every brick. She promised only to carry their questions into the hall on Friday and return with answers that could be checked. Xiu-ying erased the price of yesterday's rice and made room for the board.

傍晚前，那張椅子坐過肉販、退休教師、送貨工人與兩位顧客。他們的故事拼不成單一訴求：有人要修繕，有人要安全的新場地，也有一位婦人只希望早點收到通知，好另找工作。映書在板上寫下三個問題：哪裡不安全？改變由誰付費？誰有決定權？她不保證保住每一塊磚，只承諾週五帶著問題去開會，帶回可查證的答案。秀英擦掉昨天的米價，替那塊板子騰出位置。
