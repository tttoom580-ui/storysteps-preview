The First Lunch Ticket

1995，最後一班渡輪的午餐 · 第 1 / 25 章 · 3000–4000 · 英中對照

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

In the autumn of 1995, Lin Jiahe returned to the coastal town of Shaling with two suitcases and a notebook full of prices. Her mother lived in a little cottage above the harbor. A thin cushion covered the kitchen chair, and the windows shook under a fierce sea wind. Jiahe had spent seven years managing meals at a city hospital. Now the ferry company planned to remove the small food counter on its oldest boat. Passengers would have to bring their own lunch or pay for costly food at the island resort. Jiahe could convey the problem in one sentence: the people who depended on the ferry were not all tourists. Their diversity included fish sellers, teachers, older patients, and workers who crossed twice a day. Each had a different reason to travel, but everyone needed a reliable meal.

一九九五年秋天，林佳禾帶著兩個行李箱和一本記滿價格的筆記本，回到沿海小鎮沙嶺。母親住在港口上方的小屋裡。廚房椅子鋪著薄坐墊，猛烈的海風把窗戶吹得直響。佳禾過去七年在城市醫院管理餐食，現在渡輪公司準備撤掉最老渡輪上的小食物櫃檯。乘客不是得自己帶午餐，就是得在島上度假區買昂貴的食物。佳禾用一句話便能說明問題：依靠渡輪生活的人並不全是觀光客。他們有賣魚人、老師、年長病患，以及每天往返的工人，各有不同的旅行原因，卻都需要可靠的一餐。

Her brother Bojun called her a genius with food costs and tried to flatter her into taking the counter immediately. Their mother gave a small giggle. "A compliment is not a contract," she said. The ferry manager had prepared a visual presentation showing the counter's tremendous losses, but the figures included repairs to the entire passenger cabin. Jiahe asked for the original bills as a reference. Bojun offered a defense of the manager: the boat was old, and perhaps the counter truly cost too much. Jiahe agreed that the possibility deserved study. She refused, however, to sign a promise before she knew which costs belonged to food. A stranger watching from the door seemed pessimistic. He said that everyone wanted a cheap lunch until the bill for preparing it arrived. Jiahe wrote his warning down rather than arguing.

哥哥伯鈞稱她是懂餐費的天才，還想用稱讚哄她立刻接手櫃檯。母親輕笑一聲：「稱讚可不是合約。」渡輪經理準備了一份圖表簡報，顯示櫃檯虧損十分巨大，但其中竟包含整個乘客艙的維修費。佳禾要求查看原始帳單，作為核對依據。伯鈞替經理辯解：船很舊，也許櫃檯真的太花錢。佳禾同意應該調查這種可能，卻不肯在弄清哪些成本屬於餐食前簽署承諾。門口一名陌生人顯得悲觀，說每個人都想吃便宜午餐，直到看見烹調成本的帳單為止。佳禾沒有爭辯，而是把警告記下來。

That evening, Jiahe made twelve sample lunches using vegetables from nearby farms and rice from a local shop. She asked her mother to witness the weighing, the cooking time, and the final price. The kitchen had once been a meeting place for a parachute club, whose abandoned poster still advertised a weekend workout. Jiahe took down the poster and placed a clean temperature record on the wall instead. She did not possess a new kitchen, a delivery vehicle, or a large supply of cash. What she did have was practical experience and the willingness to show her calculations. She also put a written warning beside the samples: no lunch would go on a ferry without a safe storage plan. Selling unsafe food, even for a generous cause, would be a violation of her own rules.

當晚，佳禾用附近農場的蔬菜和本地店舖的米，做了十二份試吃便當。她請母親見證秤重、烹調時間和最後價格。廚房以前是跳傘社團的聚會場所，牆上還貼著週末體能訓練的舊海報。佳禾將海報取下，換上一張乾淨的溫度紀錄表。她沒有新廚房、運送車輛，也沒有很多現金；她擁有的只是實務經驗，以及公開計算過程的意願。她還在樣品旁寫下警告：沒有安全的保存計畫，便當就不能登上渡輪。即使出於善意而販售不安全的食物，也違反她為自己訂下的規則。

Before the trial began, Jiahe visited the ticket clerk, Mingjuan, who had been keeping a handwritten list of passengers asking about food. Some of the names belonged to people who boarded before sunrise; others came from the island clinic. Mingjuan refused to share their personal details without permission. Instead, she offered to put a blank order slip at the public desk so passengers could contact Jiahe themselves. They practiced counting payments and matching them with numbered tickets. The first attempt produced two identical numbers, and Jiahe crossed out the faulty page without hiding it. Mingjuan said that a small error on paper was better than an argument at a crowded gate. Jiahe agreed and added a final rule to her notebook: every order needed a way to be corrected before it became someone's missed lunch.

試辦開始前，佳禾拜訪售票辦事員明娟。明娟一直手寫記下詢問供餐的乘客，其中有人天未亮便上船，也有人來自島上診所。她不肯未經同意就交出乘客的私人資料，而是提議在公開櫃檯放空白訂餐單，讓乘客自行聯絡佳禾。兩人練習清點付款、配對編號餐票，第一次就產生兩張相同號碼。佳禾劃掉錯誤頁面，沒有藏起來。明娟說，紙上的小錯總比擁擠閘口的大爭吵好。佳禾同意，並在筆記本加上最後一條規則：每筆訂單都應在變成別人錯過的午餐前，有機會被更正。

After supper, her mother asked whether saving the counter was becoming a spiritual duty to the family. Jiahe shook her head. Her father had once repaired the ferry's benches, but that history gave the family no right to a public contract. The counter should serve its customers, not their memories. She planned a five-day trial with a maximum of twenty lunches a day, paid cooking hours, written orders, and full refunds when rough seas stopped the boat. If the trial lost money, they would close it. Bojun looked disappointed, then offered to carry the first order box. Jiahe accepted only after recording his hours. Outside, the harbor bell rang twice. For the first time since returning, she felt neither heroic nor trapped. She felt responsible for a small experiment whose results could be seen and checked.

晚餐後，母親問她是否把拯救櫃檯當成家族的精神責任。佳禾搖頭。父親當年曾修過渡輪長椅，但這段往事不代表家人有資格直接取得公共合約。櫃檯應該服務乘客，而不是服務家人的回憶。她規劃五天試辦，每天最多二十份便當、烹調時間按工付薪、訂單留下書面紀錄，遇到海象不佳停航則全額退款。如果試辦虧錢，就停止。伯鈞看起來有些失望，卻仍自願搬第一箱訂單；佳禾先記下他的工時才接受。外面的港鐘響了兩下。回家後她第一次不覺得自己是英雄，也不覺得被困住，只覺得自己要對一項能公開檢查的小實驗負責。
