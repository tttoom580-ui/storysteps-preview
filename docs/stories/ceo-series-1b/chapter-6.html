泥裡的信封

A Letter in the Mud

總裁，假婚約星期五到期 · 第 6 / 25 章 · 1000以下 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

The old repair shop sat beside a deep river. After a night of rain, mud covered the steps. Tessa held Julian's arm for one moment to step around a wet hole, then let go. “This place is not convenient,” Julian said, looking at his clean shoes. “History rarely waits at a clean desk,” Tessa replied. Inside, a shop owner welcomed them and found a brown envelope under an old seat. It held a note about a clock wheel and a bill from thirty years earlier. The man was surprised that anyone still wanted it. “May we take a picture?” Tessa asked. The owner agreed, but kept the paper.

舊修理店就在一條深河旁。昨夜下雨，階梯上滿是泥。泰莎短暫扶住朱利安的手臂，繞過積水的坑，隨即放手。「這地方真不方便。」朱利安看看乾淨皮鞋。「歷史很少等在乾淨的書桌上。」泰莎回答。店主歡迎他們進門，從一張舊椅子下找到棕色信封，裡面有三十年前的齒輪紀錄和帳單。店主驚訝還有人想找它。「我們可以拍照嗎？」泰莎問。店主同意，但原件仍由他保管。

The paper said that a wheel had been returned to the hotel after repair. “That means someone took it away again,” Julian said. Tessa pointed to the date. “It means the shop sent the wheel. We still do not know whether it reached the clock.” A fish jumped in the river outside, and the owner laughed at their serious faces. He offered a funny story about a customer who had tried to use a bicycle wheel in a clock. Tessa smiled but kept working. The local news could wait. She wrote down every name and asked which person could speak about the delivery.

紙上寫齒輪修好後已送回飯店。「這代表有人又把它拿走。」朱利安說。泰莎指著日期：「只代表店裡寄出，不表示它真的裝回鐘裡。」窗外河魚跳出水面，店主看著兩人嚴肅的臉笑了，說起有人試圖把腳踏車輪裝進鐘裡的趣事。泰莎笑了笑，繼續工作。地方新聞可以晚點再處理。她記下每個名字，詢問誰能證明當年送貨的經過。

The delivery book named a woman called June Bell, now an older teacher living near the river. Julian offered to send a driver to bring her in. Tessa shook her head. “She may want to meet at home, or she may not want to meet at all. Let's ask her first.” Julian called and explained the question. Mrs. Bell said she remembered carrying a small parcel to the hotel, but could not remember who took it from her. She had a copy of her note and would speak to them on Saturday. “Please do not make me famous,” she added. Julian promised not to share her name without permission.

送貨簿寫著一位叫貝爾瓊的女子，如今是住在河邊的退休教師。朱利安提議派司機接她來。泰莎搖頭：「她也許想在家見面，也可能根本不想見。先問她吧。」朱利安打電話說明問題。貝爾太太記得把小包裹帶去飯店，卻不記得誰收下。她還保留紙條副本，願意星期六談談。「請別讓我變成名人。」她補了一句。朱利安答應未經同意不公開姓名。

On the way back Julian stopped at a shop to buy Tessa dry socks. “I can pay for my own,” she said. “I know,” he answered. “I saw that your shoe was wet and wanted to help. You can say no.” She accepted one plain pair and paid the shopkeeper herself. The owner thought it funny that the rich boss looked pleased by such a small thing. Outside, the rain began to rise in a thin gray line from the river. Tessa put the envelope photographs safely away. They had found proof of a delivery, but not yet proof of a broken promise.

回程時，朱利安在商店停下，想替泰莎買雙乾襪子。「我自己付得起。」她說。「我知道，」他答，「我只是看到妳鞋子濕了，想幫忙。妳可以拒絕。」她接下一雙素色襪子，但自己付錢。店主覺得好笑：有錢總裁竟因這件小事顯得開心。外頭河面飄起灰色細雨霧。泰莎小心收好信封照片。他們找到了寄送證明，卻還沒找到承諾遭破壞的證據。

However, the shop owner had one more thing to say before they left. He took a small wooden sign from a shelf. It had once hung over his door and read FIND GOOD WORK HERE. The letters were old but clear. “People welcome a simple answer,” he said. “They do not need a rich story.” Julian promised to return with news only after the records were checked. Tessa asked the owner what would happen to the shop when he retired. He said his daughter wanted to keep it open, and he was teaching her how to read the old books. Julian listened without making an offer to buy anything. On the wet street he told Tessa that running a company sometimes meant learning not to own every useful thing in sight.

然而，離開前店主還有話想說。他從架上拿下一塊小木牌，以前掛在門上，寫著「在此找到好手藝」。字雖老卻清楚。「人們歡迎簡單答案，」他說，「不需要有錢人的大故事。」朱利安答應紀錄核對後才帶消息回來。泰莎問店主退休後店會如何。店主說女兒想接手，他正在教她讀舊帳本。朱利安聽著，沒有提出買下店面的邀約。走在濕街上，他告訴泰莎，經營公司有時要學會別把眼前每件有價值的東西都變成自己的財產。

Back at the hotel, Calvin wanted the old bill. “It belongs to the company,” he told Julian. “No,” Tessa said. “The shop owns its copy. We may use the image only for the reason the owner agreed to.” Julian accepted her answer and told Calvin he would request records through the proper office. Calvin called them slow. Tessa did not mind. She had begun to notice that the people who wanted a quick end were often the ones who asked the fewest questions. At home that night she wrote two lines: wheel returned, person unknown. A small fact was still a useful fact.

回到飯店，凱文要看那張舊帳單。「那是公司財產。」他對朱利安說。「不，」泰莎回答，「商店擁有自己的副本。我們只能依店主同意的用途使用照片。」朱利安接受了，告訴凱文會循正式管道索取資料。凱文說他們太慢。泰莎不在意。她已注意到，最急著結案的人，往往問的問題最少。當晚她在家寫下兩行：「齒輪已寄回，收件人不明。」小小的事實仍然很有用。
