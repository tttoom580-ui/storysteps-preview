舊票上的女王

The Queen on the Old Ticket

總裁，假婚約星期五到期 · 第 8 / 25 章 · 1000以下 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

Tessa and Julian took a train to a museum outside the city. Each held a book about the hotel, and Julian carried a file of safe copies. The museum worker said the visit might be boring because most records were numbers, not stories. Tessa laughed. “A wrong number can be dangerous,” she said. “It can lead to a costly mistake.” The visit cost twelve dollars each, which Tessa paid from her own research money. At noon they sat at a small table with a fork and a full plate of rice. “Do you have another idea?” Julian asked. “Listen first,” she answered. “The museum worker has not finished.”

泰莎和朱利安搭火車到市外的博物館，各自帶一本飯店史書，朱利安還拿著安全保存的文件副本。館員提醒這次參觀可能很無聊，因為紀錄大多是數字，不是故事。泰莎笑了：「錯誤的數字可能很危險，也可能導致花錢的失誤。」門票每人十二元，泰莎從自己的研究費支付。中午兩人坐在小桌旁吃飯，叉子放在滿滿的飯盤旁。「妳有其他想法嗎？」朱利安問。「先聽，」她答，「館員還沒說完。」

The museum kept an old ticket with a picture of a queen on it. It was not a royal gift. It was a funny design from the first public music day. A hand had written GRAND CLOCK HALL on the back. The worker could tell them which family had given the ticket to the museum, but not who had bought the first one. Tessa took a picture and carefully read the number at the bottom. It matched the number on Ada Mo's old letter. “That is useful,” she said. “Two papers point to the same event.” Julian wanted to call it a successful day. She reminded him that one event did not prove permanent public rights.

博物館保存一張畫有女王圖案的舊票，並不是王室贈品，只是第一次公共音樂日的有趣設計。票背有人寫著「大鐘廳」。館員能說出是哪個家族捐票，卻不知道第一張票賣給誰。泰莎拍照，仔細讀底下的編號，發現與艾達舊信的號碼相同。「這很有用，」她說，「兩份紙都指向同一場活動。」朱利安想稱今天為成功的一天。她提醒他，單一活動不等於永久公共使用權。

On the return train, a passenger knocked his bag into their table. The file almost rolled onto the floor. Julian caught it while Tessa held her drink. “Thank you,” she said. He looked pleased, then said, “I am learning not to make a great show of small things.” She answered, “That is good. I am learning you can be funny when you do not try.” He laughed, a sound she had not heard from him at the hotel. Across the aisle a child asked his mother whether the two were famous. Tessa shook her head and smiled. “No. We are just going home.”

回程火車上，有乘客的背包撞到他們的桌子。資料夾差點滑落，朱利安接住它，泰莎扶穩飲料。「謝謝。」她說。他很高興，接著說：「我正在學別把小事弄得很盛大。」她回答：「那很好。我也發現你不刻意時其實很有趣。」他笑了，那是她在飯店沒聽過的聲音。走道對面的孩子問媽媽兩人是不是名人。泰莎搖頭微笑：「不是，我們只是要回家。」

That night Calvin sent Julian a message saying the museum visit had been photographed. “The news says you are taking a romantic trip,” he wrote. Julian considered answering with anger. Instead he called Tessa and asked if she wanted him to request a correction. She did. Their signed agreement said public work might be explained, but a private trip story was wrong. The hotel issued a short statement saying they had visited a museum for clock records. It did not name the child or show the museum worker. “Truth can be simple,” Tessa told him when the correction appeared. “Even when other people prefer a larger story.”

當晚凱文傳訊說有人拍到博物館之行。「新聞說你們去浪漫旅行。」他寫。朱利安想生氣回覆，最後改打電話問泰莎要不要請媒體更正。她說要。兩人的簽署協議容許解說公開工作，不容許把研究行程說成私人約會。飯店簡短聲明：兩人去博物館尋找鐘錶紀錄，沒有公開孩子或館員姓名。「事實可以很簡單，」更正刊出後泰莎說，「即使有人更喜歡誇張的故事。」

On the train the museum worker gave them one last copy of a ticket record. The original ticket had cost one dollar, while children could come for free. Julian read the number again. “Who else paid for this room?” he asked. Tessa said the city records might tell them. At a stop a small ball began to roll down the aisle. Julian picked it up and gave it back to a child without making a speech. The mother thanked him, not knowing his name. Tessa watched from her seat. He looked comfortable with being ordinary for a moment. That was a better sign than any headline. When the train moved again, she closed the file and looked out at the fields, where the last light of the day touched the grass.

火車上，博物館員又給他們一份舊票紀錄影本。原本票價一美元，孩子免費。朱利安又讀一次數字：「還有誰替這個房間付錢？」泰莎說市府紀錄可能有答案。停靠車站時，一顆小球沿走道滾動，朱利安撿起還給孩子，沒有發表什麼話。母親道謝，根本不知道他的名字。泰莎坐著看，他有一刻很自在地當個普通人，這比任何頭條都是好訊號。火車再開動時，她闔上資料夾，望向田野，夕陽最後的光落在草上。

At home Julian studied the two matching numbers. He placed them beside the photographs of the hall and saw a small drawing of a door that had since been closed. A new question came to mind. If the hall had once sold tickets to all people, who had the right to change that rule? He wrote to the city records office rather than guessing. Then he set his phone down and waited. He was used to getting an answer with one call. With Tessa beside him on this work, he was learning that the right answer could take longer than money could buy.

回到家，朱利安研究兩個相同編號，將它們與大鐘廳照片並排，發現小圖上有一道現在已封閉的門。他想到新問題：如果當年大廳向所有人售票，後來是誰有權改變規則？他寫信向市府檔案室詢問，沒有自行猜測。接著放下手機等待。他習慣一通電話就有答案，如今與泰莎合作，逐漸學會正確答案有時不能花錢立即買到。
