西廳的名單

The West Hall List

總裁，假婚約星期五到期 · 第 7 / 25 章 · 1000以下 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

On Saturday Mrs. Bell met them by the west hall. A large table stood under a painting of a rabbit holding a flower. Mrs. Bell brought chocolate for the volunteers and a list from the old music class. “I used to collect names for this room,” she said. “My main course was reading, but every hour I let the children listen to music.” A housewife from the next street had written a note on the back: KEEP THIS PLACE FOR ALL. Tessa asked whether the note was real. Mrs. Bell nodded, though she could not say if it had ever been part of a legal agreement.

星期六，貝爾太太在西廳與兩人見面。一張大桌子放在畫作下，畫裡一隻兔子抱著花。貝爾太太帶巧克力給志工，也帶來以前音樂班的名單。「我曾幫這個房間收集報名名字，」她說，「我的主課是閱讀，但每個小時都讓孩子聽一些音樂。」隔街一位家庭主婦在背面寫著：「讓這地方屬於所有人。」泰莎問字條是否真實。貝爾太太點頭，但不確定它是否曾是法律協議的一部分。

Mrs. Bell said that the classes were popular until a wall closed the side entrance. After that, many people could not reach the room easily. “There used to be a friendly woman at the desk,” she remembered. “She would let a tired child sit without a ticket.” Julian asked who had ordered the wall. Mrs. Bell did not know. She only remembered that it happened after his grandmother died. Tessa made another note: west entrance closed, date unknown. “We should start with records, not with blame,” she said. Julian looked at the wall that separated the room from the street and wondered why he had never asked about it.

貝爾太太說，課程曾經很受歡迎，直到有人用牆封住側邊入口，很多人才難以進來。「以前櫃檯有位親切女士，」她回憶，「疲倦孩子不買票也能坐下。」朱利安問是誰命令封牆。她不知道，只記得事情發生在祖母過世後。泰莎又記下一項：「西入口封閉，日期不明。」「先查紀錄，不要先怪罪。」她說。朱利安看著隔開街道與大廳的牆，不明白自己怎會從沒問過。

The three visited a small classroom beside the hotel. There, an older singer showed them a yellow book of programs. Every Friday had a clear start time and a space for a teacher's name. One page had a drawing of the same red star over the clock. The book did not mention a private wedding hall anywhere. Julian wanted to put it in a display, but the singer said it belonged to the class. “Then we can ask to borrow it for one day,” Tessa suggested. “And we should tell visitors why it matters, not just say it is old.”

三人走訪飯店旁的小教室，一位年長歌手給他們看泛黃的節目本。每個星期五都有清楚的開始時間及老師姓名。某頁畫著鐘上相同的紅星。全本沒提到私人婚禮場地。朱利安想把它放在展櫃，歌手說那是班級的東西。「那我們可以詢問是否能借一天，」泰莎建議，「而且要向參觀者解釋它的重要，不只說它很老。」

Calvin called during the visit. He had arranged a meeting with two hotel owners who wanted to buy the hall. “You should be there, Julian,” he said. “The room is worth more than a class.” Julian glanced at the old singer and answered, “A room can have more than one kind of value. Please put the offer in writing.” After the call, he apologized for the interruption. Mrs. Bell said she was pleased that someone had finally asked what the room was for. “A real place has real people inside its history,” she said. Tessa wrote that sentence on her own list.

參觀途中凱文打電話來。他安排朱利安與兩位想收購大廳的飯店老闆會面。「你應該來，」凱文說，「房間的價值高於一堂課。」朱利安看看年長歌手，回答：「房間可以有不只一種價值。請把報價寫成文件。」掛掉電話後，他向大家為打斷談話道歉。貝爾太太很高興終於有人詢問空間原本的用途。「真正的地方，歷史裡也有真正的人。」她說。泰莎把這句話寫進自己的清單。

Before leaving the west hall, Mrs. Bell asked the visitors to look at the old list once more. Each name stood for a child who had crossed the street, entered the hall, and stayed to hear music. Some names had small stars beside them because the children helped clean up. Julian asked how the teacher had paid for the classes. Mrs. Bell said a few families gave money, while others offered time or paper. “There was never enough of everything,” she said. “But we made the room work.” Tessa wondered whether the public plan could make space for small acts like those. She wrote that question below the date of the last class. It was not a promise, only the beginning of a better design.

離開西廳前，貝爾太太請訪客再看看舊名單。每個名字都代表一個曾越過街道、走進大廳聽音樂的孩子。部分名字旁畫著小星星，因為孩子曾幫忙收拾。朱利安問老師如何支付課程費用。貝爾太太說，有的家庭捐錢，有的提供時間或紙張。「我們從來沒有什麼都足夠，」她說，「但仍讓房間運作。」泰莎思索新公共方案能不能保留這樣的小小參與。她把問題寫在最後一堂課日期下面。那還不是承諾，只是較好設計的起點。

When the visitors left, Julian asked whether Tessa would like another cup of tea. “There is a small garden behind the class,” he added. “We can sit there, or you can go home.” Tessa chose the garden for twenty minutes. They spoke about music rather than the business. Julian had once taken piano lessons but had stopped after his father told him to work harder. Tessa admitted that she loved the sound of an old clock because it marked time for people who owned nothing at all. They did not touch. The quiet felt more like a beginning than the photograph ever had.

訪客離開時，朱利安問泰莎要不要再喝杯茶。「教室後有個小花園，」他說，「我們能坐一下，也可以直接回家。」泰莎選擇在花園待二十分鐘。兩人談音樂，不談生意。朱利安小時候學過鋼琴，父親卻要求他把時間用在工作上，他便停了。泰莎說她愛老鐘的聲音，因為即使一無所有的人，也能聽它報時。他們沒有碰觸。這種安靜反而比那張照片更像真正的開始。
