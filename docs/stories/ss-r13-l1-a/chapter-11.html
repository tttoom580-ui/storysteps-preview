藍色資料夾

A Blue Folder

A cold morning brought Nina and Sol to the town office. The secretary at the front desk gave them a blue folder and asked them to wait. Inside were the bus company's past numbers, the road team's notes, and the meeting date. Nina had thought the folder would hold an answer, but it held more questions. “Maybe the town will stop the bus anyway,” Sol said. Nina looked out at a tree under the window. “Probably,” she said, “some people will disagree with us. That is why we must show what we found.” She did not want to hide the hard parts.

一個寒冷的早晨，妮娜和索爾來到鎮公所。櫃台祕書給他們一個藍色資料夾，請他們稍等。裡面有公車公司的過往數字、道路小組的筆記以及會議日期。妮娜原以為資料夾裡會有答案，卻發現有更多問題。「也許鎮上最後還是會停駛。」索爾說。妮娜望向窗下的一棵樹。「很可能有人不同意我們，」她說，「所以才要把找到的事實拿出來。」她不想隱藏困難的部分。

A nurse came in from the clinic. She wore warm pants and carried a dictionary for a language class. She said that some patients used the morning bus, but many wished for a ride home later in the day. The secretary asked whether the clinic could call everyone to ask. The nurse said they must not use patient records for this bus project. Nina agreed at once. They could put a public form in the clinic waiting room instead. That would let people choose whether to answer without sharing private health information.

一位診所護理師走進來。她穿著保暖長褲，帶著一本語言課用的字典。她說有些病人搭早班車，但許多人希望下午有回程。祕書問診所能不能打電話問每個人。護理師說，不能為了公車專案使用病人病歷資料。妮娜立刻同意。他們可以在診所候診室放公開表格，讓人自行決定是否填寫，也不必分享私人的健康資訊。

The clerk showed them a page with the cost of a new afternoon trip. It was not a small number. Fuel, a trained driver, and care for the bus all cost money. Twelve trial trips over four weeks would need about one thousand dollars. A little more money would be needed for signs. “People must be able to see what this trial costs,” he said. Nina had no wish to call the trip free just because the town paid for it. Everyone in Willow Bend would share the cost in some way.

職員給他們看一張新增下午班次的成本表。數字不小。燃料、受訓司機和車輛保養都需要錢。四週十二趟試行班次約要一千元，站牌還得另加一點費用。「大家必須看得見這次試行要花多少。」職員說。妮娜不想因為由鎮公所付費，就稱班次是免費的。柳灣鎮的人會以某種方式共同負擔成本。

After the meeting, the secretary took them past the old temple and showed them a public notice board. It stood under a roof, safe from the weather. The board had a sentence about every town meeting and a telephone number for questions. Nina asked whether they could put their bus report there. “Yes, after we check that it says who wrote it and that it is not an official decision,” the secretary answered. Sol was surprised that even a good idea needed a clear name and date. Nina thought those small details made public work easier to trust.

會面後，祕書帶他們經過老廟，來到一面公共公告欄。公告欄在屋簷下，不怕風吹雨淋。上面有每場鎮民會議的簡短說明，以及詢問電話。妮娜問能不能把公車報告貼上去。「可以，」祕書回答，「但得先確認上面寫明作者，也標示這不是正式決定。」索爾很驚訝，原來好主意也需要清楚的姓名和日期。妮娜認為這些小細節讓公共事務更值得信任。

Ada joined them for lunch and showed them the bus driver's notes. On one page, she had written that a person waited at the wrong door of the station. On another, she had written that a sign was hard to see at night. “These notes are part of my work,” Ada said, “but I still have to be careful about what I write.” Nina saw that the bus driver was not only turning a wheel. She was watching the road, thinking about people, and following rules in every kind of weather.

艾達在午餐時加入他們，給大家看司機工作筆記。有一頁記著有人在車站錯的門口等車，另一頁寫著夜間站牌難以看清。「這些筆記是我的工作一部分，」艾達說，「但我仍要小心寫下什麼。」妮娜發現司機不只是轉動方向盤；她還要觀察路況、考慮乘客需求，並在各種天氣裡遵守規定。

When the children left the office, a small car stopped at the curb. Its driver asked where to find the public bus form. Nina pointed to the office door rather than asking him to tell her his travel needs on the street. She and Sol then went to a warm room where a cook from the market was helping make lunch for town workers. A fan moved the warm air gently around the room. The cook asked whether the new afternoon trip might help people who cleaned the market after closing. Nina gave her a blank form and explained that an answer was welcome but not required. On the way home, Nina told Sol that even someone who owned a car might still have a good reason to want a bus.

孩子們離開公所時，一輛小汽車停在路邊。司機問哪裡可以拿到公車問卷。妮娜指向辦公室門口，而不是在路上追問他的交通需求。她和索爾接著進入一間溫暖的房間，市場的廚師正幫鎮上工作人員準備午餐。一臺風扇輕輕帶動暖空氣。廚師問新下午班次是否能幫收攤後清潔市場的人。妮娜給她空白表格，解釋願意回答很好，但不是必須。回家路上，妮娜告訴索爾，就算有人有汽車，也可能有希望搭公車的好理由。

On their way home, Sol wanted to call the plan “Save the Red Bus Forever.” Nina said that forever was too large a promise. The town could agree to a trial, measure what happened, and make another choice later. She suggested “A Better Chance for Route Eight.” Sol liked the new words. They were less exciting, but more true. Nina carried the blue folder against her coat. For the first time, the numbers did not seem like enemies of the bus. They were tools that could help the town choose fairly.

回家的路上，索爾想把計畫命名為「永遠拯救紅色公車」。妮娜說「永遠」是太大的承諾。鎮上可以先同意試行，觀察結果，再做下一次決定。她提議改叫「給八號路線更好的機會」。索爾喜歡新名稱。它不那麼激動人心，卻更真實。妮娜把藍色資料夾抱在外套前。她第一次覺得，數字不是公車的敵人，而是幫助鎮上做公平決定的工具。
