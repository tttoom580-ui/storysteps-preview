中心的一個聲音

A Voice at the Center

紅色公車的第二張時刻表 · 第 10 / 25 章 · 1000以下 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

At the community center, a teacher named Mr. Vale met Nina and Sol. He was helping people learn how to read bus maps online. His brother, who was blind, had asked him to help test the new signs. Nina wanted to know the right way to ask questions. Mr. Vale said, “Ask the person, not the person beside him.” When his brother arrived, Nina spoke to him directly. He told her that large print helped some people, but a spoken stop name helped him more. She listened to his voice instead of trying to guess what he needed.

在社區中心，瓦爾先生接待妮娜和索爾。他是一位老師，正教人們在線上閱讀公車地圖。他的弟弟看不見，請他協助測試新標示。妮娜想知道該怎麼問問題才恰當。瓦爾先生說：「直接問當事人，不要問他旁邊的人。」弟弟抵達後，妮娜直接跟他說話。他告訴她，大字體對有些人有幫助，但對他而言，口頭報站名更有用。妮娜認真聽他的聲音，而不是自行猜測他的需要。

The man showed them a simple way to call out a stop. “Next: library,” he said, slowly and clearly. Nina tried it once, then again. Sol used a pencil to mark the stop on a paper map. They did not need a special machine for the test, but any new system would need the bus company to approve it. A woman waiting at the center gave them an apple and a cup of water. “You are spending your day on a good question,” she said. Nina thanked her and went back to work.

那位先生示範簡單的報站方式。「下一站：圖書館。」他說得慢而清楚。妮娜練了一次，又練了一次。索爾用鉛筆在紙地圖上標出站點。測試不需要特殊機器，但若要正式採用新系統，就必須經公車公司核准。一位在中心等候的婦人送他們蘋果和水。「你們把時間花在很好的問題上。」她說。妮娜向她道謝，又回去忙了。

Outside, children played a game with a net and a ball. One child tried to kick the ball between two stones. It hit a stone and rolled toward the road. Mr. Vale asked the children to stop and move their game farther from traffic. Nina watched them pick up the ball. Even a simple game needed a safe place. She understood why the road officer had not approved a stop just because it was close to a park. The center had many people moving in different ways, and each needed room.

外面，孩子們用球網和球玩遊戲。一個孩子想把球踢過兩塊石頭之間，球卻撞到石頭，滾向馬路。瓦爾先生請孩子們停下，移到離車流更遠的地方玩。妮娜看著他們撿回球。連簡單的遊戲都需要安全場地。她更理解道路工作員為何不能只因站點靠近公園就核准。中心有很多人以不同方式移動，每個人都需要空間。

At lunch, Nina shared half of her sandwich with Sol. He asked if they could finish all their work that week. She shook her head. The town council still had to decide whether a change was possible. The road team had not finished its review. The bus company had not said how much a different timetable would cost. “I love this bus,” Sol said, “but I don't want to promise the wrong thing.” Nina agreed. She was still learning that the right way was not always the fastest way.

午餐時，妮娜把三明治分一半給索爾。他問這星期能不能把全部工作做完。她搖頭。鎮議會還要決定變更是否可行，道路小組尚未完成審查，公車公司也還沒說調整時刻表要花多少錢。「我很喜歡這輛公車，」索爾說，「但我不想承諾錯的事。」妮娜同意。她仍在學習，正確的方法不一定是最快的方法。

In the afternoon, the blind man asked whether the bus would call out every stop on the new route. Nina answered that they would recommend it, but the company would make the final choice. “That is honest,” he said. “Now please let me tell you what worked today.” He explained that a clear voice was more important than a beautiful sign for his trip. Nina wrote down his words with his permission. She did not call him an example of a problem. He was a passenger with knowledge that helped them make a better plan.

下午，那位看不見的先生問，新路線會不會每站都口頭報站。妮娜回答，他們會建議，但最後由公司決定。「這樣說很誠實。」他說，「現在請讓我告訴你們今天什麼方法有用。」他解釋，在他的旅程中，清楚的報站聲比漂亮的標示更重要。妮娜徵得同意後記下他的話。她沒有把他當成某種「問題」的例子，而是視他為了解搭乘需要、能幫助改善計畫的乘客。

Before leaving the center, Nina saw a man put ice into a cup of water for the people waiting outside. The day had become warm, and the cold water was welcome. Sol wanted to spend the rest of the afternoon making a bright new map. Nina said they should first spend a little time checking the stop names that people had just read aloud. They worked together for twenty minutes. One name had been written too small, and another was too close to a picture. They made the words larger and moved the picture away. Nina thought about the blind man's advice. A clear map and a clear voice were two ways to help passengers, not two things that needed to fight each other.

離開中心前，妮娜看到一位先生往杯子裡加冰塊，讓外面等待的人喝水。天氣變暖，冰水很受歡迎。索爾想把下午剩下的時間全拿來畫漂亮新地圖。妮娜說，他們應該先花一點時間核對剛剛念過的站名。兩人合作了二十分鐘，發現一個站名寫太小，另一個太靠近圖案。他們把字放大，移開圖。妮娜想起那位看不見的先生的建議。清楚的地圖和清楚的報站聲，是幫助乘客的兩種方法，不需要互相競爭。

When the session ended, Nina put the test sheet in her bag. She was tired but ready for the next step. The center had given her a new way to think about Route Eight. The bus was more than seats, wheels, and a road. It was also information: where to wait, when to travel, and how to know that the right stop had come. Before going home, she looked at the red bus in the street. She hoped the town would hear all the voices she had heard, not only the loudest ones.

活動結束後，妮娜把測試表收進書包。她累了，但準備好走下一步。社區中心讓她用新眼光看八號路線。公車不只是座位、車輪與道路，也包括資訊：在哪裡等、何時搭、怎樣知道已到正確站點。回家前，她望著街上的紅色公車。她希望鎮公所能聽見她聽過的所有聲音，而不只是最大聲的那些。
