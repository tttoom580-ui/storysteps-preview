雙語地圖

The Map in Two Languages

紅色公車的第二張時刻表 · 第 12 / 25 章 · 1000以下 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

At the library, Nina held up a map in two languages. Her class had worked with a new student to choose words that visitors could understand. A girl in jeans said she liked the larger letters. A boy with a bicycle asked whether a person riding a bike could use the bus stops too. Ada explained that bikes had their own rules and could not block the place where passengers waited. Nina wrote the answer in the second line of the map notes. She had learned to answer one question at a time, rather than trying to fit the whole town into one page.

在圖書館，妮娜舉起一張雙語地圖。全班和一位新同學合作，選出訪客容易理解的文字。一個穿牛仔褲的女孩說，她喜歡字比較大的版本。一個騎腳踏車的男孩問，騎車的人能不能使用公車站等候區。艾達解釋，腳踏車有自己的規定，不能擋住乘客等車的位置。妮娜把答案寫在地圖說明第二行。她已學會一次回答一個問題，而不是想把全鎮塞進一頁紙裡。

Mr. Vale brought a picture of the planet Earth from a science book. He asked the children what salt, water, and wind had to do with old roads. A grandfather answered that there had once been a small salt mine near the hills, and workers needed a way to travel to it. The old road had first been made for that work. “Roads change when people change,” he said. Nina liked the thought. The bus route did not belong to the past only. People who lived in Willow Bend now could help decide how it would serve them.

瓦爾先生帶來一本科學書裡的地球圖片，問孩子們鹽、水和風跟舊道路有什麼關係。一位爺爺回答，山丘附近以前有座小鹽礦，工人需要道路前往工作。最早那條路正是為了鹽礦而修。「人們改變，道路也會改變。」他說。妮娜喜歡這個想法。公車路線不只屬於過去，現在住在柳灣鎮的人也能幫忙決定它如何服務大家。

A little boy asked whether he could make his own map and put it on the bus. “You can draw one,” Nina said, “but it cannot be an official sign until the company checks it.” The boy gave a small bow and said he understood. Sol showed him how to pick a simple mark for each stop. He said that a clear map helped the body and mind work together: the eyes found the stop, and the feet knew where to go. Nina smiled at the idea. It was a simple way to explain a hard lesson.

一個小男孩問，他能不能畫自己的地圖放在公車上。「你可以畫，」妮娜說，「但公司檢查以前，不能把它當作正式標示。」男孩微微鞠躬，表示理解。索爾教他為每個站點選一個簡單符號。他說清楚的地圖能讓身體和腦袋一起工作：眼睛先找到站點，腳就知道往哪裡走。妮娜笑了，這是用簡單方法解釋困難的道理。

Soon a woman came in and asked for a second copy to take to her mother. She did not want a phone picture because her mother liked paper. Nina said they could make some copies, but the new times would only be ideas until the council approved them. The woman thanked her. Sol wanted to add a spring flower beside each possible new stop. Nina said that was fine if the flowers did not make the test map look like a final timetable. They put the words “For discussion” at the top in both languages.

不久，一位婦人進來，想多拿一張給母親。她不想拍手機照片，因為母親喜歡紙本。妮娜說可以影印幾張，但新時間在議會核准前都只是提案。婦人向她道謝。索爾想在每個可能的新站點旁畫春天的小花。妮娜說可以，只要花朵不會讓討論版看起來像正式時刻表。他們在頂端用兩種語言寫上「討論用」。

At the end of the day, the student who had helped with the language stood beside Nina. “I wish people would ask me what words I know before they say I need help,” he said. Nina was surprised. She had meant to be kind, but she had sometimes spoken for him. “You are right,” she answered. “What would you like to say at the meeting?” He said he wanted to explain why the words for “stop” and “station” were different. Nina gave him a place on the speaking list. That part of the plan was his own.

一天結束時，協助翻譯的新同學站在妮娜旁邊。「我希望大家先問我會哪些字，不要直接說我需要幫忙。」他說。妮娜很驚訝。她原是出於好意，卻有時替他發言。「你說得對。」她回答，「會議上你想講什麼？」他說想解釋「站牌」和「車站」兩個詞為什麼不同。妮娜把他排進發言名單。計畫中的這個部分，由他自己決定。

At home, Nina put the two-language map beside the old one. The new map had fewer pictures but clearer stop names. Her mother looked at both and said she could find the library corner faster on the new page. So Nina did not add any more drawings that night. She knew Sol loved pictures, and she did not want to hurt his feelings. The next day she thanked him for the small flower marks and explained why some had to move. He listened and said, “A map should help people first. My pictures can help in another way.” They made a little book of town views to share at school. Their bus map stayed simple, and their art did not have to disappear.

回家後，妮娜把雙語地圖放在舊版旁。新版的圖較少，站名卻更清楚。母親看了兩張，說自己在新版上更快找到圖書館轉角。於是妮娜那晚不再加圖。她知道索爾喜歡畫畫，也不想傷他的心。隔天，她感謝他畫的小花，並解釋為什麼有些需要移動。索爾聽完說：「地圖首先得幫到人。我的畫可以用其他方法幫忙。」兩人做了一本小小的鎮景畫冊，準備在學校分享。公車地圖維持簡潔，畫作也沒有因此消失。

Before leaving, Nina read the map aloud one more time. She said each stop name slowly, and Sol followed the printed line with his finger. Some parts needed more work; other parts were ready for the council to see. The wind moved the paper near the door. Nina held it down, then put it safely in the blue folder. She knew a second language would not change the cost of a bus, but it might help another person use the bus the town already had. That was worth doing, even before the trial began.

離開前，妮娜又朗讀一次地圖。她慢慢說出每個站名，索爾用手指沿著印好的路線移動。有些部分還要修改，有些已能給議會看。風吹動門邊的紙張，妮娜按住它，再安全地收進藍色資料夾。她知道第二種語言不會改變公車成本，卻可能幫助另一個人使用鎮上已經有的車。即使試行尚未開始，這也值得做。
