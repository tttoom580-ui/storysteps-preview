週末的試乘

The Weekend Ride

紅色公車的第二張時刻表 · 第 6 / 25 章 · 1000以下 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

On Saturday, Nina and Sol arrived at the station before the first bus. Ada had brought a paper box with old route maps. Nina's uncle Leo was there too. He owned a small house near the river and used a boat for work, but he wanted to see whether the red bus could help his family at the weekend. He paid for his ticket; the ride was not free. Ada reminded everyone that a quiet ride with only a few people would not prove that a new route was good. It would only help them see where the old bus went.

星期六，妮娜和索爾在首班車前就到了車站。艾達帶來一個裝著舊路線圖的紙箱。妮娜的舅舅里歐也來了。他在河邊有間小屋，工作時會搭船，但想看看紅色公車在週末能不能幫上家人的忙。他照常付了車資，這次並不是免費搭乘。艾達提醒大家，只有少數人搭的安靜班次，不能證明新路線就好；它只能幫他們看清舊公車實際走哪裡。

They finally climbed onto the bus and sat near the front. A lion was painted on the top of a child's bag. Sol laughed at its large shape. He pointed to the new sign at the door. Ada had made it with big letters and a simple picture of the bus. “We can see it clearly,” Nina said. Another passenger thought the sign meant that the route had already changed. Nina quickly explained that this was only a test of the sign, not a new bus service. She was glad he had asked before walking away.

他們終於上車，坐在前排。一個孩子的背包頂端畫著獅子，索爾看見那大大的形狀笑了。他指著車門旁的新標示。艾達用大字和簡單公車圖畫做了標示。「我們看得很清楚。」妮娜說。另一位乘客以為那表示路線已經更改。妮娜立刻解釋：這只是測試標示，並不是新的公車服務。她很高興對方先問清楚，沒有直接走開。

At the south turn, a man waved at the bus from the other side of the road. Ada did not stop in the middle of traffic. She drove to the proper stop and waited there. “That is why we need a clear sign,” she said. The man came after a minute, breathing hard. He had seen the red bus but had not known where to wait. “I felt stupid,” he said. Nina answered, “The map was not clear. Asking a question is never stupid.” The man smiled and took his seat near the window.

在南邊轉角，一個男人從馬路另一側向公車揮手。艾達沒有在車流中停下，而是開到正式站牌才等他。「這就是我們需要清楚標示的原因。」她說。過了一分鐘，那人喘著氣走來。他看到了紅色公車，卻不知道該站哪裡。「我覺得自己好笨。」他說。妮娜回答：「是地圖不清楚，問問題一點也不笨。」男人笑著坐到窗邊。

At the last stop, Uncle Leo showed them where the road met the river. The water was calm, but a boat was moving slowly past the bank. “If the bus came later, I could finish my work and still reach the market,” he said. Nina wrote the idea on a card. She also wrote that one person's wish was not enough to change a whole timetable. Sol asked why she always added that note. “Because I want the town to trust our numbers,” she answered. They ate lunch under a tree and watched the boat turn toward home.

在終點站，里歐舅舅帶他們看道路與河流交會的地方。河水平靜，一艘船正緩緩經過岸邊。「如果公車再晚一點來，我就能做完工作，還趕得上市場。」他說。妮娜把這想法寫在卡片上，也註明一個人的願望不足以改變整張時刻表。索爾問她為何總要加這句話。「因為我希望鎮上相信我們的數字。」她回答。他們在樹下吃午飯，看著小船轉向回家的方向。

On the way home, Ada asked Nina to look at the empty seats. “You can see many empty seats now,” she said, “but you must also see who got off at each stop.” The bus had carried nine people over the whole trip, though no more than four had sat together. Nina had almost written down four. She corrected her count and thanked Ada. The difference seemed small, but it mattered. If the town made a money choice based on a wrong count, people could lose a useful service.

回程時，艾達請妮娜看空著的座位。「妳現在看到很多空位，」她說，「但也要注意每站下車了誰。」整趟其實載過九人，雖然同時坐在車上的最多只有四人。妮娜差點只記成四人。她修正人數，向艾達道謝。差距看似不大，卻很重要。如果鎮上依錯誤的數字決定經費，人們就可能失去有用的服務。

When the bus came to the last stop, two people gave a wave from a coffee shop. They knew Ada and wanted to ask what she had seen on the road. Ada explained that she could drive a planned route safely, but she could not make a new turn just because someone waved. “People arrive at different times,” she said, “and a route must be clear for everyone.” The shop owner offered them coffee, but the children chose water. All the adults were welcome to talk after the ride, except the driver while she was working. Nina liked that rule. It kept Ada's eyes on the road and gave the passengers a calmer trip. She added it to her notes without making anyone's private day into a public story.

公車來到最後一站時，兩個人在咖啡店門口揮手。他們認識艾達，想問她在路上看到什麼。艾達解釋，她能安全駕駛規劃好的路線，卻不能只因有人揮手就臨時轉彎。「人們在不同時間抵達，」她說，「路線必須對每個人清楚。」店主請大家喝咖啡，但孩子們選了水。成年人都可以在行程結束後聊天，只有正在工作的司機不能分心。妮娜喜歡這條規定。它讓艾達專心看路，也讓乘客搭得更安心。她把規定記下來，沒有把任何人的私人行程寫成公開故事。

That evening Nina opened the box of maps in her room. The old sheets were not terrible, but they did not answer all the new questions. She put the passenger count beside the travel time, then made a third space for things she had seen. A good map needed more than a pretty shape. It needed the real stops, the real time, and a way for a person to know where to stand. She planned to show the three spaces to the town worker on Monday. For now, she let herself feel pleased by one honest day of work.

傍晚，妮娜在房裡打開裝地圖的盒子。舊表格並不是糟透了，只是無法回答所有新問題。她把乘客總數寫在行駛時間旁，再留出第三欄，記錄自己看見的情況。好的地圖不能只有漂亮的形狀，還需要真實的站點、時間，以及讓人知道該站哪裡的方法。她打算星期一把這三欄拿給鎮上工作人員看。此刻，她允許自己為這一天誠實的努力感到高興。
