海邊的那條路

The Road by the Beach

紅色公車的第二張時刻表 · 第 3 / 25 章 · 1000以下 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

Ada unfolded a map on a table in the station. “Route Eight follows this road, crosses the old bridge, and turns toward the beach,” she explained. The bridge was safe, but the beach stop was far from most houses. A bird sat on the window and looked into the room. Sol drew a small bird next to the bridge on his map. Nina asked what would happen if they moved the stop closer to the homes. Ada said a bus could not turn on every small street. A driver needed enough room to turn without hurting anyone.

艾達在車站桌上攤開地圖。「八號公車走這條路，過舊橋，再轉往海邊。」她解釋。橋是安全的，但海邊站牌離大部分住家很遠。一隻鳥停在窗上，往室內看。索爾在地圖的橋邊畫了隻小鳥。妮娜問，如果把站牌移到住家附近會怎樣。艾達說，公車不能在每條小街上轉彎。司機得有足夠的空間，才能安全轉向。

They followed the bus on foot for one part of the road. Nina wore clean shoes, and Milo carried a bottle of water. At the west side of the bridge they found a wide space near a small shop. A cow stood in the field beyond it. A shop owner said the space was used by trucks every morning. “You cannot put a new stop here without asking us,” she said. Nina thanked her. On paper the place had looked perfect. In real life the road belonged to many people doing different things.

他們徒步沿著公車路線走了一小段。妮娜穿著乾淨的鞋，米洛提著水瓶。橋西側有一處靠近小店的寬敞空地，後方田裡站著一頭牛。店主說，每天早上卡車都會使用那塊空地。「不能不問我們就把新站牌設在這裡。」她說。妮娜向她道謝。紙上看起來完美的地方，實際上有許多人正在以不同方式使用。

A man who was born in Willow Bend told them about a walking line beside the river. The path was clean and flat, but the last part led into wet grass. “Don't pull a person toward that path just because it is short,” he warned. “Ask what they can walk on.” Nina looked at the ground carefully. Every new idea seemed to have a hidden question. She had wanted to find one easy answer to the town's problem. Now she saw why making a bus route was work for people who knew roads and safety.

一位在柳灣鎮出生的先生，告訴他們河邊有條步行小徑。前段乾淨平坦，最後卻通往濕草地。「別因為它近，就硬拉人走那條路。」他提醒，「要問人家能走什麼樣的地面。」妮娜仔細看著地面。每個新主意似乎都藏著新問題。她本來想找個簡單答案解決全鎮問題，如今明白了安排公車路線為什麼需要熟悉道路和安全的人來做。

Near the beach station, an old woman asked them to follow her to a bench. She had taken the red bus to visit her sister for thirty years. “Will something bad happen if it ends?” Milo asked. “Not all at once,” she said. “But I will see my sister less.” Nina wrote that down without using the woman's name. Across the road, a boy was learning how to pull a little cart. Nina helped him lift one wheel off a stone. She thought about how many small acts of help could be lost when a road was no longer shared.

靠近海邊車站時，一位老婦人請他們跟她走到長椅旁。她搭紅色公車探望姊姊已有三十年。「如果停駛，會立刻出什麼大事嗎？」米洛問。「不會一下子發生，」她說，「可是我就會比較少見到姊姊。」妮娜不記下婦人的姓名，只寫下這句話。馬路對面有個男孩正在學拉小推車。妮娜幫他把一個車輪抬離石頭。她想到，若大家不再共用這條路，許多小小的幫助也可能消失。

On their way back to the station, Sol showed Ada three possible places for a new stop. Ada drew a line through two. One was too close to a turn; the other blocked a truck door. The third was beside the town library, where the road was wide. “This one may work,” she said, “but a road officer must check it.” Nina put a question mark beside the place. It was the first idea on their map that had not been rejected. That was a small step, yet it felt important.

回車站的路上，索爾給艾達看三個可能的新站點。艾達劃掉其中兩個：一個太靠近轉彎處，另一個擋住卡車出入口。第三個在鎮圖書館旁，路面夠寬。「這裡也許可行，」她說，「但得請道路人員檢查。」妮娜在那個地方畫上問號。這是她們地圖上第一個沒有被否決的想法。雖然只是一小步，卻很重要。

On the next school morning, Nina shared what the walk had taught her. She drew the wide space beside the library and the truck door near the bridge. Her classmates could see why the second place would not work as a stop. One child asked why they could not ask the trucks to move. Nina said the trucks brought food and things the town needed. Changing the bus route without thinking about those workers would only move the problem to someone else. The class made two lists: ideas that looked good on paper and ideas that still looked good after a real walk. The second list was shorter. Nina was not sorry about that. It meant they had learned something before asking the town to spend money.

隔天上學時，妮娜分享實地走訪學到的事。她畫出圖書館旁的寬敞空間，也畫了橋邊的貨車出入口。同學一眼就看出，為什麼後者不適合當站點。一個孩子問，為何不請貨車搬走。妮娜說，貨車運來小鎮需要的食物和物品。改變公車路線卻不考慮那些工人，只是把問題推給別人。全班列了兩份清單：紙上看來可行的想法，以及實際走過後仍可行的想法。第二份清單較短，妮娜卻不遺憾，因為他們在要求鎮上花錢之前已經學到新事情。

Back at home, Nina put the map on the kitchen table. Her father did not tell her which stop to choose. Instead, he asked, “What did you learn about places you did not know?” She spoke about the shop trucks, the wet path, and the woman who wanted to see her sister. Then she said, “A road is not only for the bus.” Her father nodded. The next day she would go back to school with questions, not a finished plan. She was glad she had walked beside the real road before making a big promise.

回到家，妮娜把地圖攤在廚房桌上。父親沒有告訴她該選哪個站牌，而是問：「對那些不熟悉的地方，妳學到了什麼？」她說起店家的貨車、濕滑的小徑，還有那位想見姊姊的老婦人。最後她說：「道路不只是給公車用的。」父親點頭。隔天，她要帶著問題回學校，而不是帶著完成的計畫。她很高興自己在許下大承諾前，先沿著真實的道路走過一趟。
