A Page from the Past

The reply came from Elder Ruan, the town’s oldest repair teacher. He said the ancient air system was built almost a century ago and invited Lin to his house. He was an adult when the main station was rebuilt, and he still kept tools for aid in emergencies. On his wall hung a model of the first lift. The door swung backward rather than forward, which explained why the lantern wall seemed solid when Tao pushed it. “Your instruction is not wrong,” the elder said. “But you must know which version of the machine it describes.” Lin opened her notebook at once.

回信來自鎮上年紀最大的維修老師阮長者。他說古老風道系統建於將近一世紀前，邀琳到他家。他成年時主車站曾重建，至今仍保存緊急救援用具。牆上掛著第一代升降機模型；它的門是向後開，不是向前開，這解釋了陶推提燈牆時為何覺得像實心的。「妳的說明沒有錯，」長者說，「但必須知道它描述的是哪一版機器。」琳立刻翻開筆記。

Elder Ruan gave them a design drawing and showed how to maintain a small air door. He placed a peach on the table and used it to demonstrate a round wheel. A bowl of grain stood beside it, representing the weight of goods once sent up the hill. Tao laughed at the strange lesson, but he remembered each step afterward. Ruan asked Lin to pose a question instead of pretending she knew the answer. She asked why the newer manual used a different number for the lantern wall. The elder’s face became still. He had signed a request to keep the old number, and that request had disappeared.

阮長者拿出設計圖，示範如何保養小風門。他把一顆桃子放在桌上，當作圓輪的示意；旁邊一碗穀物代表從前運上山的貨物重量。陶被奇妙的教學逗笑，之後卻記得每個步驟。阮長者請琳提出問題，不要假裝已知答案。她問為什麼新版手冊替提燈牆用了不同的編號。長者神情一沉：他曾簽名要求保留舊號碼，但申請書失蹤了。

The elder’s wife brought warm tea while he spoke about identity. “A machine has an identity too,” he said. “When you replace one part, the next worker must know what still belongs to the old design.” Lin had seen workers use the same name for two different doors. Now she understood how dangerous that habit could be. Tao placed a finger on his lap and followed the drawing without touching anything sharp. The elder asked them to explain the door in their own words. When they could do that, he agreed to stand beside the guard during a limited test.

長者的妻子端來熱茶，他繼續談到「身分」。「機器也有身分，」他說，「換掉一個零件後，下一位工人必須知道哪些部分仍屬舊設計。」琳看過工人用同樣名稱稱呼兩扇不同的門，此刻才懂這習慣有多危險。陶把手指放在腿上，照圖追看，沒有亂摸尖銳物。長者請他們用自己的話解釋風門，確定兩人真的懂了，才答應陪衛兵做有限測試。

That afternoon Elder Ruan walked with them to a shelter beside the old road. A nurse there was caring for a boy with a fever. His family had carried water from a distant well because the upper pipes were closed. Ruan showed Lin how the first lift had been designed to carry small medical supplies as well as goods. He pointed to two different arrows on the wall. One showed where a worker could walk, and the other showed where air could pass. The older generation had kept those meanings separate. A later painter had covered both arrows with one broad white line. Lin copied the marks before the light faded. She realized that the wall was not hiding only a room. It was hiding a difference between two kinds of movement. If the wrong route opened, air pressure could push against a door intended for people. Ruan asked them to remember the lesson in words a child could understand. Lin wrote, “A safe path for air is not always a safe path for feet.”

當天下午，阮長者帶他們走到舊路旁的避難所。一名護理師在照顧發燒的男孩。因為高處管線關閉，家人只好從遠井提水。阮長者讓琳看第一代升降機如何同時運送少量醫療用品與貨物。他指著牆上兩個不同的箭頭：一個表示工人能走的路，另一個表示空氣能通過的路。上一代始終把這兩種意思分開；後來的油漆工卻用一道寬白線蓋住兩個箭頭。琳趁天黑前抄下記號。她懂了，石牆不只是藏了一個房間，也藏住兩種移動方式的差別。如果錯誤通路被打開，氣壓可能推到原本供人通行的門上。阮長者請他們把教訓寫成孩子也看得懂的話。琳記道：「讓空氣安全通過的路，不一定適合人的腳步。」

The elder asked Lin to leave her drawing in the shelter for families who could not travel to the archive. She made a second copy instead of moving the original. A useful lesson should be available where people needed it most, not only where records were stored.

長者請琳把圖留在避難所，讓無法前往檔案室的家庭閱讀。琳沒有移走原件，而是再抄一份。有用的知識應放在最需要它的人身邊，不該只留在存放紀錄的地方。

Outside the house, a stranger asked whether the town planned to swim through the old water tunnel. Lin said no. That tunnel had a fast current, and the manual did not treat it as a safe route for people. The stranger smiled as if he had wanted an exciting tale. Lin felt a tear of worry rather than joy; rumors made the work harder each day. Elder Ruan came to the door and said it was typical for people to imagine shortcuts when they were afraid. He asked them to use simple signs showing which doors were safe and which were not. Lin promised to return with clear drawings.

屋外，一位陌生人問鎮民是不是打算游過舊水道。琳說不，水流很急，手冊也沒把它列作人能安全通行的路。陌生人笑了，彷彿很想聽驚險故事。琳心裡難過得幾乎落淚，因為每天的傳言都讓工作更難。阮長者走到門口，說人害怕時常幻想捷徑。他請兩人製作簡單標誌，清楚區分安全與不安全的門。琳答應帶著明白易懂的圖再來。
