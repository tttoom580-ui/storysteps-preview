為活著的人投票

A Vote for the Living

遊戲降臨後，我只想開一家修理店 · 第 5 / 25 章 · 3000–4000 · 英中對照

The challenge to the museum reached every screen in District Twelve. Vale's private security agency claimed the building had no living owner because its railway company had closed years ago. Rina pointed to the crowded hall. "What about the people who use this place every day?" she asked. The system answered with a new task: HOLD A PUBLIC CONGRESS BEFORE SUNSET. Lin knew a vote would not repair any broken machine, but it could establish the people as a group. She asked everyone to meet near the old reception desk, where the rules could be read aloud.

挑戰博物館所有權的訊息傳遍了第十二區所有螢幕。維爾的私人保全機構聲稱，經營鐵路的公司早在多年前關閉，因此建築已經沒有在世的所有者。莉娜指向擠滿人的大廳。「那每天使用這地方的人呢？」她問。系統給出新任務：「日落前召開公共大會。」林澄知道投票修不好機器，但可以讓居民以共同體的身分站出來。她請大家到舊接待櫃檯附近集合，讓所有人都能聽見規則。

Some residents wanted to adopt Vale's plan. He promised food, guards, and clean rooms, and his promises were attractive to families who had spent two nights in fear. Others called him a robber and demanded that he be driven away. Lin refused both shortcuts. "His offer may help someone today," she said, "but signing away the building is a permanent decision." Her words did not end the argument. A child's frightened cry made several adults fall silent. Lin remembered how easily public terror could turn fear into anger at the wrong person.

有些居民想採納維爾的方案。他保證提供食物、守衛和乾淨房間。對那些已經恐懼地度過兩夜的家庭而言，這些條件很有吸引力。其他人則叫他強盜，要求把他趕走。林澄拒絕這兩種草率做法。「他的提議或許今天能幫助某些人，」她說，「但簽字交出建築物是永久決定。」她的話並沒有立刻平息爭論。一名孩子害怕地哭了起來，幾位大人才安靜下來。林澄想起，群眾的恐懼有多容易變成對錯誤對象的憤怒。

Rina placed the maintenance records on a table decorated with paper flowers. The simple decoration was left from the museum's last school exhibition, and she could not bear to remove it. "We should cherish what people built here," she said. A retired teacher presented a fundamental question: did ownership mean holding a certificate, or did it also mean caring for a place? Her students had learned both answers in history class. Now the city's future depended on which answer the system would accept. The crowd began to listen rather than shout.

莉娜把維修紀錄放在一張用紙花裝飾的桌子上。那些簡單的裝飾是博物館上一次學校展覽留下的，她捨不得拆掉。「我們應該珍惜人們在這裡建立的東西。」她說。一名退休教師提出根本問題：所有權只代表持有證書，還是也包括照顧一個地方？她的學生以前在歷史課上學過兩種答案。如今城市的未來，竟取決於系統會接受哪一種。人們漸漸開始傾聽，不再只顧著喊叫。

Vale's representative complained that the records were primitive and incomplete. Lin agreed that some pages were missing. Rather than hide that weakness, she invited witnesses to describe their own repairs. A baker spoke about the museum kitchen. The bus driver described a heating pipe. A child named Pei explained how her grandmother had fixed a window so visitors could see the harbor scenery. The system recorded each interaction and compared it with the surviving index. One by one, the names matched. Even the researcher who had supported closing the office admitted the old records were genuine.

維爾的代表抱怨那些紀錄原始又不完整。林澄承認確實有幾頁遺失，但她沒有掩飾這個弱點，而是請見證人描述自己的維修經驗。麵包師傅談到博物館的廚房；公車司機說明修過的暖氣管；一個叫佩的小女孩解釋，她的祖母如何修好窗戶，讓參觀者能望見港口風景。系統記錄了每段互動，再與保存下來的索引比對。名字一個接一個吻合。連曾支持關閉辦公室的研究員，也承認這些舊資料是真實的。

An influential merchant warned that fighting Vale would discourage future investors. A man standing beside him said anyone who opposed the company was a dangerous witch or wizard who had tricked the screens. The accusation horrified Pei, who had used a repair screen herself. Lin asked the merchant to explain how a public vote could prevent private trade. He had no clear answer. "A strong community can buy, sell, and cooperate with businesses," Lin said. "But it must be free to refuse. That is the difference between a customer and a prisoner."

一位頗有影響力的商人警告，與維爾對抗會使未來投資者卻步。他旁邊的男人還說，反對公司的人都是欺騙螢幕的危險巫師或女巫。這項指控把曾經親自操作維修畫面的佩嚇壞了。林澄請商人解釋，公共投票究竟怎麼會妨礙私人交易，他卻答不出清楚的理由。「強健的社區仍然可以和商家買賣、合作。」林澄說，「但人們必須有拒絕的自由。這就是顧客和囚犯的差別。」

Before the vote, Lin spoke about her own conscience. She had once repaired a machine for a company without asking why its safety alarm had been disabled. No one was hurt, but she knew that ignoring the warning had been shameful. "Good behavior does not mean trusting every order," she said. "It means checking the result of our actions." She expected disagreement, yet more people raised their hands than she had imagined. Rina counted each vote aloud while the translator repeated the numbers in three languages. The final outcome was clear: the museum would remain public.

投票前，林澄談起自己的良心。她以前曾替一家公司修過機器，卻沒追問為什麼安全警報被關閉。雖然沒有人受傷，但當她發現自己忽略了什麼時，仍知道忽略警訊是可恥的。「良好的行為不是盲目相信所有命令。」她說，「而是檢查自己行動的後果。」她原以為會有很多人反對，舉手支持的人數卻超出想像。莉娜大聲計票，翻譯員把數字用三種語言重複說出。最後結果很清楚：博物館繼續保持公共所有。

The system recognized the vote, confirmed the witnesses, and restored the museum's public status. For the first time, two nearby districts shared one protected passage overnight. People carried blankets and clean water through it, not weapons. Lin watched the light reach the clinic road and felt a moment of relief. But when she checked the old index again, she found an erased row bearing her father's name and a date only one day before the maintenance office closed. She could not yet explain what he had discovered. She only knew that the next repair would require more than tools.

系統承認了投票、確認見證人，並恢復博物館的公共身分。附近兩個街區第一次共享了一條能維持整夜安全的通道。人們沿著通道搬運的是毯子和乾淨的水，而不是武器。林澄看見光延伸到診所道路，終於稍微鬆了一口氣。但當她再次檢查舊索引時，發現一行被擦除的資料，上面留著父親的名字，日期恰好是維修辦公室關閉前一天。她還不知道父親發現了什麼，只知道下一次修理需要的將不只是工具。
