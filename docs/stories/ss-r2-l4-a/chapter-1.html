A Light Behind the Counter

At seven in the evening, Lin Cheng was sweeping the floor of her repair shop with an old broom. Above the shop, the Moon Harbor Theater was preparing an opera that few people could afford to see. Its owner, Mrs. Zhao, stood beside the counter, practicing a song from her youthful years. Lin was twenty-eight, but she had already repaired the theater's lights for twelve years. She liked broken things because they usually told the truth about what had happened to them.

晚上七點，林澄正拿著一把舊掃帚打掃修理店的地板。店鋪樓上，月港劇院正在準備一齣沒多少人負擔得起門票的歌劇。劇院老闆趙女士站在櫃檯旁，練唱年輕時的歌曲。林澄二十八歲，卻已經替劇院修理燈具十二年。她喜歡壞掉的東西，因為它們通常會誠實地告訴她，自己究竟發生了什麼事。

A strange phenomenon interrupted the rehearsal. Every window showed the same blue screen, although the power had gone out. A voice announced, "The Civic Survival Assembly begins now." Beneath it appeared the seal of an unfamiliar ministry and a bright slogan: Every district must earn its light. Outside, traffic stopped. People raised their phones, but no signal reached anyone. Lin touched a dark window. It felt as cold as a piece of ice, and the letters remained even when she closed her eyes.

一種奇怪的現象打斷了排練。雖然電力已經中斷，每扇窗戶卻都顯示出相同的藍色畫面。一個聲音宣布：「市民生存集會現在開始。」畫面下方出現陌生部門的徽章，以及醒目的標語：「每個街區都必須自己贏得光明。」屋外的車流停住了。人們舉起手機，卻聯絡不到任何人。林澄摸了摸黑暗的窗戶，觸感冰冷得像一塊冰。即使閉上眼睛，那些字仍留在她的視野裡。

The screen displayed three rules. At nine each night, unprotected streets would become challenge zones. A district could protect itself by lighting a public beacon. Repairs could supply energy to a beacon, but each registered person had only four work credits per day. No one could transfer another person's credits without consent. Lin read the rules twice, looking especially at the sentence about repair usage. "This is not ordinary entertainment," Mrs. Zhao whispered. "No," Lin said. "And we have less than two hours to understand it."

畫面顯示出三條規則。每晚九點，沒有受到保護的街道將變成挑戰區。每個街區都能透過點亮公共信標保護自己。修理設備可以替信標提供能量，但每位完成登記的居民每天只有四點工作額度；未經同意，任何人不得轉移別人的額度。林澄把規則讀了兩遍，特別注意關於修理用途的句子。「這可不是普通的娛樂活動。」趙女士低聲說。「不是。」林澄回答，「而且我們只剩不到兩個小時可以弄懂它。」

Then a sharp smell came from the theater basement. A bottle of cleaning acid had fallen from a shelf when the building shook. It was eating through the insulation around a copper wire. Lin turned off the old switch and shouted for everyone to step back. Niko, a messenger from the next street, ran toward the smoke. "I'll pull it out!" he cried. "Don't," Lin warned. "If the wire touches the water, you may be hurt. Find me the dry wooden board by the stairs."

接著，劇院地下室傳來刺鼻的味道。建築物震動時，一瓶清潔用酸液從架上摔了下來，正在腐蝕一條銅線的絕緣層。林澄關掉舊式開關，大聲要所有人退後。隔壁街的送貨員尼可朝冒煙的地方衝去。「我把它拉出來！」他喊道。「別碰。」林澄警告，「電線碰到水，你可能會受傷。去樓梯旁找那塊乾燥的木板給我。」

They could not construct a new power station in one evening, but they could fix the existing emergency lamp. Lin replaced the damaged wire while Niko held a flashlight and Mrs. Zhao kept the basement door open. A blue number appeared over Lin's hand: REPAIR VALUE 6. Her four work credits vanished, and the lamp turned on. Outside, the public beacon gained six points out of twenty. Lin stared at the screen. "That is the exchange," she said. "Effort becomes light, but there is a daily limit."

他們不可能在一個晚上建造全新的發電站，但可以修好現有的緊急照明燈。林澄替換受損電線，尼可舉著手電筒，趙女士則把地下室的門撐開。一串藍色數字出現在林澄手背上：「修理價值：6」。她的四點工作額度消失了，照明燈隨即亮起。屋外的公共信標也獲得了二十分之六的分數。林澄盯著畫面。「原來這就是交換方式。」她說，「勞動能變成光，但每天有額度限制。」

A group in gray jackets arrived just then. Their leader, Vale, claimed his company could choose a safe route for everyone, but only if residents signed away their equipment. His facial expression was calm; his men stood in a shady doorway with empty carts. "Only a coward would refuse professional protection," Vale told the crowd. Lin noticed that his form asked for permanent ownership, not temporary permission. "You are offering help in exchange for our future," she said. "That is not the same thing."

這時，一群穿著灰色外套的人走了過來。領頭的維爾聲稱，他的公司能替所有人選出安全路線，條件是居民必須簽字交出設備。他的臉部表情十分平靜，手下卻推著空車，站在陰暗的門口。「只有懦夫才會拒絕專業保護。」維爾對人群說。林澄注意到，表格要求的是永久所有權，而非暫時使用許可。「你拿幫助交換我們的未來。」她說，「那根本是兩回事。」

Vale stepped forward to interfere with the lamp. Lin blocked his path, but she did not touch him. Instead, she asked the watching residents to read the three rules aloud. The rule about consent was short and persuasive. A baker offered to fix a broken oven door. A bus driver knew how to repair a loose wheel. Mrs. Zhao could mend torn stage curtains. "If we cooperate," Lin said, "we can finish the remaining fourteen points without giving away our tools." The others began to move.

維爾往前一步，想干涉照明燈的運作。林澄擋住他的路，卻沒有碰他。她請圍觀居民把三條規則朗讀出來。關於同意權的規則簡短，而且很有說服力。一名麵包師傅願意修理壞掉的烤箱門；公車司機知道如何修好鬆脫的輪子；趙女士則能縫補破掉的舞台布幕。「只要我們合作，」林澄說，「就能湊齊剩下的十四分，不必把工具交給別人。」大家開始行動。

The repairs worked, though not every attempt earned points. The bus wheel gained three, the oven gained five, and the curtains gained only one. Lin borrowed a hand drill from the theater and showed a neighbor how to repair a loose public bench for another five. At eight fifty-nine, the beacon lit the square. Beyond its warm circle, the road changed into a field of black glass. Vale backed away with his unsigned papers. Nobody celebrated. They watched the silent road and understood how little space their new safety covered.

修理確實有效，雖然不是每次嘗試都能得到分數。公車車輪得了三分，烤箱得了五分，布幕卻只有一分。林澄從劇院借來手鑽，教一位鄰居修好公共長椅鬆掉的零件，又獲得五分。八點五十九分，信標終於照亮了廣場。在溫暖光圈之外，道路變成一片黑色玻璃。維爾拿著沒人簽署的文件退了回去。沒有人慶祝。他們望著沉默的街道，終於明白新得到的安全範圍有多麼狹小。

When the clock struck nine, the beacon produced a final message: DISTRICT TWELVE, PROTECTION VALID FOR ONE NIGHT. A second line appeared beneath it: ORIGINAL MAINTENANCE RECORD MISSING. Lin took a photograph with an old instant camera; the picture showed the message clearly, even though digital phones had stopped working. Mrs. Zhao put the photograph beside the cash register. "Are you opening tomorrow?" she asked. Lin looked at the people still waiting outside. "Yes," she answered. "But tomorrow we will repair more than lamps."

九點鐘聲響起時，信標出現最後一則訊息：「第十二區，保護效力一晚。」下一行寫著：「原始維修紀錄遺失。」林澄用舊式拍立得相機拍了張照片。即使數位手機已無法正常運作，照片仍清楚顯示那則訊息。趙女士把照片放在收銀機旁。「你明天還開店嗎？」她問。林澄看向門外仍在等待的人們。「開。」她回答，「但明天我們要修的，不會只是燈。」
