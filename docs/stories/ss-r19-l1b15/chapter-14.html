At the fair, the librarian opened the box before teachers and town families. Before anyone called the day strange, Sora had noticed how quiet the old town square could become when people were worried. The smallest sounds seemed clear, and the sound of a street clock made the place feel both familiar and new. Rather than act as if every answer was obvious, Sora opened the paper route map and began with what had really happened. The word “bottle” appeared in an example someone had written earlier, and Hana asked if the example fit their situation. “Let us keep ‘police’ in our word notes,” Sora said. “We can check its meaning again if we need it.” In the corner of the paper route map, Sora found the word “total” beside an old drawing and stopped to ask what it meant.

Inside were records proving that the school land had been donated for children to use. This was the point where a simple wish met the world as it really was. Sora wanted to help, but wanting did not make every road safe or every rule fair. Hana asked what the other people involved might lose if the wrong choice were made. The answer took longer than either had expected. They agreed not to build the rest of their plan on a fact they had never checked. Ms Day kept counting the students. Knowing the right year mattered, but getting everyone home mattered more. A little note used “ant” as its key word; Sora kept the note because the rest of the line was still useful. The word “sick” appeared in an example someone had written earlier, and Hana asked if the example fit their situation.

Hana asked who should hear the news next, and whether it belonged to them to share it. They chose the careful answer over the exciting one, though excitement was still in the air.
