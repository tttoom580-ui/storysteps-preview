The first witness who asked to correct his statement was a sculptor named Bren, known for a statue in the parliamentary square. He arrived wearing a cheerful, almost jolly expression that vanished when he saw the recording table. He had been told to allege that Tavi carried a lamp through the archive door, even though he had never met the copyist. A man with a silver mustache had brought him a draft and promised payment for a simple signature. Bren was ashamed. He had imagined that a harmless caption for a public monument could carry no real consequences. Serin asked him to identify the words he had actually spoken and the words written for him. The difference was painfully clear. His original account mentioned smoke and a closed gate; the official page had turned it into an eyewitness accusation.

第一個要求更正證詞的人是雕刻家布倫，議會廣場上的一座雕像出自他手。他帶著近乎開朗的笑容走進來，看見記錄桌後笑容立即消失。有人要他聲稱塔維提燈穿過檔案庫大門，儘管他從未見過這名抄寫員。一名留著銀色鬍子的男子送來草稿，保證只要簽名就會付錢。布倫很羞愧。他原以為給公共紀念碑寫一行簡單說明不會造成嚴重後果。瑟琳請他指出哪些話真正出自自己，哪些是別人替他寫的，差異令人痛心。他原先說的是煙霧與緊閉的大門，官方文書卻把它改成目睹縱火的指控。

Bren led the investigators to a rehearsal studio on the city's outskirts. The building was an oasis of calm in the crowded outskirts. It belonged to an extracurricular academy where students learned public speaking. An administrator had tried to beautify its entrance with flowering shrubs, and a gardener was still there to mow the lawn. Inside, a small room held a stack of identical scripts. Each began with the phrase about the eastern window swallowing the moon. Jori recognized the rhythm of a theatrical climax, not a natural witness description. The academy's hospitable caretaker offered tea and explained that a visiting official had rented the room to train court volunteers. He remembered the official measuring out an ounce of black powder for a demonstration of smoke effects. Serin took no sample without the caretaker's consent and a proper warrant. Their priority was the script, not a theatrical accusation about hidden weapons.

布倫領調查員前往城市郊區的一間排練室。那棟建築是擁擠郊區裡的一處平靜綠洲，屬於一所教授公開演說的課外學院。管理員正努力用花木美化入口，園丁還在修剪草坪。小房間裡堆著一疊內容完全相同的稿紙，每張都以「東窗吞下月亮」開頭。喬里聽出那是舞台劇高潮的節奏，不像證人自然描述。熱情好客的管理員端茶招待，說一位外來官員曾租下房間訓練法庭志工。他記得官員為了示範煙霧效果，量出一盎司的黑色粉末。瑟琳沒有在缺乏同意與正式命令時取樣；眼前的重點是稿紙，不是用戲劇化指控去臆測暗藏武器。

The studio clock revealed a more useful detail. It showed that a group rehearsal and a court registration had occurred at simultaneous times, in places separated by a long carriage ride. The same supervisor was named as personally present at both. Serin asked the caretaker to extract the rental entries from the bound account book rather than tear out the page. He complained that an official had been calling daily to nag him about destroying the drafts. He had refused because the papers belonged to the academy. Jori saw the intimidation as ruthless; Serin insisted on describing the actual calls and their dates. If they rushed to labels, they might lose the simplicity of the contradiction. Either the supervisor traveled impossibly fast, or one attendance record was false. The books could answer that narrower question.

排練室時鐘揭露了一項更有用的細節。帳冊顯示，一場集體彩排與一次法庭登記發生在相同時間，地點卻相隔很長的馬車路程；同一名主管還被記為親自出席兩處。瑟琳請管理員從裝訂帳冊摘錄租借紀錄，而不是撕下整頁。管理員抱怨有官員每天打電話嘮叨，要求他毀掉草稿；他拒絕了，因為紙張屬於學院。喬里認為這是殘酷威嚇，瑟琳卻堅持先記下實際電話內容和日期。若急著貼標籤，就可能失去矛盾本身的清晰度。不是主管以不可能的速度移動，就是其中一份出席紀錄有假。帳冊能回答較狹窄的問題。

In parliament that afternoon, Serin requested a limited preservation order for the studio's documents. The opposition accused her of intruding on artistic freedom; a minister's ally said the state should be allowed to rehearse emergencies. Serin agreed that rehearsals were lawful. What mattered was whether prepared scripts had later been submitted as independent memories. She displayed only the repeated phrase, not Bren's private confession, and let the members inspect the wording. A small coalition from both sides voted to preserve the papers until a judge could review them. The motion passed without celebration. As Serin left, Bren asked whether his honest correction would erase the harm of his first signature. She told him it would not, but his correction could prevent more harm. He accepted the answer and went home to speak with the people who had believed him.

當天下午，瑟琳在議會申請一份範圍有限的文件保全令。反對者指她干涉藝術自由；大臣盟友則說政府應有權排演緊急事件。瑟琳同意彩排合法，重點在於準備好的劇本是否後來被當作獨立記憶送交法庭。她只展示重複句子，沒有公開布倫私人更正內容，並讓議員檢查用語。兩派少數議員組成的聯盟表決同意暫時保全文書，等待法官審查。動議通過，無人慶祝。離開時，布倫問誠實更正能否抹去最初簽名造成的傷害。瑟琳告訴他，不能，但至少能避免更多傷害。他接受答案，回家向曾相信自己的人說明。

Later, the Regent's secretary warned that public patience was beginning to slump. People wanted a villain, a dramatic rescue, and a simple end. Serin wanted those things too, though she had learned to distrust that longing. She looked at the preserved scripts and imagined six people practicing the same lie until it became easier to repeat than to resist. The conspiracy, if there was one, might not depend on a single brilliant criminal; it could depend on ordinary fear and convenience. She wrote to Tavi explaining why the investigation remained open and what he could expect next. She gave him a route to challenge her decisions through another officer. He replied with only two sentences: he wanted his name cleared, and he wanted the other witnesses to be safe. Serin pinned the reply above her desk as a reminder that justice had more than one claimant.

稍後，攝政王祕書警告民眾耐心正逐漸下滑。人們想要壞人、戲劇性的拯救，以及簡單結局。瑟琳也想要這些，只是已學會懷疑自己的渴望。她望著被保全的劇本，想像六個人反覆練習同一個謊言，直到重複比反抗容易。倘若真有陰謀，也許它不靠某個天才罪犯，而是靠一般人的恐懼與求方便。她寫信向塔維說明調查為何仍未結束，以及接下來會發生什麼，也提供向其他官員申訴的管道。他只回了兩句話：他想洗清姓名，也希望其他證人安全。瑟琳把回信釘在桌上，提醒自己正義不只屬於一個人。
