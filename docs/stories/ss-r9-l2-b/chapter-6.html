The Raincoat Parade

雨季借你一把傘 · 第 6 / 25 章 · 1000–2000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

On the eve of the campus festival, Dean He asked Iris and Jun to accept a new responsibility. The main building would close early, and a crowd was expected at the western bus stop. Their ten umbrellas could not serve everyone. Iris made a short list of priorities: dry waiting space, clear signs, and a safe way to report damage. A volunteer in a yellow raincoat offered to examine every handle before the event. Jun said that the team's favorite part of the project should be honest limits, not impressive numbers. They would send people toward the covered hall when the umbrellas ran out, rather than promise something the rack could not give.

校園節慶前夕，何主任請艾莉絲和俊承擔新的責任。主教學大樓將提早關閉，西側公車站預計會有大批人潮。十把傘不可能照顧所有人。艾莉絲列出簡短的優先事項：乾燥的等候空間、清楚標示，以及安全的損壞回報方式。一名穿黃色雨衣的志工願意在活動前逐一檢查傘柄。俊說，團隊最值得自豪的應該是誠實說明限制，而不是漂亮的數字。雨傘用完時，他們會引導人們去有遮棚的走廊，不會承諾傘架做不到的事。

A musician from the arts club played a slow tune while volunteers hung signs along the sidewalk. A child pointed at a giraffe on a painted festival banner and asked why it wore boots. Iris laughed and let the child draw an umbrella beside the animal. The official festival plan, however, showed a food stand directly in front of the return area. Jun brought the map to the organizer and asked for a change. The organizer was sensitive to public complaints and first treated the question as an attack. Iris explained the problem with a drawing: a wet umbrella could block a person using a cane. The stand was moved without a public argument.

藝術社一名樂手演奏緩慢的曲調，志工則沿人行道掛上標示。小孩指著節慶布條上的長頸鹿，問牠為何穿靴子。艾莉絲笑著讓孩子在動物旁畫一把傘。不過，正式活動圖上有一個食物攤位，正好擋在歸還區前。俊拿著地圖去請主辦人調整。主辦人很在意公眾抱怨，一開始把問題當成攻擊。艾莉絲用圖畫說明：濕雨傘可能擋住持手杖的人。攤位最後移走，沒有引發公開爭吵。

The repair lesson began under a wide roof. Mrs. Lan showed the group how to check a loose joint and when to stop rather than try to repair a cracked frame. Jun read a short piece of poetry from a former student who had walked this route during a storm years earlier. The poem said that a small light could carry the soul of a town through a dark night. Iris found the line beautiful, but she did not put it on the safety sign. A warning needed plain words. She wrote “Do not use a broken umbrella” in large letters and placed a damaged example beside it. Jun explained that the term 'safe shelter' referred only to rooms approved by the campus office.

修繕課在寬大的屋頂下開始。蘭女士示範如何檢查鬆動接頭，以及遇到傘架裂開時應停止使用，而不是硬修。俊讀了一首多年前學生寫的短詩；那名學生曾在暴風雨中走過這條路。詩說，一點微光也能在黑夜裡承載一座城鎮的靈魂。艾莉絲覺得詩句很美，但沒有把它放在安全標示上。警告需要直接的文字。她用大字寫下「不要使用損壞的雨傘」，旁邊放一把損壞的示範傘。 俊解釋，「安全避難處」這個術語只指校方核准的室內空間。

Before sunset, the university announced that the storm might grow stronger. The team checked the weather again and reduced the outdoor hours. A visitor from the western coast remembered a war museum exhibit about people sheltering strangers during difficult times. He said the memory made this small service feel meaningful. Jun thanked him but reminded volunteers that they were not a rescue team. They would close the rack if the wind became unsafe. Iris noticed how often his careful words saved the project from being described as a grand promise. She once found that habit annoying. Now she understood that trust grew when promises matched ability.

日落前，學校宣布暴風雨可能增強。團隊再次確認天氣，縮短戶外服務時間。一名來自西岸的訪客提起戰爭博物館的展覽，內容是艱難時期人們如何接納陌生人避雨。他說，這段記憶讓小小的服務更有意義。俊謝謝他，也提醒志工他們不是救援隊；若風勢太強就必須關閉傘架。艾莉絲注意到，俊謹慎的說法常讓計畫不至於被描述成宏大的承諾。過去她覺得這習慣煩人，現在卻懂得：當承諾符合能力，信任才會成長。

At eight, the first heavy shower reached the gate. Volunteers guided students to the hall in pairs. The musician stopped playing so announcements could be heard clearly. When a strong gust turned one umbrella inside out, Iris removed it from service and wrote its number in the log. Jun checked that nobody had been hurt. They shut the outdoor rack before the official warning became severe. Some students were disappointed, but the covered route stayed open. “We did less than we planned,” Iris said as they packed up. “We did what was safe,” Jun answered. She realized those were not opposite truths.

晚上八點，第一波大雨抵達校門。志工兩人一組，把學生引導進走廊。樂手停下演奏，讓廣播可以聽清楚。一陣強風吹得一把傘翻面，艾莉絲立刻將它停用，並把編號寫進紀錄。俊確認沒有人受傷。正式警戒升高前，他們就先關閉戶外傘架。有些學生失望，但有遮棚的路線仍可通行。「我們做的比計畫少，」收拾時艾莉絲說。「但做的是安全的事，」俊回答。她發現這兩句話並不矛盾。

When the festival ended early, Jun walked Iris toward the library instead of the bus stop. They both had dry shoes but soaked sleeves. He asked whether she regretted the project now that it had brought more work than praise. Iris said no. She was disappointed in the weather, not in their choices. A quiet pause followed, comfortable enough that neither needed to fill it. Jun told her his evening education classes ended late because he worked at a community center in the mornings. Iris told him she hoped to apply for a design fellowship next year. Their goals were different, but they finally spoke about lives beyond the umbrella rack.

節慶提早結束後，俊陪艾莉絲往圖書館走，而不是去公車站。他們的鞋子還乾著，袖子卻濕了。他問她，這個計畫帶來的工作比稱讚還多，她會不會後悔。艾莉絲說不會；她失望的是天氣，不是他們做的選擇。接著是一段舒適的安靜，不需要誰刻意說話。俊告訴她，自己早上在社區中心工作，所以教育系晚間課程常到很晚才結束。艾莉絲說，她希望明年申請設計研究獎助。兩人的目標不同，但終於聊起傘架以外的生活。
