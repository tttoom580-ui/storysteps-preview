A Library Beneath the Courtyard

星債未償：我們替夜空記住名字 · 第 7 / 25 章 · 4000–5000 · 英中對照 · 校訂完成

The next observatory had been purchased through an acquisition that no one in the surrounding neighborhood remembered approving. Its new owner lived in a house with an enormous backyard, where a watchman collected a bonus for reporting trespassers. Xu found a chunk of white mineral near the gate and rubbed it between his fingers. The powder contained calcium, useful for repairing old mortar but not the silver lenses inside. A museum label called the dome a colonial masterpiece. A republican historian had challenged that description in an earlier essay, arguing that public ownership did not begin with the new regime. Yin could comprehend the artistic praise, yet he disapproved of the silence surrounding the workers who had built it. The implication was clear: if ownership of the building could be separated from ownership of its shared records, the missing star-lines could move without anyone asking why. Their permit allowed entry to the public library, not to the sealed storehouse. Rather than attempt the risky act of penetrating the lock, they asked the librarian to show them the original catalogue. She said the older index was kept under the courtyard floor.

下一座觀星臺曾經歷一次收購，附近居民都不記得曾同意這件事。新主人住在後院寬廣的宅邸，守衛靠通報闖入者領額外獎金。徐岸在門邊拾起一塊白色礦物，指尖一搓就成粉末。粉裡有可修補舊牆泥灰的鈣，卻不適合修理銀鏡。博物館標籤稱圓頂是殖民時代的傑作。一名主張共和制度的歷史學者早在文章中質疑這說法，認為公共所有權並非新政權才開始。殷洛理解藝術讚美，卻反對它完全不提建造工人的貢獻。背後問題很清楚：如果建築的所有權能與公共紀錄的所有權分離，失蹤星線就可能在無人察覺下移走。他們的許可只能進公共圖書室，不能進封存倉庫。兩人沒有冒險硬闖鎖門，而是請圖書員拿原始目錄；她說舊索引藏在院子地板下。

A sophomore student from the local academy volunteered to help. He showed them a spacious room beneath the courtyard, lit by narrow windows. Its central shelf resembled a spine, with hundreds of thin booklets stacked along either side. The student explained that the books contained public declarations made during the city's founding. Some were merely symbolic, while others established the paths used for transit of food and medicine. A scrap of paper inside the catalogue warned readers about secret surveillance during an earlier political struggle. Xu understood why the librarian wanted witnesses present before anything was removed. The room had been raided more than once, and survivors had marked where pages were torn out. Yin photographed the shelves by drawing them carefully; a true photographic device was forbidden in the archive, but drawing was allowed. The student watched both men sign the visitors' log and said it was the first time inspectors had ever asked his permission to record his help.

地方學院一名二年級學生主動協助，帶他們走進院子底下的寬敞房間，窄窗透進微光。中央書架像脊椎，兩側堆疊數百本薄冊。學生說書中記載建城時的公共宣言，有些只是象徵，另一些則確立運送食物與藥物的通行路線。目錄裡夾著一張紙，警告讀者早年政治衝突曾有秘密監視。徐岸明白圖書員為何要求在見證人在場時才移動物品。書庫被搜查過不止一次，生還者在遭撕頁處留了標記。殷洛以細緻繪圖記錄書架；這裡禁止攝影器材，卻允許繪圖。學生看著兩人簽訪客簿，說這是他第一次遇到檢查者先徵求同意才記錄他的協助。

Behind the lowest shelf Xu found a repair ledger from the generation that built the dome. The first entries described ordinary leaks, cracked tiles, and adjustments to a revolving roof. Then the dates jumped forward by eight years. A new owner had installed a route that carried witness copies out of the library and into a private collection. The signatures on those pages were noticeably different from the earlier ones. Yin did not claim they were forged; handwriting could change with age or injury. Instead he compared their order with the public minutes. The minutes described a vote held two days after the copies had already been moved. That reversal could not be explained by handwriting alone. Xu asked the student whether the public knew about it. The student admitted that he had never seen those pages in class. His history course ended with the dedication ceremony, when everyone smiled beneath the dome. There had been no lesson about the years that followed.

最底層書架後，徐岸找到圓頂建造年代的修理日誌。前幾項只是漏水、裂瓦和旋轉屋頂的調整，日期卻突然跳過八年。新主人設置一條通道，把見證副本從公共書庫搬到私人收藏。那些頁上的簽名與前段明顯不同。殷洛沒有立刻說是偽造，因為年齡或傷勢都可能改變字跡；他改把順序與公開會議紀錄比對。紀錄顯示表決是在副本搬走兩天後才舉行，這種倒置不能只靠筆跡變化解釋。徐岸問學生公眾知不知道。學生坦承課堂從未看過那些頁面。歷史課只教到圓頂落成典禮，大家在頂下微笑，之後的年月根本沒有出現在教材裡。

As evening fell, the owner arrived and demanded that the visitors leave. The librarian showed him their valid permit and reminded him that the books were protected under the founding agreement. He threatened to close the courtyard for repairs. Xu offered to repair the loose floor panel immediately, on condition that the public catalogue remain accessible. The owner refused. Yin suggested a neutral solution: the panel would be secured for safety, the books would be inventoried by the school and library, and all sides would meet the next morning. No one celebrated. The owner might still resist, and the documents could still be moved overnight. The librarian recruited two neighbors to keep lawful watch from the street. Walking toward their lodging, Xu asked Yin why he always sounded patient during arguments. Yin answered, "Because I am frightened, and I need a way to keep that fear from deciding for me." Xu wanted to hold his hand, but instead he stayed beside him until the lanterns came on.

傍晚屋主到場，要求訪客離開。圖書員出示他們的有效許可，提醒他書籍受建城協議保護。屋主威脅以修繕為由關閉院子。徐岸願意立刻修理鬆動地板，條件是公共目錄保持開放；屋主拒絕。殷洛提議中立方案：先固定地板確保安全，由學校與圖書館共同清點書冊，隔日召開會議。沒人慶祝，屋主仍可能阻撓，文件也可能在夜間被移走。圖書員找兩名鄰居從街上合法守望。前往住處途中，徐岸問殷洛為何爭論時總聽起來有耐心。殷洛回答：「因為我害怕，需要一個方法，不讓恐懼替我做決定。」徐岸想握他的手，最後只是一直陪在他身旁，直到街燈亮起。
