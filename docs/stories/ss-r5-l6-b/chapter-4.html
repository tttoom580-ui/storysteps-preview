Under the Museum Roof

Soren arranged for Nora to visit the city's small museum, where an archaeology exhibit explained how previous generations survived storms. She admired the exhibit's careful aesthetic, but its captions contained only approximate flood heights. An attendant with a silver badge introduced them to the curator, who was preparing a fire-safety exercise after a small blaze in a storage room. The museum wanted better warning signs and could offer a place to observe visitors. Nora declined to promise a working product before testing. Years in a company obsessed with prestige had taught her how easily an attractive demonstration could conceal a missing warranty or a dangerous assumption.

索倫安排諾拉參觀市立小博物館，館內的考古展介紹歷代居民如何度過暴風雨。她欣賞展覽細緻的美感，卻發現說明牌只寫了大約的洪水高度。一名佩戴銀色識別章的職員帶兩人見館長，館長正因儲藏室曾有一場小火而準備消防演練。博物館需要更好的警示標誌，也願提供觀察訪客的場所。諾拉拒絕在測試前承諾產品能用。她在迷戀聲望的公司待了多年，知道漂亮展示多容易遮住缺失的保固或危險假設。

The curator wanted to preside over every design meeting. Soren explained gently that museum staff could guide visitors, while engineers and accessibility testers had separate responsibilities. A visiting scholar joked that she was a disciple of an older curator who had hated new technology. Nora said their aim was not to privatize the building's safety knowledge but to make clear instructions available to everyone. The scholar asked whether an implicit agreement to share data would be enough. “No,” Nora replied. “We need written permission and a clear description of what we collect.” The answer caused some fuss, but it also made the curator trust her judgment.

館長想親自主持所有設計會議。索倫溫和地說，館方可以指引訪客，工程師與無障礙測試者則各有不同職責。一名訪問學者打趣說，自己是那位討厭新科技的老館長門生。諾拉表示，她們不是要把館舍安全知識私有化，而是讓明確指示能提供給每個人。學者問，默示同意資料共享是否已經足夠。「不行，」諾拉回答，「我們需要書面同意，並清楚說明蒐集哪些資料。」這引起一陣小騷動，卻也讓館長更信任她的判斷。

In the restoration room they watched an old signal lamp radiate a soft circle of light. A technician demonstrated its rotation, then showed how a veil of smoke could make even a bright lamp difficult to see. Nora took notes for the future test plan. On a shelf lay a shaver, a pair of work gloves, and a stray boot recovered from a shipwreck. Such ordinary objects told stories more clearly than grand speeches. Soren asked whether she had developed an addiction to measuring every possibility. She smiled and admitted she was frightened of missing something. He answered that proper review required several people, not one exhausted person trying to predict the world.

在修復室裡，他們看著一盞舊訊號燈向四周散發柔光。技師示範燈具如何旋轉，又解釋一層煙幕足以讓明亮的燈也難以看清。諾拉把這些觀察記進日後測試計畫。架上放著一把刮鬍刀、一雙工作手套和從沉船找到的落單靴子。這些平凡物品比宏大演說更能講故事。索倫問她是否已經對衡量每種可能性上癮。她笑著承認自己害怕漏掉細節。他回答，正當審查需要很多人共同參與，而不是一個筋疲力盡的人妄想預測全世界。

Afterward they visited a vineyard beyond the city, where the museum ran a workshop about soil, rain, and local history. Nora had expected a lecture; instead children measured water in jars and asked why some roots survived floods. The instructor said that learning often began with the courage to look foolish. The remark seemed to enlighten Nora more than another polished presentation would have. She described her plan for a simple public warning system, including light, vibration, and printed directions. The instructor offered a future field test but warned that outdoor equipment would need proof against moisture. Nora thanked her and wrote down the limitation before the invitation.

隨後兩人到城外葡萄園，參加博物館舉辦的土壤、雨水與地方史工作坊。諾拉原以為要聽講座，卻看見孩子們用玻璃罐量水，問為什麼某些樹根能撐過洪水。講師說，學習往往始於敢於顯得愚笨的勇氣。這句話帶給諾拉的啟發，勝過另一場光鮮的簡報。她介紹自己的公共警示系統構想，包括燈光、震動與印刷指引。講師願意將來提供戶外測試場地，但提醒設備必須先證明能抵抗潮濕。諾拉道謝，並在邀請之前先寫下這項限制。

On the return train, Soren told Nora he liked working beside her because she changed her mind when evidence changed. She felt warmth and suspicion at once; Julian had praised her intelligence shortly before asking her to surrender it. Soren appeared to notice her hesitation. He said she owed him no answer, and the project would continue under the written agreement either way. They spent the rest of the journey discussing the museum's emergency exits. Nora was grateful that he did not demand a personal response as payment for professional respect. She opened her notebook and made a final heading: “What we still do not know.”

回程火車上，索倫告訴諾拉，他喜歡與她共事，因為她會隨證據改變看法。她同時感到溫暖與戒備；朱利安也曾在要求她放棄判斷之前讚美她聰明。索倫似乎看出了她的遲疑。他說她不欠任何回答，不論如何，計畫都會依書面協議繼續。餘下車程兩人只談博物館的逃生出口。諾拉很感激他沒有要求私人回應，當作尊重專業的報酬。她打開筆記本，寫下最後一個標題：「我們仍不知道的事。」

The museum sent a follow-up letter identifying the exact conditions for a future trial: no real emergency broadcast, trained observers, visible exit signs, and the ability to stop the exercise at once. Nora thanked the curator for a contract more useful than a flattering invitation. She placed the conditions beside the studio calendar and assigned each to a named reviewer. The rules seemed strict, yet they protected the museum as well as her own team. When Edda asked whether the museum was too cautious, Nora said that people trusted a public building precisely because its managers were required to take such questions seriously.

博物館寄來後續信件，列出未來測試的確切條件：不得發送真正的緊急廣播、須有受訓觀察員、出口標誌必須清楚、演練也要能立刻中止。諾拉感謝館長，因為這份合約比奉承的邀請更實用。她把條件放在工作室行事曆旁，為每項指定審查者。規則看似嚴格，卻同時保護博物館與團隊。艾達問館方是否太謹慎，諾拉回答，人們信任公共建築，正因管理者有義務認真看待這類問題。
