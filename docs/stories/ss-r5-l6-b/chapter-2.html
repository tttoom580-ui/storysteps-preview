有兩扇窗的工作室

The Workshop with Two Windows

這次，我把自己的名字留在門上 · 第 2 / 25 章 · 5000–6000 · 英中對照

The vacant print shop on Lantern Street was shabby, but it had two broad windows and a sink that still worked. Nora promised the landlord she would abide by the fire regulations before inviting anyone inside. An electrician discovered a dangerous fuse and advised her not to run machinery that week. She asked for the estimate in writing, then affirmed the revised budget in her notebook. Her savings would expire sooner than she liked if every surprise cost this much. When a novice volunteer offered to repair the wiring without training, she thanked him and refused. It was a small, necessary decision: this workplace would not repeat the old company's habit of rewarding reckless shortcuts.

燈街上的空印刷店十分破舊，但有兩扇寬大的窗戶，洗手槽也還能用。諾拉向房東承諾，邀請任何人進入前都會遵守消防規定。電工發現一個危險的保險絲，建議她本週不要開動機器。她要求書面估價，再在筆記本中確認修改後的預算。如果每次意外都這麼昂貴，她的積蓄很快就會用完。一名新手志工提議不經訓練就修好線路，她道謝後拒絕。這是必要的小決定：新工作室不再複製舊公司獎勵冒險捷徑的習慣。

Across the street, a community café displayed a wooden alligator on its shelf. The owner kept a record player near the counter; on slow evenings a deep bass voice drifted into the street. Her hospitality was practical rather than ceremonial. She brought Nora a liter of soup and bread with orange syrup, saying that ambitious people often forgot dinner. Nora laughed, then admitted that the owner's infectious humor made the empty shop feel less lonely. A notebook behind the counter recorded colloquial phrases from customers in several languages. Nora wondered whether commonplace words like “safe” and “ready” meant the same thing to everyone. She decided her future warning signs would need testing, not guesses.

街對面社區咖啡館的架子上放著一隻木製短吻鱷。老闆在櫃檯旁留著唱片機，冷清的夜晚會有低沉的男低音飄到街上。她的待客之道很實際，並不講究排場。她端來一公升湯和淋了橙糖漿的麵包，說野心大的人常忘記吃晚飯。諾拉笑出聲，也承認老闆極具感染力的幽默，讓空蕩的店面沒那麼寂寞。櫃檯後的筆記本記錄著多種語言的口語說法。諾拉想，像「安全」「準備好了」這些平凡字眼，對每個人的意義是否相同？她決定未來的警示標誌必須經過測試，不能靠猜。

A man arrived at twilight carrying a rolled drawing and a tool case. His name was Soren Wu, an accessibility consultant referred by the neighborhood association. He pointed out that the pavement outside the door had a slight tilt that might endanger wheelchair users. Nora felt a rash impulse to explain why she already understood accessibility. Instead, she asked him to measure the slope with her. He said he was not there to stand in a spotlight or rescue a troubled founder. His own project, an accessible public route through the market, needed reliable warning signs. They could cooperate for that limited purpose, or not. Nora appreciated the clarity.

黃昏時，一名男子帶著捲起的圖紙與工具箱到來。他叫索倫・吳，是社區協會介紹的無障礙顧問。他指出門外的人行道略有傾斜，可能危及輪椅使用者。諾拉一瞬間想衝動辯解，說自己早就理解無障礙設計；但她改口請他一起測量斜坡。索倫說他不是來搶聚光燈，也不是來拯救困境中的創辦人。他自己的計畫，是打造一條穿過市場的無障礙公共路線，需要可靠的警示標誌。他們可以為這個有限目標合作，也可以不合作。諾拉珍惜這份清楚。

The café owner invited them to a celebration for the district's oldest resident. A bottle of whiskey remained unopened on the table because the guest of honor preferred tea. Someone suggested hiding the damaged shop behind attractive curtains for an investor photograph. Nora said it would be impossible to reconcile that appearance with the inspection report. Soren agreed, but he did not speak over her. She noticed how unusual that felt. He asked whether she wanted feedback on the layout, and waited for an answer. That night she made two lists: repairs that could not wait, and decorations that could. The first list was longer. It also made the beginning more honest.

咖啡館老闆邀請兩人參加社區最年長居民的慶祝聚會。桌上有一瓶未開封的威士忌，因為壽星寧願喝茶。有人建議在投資人拍照時，用漂亮窗簾遮住受損的店面。諾拉說，那種外觀不可能與檢查報告中的事實相容。索倫同意，卻沒有搶著替她發言。她注意到這種感覺竟如此陌生。他先問她是否想聽動線意見，等她回答才開口。那晚她列出兩張表：不能拖延的修理，以及可以延後的裝飾。第一張表比較長，卻也讓這個起點更加誠實。

Before locking up, Nora turned off the lights and stood by the window. She could see the café, the bus stop, and the uneven pavement that would soon need a public ramp. Her old life had been arranged around spectacular presentations; this one was taking shape through measurements, invoices, and soup. She did not know whether the new business would survive or whether Soren would remain involved. For the first time in months, uncertainty felt different from danger. She recorded each promise she had made that day and who had agreed to it. A written boundary, she thought, was not the opposite of trust. It was a place where trust could begin.

鎖門前，諾拉關掉電燈，站在窗邊。她看得見咖啡館、公車站，以及很快就需要公共坡道的崎嶇人行道。她過去的人生圍著華麗簡報轉，如今卻由測量、發票和一碗湯慢慢成形。她不知道新公司能否活下去，也不知道索倫會不會繼續參與。數個月來第一次，不確定感與危險感竟有了不同。她記錄當天自己做出的每一項承諾，以及誰同意了它。她想，白紙黑字的界線並非信任的反面，而是信任可以開始的地方。

When Soren left, Nora measured the shop once more. The west window would let her observe the street, but afternoon sun could make screens difficult to read. She sketched a movable shade and noted that any permanent construction required the landlord's approval. Tomorrow she would visit a bank, inquire about a business account, and ask Mina which professional fees were essential. She did not expect Soren to pay for the work, and he had made no such offer. That absence of rescue was unexpectedly reassuring. She was free to welcome help without turning a new acquaintance into a new source of authority over her life.

索倫離開後，諾拉又量了一次店面。西側窗戶讓她能看到街道，但午後陽光可能使螢幕難讀。她畫下活動遮陽板，並記下永久施工須經房東同意。明天她要去銀行詢問商業帳戶，也要問米娜哪些專業費用必要。她不期待索倫替她付錢，他也沒提出這種邀約。這份沒有拯救姿態的相處，竟令她安心。她可以歡迎幫助，卻不必把新認識的人變成掌握自己人生的新權威。
