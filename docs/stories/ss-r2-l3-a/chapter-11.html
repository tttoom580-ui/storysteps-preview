The Red Receipts

這座圖書館，禁止替別人作答 · 第 11 / 25 章 · 2000–3000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

Mira received a parcel from a person who signed only as “A Reader.” It contained copies of seven red receipts and a short letter claiming that the library had mistaken obedience for trust. The original questions had concerned hunger, missing wages, and a criminal case that was never heard. Someone had accepted false answers on behalf of the question owners. Each owner then appeared in a new record with a blank space where a memory should have been.

米拉收到署名「一名讀者」的包裹，裡面有七張紅色收據副本和一封短箋，指稱圖書館把服從誤認成信任。原先的問題涉及飢餓、欠薪以及一樁從未審理的刑案。有人替提問者接受了假答案；隨後每個提問者的資料上，都出現了一處本該存放記憶的空白。

Ada explained that a blank did not prove the memory had been destroyed. The old filing system placed disputed answers in a separate room until the owner chose whether to accept or withdraw them. Hale had begun calling that room the “correction office.” Mira asked to see its entry log. The log showed people entering but almost nobody leaving. Tomas recognized his mother’s name, and for a moment his careful voice failed him.

艾達解釋，空白不等於記憶已被消滅。舊制度把有爭議的答案放進另一間房，直到提問者決定接受或撤回。黑爾把那裡改稱「更正辦公室」。米拉要求查看出入紀錄，發現很多人進去，幾乎沒人出來。托瑪斯認出了母親的名字，向來鎮定的聲音瞬間發顫。

The first receipt in the parcel described a bloody bandage found near a collapsed kitchen, while a cool breeze entered through an open window. A shopkeeper had asked for credit to repair the roof after a storm. Instead, an official labeled him a criminal for failing to pay a fee he had never been told about. His daughter wrote an emotional letter about their bad fortune and the hunger that followed. A stray dog came to lick crumbs from the last loaf of bread. A parcel of medicine arrived, along with a small bag of pepper, but the delivery worker found that the family had already moved. At a public meeting, the official gave a confident pitch: he claimed the shopkeeper had tried to pretend the roof was damaged before the storm. Neighbors stood to resist that claim. One remembered the owner's torn sleeve, another described his slender build as he climbed the steep slope behind the house, and a third recalled the twist in a cable that had broken before the rain. Their union collected these accounts rather than choosing a single speaker. A worker with a bruised wrist signed the final statement himself. The record revealed that no one had investigated the official's original accusation before it entered the library as an accepted answer.

包裹裡第一張收據記錄廚房倒塌處的一條血跡斑斑的繃帶，涼風吹進開著的窗。店主曾申請信用貸款修理暴風雨後的屋頂，官員卻因他沒繳一筆從未告知的費用，便把他叫作罪犯。女兒寫了充滿情緒的信，描述厄運與隨後的飢餓。流浪狗舔掉最後一條麵包的碎屑。一包藥和少許胡椒送來時，送貨員發現一家人已搬走。公開會議上，官員提出一套自信說詞，聲稱店主假裝屋頂早在暴風雨前就損壞。鄰居起身反對。一人記得店主破掉的袖子，另一人說他瘦長的身影沿屋後陡坡攀爬，第三人記得雨前電纜已扭曲斷裂。工會蒐集這些證詞，而不是只挑一位發言者。一名手腕瘀傷的工人親自簽署最後聲明。紀錄顯示，官員最早的指控在進入圖書館成為被接受的答案前，根本沒人調查。

Tomas kept returning to his mother's entry in the log. He had spent years believing she had simply chosen to leave the city. The thought that her question might have been taken from her was harder to bear than a clear accusation. Mira did not tell him that everything would be repaired. She offered to review the dates with him whenever he wished. He said yes after a long silence. Together they discovered that his mother had signed a request to keep her question open, while a later sheet claimed she had agreed to a final answer. The second sheet bore no independent witness. They copied both documents and left the originals where they belonged. Tomas asked whether the evidence meant his mother had never consented. Mira answered only that the record did not show valid consent. He thanked her for making that difference clear. For him, careful words were becoming a form of hope. He had something real to pursue, not merely an old story about abandonment.

托瑪斯一直回頭看母親在出入簿上的紀錄。他多年來相信母親只是選擇離開城市。想到母親的問題可能被奪走，比明確指控還難受。米拉沒有說一切都會修復，只提議在他願意時一起核對日期。他沉默很久才答應。兩人發現母親曾簽署要求保留問題未解，後來一張紙卻稱她同意最終答案，而後者沒有獨立見證人。他們複製兩份文件，把正本留在原位。托瑪斯問這是否表示母親從未同意。米拉只回答，紀錄沒有顯示有效同意。他感謝她講清楚這個差異。對他而言，謹慎用詞正變成希望：他有真實的事可以追查，而不只是關於遺棄的舊故事。

A red receipt might look like a small administrative error to someone who never needed the service. To Tomas it carried his mother's unanswered words. Mira placed his copy in a protective sleeve and let him decide when to read it again.

對不需要這項服務的人而言，紅色收據或許只是小小行政錯誤；對托瑪斯卻是母親未說完的話。米拉把副本裝進保護套，讓他決定何時再閱讀。

Mira asked him whether he wanted to continue. Tomas said yes, but only if the search remained careful enough to protect the people recorded there. She agreed. Their work was no longer an exciting puzzle. Real lives were attached to every line, and each line deserved the same attention June would have wanted for her own name.

米拉問托瑪斯是否還要繼續。他說願意，但調查必須足夠謹慎，不能傷害被記錄在裡面的人。米拉答應了。這已不再是令人興奮的解謎遊戲；每一行字都連著真實人生，也值得得到小君希望自己名字得到的重視。
