婚禮照片

The Wedding Photograph

不必編造的約會 · 第 11 / 25 章 · 1000–2000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

The new exhibit needed an author for each story card. Lian had written most of them, but the memories belonged to the people who shared them. At the town archive, a guard led the group through a narrow hall to a desk full of old photographs. One showed a wedding held outside the reading room sixty years earlier. A priest stood near the gate, and a waiter carried tea to the guests. Lian was surprised that the building had served so many different purposes. Jun measured the frame in centimeters and said a copy could fit beside the main stage without blocking the path.

新展覽需要替每張故事卡標明作者。蓮寫了大部分文字，但記憶屬於分享的人。鎮公所檔案室裡，一位警衛帶大家穿過狹窄走廊，到擺滿舊照片的桌前。一張照片拍到六十年前在閱覽室外舉行的婚禮，牧師站在門邊，侍者向客人送茶。蓮沒想到這棟建築用途如此多樣。俊用公分量相框，說影本可放在主舞臺旁，又不會擋住走道。

The photographer's daughter came to meet them. She was a skilled teacher who had kept her father's records. She told them that the bride and groom had both worked at the library and later helped plant trees. A crow had nested above the window that morning, and the family still laughed about its loud calls. Lian wanted to include that detail because it made the scene alive. The daughter agreed but asked them not to write a false romantic story about the couple. “Their marriage was not a puzzle for strangers to solve,” she said. “It was their own life.”

攝影師的女兒來見他們。她是有經驗的老師，保存著父親的紀錄。她說新郎與新娘都曾在圖書室工作，後來還一起種樹。婚禮當天有隻烏鴉在窗上築巢，家人至今仍笑牠叫得很大聲。蓮想把這個細節放入展覽，讓畫面活起來。女兒同意，但請他們不要替新人編造浪漫故事。「他們的婚姻不是陌生人可以解的謎，」她說。「那是他們自己的人生。」

Outside the archive, Jun noticed Lian's raised eyebrow. “You disagree with our script now?” he asked. “With one part,” she said. “Our fictional couple keeps talking about saving places, but we have not shown how other relationships matter.” She suggested adding friends, families, and neighbors to the story. Jun did not argue. Nor did he ask whether she was hinting at them. They sat on a bench and rewrote a scene so that the main characters asked older residents for help instead of acting like heroes. The result felt less dramatic, but more human. Lian thanked him for listening carefully.

走出檔案室，俊注意到蓮挑起眉。「妳不同意我們的劇本了？」他問。「其中一部分，」她說。「虛構情侶老在談拯救地方，卻沒演出其他關係的重要。」她建議加進朋友、家庭和鄰居。俊沒有爭辯，也沒問她是否在暗示兩人。他們坐在長椅上改寫一幕，讓主角向長者求助，而非自以為英雄。新版本少了戲劇性，卻更有人味。蓮謝謝他細心傾聽。

Back on campus a student complained about the small entrance fee for the festival. The fee belonged to the university, not the clubs, and Lian agreed that visitors should be told where it went. Mei helped write a sign explaining that the fee paid for cleaning and safety staff. A classmate wanted to answer the complaint with a rude joke; Lian stopped him. “People should not have to enjoy our tone before they can ask a question,” she said. Jun checked the sign and asked whether free entry was available for children. It was, but the rule had been printed too low to read easily.

回到校園，一位學生抱怨節慶的入場費。費用由大學收取，不屬於社團，蓮同意應清楚告訴訪客錢用在哪裡。美寫了一塊牌子，說明費用支付清潔與安全人員。一位同學想用無禮的笑話回擊抱怨者，蓮阻止他。「不能要求人先喜歡我們說話的語氣，才有資格提問，」她說。俊檢查牌子，詢問孩子能否免費入場。可以，但規則印得太低，並不容易看清。

On Friday they rehearsed beneath the trees. A pale afternoon light fell across the stage, and the wind made the paper banners move. Jun played the person who believed one perfect design could save the town. Lian played someone who asked him to see the people standing beside him. They spoke their lines, stopped, then tried them again. During the pause Jun said, “I think I used to be that proud.” Lian answered, “I think I used to enjoy proving you wrong.” It was the nearest they had come to admitting how much their old rivalry had cost them.

星期五，他們在樹下彩排。蒼白午後光線落在舞臺上，風吹動紙旗。俊扮演相信完美設計可以拯救城鎮的人，蓮則扮演提醒他看見身旁人們的角色。他們念了臺詞，停下，又試一次。休息時俊說：「我想我以前真的那麼驕傲。」蓮回答：「我想我以前也太享受證明你錯了。」這是兩人最接近承認過去競爭造成多少損失的一次。

That evening the photographer's daughter sent written permission for three pictures. She included a line about the wedding: “Please show that people can care for a place without being famous.” Lian read it aloud. Jun looked from the old photograph to the stage they had built together. “We should use her words at the end,” he said. Lian wrote them on a card and placed the card next to the route map. The exhibition's purpose was becoming clearer. Its goal was not to make visitors admire two charming students. It was to make them notice the many ordinary people who held a town together.

傍晚，攝影師的女兒寄來三張照片的書面使用同意，還附上一句關於婚禮的話：「請讓人看見，不出名也可以關心一個地方。」蓮念出來，俊從老照片看向兩人一起搭起的舞臺。「把她的話放在結尾吧，」他說。蓮寫在卡片上，放到路線圖旁。展覽目的愈來愈清楚：不是讓訪客欣賞兩名迷人的學生，而是讓人看見那些共同撐起城鎮的普通人。

The photographer's daughter also remembered a path from the garden toward a small forest beyond the town. Lian did not add it to the visitor route because it had not been checked. She saved the memory on a separate card for anyone researching the area's history later.

攝影師的女兒也記得有條路從花園通往鎮外的小森林。蓮沒有把它加入訪客路線，因為尚未安全測試。她另存一張卡片，留給日後研究區域歷史的人。

Before leaving, Jun asked whether Lian wanted to share a late meal. “Not as a scene,” he added. “Just because we both forgot dinner.” She thought for a moment and agreed. They chose a small place near the station and spoke about books, family meals, and the music they hated as children. Neither used the word date. When they parted, Jun said he had enjoyed the evening. “I did too,” Lian answered. She walked home with an unfamiliar warmth. It had grown without a script and without an audience, which made it harder to name and easier to trust.

離開前，俊問蓮要不要一起吃晚餐。「不是演出，」他補充。「只是我們都忘了吃飯。」她想了一會兒便答應。兩人選了車站附近的小店，談書、家常菜，以及童年討厭的音樂。沒有人說出「約會」兩字。分開時俊說他很享受今晚。「我也是，」蓮回答。她帶著陌生的溫暖走回家。那份感覺沒有劇本、沒有觀眾，因而更難命名，也更容易相信。
