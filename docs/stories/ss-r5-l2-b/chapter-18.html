A Candle in the Hall

不必編造的約會 · 第 18 / 25 章 · 1000–2000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

The first evening exhibition opened under a cool sky. Before visitors arrived, an artist from the town placed a small candle inside a covered lantern. She explained that the light stood for all the folk stories people had carried through difficult years. Jun checked that the flame was safe and far from paper. Mei placed a sheet with emergency rules beside the entrance. Lian welcomed the first guests and received their written comments. The arrival of real visitors made her hands shake. She wanted the event to be independent of the contest, but the judging committee would visit later. For now, the public came first.

第一場晚間展覽在微涼天空下開幕。訪客來前，一位鎮上藝術家把小蠟燭放入有罩燈籠，解釋火光象徵人們走過艱難歲月而保存的民間故事。俊確認火焰安全、遠離紙張。美在入口放上緊急規則單。蓮迎接第一批客人，收下書面意見。真實訪客到來讓她雙手發抖。她希望活動不受競賽控制，但評審之後仍會到場。至少今晚，大眾優先。

At the second stop, an older man made a claim that the room had once been used as a secret bar. He remembered people laughing late at night. Another visitor said the sound probably came from a school party, not a place that sold drinks. Lian did not decide which memory was at fault. She asked both people for dates and wrote their statements separately. “A recent memory may be clearer,” Jun said, “but it can still be wrong.” Lian agreed. The exhibit would not burn one person's story to make another look stronger. It could show that people remembered the same place in different ways.

第二站，一位老人聲稱閱覽室以前曾是秘密酒吧，記得有人深夜大笑。另一位訪客說聲音可能來自學校聚會，不是賣酒的場所。蓮沒有判定誰的記憶有錯，只問兩人時間，分別記錄說法。「較近的記憶可能比較清楚，」俊說，「但仍可能出錯。」蓮同意。展覽不會為了讓一個說法更強，就燒掉另一人的故事。它可以呈現人們如何不同地記得同一地方。

A child pointed to a paper whale hanging above the waterway model. His mother explained that the school had once held a sea story day, though the town was far from the ocean. Jun showed him how to move the whale along a string. The boy laughed and asked whether the whale could swim backward. “In our story, perhaps,” Lian replied. “In real life, we should ask someone who studies whales.” The mother appreciated that they treated play as play and facts as facts. The boy took a photograph of the model and promised to draw his own when he got home.

一個孩子指著水道模型上方的紙鯨魚。母親說學校以前辦過海洋故事日，雖然城鎮離海很遠。俊示範如何讓鯨魚沿線移動。男孩笑著問牠能不能倒著游。「在我們的故事裡，也許能，」蓮回答。「真實情況要去問研究鯨魚的人。」母親欣賞他們分清遊戲與事實。男孩替模型拍照，說回家也要自己畫一個。

Near the food table, a cockroach appeared under a chair. A volunteer nearly dropped a tray of snacks, but Mei guided people to another table while the staff dealt with the problem. The event continued without panic. Lian wrote an incident report because a public display needed mature care, even when the trouble seemed small. Jun checked the cleaning schedule and asked the school to place covered bins nearby. They did not hide the event from the committee. Their safety promise mattered most when something went wrong, not when every photograph looked clean.

食物桌旁，一隻蟑螂從椅下爬出。一位志工差點打翻點心盤，美引導大家換桌，讓工作人員處理問題。活動沒有恐慌地繼續。蓮寫了事件紀錄，因為公共展覽即使出現小問題，也要成熟處理。俊檢查清潔表，請校方在附近放有蓋垃圾桶。他們沒有向委員會隱瞞。安全承諾最需要在出事時實踐，而不是照片漂亮時才說。

When the judging committee arrived, Lian invited its members to walk the same route as everyone else. She did not move them to the front or reserve special seats. Jun explained the design changes and then stepped aside so a volunteer could speak about her own work. The judges seemed pleased by the shared voices. They asked for proof that the materials had been approved for public use. Lian opened the folder of permissions. It was not a dramatic scene, yet she felt proud. Their project had become something that could stand on clear records rather than the charm of its two leaders.

評審委員會到場時，蓮請委員與一般訪客走同一條路，不插隊也不保留特殊席位。俊解釋設計變更後退到旁邊，讓志工介紹自己的工作。評審似乎欣賞這些不同的聲音。他們要求確認材料有公開使用許可。蓮打開授權資料夾。這不是戲劇性場面，她卻很驕傲。計畫已能靠清楚紀錄站得住腳，而不只是依賴兩位領導者的魅力。

After the event, Lian and Jun sat on a bench for a long time. The hall lights grew dim as volunteers packed the displays. Jun admitted that months ago he would have cared only about the judges' comments. Lian said she would probably have remembered only the people who argued against her. Now both were thinking about the visitors' questions and smiles. The change had not come suddenly. It had grown through arguments, apologies, and many new attempts. She wanted to move closer to him but chose to wait until the exhibition was truly over.

活動結束後，蓮與俊在椅子上坐了很久。大廳燈光漸暗，他們看著志工把每塊展板小心收好。俊承認，幾個月前他大概會只在乎評審說了什麼。蓮則說，她以前大概會只記得誰反駁了自己。現在兩人想到的卻是參觀者留下的問題與笑容。那份成長不是突然發生，而是許多次爭論、道歉與重新嘗試累積而成。她很想靠近他，仍選擇等到展覽結束。

Near the exit, Jun found that a switch beside the old display lights had been left on. He turned it off with the staff member present and added the check to their closing list. The small action reminded Lian that a safe show depends on what happens after the guests have gone.

出口旁，俊發現舊展示燈旁的開關還開著。他在工作人員陪同下關掉，並把檢查列入打烊清單。這個小動作提醒蓮，安全活動也取決於客人走後做了什麼。

That night Mei counted the comments: most visitors had found the route clear, and many asked when the reading room might reopen. Lian prepared a public summary with both praise and problems. Jun added a note thanking the cleaners and staff. They sent the file before sleeping. The next morning a newspaper would print a short report about the event. Neither knew how strangers would read it. Still, they had done the work they promised. Lian turned off her lamp knowing that trust could survive an imperfect night when people told the truth about it.

那晚美統計意見：多數訪客覺得路線清楚，很多人問閱覽室何時能重新開放。蓮準備公開摘要，同時寫進讚美與問題。俊附上感謝清潔與工作人員的註記。他們睡前送出文件。隔天報紙將刊登活動短報導，沒人知道陌生人會如何解讀。至少他們做了承諾的事。蓮關燈時知道，即使一晚並不完美，只要誠實面對，信任仍能留下。
