安全大門的代價

The Price of a Safe Door

不必編造的約會 · 第 13 / 25 章 · 1000–2000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

The repair engineer met the clubs in a room above the market. He laid out a metal chart showing the amount of wood and support needed for the old roof. The price was higher than they had expected. Jun asked whether a lighter repair could match the safety rules. The engineer shook his head. “I will not work against the evidence to save a few coins,” he said. Lian respected that answer. The building was an old treasure to the town, but its beauty did not make it safe. They needed leadership that could tell people what was possible rather than what sounded pleasant.

維修工程師在市場樓上的房間與社團見面。他攤開金屬結構圖，列出舊屋頂需要的木材和支撐數量。價格比他們預想高。俊問較輕便的修復能否符合安全規則，工程師搖頭。「我不會為了省幾個錢就違背證據，」他說。蓮尊重這個回答。這棟建築是鎮上的老寶物，但美麗不代表安全。他們需要敢於說出可行範圍、而不只講好聽話的領導。

At the festival costume room, Mei showed them clothing for their stage characters. One coat belonged to a fictional prince, and a purple dress belonged to a princess. The pieces came from a student play, not from a fashion brand. Lian rejected the idea of wearing a crown to suggest that the main characters were special. “Our story is about ordinary people,” she said. Jun tried on a simple jacket and looked more comfortable. A spider had made a web in the corner; he carefully moved the costume box instead of harming it. Small choices revealed character more clearly than a golden crown.

節慶服裝室裡，美展示舞臺角色的衣服。一件外套屬於虛構王子，一件紫色裙子屬於公主。衣物來自學生戲劇，不是時尚品牌。蓮拒絕戴王冠暗示主角特別高貴。「我們講的是普通人，」她說。俊試穿簡單外套，看起來更自在。角落有隻蜘蛛結網，他小心移動服裝箱，沒有傷害牠。小小的選擇，比金冠更能呈現性格。

The clubs set up a table with sample objects from the old reading room. A grandfather showed them how to weigh one gram of tea with a tiny balance. A mother explained why children used to bathe before entering the shared reading space during summer events. A former worker remembered falling asleep beside the window after a late shift. None of these memories could be treated as a strict rule for everyone. They belonged to particular people and times. Lian wrote each name, and Jun checked that their owners agreed to have the objects shown. The exhibition was becoming a collection of living voices.

社團擺起一張桌，展示舊閱覽室使用過的物件。爺爺教他們用小秤量一公克茶葉；一位母親說孩子以前夏季活動進公共閱覽區前要先洗澡；一名老工人記得下夜班後曾在窗邊睡著。這些記憶都不能當作所有人的嚴格規則，它們屬於特定的人和年代。蓮記下姓名，俊則確認物主同意展示物品。展覽愈來愈像活生生聲音的集合。

An engineering student brought a model aircraft to show how a roof might resist strong wind. He explained the angle carefully and kept the small machine on the table instead of flying it near visitors. Another volunteer asked whether the roof would withstand an earthquake. The engineer said the teams needed a separate inspection for that question. Lian liked the honesty. “One match between a model and a real wall does not prove everything,” she said. Jun added a note to the plan: no public claim about safety without the right test. Their grant proposal could not depend on guesses.

一位工程系學生帶來模型飛機，示範屋頂可能如何抵抗強風。他仔細解釋角度，讓小機器待在桌上，沒有在訪客附近放飛。另一位志工問屋頂能否承受地震。工程師說那需要另行檢查。蓮欣賞這種誠實。「模型和真牆有一項吻合，不代表證明了所有事，」她說。俊在計畫上加註：沒有適當測試，就不能公開宣稱安全。補助提案不能靠猜測。

When the meeting ended, Jun found a note from his grandmother. She remembered the reading room as a place where neighbors shared skills, not as a monument to one family. She wanted the building repaired, but she was clear that students should not be asked to pay for it alone. Lian read the note and said the town needed to lead the repair process. Their contest could offer a design and a public plan, not replace government duties. Jun agreed. For once, they did not disagree about the next step. They were both ready to ask for a fair public discussion.

會議結束，俊收到祖母的訊息。她記得閱覽室是鄰居分享技藝的地方，不是紀念某一家的建築。她希望修好房子，但也清楚表示不能只要求學生付錢。蓮讀完，認為鎮公所該主導修復程序。他們的競賽可以提出設計和公開方案，不能替政府承擔責任。俊同意。這次他們對下一步毫無分歧，都準備要求一場公平的公共討論。

That evening Lian met Jun beside the garden fence. The rain had stopped and the leaves shone under the streetlight. “Do you ever wish the contest were only about winning?” she asked. Jun thought a long time. “Yes, when I am tired,” he said. “But I would hate winning for something that failed the people who trusted us.” She looked at him with a new respect. He was trying to balance pride with duty. So was she. Their stage characters could speak grandly about love; the real two of them were learning to keep difficult promises about work.

那晚，蓮在花園圍欄旁遇見俊。雨停了，葉子在街燈下閃亮。「你有時會希望比賽只關乎輸贏嗎？」她問。俊想了很久。「累的時候會，」他說。「但若贏的是一件辜負信任者的作品，我會更難受。」蓮以新的尊重看著他。他正試著平衡自尊與責任，她也是。舞臺角色可以大聲談愛，真實的兩人卻在學習履行困難的工作承諾。

An older gentleman in the audience asked how many degrees the roof might shift under a strong wind. The engineer explained that the exact degree of movement required proper testing. Jun appreciated the natural caution in his answer. No one should turn an estimate into a promise about public safety.

聽眾席一位年長紳士問強風時屋頂可能偏移多少度。工程師說精確角度必須透過適當測試才能知道。俊欣賞他自然流露的謹慎態度。沒有人應把估算變成公共安全承諾。

Before parting, Jun asked whether Lian would help him prepare the next public meeting. She agreed, then asked one condition of her own: they would explain the budget in ordinary words instead of hiding behind charts. “If you can explain it to a friend, you understand it,” she said. “And if I cannot?” Jun asked. “Then we learn together.” He smiled at that. Lian walked home knowing the cost of the roof was still large and the road ahead uncertain. Yet she no longer felt she had to carry the whole problem alone.

分開前，俊問蓮願不願意一起準備下一場公開會議。她答應，但提出條件：要用普通人能懂的話解釋預算，不要躲在圖表後面。「能向朋友講清楚，才算真正理解，」她說。「如果我講不清呢？」俊問。「那我們一起學。」他笑了。蓮走回家時知道屋頂費用仍很高，前路仍不確定，卻不再覺得自己必須獨自承擔所有問題。
