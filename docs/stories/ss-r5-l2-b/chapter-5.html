工坊裡的雷聲

Thunder in the Workshop

不必編造的約會 · 第 5 / 25 章 · 1000–2000 · 英中對照

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

The workshop smelled of warm wood and hot metal when Lian arrived on Tuesday. Jun was testing a small electrical sign, and his assistant was watching the wires. A sudden noise from outside made everyone jump. Thunder rolled across the campus; seconds later, the lights went out. Jun unplugged the machine at once. Nobody was hurt, but the unfinished signs lay dark on the tables. Lian had an appetite for solving problems, yet she knew this one needed care. “Tell me what is safe to touch,” she said. Jun thanked her for asking instead of reaching for the wires.

星期二蓮到工坊時，空氣裡有溫木頭和熱金屬的味道。俊正測試一塊電動標示，助手盯著線路。外頭突然傳來巨響，大家都嚇了一跳。雷聲滾過校園，幾秒後燈就熄了。俊立刻拔掉機器插頭。沒有人受傷，但未完成的標示全都暗在桌上。蓮很想立刻解決問題，卻知道這件事必須小心。「告訴我哪些東西能安全碰，」她說。俊感謝她先問，而不是伸手去抓電線。

Their first method was simple: check the power, mark the damaged parts, and wait for the building staff. Jun paid careful attention to every connection. One new member called the whole design a brilliant failure; she meant it as a joke, but Lian saw how tired Jun looked. “We can praise the parts that work and fix the parts that do not,” she said. He drew a small diagram to show the team where the problem began. The trouble was not a conflict between their clubs. A storm had reached an old building with old wires, and they needed support from people trained to repair it.

他們採取簡單步驟：確認電源、標出損壞部位，等候校舍人員。俊仔細查看每個接點。一位新社員開玩笑說整個設計是「精彩的失敗」，蓮卻看出俊很累。「有效的部分可以稱讚，壞掉的部分再修，」她說。他畫了一張小圖，向大家指出問題起點。這不是兩個社團的衝突，而是暴風雨遇上老舊建築線路；他們需要有資格的人協助修理。

When the power returned, Mei arrived with hot noodle soup. She had brought too much, so everyone shared. Someone joked that the soup could wake a sleepy rooster. A classmate from the north wore a wool coat even though the workshop was warm; her family lived in a snowy town, and she always expected cold weather. As the rain began to spread across the windows, Lian listened to stories about the volunteers' homes. The town exhibition was becoming more than dates on a wall. Every person carried a place in memory, and those places shaped the way they noticed danger or felt welcome.

電力恢復後，美帶來熱麵湯。她帶得太多，於是大家一起分享。有人開玩笑說這湯能叫醒睡著的公雞。一位北方來的同學即使工坊很暖仍穿著羊毛外套；她家住在多雪的城鎮，總是預期會冷。雨滴沿窗面散開，蓮聽著志工談自己的家鄉。城鎮展覽不再只是牆上的年代。每個人記憶裡都有地方，並影響他們如何看見危險、如何感到被歡迎。

A parent visiting the campus showed them an old newspaper about a domestic group that had once run the reading room. Its members collected books for children and offered free lessons. Some photographs had damaged skin on their edges, but the faces could still be seen. Lian noticed a mass of handwritten names on the back of one photograph. Jun discovered that the woman standing beside the door was his grandmother's friend. It was their first personal connection to the room. “If we use her story,” Lian said, “we must ask the family first.” Jun agreed, even though he wanted an answer immediately.

一位來校參觀的家長拿出舊報紙，介紹曾管理閱覽室的本地民間團體。成員為孩子收書，也提供免費課程。有些照片邊緣的相紙表面已受損，臉卻仍看得清。蓮注意到照片背後密密麻麻的手寫姓名。俊發現站在門邊的女人是祖母的朋友。這是閱覽室第一次與他們的個人記憶產生連結。「如果要用她的故事，」蓮說，「我們得先問家人。」俊即使急著知道答案，也同意了。

Late that afternoon the storm passed. Outside, water fell from the roof in a steady line. The group could not finish the signs, but they could still complete their notes. Lian offered to write the next public update. She would say exactly which work had stopped, why, and what help they needed. “Whenever something breaks, people ask whose fault it is,” Jun said. “Sometimes that is right. But first we should keep everyone safe.” Lian understood that he was not trying to escape responsibility. He was choosing a useful order for action. She added his words to the report.

傍晚暴風雨過去，屋外雨水仍沿著屋簷穩定落下。小隊無法完成標示，卻仍能整理紀錄。蓮主動撰寫下一次公開進度：哪些工作停了、原因是什麼、需要什麼幫助，都要寫清楚。「每當東西壞掉，大家就問是誰的錯，」俊說。「有時這很合理，但第一步應先確保安全。」蓮知道他不是逃避責任，而是在選擇有效的行動次序。她把他的話也寫進報告。

That evening Jun took Lian to the covered walkway near the library. They did not hold hands. They watched water drip from the roof and talked about whether the exhibition should use live electric lights at all. “Paper signs would survive a power failure,” Lian said. He smiled. “And they would fit your stories better.” She looked at him in surprise. It was the first time he had offered to make his design smaller so her writing could be clearer. A growing trust was taking the place of their old need to defeat each other.

晚上俊帶蓮走到圖書館旁有遮雨棚的步道。兩人沒有牽手，只看著屋頂滴水，討論展覽究竟需不需要通電燈箱。「紙標示就算停電也能用，」蓮說。他笑了：「而且比較適合妳的故事。」她驚訝地看著他。這是俊第一次主動縮小自己的設計，好讓她的文字更清楚。逐漸增加的信任，開始取代過去非得擊敗彼此的心情。

On the last page of the report, Mei pasted a photograph of the locked reading room. The storm had washed dirt from the doorway. Under the door was a painted number that matched the missing page in the garden file. Lian stared at it. If the old building and the garden had been managed together, their history belonged in one story. She sent a message to the town archive asking for permission to see the complete record. Jun replied with one word: “Together.” The word was not a romantic promise. For that night, it was enough.

報告最後一頁，美貼了一張上鎖閱覽室的照片。暴雨沖掉門口的泥，露出門下方畫著的編號，竟與花園檔案缺失頁面的編號相同。蓮盯著它。如果閱覽室與花園原本共同管理，兩者的歷史就該一起講。她寫信向鎮公所檔案室申請查看完整紀錄。俊只回了一個字：「一起。」那不是愛情承諾，但在那個夜晚，已經足夠。
