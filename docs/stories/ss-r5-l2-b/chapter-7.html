牆後的房間

The Room Behind the Wall

不必編造的約會 · 第 7 / 25 章 · 1000–2000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

A librarian named Mrs. Chen gave the clubs advice about how to address the town archive. They needed to ask for an actual record, not just tell a sad story. “Use the official form,” she said, setting it beside a cup of tea and a piece of toast. Lian read the instructions twice. Jun turned the page over and found a note about the building's origin: it had once been part of a shared school and library. That detail changed their whole plan. If the reading room had served the public, they could ask why access had ended instead of simply asking to borrow an old room.

陳姓圖書館員給社團一些建議，教他們如何正式向鎮公所檔案室提出申請。他們需要索取真實紀錄，而不是只講悲傷故事。「用官方表格，」她說，把表格放在茶杯和一片吐司旁。蓮把說明看了兩次。俊翻到背面，發現一條關於建築起源的註記：它曾經是學校與圖書室共用的一部分。這個細節改變了整個計畫。如果閱覽室原本服務大眾，他們就能問為何停止開放，而不只是請求借用舊屋。

At the photography club, an expert lent them a camera lens that could take clear pictures of small text. Lian learned how to hold the old pages flat without pressing them too hard. Jun used a soft brush to sort dust from the frames. One photograph showed a child wearing a bright necklace and standing beside a china bowl. Another showed a boy making a naughty face behind the librarian's chair. The pictures were pleasant, but the pair knew that smiling faces alone could not prove who owned the room. A memory was a starting point, not a final answer.

攝影社的一位專家借給他們能清楚拍攝小字的鏡頭。蓮學著在不過度施壓的情況下攤平舊紙，俊用軟刷整理相框上的灰塵。一張照片裡，孩子戴著鮮豔項鍊站在瓷碗旁；另一張則是一個男孩躲在圖書館員椅後做鬼臉。照片令人愉快，但兩人明白，笑臉不能證明房屋所有權。回憶是起點，不是最後答案。

That afternoon Mei brought a listener from the local history group. He remembered the room closing after a heavy rain, yet he also remembered the door being open for several weeks afterward. His memory did not beat the written date in the repair report; instead, it raised a question. Why had the building been used after the recorded closure? Lian asked him to speak slowly and recorded only what he agreed to share. When he said he felt like a prisoner of the past, she did not repeat that line on the public poster. She would ask his permission before using anything so personal.

下午，美帶來地方文史團體的一位聽眾。他記得閱覽室在大雨後關閉，卻也記得之後幾週門曾開過。他的記憶不能推翻維修報告上的日期，卻提出了新問題：為何正式關閉後仍有人使用？蓮請他慢慢說，只記錄他同意分享的內容。當他說自己像被過去困住的囚犯，蓮沒有把那句話直接放上海報。如此私人的感受，必須另外得到同意才可以使用。

By midnight, the four main volunteers had finished comparing the copies. None of the pages showed a legal order to close the room forever. One page assigned the garden a new role as a temporary meeting place. Another listed a repair fund that had never been fully spent. Jun spread the papers over the table. “If we tell people this proves someone stole money, we will be wrong,” he said. Lian agreed. “But it does prove the record is incomplete.” She had always feared that speaking carefully would make her story weak. Instead, the exact words made it stronger.

到了午夜，四位核心志工終於比對完影本。沒有任何頁面記載永久關閉閱覽室的法律命令。一頁把花園指定為臨時集會場所，另一頁列著未完全支出的維修基金。俊把文件攤滿桌。「如果說這能證明有人偷錢，我們就錯了，」他說。蓮同意：「但它確實證明紀錄不完整。」她一直怕謹慎措辭會讓故事變弱，現在卻發現精確反而有力量。

On the walk home, Jun asked what Lian did when she could not sleep. She told him she played quiet music and watched the streetlights. “I build things in my head,” he replied. “Usually they fall down before I make them.” She liked hearing a less confident side of him. He was not the perfect winner she had imagined when they were rivals. “You make mistakes?” she teased. “A whole list,” he said. Neither wanted to turn the conversation into a confession. Yet both seemed unwilling to end it. They took the long way back to the dormitories without pretending it was a date.

回家的路上，俊問蓮睡不著時會做什麼。她說會聽安靜的音樂、看街燈。「我會在腦中造東西，」他回答。「通常還沒真的造，它們就先倒了。」蓮喜歡聽見他不那麼自信的一面。他並不是她在競爭時想像的完美勝利者。「你也會犯錯？」她打趣。「整整一張清單，」他說。兩人都不想把對話變成告白，卻都捨不得結束。他們繞遠路回宿舍，也不必假裝那是約會。

The next morning the archive office finally accepted the request. It would provide the remaining papers after checking for private information. Lian thanked Mrs. Chen and offered to help clean the reading corner at the present library. Jun joined her. They spent an hour moving books, making signs, and asking children what stories they liked. One child wanted dragons; another liked trains. Their answers reminded Lian that a public room was made for people, not only for records. She looked through the glass toward the locked building. The door was still shut, but at least the question of opening it was now on the table.

隔天早上，檔案室終於受理申請，表示在檢查個人資料後會提供其餘文件。蓮向陳女士道謝，並主動幫忙整理現有圖書館的閱讀角。俊也加入。他們花一小時搬書、做標示，問孩子喜歡什麼故事。有人喜歡龍，有人喜歡火車。這些回答提醒蓮，公共空間是為人存在，而不只是為文件存在。她隔著玻璃望向上鎖的舊建築。門仍關著，但如何打開它已經正式成為能討論的事。

That night Mei asked whether Lian had changed her mind about Jun. Lian considered the question. “I used to think his only goal was to win,” she said. “Now I know he wants to make places useful.” Mei smiled but did not press. “Then keep learning who he is,” she said. “You do not need to decide the ending before you know the middle.” Lian wrote those words in the margin of her script. A real relationship, she thought, should allow people to be more than the roles a story had given them.

當晚，美問蓮是否改變了對俊的看法。蓮想了想。「以前我以為他只想贏，」她說。「現在知道他想讓空間真正派上用場。」美微笑，沒有追問。「那就繼續了解他，」她說。「還不知道中間的故事，不必先決定結局。」蓮把這句話寫進劇本旁。她想，真實的關係應該讓人不只活成故事裡安排的角色。
