西街傳來的歌

A Song from the West Street

不必編造的約會 · 第 6 / 25 章 · 1000–2000 · 英中對照 · 校訂完成

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

The team took a bus toward the western side of town to meet people who remembered the reading room. Rain still covered the sidewalk in small pools, so Lian wore a yellow raincoat. A retired musician greeted them in front of an old building. He said the room had once held poetry evenings and children's music lessons. A large crowd used to gather on the eve of the spring festival, when people carried paper lanterns through the street. Lian asked whether he had photographs or an official record. He invited them in and opened a wooden box filled with programs. The paper smelled of dust and time.

小隊搭公車前往鎮西側，拜訪還記得閱覽室的人。人行道上仍有小水窪，蓮穿上黃色雨衣。一位退休音樂家在老建築前迎接他們，說那個空間過去辦過詩歌之夜和兒童音樂課。春節前夕常有大批人聚集，提著紙燈走過街道。蓮詢問是否有照片或正式紀錄。老人請他們進屋，打開裝滿節目單的木盒，紙張帶著灰塵和歲月的味道。

The musician's favorite program showed a young woman beside a hand-drawn giraffe. Children had made animal pictures for a visiting artist. Lian asked why the drawings had never appeared in the public files. “They were not considered important,” the man said. “But the children gave their soul to them.” Jun examined a torn corner and offered to repair only the copy, not the original. The musician watched his careful hands and smiled. “You two argue like old friends,” he said. Lian explained that their stage relationship was fictional. He laughed kindly. “Then your real work is still worth seeing.”

音樂家最喜歡的一張節目單上，年輕女子站在手繪長頸鹿旁。當年孩子為來訪藝術家畫了動物圖案。蓮問為何公文檔案沒有這些畫。「當時沒人認為重要，」老人說。「但孩子把心都放進去了。」俊查看破損角落，提出只修補複印件、不動原件。音樂家看著他謹慎的手笑了：「你們吵起來像老朋友。」蓮解釋臺上關係只是虛構角色。他和善地笑說：「那你們真正做的事仍值得一看。」

At the next building, a former librarian showed them a narrow repair form. The reading room had closed after a storm damaged its roof, but the repair money had been moved elsewhere. Nobody in the neighborhood could remember when that change became permanent. Jun examined the form without touching the original pages. The woman was sensitive about the room because she had helped open it years before. Lian did not rush her questions. “We want to understand the old rules, not begin another war,” she said. The librarian nodded and suggested that they ask the town office for the budget record.

下一棟建築裡，一位前圖書管理員拿出狹長的維修表。閱覽室曾因暴風雨損壞屋頂而關閉，但維修經費後來轉作別用。鄰里沒人記得這個變動何時變成永久安排。俊細看表單，沒有碰原頁。這位女士對閱覽室感情深，因為當年她也參與開辦。蓮沒有急著追問。「我們想理解舊規則，不想開啟另一場戰爭，」她說。女士點頭，建議他們向鎮公所查預算紀錄。

For lunch they sat in a small public garden behind the station. Lian found a stone shaped like a sleeping animal, and Jun made a silly guess about its history. She laughed harder than she expected. “You are allowed to be funny,” she told him. “I did not know you had made that rule,” he replied. Their new friendship still had an uncertain term. They knew how long the contest would last, but not what they would choose afterward. Lian did not want to turn a good afternoon into a question about romance. She let the moment remain simple.

午餐時，他們坐在車站後的小公共花園。蓮找到一塊形狀像睡著動物的石頭，俊胡亂猜它的來歷，逗得她笑得比想像中開心。「原來你也可以很好笑，」她說。「我不知道妳訂過這條規則，」他回答。他們的新友誼仍沒有明確期限：知道競賽何時結束，卻不知道之後會怎麼選。蓮不想把好好的午後變成愛情問答，就讓那一刻保持單純。

Back on campus, the town office replied. Its record desk could examine the request, but the archive might need a week to release the papers. In the meantime the clubs were free to build a public display from material whose owners had given permission. Mei suggested placing the old poetry programs beside recordings of present-day students. That would show how one room had served different people in different years. Jun offered a simple frame with large letters that could be read from a seated position. Lian wrote the first introduction and asked the musician to check every fact.

回到校園，鎮公所回覆了。檔案室可以受理申請，但可能要一週才能公開文件。這段期間社團仍可使用已取得權利人許可的材料製作展示。美提議把舊詩歌節目單放在現代學生的聲音旁，呈現同一間屋子在不同年代服務過的人。俊設計簡單框架，讓坐著的人也能讀到大字。蓮寫出第一篇引言，請音樂家逐項核對事實。

The retired musician later sent a recording of a song about walking home after rain. It did not belong to the clubs, so Lian asked for written permission before using it. Jun listened through headphones and said the final line reminded him of his grandmother. They walked across the quiet quad without pretending to be their stage characters. “Does it bother you that people talk about us?” he asked. “Sometimes,” she admitted. “But I care more about whether you listen when I say no.” Jun stopped walking and looked at her. “I will,” he said. For the first time, she believed him without needing a document.

退休音樂家稍後寄來一段雨後回家的歌聲錄音。作品不屬於社團，蓮先索取書面授權才考慮使用。俊戴耳機聽完，說末句讓他想起祖母。他們穿過安靜校園，沒有扮演劇中的角色。「大家談論我們，會讓妳困擾嗎？」他問。「有時會，」她承認。「但我更在乎說不的時候，你會不會聽。」俊停下腳步看她。「我會。」第一次，她不必看文件就相信了他。

Before they left, the musician asked if they would accept a copy of an old program as a gift. Lian agreed to receive the copy but insisted that the original should remain with him. Jun wrote a thank-you note naming the source so that the gift would never become an anonymous object.

離開前，音樂家問他們是否接受一份舊節目單影本作為禮物。蓮願意收影本，但堅持原件留在老人手中。俊寫下附有來源姓名的謝卡，避免贈品變成沒有出處的物件。

At home, Lian added the western street to the route map. It made the walk longer, but the music and the stories gave the place a purpose. The clubs were no longer seeking a perfect romantic path. They were learning to connect living neighbors with a forgotten building. She sent Jun one last note: “The next rehearsal needs fewer hearts and more benches.” He replied, “I can build benches. I cannot promise fewer hearts from Mei.” Lian smiled in the darkness. She had not planned to enjoy working with him. Plans, she was discovering, were useful only when people could change them.

回家後，蓮把西街加進路線圖。路程變長了，但音樂和故事讓這段路有了意義。社團不再尋找完美的浪漫步道，而是學著讓現在的鄰居與被遺忘的屋子重新連結。她傳給俊最後一則訊息：「下一次彩排需要少一點愛心，多一點長椅。」他回說：「長椅我能做。美畫的愛心我無法保證會變少。」蓮在暗處笑了。她從沒計畫過會喜歡與他合作，卻逐漸發現，計畫的價值在於人們仍能修改它。
