A Date We Did Not Invent

不必編造的約會 · 第 25 / 25 章 · 1000–2000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

The award ceremony took place in the same hall where the clubs had first argued about their plan. Lian stood beside Jun, looking at a chart of the final scores. Their main aim had been to make a safe, honest exhibition. The judges now announced that their team had won the community prize for its clear records, shared work, and careful treatment of visitors. The room filled with applause. For a moment Lian felt the old anger of competition rising, then watched it pass. Winning was pleasant, but she did not want to live at the border between pride and fear again.

頒獎典禮在兩社團最初為計畫爭論的同一座禮堂舉行。蓮站在俊旁，看著最後分數圖表。他們的主要目標一直是辦安全又誠實的展覽。評審宣布團隊以清楚紀錄、共同工作及細心對待訪客，獲得社區獎。掌聲充滿房間。蓮一瞬間感到過去競爭的怒氣又湧上來，隨後看著它退去。獲勝令人高興，但她不想再活在驕傲與恐懼的邊界上。

The school offered the clubs a small fund to keep the outdoor route available. The town agreed to begin a professional study of the old reading room, but made no promise about when its doors might open. Lian read the decision and was glad that each step had been named honestly. Jun thanked the student team, the local workers, and the families who had shared their stories. He did not call himself the master of the project. He was one maker among many. Lian looked at his face and thought he seemed more at ease than he had been on the first day.

學校提供社團一筆小額經費，維持戶外路線可用。鎮公所同意啟動閱覽室的專業評估，卻不承諾何時開門。蓮讀完決議，很高興每一步都誠實列明。俊感謝學生團隊、地方工人，以及願意分享故事的家庭。他不稱自己為計畫的主人，而只是眾多製作者之一。蓮看著他，覺得他比第一天自在許多。

After the ceremony, a little girl approached their memory table. She was Mei's niece and had brought a drawing with careful spelling. It showed the old reading room with a wide door, flowers at its side, and several people inside. “Will it look exactly like this?” she asked. Lian answered, “We do not know yet. But people are now asking how to make it safe.” The girl nodded, curious rather than disappointed. She placed the drawing beside the other hopes for the future. Lian thought the child's question was the right ending for their public story: a door not yet open, but no longer forgotten.

典禮後，一個小女孩走向記憶桌。她是美的姪女，帶來一張拼字寫得很仔細的畫。畫上是有寬門的閱覽室，旁邊有花，裡面坐著好幾個人。「以後真的會長這樣嗎？」她問。蓮回答：「還不知道，但大家現在已在討論怎麼讓它安全。」女孩點頭，顯得好奇而不是失望，把畫放在其他未來想像旁。蓮覺得這就是公共故事合適的結尾：門尚未開，卻不再被遺忘。

The two clubs gathered for a final photograph. This time nobody added a secret heart to make their appearance more interesting. The picture showed volunteers carrying maps, holding signs, and laughing at tired faces. Mei asked the group to post it with a list of every helper's name. Lian agreed, and Jun checked that everyone was comfortable being included. The photograph would seem ordinary to strangers. To the people inside it, each smile held months of work. Their shared project had not been perfect, but the effort had fit the needs of the town better than the first grand plan.

兩個社團集合拍最後的合照。這次沒有人偷偷畫愛心，讓畫面看起來更有趣。照片裡志工拿著地圖、舉著標示，笑著彼此的疲倦表情。美請大家發文時附上每位協助者姓名。蓮同意，俊確認每個入鏡的人都願意。對陌生人而言，照片或許普通；對裡面的人，每個笑容都含著幾個月的工作。共同計畫並不完美，卻比最初宏大的方案更符合城鎮需要。

That evening Lian and Jun returned to the riverside path where they had spoken after the debate. The sky was clear. Without a stage or an audience, Jun asked whether she would like to go on a real date next Saturday. “Just us,” he said. “And we can change our minds.” Lian smiled. “Yes. I would like that.” She added that she still wanted to finish her book and take an internship next year. He said he would never ask her to stop. She promised nothing about forever. They were choosing one honest afternoon, and that was enough to begin.

那晚蓮與俊回到辯論後一起散步的河邊小路。天空晴朗。沒有舞臺也沒有觀眾，俊問她下週六願不願意真正約會。「只有我們兩人，」他說。「也都能改變主意。」蓮微笑：「好，我願意。」她補充自己仍想完成一本書，明年也要去實習。他說不會要求她停下。她沒有承諾永遠，只選擇一個誠實的午後。這樣就足以開始。

As they walked, Lian remembered the paper agreement from the first day. It had said they could play a couple only when both agreed, and that either could stop without giving a reason. The rules had helped them protect one another when rumors grew loud. Now they no longer needed to obey a stage script, but they still wanted to honor those choices. Jun asked whether he could hold her hand. She answered yes and reached for him. The simple gesture felt warmer than any rehearsed line. No company, contest, or photograph had made it happen. They had chosen it themselves.

走著走著，蓮想起第一天寫的紙上協議：只有雙方同意才能演情侶，任何人都能不必解釋就停止。那些規則在謠言喧鬧時保護了他們。如今不必再遵守舞臺劇本，兩人仍想尊重彼此選擇。俊問能否牽她的手。她說可以，主動伸手。簡單的動作比任何彩排臺詞都溫暖。沒有公司、競賽或照片替他們促成，是他們自己選的。

At the edge of the hall a small group played classical music while volunteers put away chairs. The town official said justice in this project meant hearing many voices, not rewarding only the loudest one. The prize included a modest grant plus a promise of rapid review for the next safety study, not a promise to open an unsafe door.

禮堂邊，一小群人演奏古典音樂，志工則收拾椅子。鎮公所人員說，這個計畫所追求的公正，是聽見許多人的聲音，不只獎勵最大聲的一位。獎項包含一筆適度補助，加上加速審查下一次安全評估的安排，而不是承諾打開不安全的大門。

The next morning Lian returned to the old garden alone. Sunlight moved across the wall, and the outdoor signs still stood where the volunteers had placed them. She saw a student reading the history card and an older visitor resting on a bench. A worker was emptying the garbage bin. Life had continued without a grand speech. Lian took out her notebook and wrote one final line: “A place belongs to the people who care for it, and a story belongs to those who are free to choose its next page.” Then she went home to prepare for an ordinary Saturday that no longer needed pretending.

隔天早上蓮獨自回到老花園。陽光移過圍牆，戶外標示仍立在志工放置的位置。她看見學生讀歷史卡片，長者坐在長椅休息，工人正在清理垃圾桶。生活沒有盛大演說也繼續前進。蓮拿出筆記本寫下最後一句：「地方屬於願意照顧它的人；故事則屬於能自由選擇下一頁的人。」接著她回家，準備迎接一個再也不需要假裝的普通週六。
