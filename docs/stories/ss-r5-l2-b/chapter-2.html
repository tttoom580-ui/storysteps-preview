社員投票

The Membership Vote

不必編造的約會 · 第 2 / 25 章 · 1000–2000 · 英中對照

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

The first trouble arrived before the first rehearsal was finished. The Paper Lantern Club wanted to cancel its old project and move its money to the walking exhibition. Several members called the change selfish. Jun suggested a trial vote instead of a quick order. “A club is not a chess board,” he said. “People are not pieces that we move.” Lian wrote every concern on a large display. Her best friend, Mei, worried about the loss of the club's independent name. Others wanted the beauty of the garden to remain part of the plan. They were asking fair questions, not trying to stop her.

第一次彩排還沒結束，麻煩就來了。紙燈社想取消原本的計畫，把經費改用於步行展覽。幾名社員認為這樣很自私。俊建議先試行投票，而不是直接下命令。「社團不是棋盤，」他說。「社員不是任人移動的棋子。」蓮把每項疑慮寫在大型展示板上。好友美擔心社團會失去獨立名稱，其他人則希望花園之美仍是計畫的一部分。他們提出的是合理問題，不是故意阻撓她。

A guide from the student office explained the grant rules. Membership in either club did not force anyone to join the new team. They could keep their old activities and share only the work they chose. The current plan needed a stronger message about why visitors should care. “We are not selling romance,” Lian told the room. “We are asking whether our town remembers the people who built its shared places.” Jun showed photographs of the garden's growth over twenty years. Even the oldest tree had changed. That made the question easier to see, and a few members began to respond.

學生事務處的指導員說明補助規則。加入任一社團不代表必須參與新團隊，大家可以保留舊活動，只分享自己願意做的工作。當前計畫需要更明確地說明參觀者為何應該在意。「我們不是在賣浪漫，」蓮對大家說。「我們想問的是，這座城還記不記得建造公共空間的人。」俊展示花園二十年來成長的照片。連最老的樹都已改變。問題變得具體，一些社員開始回應。

During the break, Mei brought slices of pear and a small speaker. She proposed recording short voices from former visitors instead of writing every story in a single voice. A first-year student offered to hunt through old newspapers. Another wanted to build signs that children could read by themselves. Lian had been trying to carry every part of the project on her own. Watching people offer ideas made her question that habit. Jun whispered, “The worst thing would be a contest winner that nobody enjoys making.” He had lost a design prize once because he refused to listen to his team. He did not hide that mistake.

休息時，美帶來切好的梨和小喇叭。她提議錄下老訪客的短短聲音，而不是讓所有故事都由一個人敘述。一年級學生願意找舊報紙，另一人希望製作孩子也能自行閱讀的標示。蓮一直想獨自扛起所有工作。看著大家提出點子，她開始懷疑這種習慣。俊低聲說：「最糟的是贏了獎，卻沒有人喜歡製作過程。」他以前因為不聽隊友意見而失去設計獎，並不隱瞞這個錯誤。

By evening, the new proposal had three parts: a route, a story, and a place for visitors to leave their own memories. There would be no chain of commands from one leader to every volunteer. Each small group would choose a task and report its progress. The plan was not super exciting on paper, but it made work possible. A member asked if the two leaders would act as lovers again. “On stage, yes,” Lian answered. “In real life, do not decide for us.” Jun looked surprised by the firmness of her voice, then moved his chair next to hers to answer the budget questions.

傍晚，新提案分成三部分：路線、故事，以及讓參觀者留下記憶的地方。工作不再由一位社長一層一層下令，而是各小組自選任務、回報進度。這份計畫寫起來不算超級刺激，卻變得可執行。一名社員問兩位領導者是否還要演情侶。「在舞臺上，會，」蓮回答。「現實生活請不要替我們決定。」俊對她堅定的語氣有些驚訝，接著把椅子移到她旁邊，共同回答預算問題。

After the vote, Lian stayed to clean the room. Jun put two cups of tea beside her. “You could have made that easier by telling people what they wanted to hear,” he said. “I know,” she replied. “But then I would lose respect for my own words.” He told her the small park behind his childhood home had been replaced by a parking area. No one had asked the children whether they minded. Lian saw that his work was not only about machines or winning. He was trying to give ordinary people a place in decisions that affected their lives. She felt the first sign of respect for him.

投票結束後，蓮留下來整理教室。俊放了兩杯茶在她旁邊。「妳只要說些大家愛聽的話，就能更容易過關，」他說。「我知道，」她回答。「但那樣我會不尊重自己的話。」他說童年住家後面的小公園曾被改建成停車場，沒有人問過孩子介不介意。蓮明白他的工作不只是機器與得獎。他想讓普通人在影響自己的決定中有發言的位置。她第一次真正尊重他。

The final count surprised everyone. Most members approved the trial, but only for three weeks. They wanted to see a real walking route before giving full support. Lian accepted the limit without complaint and posted the results. Mei told her that a short test could be more honest than a promise to change the world. Jun offered to guide the first group of visitors himself. He did not ask Lian to thank him. As the room emptied, she studied their two names on the plan. They were not a couple, but the work had started to join them.

最後票數讓大家意外。多數社員同意試行，卻只給三週時間。他們想先看到一條真正能走的路，才願意全面支持。蓮接受限制，並公開結果。美提醒她，短期測試可能比「改變世界」的大話更誠實。俊主動說要帶第一團參觀者。他沒有要求蓮道謝。教室漸漸空了，她看著計畫上並排的兩個名字。他們不是情侶，但工作已開始把兩人連在一起。

When Jun collected the ballots, he asked one student to check the numbers. He did not want to call himself the owner of every good idea. Lian noticed his effort and realized that sharing responsibility could protect a person's sense of self as well as the future of a club.

俊收票時請一名學生核對票數。他不願把自己當作所有好點子的主人。蓮看見他的努力，明白分擔責任既能保護社團未來，也能讓每個人保持自我。

On the way back to her dormitory, Lian got a message from an unknown account. It claimed the Makers Club had already arranged the whole contest. The note included no name, date, or proof. Her first thought was to ask Jun in anger. Her second was to remember how fairly he had answered hard questions that day. She saved the message instead. Before she trusted a claim, she would test it. That choice was not romantic and would never appear on a festival poster. It was still the kind of quiet change from which a good partnership could grow.

回宿舍的路上，蓮收到陌生帳號的訊息，聲稱創客社早已安排好整場競賽。訊息沒有姓名、日期，也沒有證據。她第一個念頭是憤怒地質問俊，第二個念頭卻想起他當天如何公平回答難題。她先保存訊息。信任指控之前，必須先查證。這個選擇不浪漫，也不會登上節慶海報，卻是一段好合作關係可能開始的安靜改變。
