北門的草圖

A Drawing of the North Door

把地板留給每一種舞步 · 第 3 / 25 章 · 2000–3000 · 英中對照

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

Lian spent Monday interviewing former dancers about their background, not merely collecting pictures for a publicity wall. Mrs. Wu brought a bamboo basket filled with a bundle of class cards. Each carried a name, a year, and sometimes a note about the person who had first invited the student to dance. Ron sat down for a chat while Cora examined the cards. A corner of one card had a small chip missing; its owner had kept it after the studio closed for repairs twenty years earlier. Lian realized that the old hall held many kinds of memory. Yet she refused to treat memory as a reason to ignore the present. She asked permission before copying any private name.

星期一，黎安訪問以前的學員，了解他們的背景，而不只是蒐集照片做宣傳牆。吳女士帶來一個竹籃，裡面是一捆課程卡。每張卡都有姓名、年份，有時還寫著當初邀請那個人跳舞的是誰。羅恩坐下聊天，可拉則仔細看卡片。有一張卡缺了一小角；主人在二十年前舞廳因維修關閉時留下它。黎安發現舊舞廳收藏著許多不同的記憶，但她不願讓回憶成為忽略現況的理由。複製任何私人姓名之前，她都先徵求許可。

At noon they visited a timber dealer who also sold used stage boards. He agreed to explain his prices, but asked them not to publish an estimate before he had inspected the building. Their discussion remained civil even when Ron questioned the quality of a sample. Lian wanted the planning process to deepen trust rather than turn every disagreement into a fight. “A democratic meeting means people can disagree and still have a chair at the table,” she said. They stopped to dine at a small cafe downtown. The owner offered a doughnut to each volunteer and asked whether the lessons would finish early enough for restaurant workers to attend after their shifts.

中午，他們拜訪一位兼售二手舞台板材的木材商。對方願意解釋價格，但要求在親自檢查建物前，不要公開估價。羅恩質疑樣品品質時，談話仍保持禮貌。黎安希望規劃過程能加深信任，而不是把每次意見不同都變成爭吵。「民主的會議意味著大家可以不同意，卻仍有位子坐在桌邊。」她說。他們到市中心的小咖啡館用餐。老闆送每位志工一個甜甜圈，也詢問課程是否能早些結束，好讓餐廳員工在下班後參加。

The north entrance stood beyond an old garden where a pigeon nested under the roof. Cora drew a mighty arrow across the site map to show the route she preferred, then laughed at her own dramatic mark. Ron said the step at the doorway was small to him but a serious barrier to wheels. Crossing it would require a short ramp and enough space to turn. Lian had assumed one leap in a dance could solve almost anything; watching Cora measure the doorway taught her how little that idea helped here. They surveyed the ground with a borrowed level. The numbers showed that the ramp could fit, but the garden fence would have to move.

北側入口位於舊花園後方，屋簷下有鴿子築巢。可拉在基地圖上畫了一個大大的箭頭，表示自己偏好的路線，隨後又笑自己畫得太誇張。羅恩說，門口那一小階對他不算什麼，對輪子卻是嚴重障礙。要跨過去，必須有短坡道與足夠的轉彎空間。黎安曾以為舞蹈裡的一個跳躍能解決幾乎所有難題；看著可拉量門框，她才知道那個想法在這裡毫無用處。他們借來水平儀測地面。數字證明坡道放得下，但花園圍欄必須移動。

At the afternoon poll, residents spoke about poverty as well as art. A cleaner explained that she could not afford to miss work for rehearsals; another worker offered to lead short sessions before breakfast. Lian watched how people react when a planner promises help without asking what help means. Some smiled politely, while others stopped speaking. For the sake of a stronger project, she divided the suggestions into needs they could meet now and ideas that required outside support. Ron wrote the word substance beside each proposal with evidence: a price, a route, a schedule, or a named person willing to take responsibility. Beautiful words alone would not be enough.

下午的民調中，居民談藝術，也談貧困。一位清潔工說，她不能為排練而缺班；另一位工人願意在早餐前帶短課程。黎安觀察人們聽到規劃者未先詢問、便承諾幫助時的反應。有些人禮貌微笑，有些人卻不再說話。為了計畫真正有用，她把建議分成現在能做到的，以及需要外部支持的。羅恩在每項提案旁寫下「實質內容」與證據：價格、路線、時程，或願意負責的具名人員。漂亮話本身不夠。

Their first drawing took shape on a large sheet of paper. A colored line led from the bus stop to the north door and then to the flat portion of the dance floor. Beside it they marked a rest area, water station, quiet room, and an alternative exit. Cora asked them not to place wheelchair dancers in a single corner. “I want to choose where I move,” she said. Lian erased the fixed wheelchair symbol from the plan and replaced it with several possible routes. It was a small change in ink and a large change in thinking. No performer should be turned into part of the furniture.

他們的第一張設計圖逐漸成形。一條彩色線從公車站通往北門，再延伸到舞廳平坦的區域。旁邊標出休息區、飲水站、安靜空間和備用出口。可拉請他們不要把輪椅舞者全部安排在同一個角落。「我要自己選擇移動的位置。」她說。黎安擦掉固定的輪椅圖示，改畫出幾條可選的動線。墨水上的改動很小，思考方式的改變卻很大。任何表演者都不該被當成家具的一部分。

Before leaving, Lian called the timber seller again. She promised to send the actual measurements and ask for an estimate that named materials separately from labor. Ron had also found a graduate engineer willing to advise them about the ramp. The next stage would be less romantic than drawing arrows and listening to stories. It would involve forms, limits, uncomfortable costs, and honest answers. Lian folded the map carefully so its new lines would not disappear. For the first time, she could imagine a hall that belonged to the people standing in it, not just to those who remembered it.

離開前，黎安再次打給木材商，答應提供實際尺寸，並請他將材料與工資分開估價。羅恩也找到願意提供坡道建議的工程研究生。下一階段會比畫箭頭、聽故事少了些浪漫，卻必須面對表格、限制、不舒服的成本與誠實答案。黎安小心摺好地圖，不讓新畫的線消失。她第一次能想像一間真正屬於當下使用者、而不只屬於懷舊者的舞廳。
