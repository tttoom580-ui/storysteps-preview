燈光下的地板

The Floor Beneath the Lights

把地板留給每一種舞步 · 第 1 / 25 章 · 2000–3000 · 英中對照

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

On the morning Lian returned to Cedar Hill, a child carried a berry pie through the square while workers folded the tents of a traveling circus. The town had changed, but the old post office still stood behind the fountain. Its second floor had once been a dance hall. Lian, twenty-nine, had learned her first steps there before leaving to work as a professional choreographer. Now a paper notice on the door announced that the hall would close in eight weeks. Someone had decorated the notice with a fairy made of silver paper, perhaps to soften the news. Lian did not want a fancy farewell party. She wanted the floor to remain open for people who still needed it.

黎安回到杉丘鎮的早晨，一個孩子端著莓果派穿過廣場，工人則正在收起巡迴馬戲團的帳篷。小鎮改變了，噴水池後面的舊郵局卻還在。二樓曾是一間舞廳。二十九歲的黎安小時候在那裡學會第一個舞步，後來離鄉成為專業編舞者。如今，門上的公告說舞廳八週後就要關閉。有人用銀紙做了仙子貼在公告上，也許想讓壞消息不那麼難受。黎安不想辦一場華麗的告別派對，她希望這塊地板繼續讓需要它的人使用。

At the entrance, Mrs. Wu handed Lian a copy of the building deed. The town owned the stairs and roof, but an arts trust managed the second floor. A broken heater stood beside a cupboard full of shoes, and a narrow wooden hut behind the hall stored festival equipment. From a small radio came the soft sound of jazz. Mrs. Wu explained that the trust had received an offer from a commercial events company. The buyer promised to repair the roof, but wanted the entire floor for private celebrations. “It is easier to pay for one large wedding than fifty ordinary lessons,” she said. Lian understood the figures, yet she wondered who had been allowed to speak before that offer was accepted.

入口處，吳女士遞給黎安一份建物契據。鎮公所擁有樓梯與屋頂，二樓則由藝術信託管理。壞掉的暖爐放在鞋櫃旁，舞廳後方有間狹小木棚，收著節慶器材。小收音機播出柔和的爵士樂。吳女士說，信託收到一家商業活動公司的出價。買方答應修屋頂，卻想把整層樓留給私人宴會。「辦一場大婚禮，比替五十堂普通課付費容易多了。」她說。黎安懂得這筆帳，卻想知道在接受提案前，有誰真正得到發言機會。

A jet passed high over the roof while a street magician finished his show below. His last trick was to pour a full pint of red water into an empty hat. Children called it a miracle. Lian smiled, but she knew the hall would need more than tricks. The town council had placed the final decision on the agenda for the next meeting, and the discussion was already becoming political. One council member spoke about tourism; another spoke about families who could not afford expensive classes. Lian had come home planning to rest for a month after a difficult tour. Instead, she found herself reading the notice again and wondering whether she could offer a practical third choice.

一架噴射機飛越屋頂，廣場下方的街頭魔術師也完成表演。他的最後一招，是把整整一品脫紅色的水倒進空帽子。孩子們叫那是奇蹟。黎安笑了，但她知道舞廳需要的不只是魔術。鎮議會已將最後決定排入下次議程，討論也逐漸變成政治話題。一位議員談觀光，另一位談負擔不起昂貴課程的家庭。黎安原本打算在辛苦的巡演後回鄉休息一個月，如今卻再度看著公告，思索能不能提出務實的第三種選擇。

On the stairs she met Ron, a thirty-one-year-old lighting technician who also taught rhythm classes. He had nearly decided to quit the community arts center because its volunteers were exhausted. “I will not rob a private company of a fair offer,” Lian said, “but the town should hear what it loses.” Ron pointed to a loose screw in the wooden rail and tightened it before letting her pass. That small action mattered to her. He did not promise to save the building with a speech. He noticed a problem, found a tool, and made one part safer. They agreed to inspect the hall before announcing any plan. A beautiful performance would mean nothing if the building itself was unsafe.

樓梯上，她遇見三十一歲的燈光技師羅恩，他也在社區藝術中心教節奏課。中心的志工太疲累，讓他差點決定辭職。「我不會剝奪私人公司合理出價的權利，」黎安說，「但鎮民應該知道自己會失去什麼。」羅恩指著木扶手上一顆鬆動的螺絲，鎖緊後才讓她通過。這個小動作對她很重要。他沒有靠一段演說承諾拯救整棟樓，而是看見問題、找到工具，先讓一處地方更安全。他們決定在公布任何計畫前先檢查舞廳。若建築本身不安全，再美的演出都沒有意義。

Upstairs, Lian found colored tape showing various positions from an old dance class. She asked Ron to give a signal while she crossed the floor, then tried a slow spin near the windows. One board moved beneath her shoe. She stopped at once. “We can tame our excitement for one day,” she told him. Ron marked the weak area instead of pretending it was fine. They made their first list: inspect the floor, ask the dancers what they wanted, learn the cost of repairs, and find out who controlled the hall. The list contained no music and no grand promises, but Lian felt a small return of confidence as she wrote it.

到了樓上，黎安看見彩色膠帶標出舊舞蹈課的各種站位。她請羅恩發出訊號，自己走過地板，再在窗邊慢慢旋轉。一塊木板在鞋底下移動，她立刻停住。「我們可以先克制一天興奮的心情。」她對他說。羅恩標記那塊鬆動區域，沒有假裝一切正常。他們列出第一份清單：檢查地板、詢問舞者想要什麼、了解修繕費，以及弄清誰有管理權。清單上沒有音樂，也沒有宏大的承諾，但黎安寫完時，感到一點信心正在回來。

Before locking the door, Lian opened a window and listened to the town settling into its evening sounds. She remembered being eight years old, too shy to stand in the front row, and a teacher who had moved the chairs so every child could see. Perhaps that was what a dance hall really offered: not a perfect stage, but room to take part without asking permission to be important. She took a photograph of the warning notice and sent it to the arts trust with a request for the inspection records. Tomorrow she would invite people to speak. Tonight she would make sure nobody stepped on the loose board.

鎖門前，黎安打開窗戶，聽著小鎮逐漸安靜的聲音。她想起八歲時的自己，害羞得不敢站第一排，老師便移開椅子，讓每個孩子都能看見。也許舞廳真正提供的，不是完美舞台，而是每個人都不必先證明自己重要，就能參與的空間。她拍下警告公告，寫信向藝術信託索取檢查紀錄。明天，她要邀請居民發聲；今晚，她先確定沒有人踩上鬆動的木板。
