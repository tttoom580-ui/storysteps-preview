A Budget With Empty Lines

把地板留給每一種舞步 · 第 2 / 25 章 · 2000–3000 · 英中對照

The following morning the hall filled with people who had not spoken to one another for years. Mr. Cao, a retired schoolteacher, said the old dances looked like a beast that could never be controlled. His joke made several people laugh, but Cora, a twenty-six-year-old wheelchair dancer, did not laugh. For a decade she had been told that community events were open to everyone, although she could not reach their stages. Across the road a bowling club was requesting money for new lanes. Lian placed all three proposals on a board so nobody could pretend that the town's budget had only one possible use. The question was not whether dance deserved money more than other activities. It was what each plan would actually provide.

隔天早晨，多年沒交談的人陸續走進舞廳。退休教師曹先生開玩笑說，老式舞步像一頭永遠馴服不了的野獸。有人笑了，但二十六歲、使用輪椅的舞者可拉沒有笑。十年來，她不斷聽到社區活動向所有人開放，卻總是上不了那些舞台。街對面的保齡球社也正在申請經費更新球道。黎安把三份提案都貼到板上，讓誰也不能假裝鎮上的預算只有一種用途。問題不是舞蹈是否比其他活動更值得補助，而是各種計畫究竟能提供什麼。

Ron arrived with a first-aid kit and a map of bus stops. A passenger who worked the late shift had written that the last bus left before evening classes ended. Another resident worried that narrow shoe lace caught in the broken floor. Professor Shen from the local college offered to check the building's public use rules without charging a fee. He wore a bright necktie that made him easy to spot in the crowd. Lian asked each speaker to describe a real problem instead of using general praise or blame. She was surprised by how expressive the room became. People were no longer arguing about a memory. They were describing what they needed to enter, learn, and leave safely.

羅恩帶著急救包和公車站地圖抵達。一位上晚班的乘客在紙上寫著，最後一班車總是在夜間課程結束前開走。另一位居民擔心鞋帶會卡進破裂的地板。地方大學的沈教授主動提出免費檢查建物公共使用規定。他繫著鮮豔的領帶，在人群中很顯眼。黎安請每位發言者描述真實問題，不要只作籠統的讚美或指責。大家表達得那麼生動，讓她感到驚訝。人們不再只是爭論一段回憶，而是開始說明自己如何安全地進入、學習與離開。

At lunch, a man who smelled of beer shouted that the plan was a waste of tax money. Another volunteer whispered that he was drunk. Lian chose not to repeat the label. She asked him what he feared losing. “My mother's rent keeps rising,” he answered, suddenly quiet. “I don't want another club for the rich.” His words did not excuse shouting, but they changed the conversation. If the new hall required costly fees, it would frighten away many of the people the project claimed to serve. Cora suggested a clear fee policy with free trial classes and paid instructors. Lian wrote those ideas under a new heading: access is not a feeling; it has a price and a plan.

午餐時，一名身上帶酒味的男子大喊，這項計畫只是在浪費稅金。有人低聲說他喝醉了。黎安沒有重複這個標籤，而是問他害怕失去什麼。「我母親的房租一直漲，」男子突然安靜下來，「我不想再看到只有有錢人能參加的社團。」大聲叫嚷並不因此合理，但這番話改變了討論。若舞廳收費昂貴，就會嚇退原本想服務的人。可拉建議公布清楚的費率、提供免費體驗課，也給老師合理薪資。黎安在新標題下寫下這些建議：無障礙不只是感覺，而需要費用和規劃。

The committee's permit for public events had expired, and the original application did not mention wheelchair access. Ron explained the risk of inviting an audience before repairing the exit. A greedy promoter might ignore that detail, but he refused to do so. Professor Shen studied the town's geography and pointed out that the north entrance was almost level with the street; it might be cheaper to improve that route than to build a new lift. For the first time, Lian could see a solution with numbers attached. They would need an engineer's report, not a guess. She also noted that an accessible route had to remain clear during performances, not just during a quiet inspection.

委員會的公共活動許可已過期，原始申請書也沒有提到輪椅通行。羅恩解釋，在修復出口前邀請觀眾會有什麼風險。貪心的活動商可能忽略細節，他卻拒絕這樣做。沈教授研究鎮上的地形，指出北側入口幾乎與街道同高，改善那條路可能比新建電梯便宜。黎安第一次看見能夠估算成本的解法。他們需要工程師報告，而不是猜測。她也記下，無障礙通道必須在表演時保持暢通，不是只有安靜檢查時才好走。

Near sunset, the council opened a public poll to ask residents what kinds of classes they would attend. Every registered voter could comment, but the organizers also accepted statements from young adults and visitors who could not vote in town elections. “I regret that I never asked this before,” Mrs. Wu said. “I was trying to protect the building, and I forgot the people who used it.” Lian showed her the draft budget with several visible blank lines. Those spaces were intentional. They stood for transport, trained guides, emergency planning, and any need that residents might still identify. No one voted to save the hall that evening. They agreed instead to investigate whether it could become useful again.

傍晚前，議會開放民意調查，詢問居民願意參加哪些課程。有選民資格的人可以發言，主辦者也接受沒有地方投票權的年輕人和訪客提出意見。「我後悔以前沒問過這些。」吳女士說，「我忙著保護建築，卻忘了使用它的人。」黎安拿出草擬的預算，裡面仍留著幾行明顯的空格。那些空格是刻意保留的，代表交通、受訓引導員、緊急計畫，以及居民之後可能提出的需求。那晚沒有人投票決定保留舞廳。大家同意先調查，它是否真的能重新成為有用的地方。

When the last visitor left, Cora stayed beside the empty board. She told Lian that she was tired of appearing in posters while other people decided what accessible dancing should look like. Lian asked whether she would serve as a paid artistic partner, not an unpaid symbol. Cora agreed to discuss the offer after reading the contract. Ron watched Lian leave the amount for her fee in the budget instead of erasing it to make the totals look nicer. He admired that choice. Lian was not sure she could win the grant, but she knew what she would not do to win it. The college also offered to let dancers use a classroom during the coming semester, provided the group sent a clear schedule.

最後一位訪客離開後，可拉仍坐在空白板旁。她告訴黎安，自己厭倦被放上宣傳海報，卻由別人決定無障礙舞蹈該長什麼樣。黎安問她願不願意成為有薪的藝術合作夥伴，而不是無薪象徵。可拉說會讀完合約再談。羅恩看見黎安把她的酬勞留在預算中，沒有為了讓總額好看而刪掉。他欣賞這個選擇。黎安不知道能否取得補助，但她知道自己不願用什麼方式換取勝利。 地方大學也願意在下個學期借教室給舞者，條件是團隊先送出清楚課表。
