長椅下面的訊息

The Message Under the Bench

十點之後，還有人在等 · 第 6 / 25 章 · 1000–2000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

On the eve of the spring arts festival, a crowd filled the main building. Iris and Noah were asked to examine a message said to be found under a bench beside the western sidewalk. A musician had used that bench as a meeting place for years. The paper looked old, but it was not clear when it had been left there. “Do not accept a good story before the facts,” Noah said. Iris agreed. They walked toward the bench in light rain. She wore a yellow raincoat; he carried an umbrella that opened on only one side. “You are very good at machines,” she teased. “Not always at umbrellas.”

春季藝術節前夕，主樓湧入人群。有人在校園西側人行道旁的長椅底下找到紙條，請怡思與諾亞幫忙查證。一位音樂家多年來一直把那張長椅當約定地點。紙張看起來老舊，卻不知道究竟何時留下。「不要因為故事動人就先相信，」諾亞說。怡思同意。兩人冒著細雨往長椅走去。她穿黃色雨衣，他撐的傘卻只有一側張得開。「你很會修機器，」她取笑他，「但雨傘不一定修得好。」

The note was a piece of poetry about a soul waiting after the last bus. It did not name a person or ask for money. Iris wondered whether it came from the same writer as the card they had found the night before. Noah carefully placed it in a dry envelope. A storm warning had been posted for the weekend, so they did not stay outside to search for more. At the student office, an official checked whether the note belonged to a private collection. She told them it was safe to study the text, but they needed permission before broadcasting anyone's voice reading it.

那張紙是一篇關於末班車離去後仍在等候的詩作，沒有署名，也沒有要求金錢。怡思猜想它是否出自前一晚那張卡片的作者。諾亞小心把紙放進乾燥信封。週末已有暴風雨警報，他們沒有繼續留在外頭搜索。回到學生事務處，一位正式職員確認紙條是否屬於私人收藏。她說可以研究文字，但若要公開播放任何人朗讀的聲音，仍應取得同意。

At noon the festival director brought a sensitive problem. A student had lost a notebook of poems, and several pages described a family affected by war. The student did not want that history made public. Iris promised to use only a simple notice about a missing notebook. The director was surprised. “Wouldn't the story bring more listeners?” he asked. “Maybe,” Iris said, “but the owner matters more than the number of ears.” Noah looked at her with a new kind of respect. When he had first met her, she had wanted every sound to be part of a program. Now she could choose silence.

中午，藝術節主任帶來一件敏感的事。一位學生遺失了詩集筆記，其中幾頁寫到家人受戰爭影響的經驗。學生不想公開那段歷史。怡思答應只播報「遺失筆記本」的簡單訊息。主任很意外。「這樣的故事不是能吸引更多聽眾嗎？」他問。「也許吧，」怡思說，「但失主比收聽人數更重要。」諾亞以新的眼光看她。初見面時，她總想把一切聲音變成節目；如今她懂得選擇沉默。

A repair worker came to check the festival speakers. He loved the old poems and called the bench his favorite quiet place. Iris asked what he remembered about the station. He said that toward the end of each school term, students used to leave letters for the night staff. “They were never official prizes,” he said. “Just small thanks.” He pointed to a sign showing a giraffe in a raincoat. The picture had once marked the route to the evening office, so new students could find it. Noah recognized the faded drawing. His grandmother had painted the first version.

一位維修工來檢查藝術節的喇叭。他喜歡舊詩，說那張長椅是自己最喜愛的安靜地方。怡思問他是否記得廣播站的往事。他說，每學期快結束時，學生都會留下給夜班人員的信。「那從不是官方獎品，」他說，「只是小小的感謝。」他指著一張穿雨衣長頸鹿的標示圖。那圖以前是夜間辦公室的路標，讓新生不會迷路。諾亞認得褪色的畫：第一版就是他祖母畫的。

The director let them use a short section of the festival wall to explain the trial. They posted a notice about the lost notebook, their rules for returns, and the hours of the desk. Iris added a line: “You may tell us less than your whole story.” A student from another department asked why they would advertise something so modest. Noah answered, “Because it is true.” A little later, the student with the missing poems arrived and thanked them for protecting the pages. She wanted the notebook returned, not a public lesson about her family. Iris understood exactly.

主任讓他們使用藝術節的一小面牆，說明夜間服務試辦。他們貼上遺失筆記本公告、認領規則與櫃檯時間。怡思加了一句：「你可以只告訴我們故事的一小部分。」另一系的學生問，為何要宣傳這麼不起眼的服務。諾亞回答：「因為這是真的。」不久，遺失詩集筆記的學生前來，謝謝他們保護那些頁面。她只想領回筆記本，不願讓家事成為公開教材。怡思完全懂。

When the evening crowd went home, Noah finally spoke about his grandmother. She had believed that the lost-property desk was not simply a place for objects. It had also been a safe waiting point for anyone who missed the last bus. “She kept tea and a list of rides,” he said. “But the list disappeared during the move.” Iris did not promise to find it by magic. She asked whether he wanted help searching official records. “Yes,” he said, then hesitated. “And thanks for not making me speak sooner.” They walked out under the same broken umbrella, laughing when rain fell on both of them.

晚間人潮離去後，諾亞終於談起祖母。她一直相信失物招領處不只是放東西的地方，也是錯過末班車的人能安全等候的據點。「她備著熱茶和共乘名單，」他說，「但搬遷時那張名單不見了。」怡思沒有承諾像變魔術一樣把它找回來，而是問他願不願意一起查正式紀錄。「願意，」他停了一下，「也謝謝妳沒逼我早點開口。」他們走在那把壞掉的傘下，雨水同時淋上兩人，兩人都笑了。
