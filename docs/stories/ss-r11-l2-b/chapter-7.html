午夜的項鍊

The Necklace at Midnight

十點之後，還有人在等 · 第 7 / 25 章 · 1000–2000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

At midnight, Iris answered a call from a quiet listener who had lost a silver necklace. The caller knew the shape of its small stone but could not remember the exact place. She wanted Iris to give her name and address on air so someone might bring it home. Iris offered different advice: leave a private form at the desk, and the team would sort through the objects already found. The woman was disappointed. “Your radio is pleasant,” she said, “but I need real help.” Iris did not argue. She asked Noah to open the record book and check the whole list of items.

午夜，一位聲音輕柔的聽眾打來，說自己遺失了一條銀項鍊。她知道上面小石頭的形狀，卻不記得確切掉在哪裡。她希望怡思在廣播裡說出她的名字和住址，好讓撿到的人送回來。怡思提供另一種建議：在櫃檯留下私下填寫的表單，團隊會整理已找到的物品。女士有些失望。「你們的廣播很悅耳，」她說，「但我需要真正的幫忙。」怡思沒有爭論，而是請諾亞打開紀錄簿，查看整份清單。

A boy from a cooking class had turned in a bag containing china cups and pieces of toast. Iris asked him why there was a necklace drawn on the outside. He said it was a symbol used by a student theater group, not a clue about the bag's origin. “I made a drawing, not a secret map,” he added. His friends called him naughty for confusing everyone, but Noah said there was no reason to be rude. The group was learning to separate a funny picture from a useful fact. Iris put the bag away and asked the theater office to describe any item it had lost.

烹飪課的一名男生交來袋子，裡面裝著瓷杯和幾片吐司。怡思問他，為什麼袋子外面畫著項鍊。他說那是學生劇團的標誌，和袋子的來源沒有關係。「我畫的是圖，不是藏寶地圖，」他補充說。朋友笑他太淘氣，害大家混淆，諾亞卻覺得沒必要無禮。團隊正在學著分清好笑的圖案和有用的事實。怡思把袋子收好，請劇團辦公室另行描述他們是否遺失物品。

In the morning, an expert from the audio department checked the old microphone lens on a camera that had been left behind. He found no damage. “Somebody beat the dust out of it with a cloth,” he said. “That helped.” Iris asked whether a better lens would make her recordings more honest. He laughed. “Equipment changes what you can hear. It cannot choose what you should say.” She wrote that down. The expert also explained that a former night guard had kept the old log in a lower drawer, not in the station office. It was the first clear clue about where to look next.

早晨，一位音訊系的專家檢查了被遺留攝影機上的鏡頭。他發現並沒有損壞。「有人用布把灰塵拍掉了，」他說，「很有效。」怡思問，更好的鏡頭是否能讓錄音更誠實。專家笑了：「設備只能改變你聽見什麼，不能替你決定該說什麼。」她把這句話記下來。專家也說，過去夜間警衛把舊紀錄存放在下面的抽屜，不是在廣播站辦公室。這是他們第一次得到明確的尋找方向。

The next evening the necklace owner returned with a photograph and a receipt. Noah compared both with a necklace found near the east stairs. The marks matched. “I thought I was a prisoner of my own mistake,” she said with a nervous smile. “I feared somebody would keep it forever.” Iris handed it over after the official check. No camera faced them; the program did not need a picture of the woman's relief. Iris had played a small role by listening to the actual owner, and Noah had checked the facts. Neither of them could have completed the return alone.

隔天晚上，項鍊失主帶著照片和收據回來。諾亞拿兩者和東側樓梯旁找到的項鍊核對，刻痕完全吻合。「我以為自己被錯誤困住，像個囚犯，」她緊張地笑著說，「很怕有人會永遠拿走它。」完成正式確認後，怡思才把項鍊交還。沒有鏡頭拍著她們；節目不需要拿女子鬆一口氣的表情當畫面。怡思的角色是傾聽真正的失主，諾亞負責核對事實。少了任何一人，都無法完成這次歸還。

They celebrated with toast from the kitchen. Iris asked Noah if every return felt this good. He said, “None of them are exactly alike, but yes, I like this part.” She wanted to ask about the drawer in the old guard room, yet she knew he had a test that afternoon. “You can rest,” she told him. “We do not have to solve the past tonight.” His answer was unexpected. “I would rather look tomorrow with you than today without you.” Iris felt her cheeks grow warm. She could not tell whether he meant the work or something more, and for now she liked not knowing.

兩人拿廚房的吐司小小慶祝。怡思問諾亞，每次歸還物品都這麼開心嗎。他說：「沒有兩次完全一樣，但沒錯，我喜歡這部分。」她想問舊警衛室抽屜的事，卻記得他下午還有考試。「你可以休息，」她說，「我們不必今晚就解開過去。」他的回答出乎意料：「我寧願明天和妳一起查，也不想今天自己去。」怡思的臉微微發熱。她不確定他說的是工作，還是別的意思；此刻，她喜歡這種不確定。

Later Iris made a fresh form for lost jewelry. It asked about an item's shape, material, and hidden mark, but not about a person's private relationships. The old form had been copied so many times that it was hard to read. She printed a clear version and invited the caller to review it. “Thank you for telling us what did not work,” she said. The woman smiled. “I was afraid you would ignore me because I was angry.” Iris answered, “Your anger told us there was a problem. It did not mean we should break the rule.” Their small service became a little stronger.

後來，怡思替遺失首飾製作新表單。表單詢問形狀、材質與隱藏刻痕，卻不問私人的感情關係。舊表格影印太多次，已經很難閱讀。她印了清楚版本，邀請那位聽眾一起檢視。「謝謝妳指出不方便的地方，」怡思說。女士笑了：「我還怕自己生氣，你們就不理我。」怡思回答：「妳的怒氣讓我們知道有問題，卻不代表該破壞規則。」他們的小小服務又更堅實了一點。
