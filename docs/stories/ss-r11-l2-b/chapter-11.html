婚禮寄來的信

A Letter from the Wedding

十點之後，還有人在等 · 第 11 / 25 章 · 1000–2000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

The old files led Iris and Noah to a small wedding hall beside the forest road. A former guard had helped at a wedding there many years earlier, and the hall still kept a box of lost objects. The waiter at the door was puzzled by their visit. “Are you here to write about the bride?” he asked. Iris shook her head. “We are students looking for records of the night desk. We will not include anyone's private relationship without permission.” A crow called from the roof. Noah lifted one eyebrow and whispered that the bird sounded like a very rude editor. Iris tried not to laugh.

舊檔案引領怡思與諾亞來到森林路旁的小婚禮會館。一名前警衛多年前曾在這裡幫忙辦婚禮，會館還留著一箱遺失物。門口的服務生對他們的造訪很困惑。「你們是來寫新娘的故事嗎？」他問。怡思搖頭：「我們是來找夜間櫃檯紀錄的學生。沒有同意，我們不會寫別人的私人關係。」屋頂傳來烏鴉叫聲。諾亞挑起一邊眉毛，低聲說那隻鳥聽起來像個非常無禮的編輯。怡思忍不住想笑。

The hall manager knew a priest who had helped organize the event. He was now retired but still visited the neighborhood every week. He remembered that the missing box had been carried through a narrow door during a rainstorm. “It was not a wedding gift,” he said. “It was a guard's book of messages.” He could not say who had written every page, nor did he pretend to know. The manager asked for a small copying fee. Iris paid it and kept the receipt. She wanted an honest paper trail, because a good story without proof could harm the very people it praised.

會館經理認識當年協助辦活動的一位神父。神父已退休，每週仍會來附近走走。他記得有人在暴雨中把一個箱子穿過狹窄的門搬進來。「那不是結婚禮物，」他說，「而是警衛的一本留言簿。」他說不出每一頁由誰寫，也不假裝知道。經理收取少量影印費。怡思付了錢，保留收據。她希望留下可靠的紙本紀錄，因為沒有證據的好故事，也可能傷害被稱讚的人。

Inside the box lay a small stage program, a note from a skilled musician, and a page signed by the author of the old night-service rules. The page included a drawing of a window exactly one centimeter above a blue line. Noah recognized his grandmother's writing but disagreed with Iris about its meaning. She thought the line showed a map; he thought it was a sign for the old counter. “We can disagree without choosing a winner now,” Iris said. Noah smiled. “You have learned my best lesson.” She answered, “And you have learned to ask a question before turning a page.”

箱中有一張小型舞臺節目單、一位熟練音樂家留下的便條，以及夜間服務舊規則作者簽名的頁面。頁上畫著一扇窗，恰好在藍線上方一公分。諾亞認出祖母的筆跡，卻和怡思對它的意思有不同意見。她以為藍線是地圖，他認為是舊櫃檯的標記。「我們可以先保留分歧，不必現在分勝負，」怡思說。諾亞微笑：「妳學會我最好的那課了。」她回答：「你也學會翻頁前先問問題。」

As they left, a pale woman approached them. She was the former guard's friend and had heard that students were researching the old desk. She spoke with care. The service had been started by several workers together, not one heroic person. “Please include the cleaners, the drivers, and the kitchen staff,” she said. “They guarded the door while the others searched.” Iris realized the puzzle had more than one answer. It was not about who deserved the most praise; it was about how people had shared responsibility. Noah thanked the woman and promised to bring his grandmother to a meeting if she wished to come.

離開時，一位臉色蒼白的女士走向他們。她是前警衛的朋友，聽說有學生在研究舊櫃檯。她謹慎地說，這項服務是多名工作者一起創立的，不是某一個人的英雄事蹟。「請記得清潔員、司機和廚房人員，」她說，「有人去找東西時，他們守著門。」怡思發現謎題不只有一個答案。重點不是誰最值得獲得讚揚，而是人們如何分擔責任。諾亞謝謝女士，答應若祖母願意，會陪她參與會議。

Before the bus arrived, they copied only the cover and the signed index of the book. The owner of the hall did not want the messages inside shared without consent, and Iris agreed at once. Noah helped make a list of names that could be approached privately. Some writers might have moved away; some might not want to remember those nights. The list was not a promise of interviews. It was a plan for asking questions with care. Iris noticed that Noah had stopped guarding every decision as if it belonged only to him. He was learning to trust her judgment, and she wanted to deserve that trust.

公車到站前，他們只影印了留言簿封面與有簽名的索引。會館主人不希望未經同意就公開內頁訊息，怡思立刻同意。諾亞協助列出能私下詢問的姓名。有些作者可能早已搬走；有些人可能不願回想那些夜晚。這份清單不是訪談保證，而是小心提出問題的計畫。怡思發現，諾亞不再把每項決定都當成只能由自己把關。他開始信任她的判斷，她也希望自己值得那份信任。

On the bus home, Iris asked whether Noah was upset that the story was changing. “A little,” he said. “I wanted the book to be all about Grandma. But she would hate that.” Iris touched the copying receipt in her pocket. “A relationship is not made smaller when more people are part of the story,” she replied. They sat in silence for several stops. When the bus turned a corner, his hand rested near hers. She did not take it, but neither moved away. Something between them was beginning to feel warm and possible, without any need to name it yet.

回程公車上，怡思問諾亞，故事發展和想像不同，會不會讓他難過。「有一點，」他說，「我本來希望整本留言簿都在寫祖母，但她會討厭那樣。」怡思摸了摸口袋裡的影印收據。「有更多人參與故事，不會讓關係變得比較小，」她回答。兩人在好幾站之間安靜坐著。公車轉彎時，他的手停在她的手旁。她沒有握住，他也沒有移開。某種溫暖又可能發生的東西，正在兩人之間形成，還不需要立刻取名。
