大廳背後的代價

The Price Behind the Lobby

總裁，請先撤回那則聲明 · 第 2 / 25 章 · 2000–3000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

The next morning Yuqing met Xu Lu, the worker representative, in a small meeting room away from the main lobby. Lu did not bring a lawyer or a group of supporters. She brought a notebook with dates and names. “Before you ask us to trust the company,” she said, “show us what happened to our complaints.” Yuqing did not promise an answer. She promised that Lu could read any quotation before it appeared in a report.

隔天早上，予晴在遠離大廳的小會議室見到員工代表許露。許露沒有帶律師或支持者，只帶了一本記著日期和名字的筆記本。「在要我們信任公司以前，」她說，「先讓我們知道申訴後來怎麼了。」予晴沒有保證能給答案，只保證報告引用她的話之前，會先讓她確認。

The group had grown for a decade, but the budget for staff training had barely changed. A professor who studied local geography had once warned that the hill hotel was hard to reach by public transport. A late passenger could miss the last bus and walk past an empty bowling center. In one record a night worker wrote that the route could frighten anyone alone. Another called the long shifts a beast that ate the evening. The complaints were expressive and specific, yet their final status read “solved.”

集團已成長十年，但員工訓練預算幾乎沒有改變。研究地方地理的一位教授曾警告，山丘上的飯店不容易搭大眾運輸到達。晚班乘客若錯過末班車，就得經過空無一人的保齡球館走回去。一名夜班員工寫道，獨自走那條路會讓任何人害怕。另一人把長工時稱作吞掉夜晚的野獸。這些申訴寫得具體而有感情，結案狀態卻都寫著「已解決」。

Yaochuan arrived with a list of proposed improvements and a sharply folded necktie in his hand. He said the company would buy a safety kit for each department and seek a transport permit. Yuqing asked whether the staff had requested those things. “If the problem is the bus schedule, a kit does not change it,” she said. He answered that quick action mattered. She agreed, but pointed out that taking action without understanding the problem could waste money and trust.

曜川帶著改善清單走來，手裡拿著一條折得整齊的領帶。他說公司會替每個部門購買安全用品組，並申請交通許可。予晴問，員工是否要求這些東西。「問題若是公車班次，一套工具不會改變班表。」她說。他回答迅速行動很重要。她同意，但指出不理解問題就行動，可能同時浪費金錢與信任。

On a noticeboard a poster from the previous semester showed workers in costumes trimmed with lace. The picture was cheerful, but someone had written beneath it that a drunk guest had followed a cleaner into the corridor. A visible camera had recorded the incident, yet the file was missing. The hotel director warned against giving a greedy reporter too much information. Lu replied that a voter in the next staff election had a right to know whether security reports were being kept. Yuqing wrote down the risk and the possible regret of concealing it.

公告板上有張上學期活動的海報，員工穿著鑲蕾絲的服裝，照片十分歡樂；但有人在下方寫著，一名醉酒客人曾跟著清潔員走進走廊。明顯可見的攝影機拍下了事情，檔案卻不見了。飯店經理警告不要給貪婪的記者太多資料。許露回應，下次員工選舉的投票者，有權知道保全報告是否被保存。予晴記下風險，也記下隱瞞可能帶來的後悔。

Yuqing asked the records manager to preserve the raw complaint files before anyone changed their labels again. She requested a complete list of who could edit the database, when each access occurred, and which reasons were recorded. The manager seemed nervous. “The system is old,” he said. “Then give us the old rules,” she answered. Yaochuan stood by the door and listened. It was the first time Yuqing saw him refuse to rescue an uneasy manager with a convenient explanation.

予晴請檔案管理員先保存原始申訴檔，避免有人再改動分類。她要求提供資料庫編輯權限名單、每次存取時間與記載的理由。管理員看起來很緊張。「系統很舊。」他說。「那就提供舊規則。」她回答。曜川站在門邊聽著。這是予晴第一次看到他沒有替不安的主管找一個方便的藉口。

At lunch Lu explained that not every worker wanted the same answer. Some needed safe transport; others wanted pay records corrected or the right to change a shift without punishment. “Please don't turn us into one unhappy face,” she said. Yuqing drew a line beneath that sentence. She had seen companies offer flowers, gifts, and inspirational speeches when workers asked for simple records. She did not want to repeat the error. She and Lu agreed to create separate questions for separate departments, with no promise that anyone had to appear on camera.

午餐時，許露解釋，不是每位員工都想要相同答案。有些人需要安全交通，有些人想改正薪資紀錄，或希望換班不受處罰。「請不要把我們變成一張不開心的臉。」她說。予晴在那句話下畫線。她看過公司用花束、禮物與勵志演說回應員工對簡單紀錄的要求，她不願重蹈覆轍。她與許露約定為不同部門設計不同問題，並且不要求任何人上鏡頭。

Lu placed a blank sheet beside her notebook and asked Yuqing to imagine an employee who had never filed a complaint before. The person might not know which department to approach or whether the manager could see a private message. A simple list of contact points could remove some fear, but it would not help if no one answered. Yuqing suggested a test: send an anonymous sample question through the official system, record its path, and see whether a human response arrived on time. Yaochuan approved the test without asking to know the sender. Lu looked at him carefully, still unwilling to celebrate. Yuqing understood. A good first response was evidence that a process could work once, not a guarantee it would always work.

許露把白紙放在筆記本旁，請予晴想像一位從未提出申訴的員工。那人也許不知道該找哪個部門，或經理能否看到私人訊息。簡單的聯絡清單可以減少恐懼，但沒人回覆就仍然無用。予晴提議測試：透過正式系統送出匿名示範問題，記錄傳遞過程，確認能否準時得到真人回覆。曜川批准，沒有要求知道寄件人。許露仔細看著他，仍不願慶祝。予晴理解。一次良好回覆只證明流程這次能運作，不保證永遠如此。

That evening Yaochuan called Yuqing into his office. He expected a neat report of improvements. Instead she gave him a page of unanswered questions and a table of document gaps. He read it twice before asking her to stay for another meeting. She refused: her contract had fixed working hours, and she had plans with her sister. He looked surprised but accepted the boundary. Alone in the elevator, Yuqing wondered whether his silence meant anger or the beginning of attention. She chose not to guess. Tomorrow's records would speak more clearly.

當晚曜川把予晴叫進辦公室，原以為她會交來整齊的改善報告，收到的卻是一頁未解問題和文件缺口表。他看了兩遍，請她再留下開會。她拒絕了：合約已訂明工時，她還和妹妹有約。他很意外，卻接受這條界線。獨自在電梯裡時，予晴不確定他的沉默代表憤怒，還是開始真正聽人說話。她決定不猜，隔天的紀錄會更清楚。
