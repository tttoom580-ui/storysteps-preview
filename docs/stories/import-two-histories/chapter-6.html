頻率與沒有名字的演奏者

The Music of Frequencies

Yicheng had expected time travel to resemble a dream. Instead, 1895 smelled of smoke, wet earth, and coarse cloth. Zhaoyue gave him a coat and assured him that nobody would notice the unusual stitching if he kept his head down. They escaped through the musicians' entrance while the silver-ringed man searched the stage. Behind a wall, an elderly conductor called after them. He had been teaching an orphan choir for the theatre's anniversary and knew more about the clock than he admitted. “You do not need another key,” he whispered. “You need the correct frequency.” His hands shook as if even naming the idea were dangerous.

以誠以為穿越時空會像一場夢。 然而1895年的氣味很真實：煙、濕土與粗布。昭月給他一件外套，保證只要低頭走路，沒人會注意奇怪的縫線。兩人從樂師入口逃跑，戴銀戒指的人仍在臺上搜查。牆後，一位年老的指揮叫住他們。他正在為戲院週年紀念教孤兒合唱，對銅鐘顯然知道得不少。「你們不需要新鑰匙，」他低聲說，「你們需要正確的頻率。」他的手顫抖著，彷彿光是說出這個觀念就很危險。

The conductor explained that the clockmaker came from a family that had served several generations under an old dynasty. His ancestors understood the behavior of vibrating metal better than anyone in the city. Later, a diligent apprentice devised a way to make two pieces of copper answer each other across great distances. Their first evaluation suggested nothing supernatural: certain notes could cause another object to reflect the same vibration. Then one experiment involved two clocks separated not by space but by time. The discovery complicated every assumption they had made. Yicheng listened with the concentration he normally saved for broken radios.

老指揮說，鐘匠家族曾侍奉舊王朝好幾代人。 他們很懂金屬震動。後來有個勤奮的學徒設計方法，使相距遙遠的兩塊銅也能互相應和。初步評估看不出神祕之處：特定音符會讓另一個物體產生相同振動。可是在一次實驗中，兩座鐘相隔的不是空間，而是時間。這發現使他們原來所有假設變得複雜。以誠聚精會神地聽，像平常修理故障收音機一樣。

They hid in an abandoned workshop where mineral dust covered the floor and foam gathered on rainwater leaking through the roof. Wenbo arrived with food for needy families who had taken refuge nearby. He had organized a small campaign to record losses, though he insisted that the work was about people, not slogans. Zhaoyue admired his courageous patience. Yicheng, meanwhile, searched the tables and found a cord attached to a tuning fork. On the wall hung an imitation of the copper clock; it could not open a doorway, but it responded faintly when he hummed the conductor's melody. A pattern was beginning to emerge.

他們躲進一間廢棄工作坊。 地上覆著礦物粉塵，雨水從屋頂滴下，在桶中泛起泡沫。文博帶食物來給附近避難的貧困家庭。他發起小小的紀錄活動，強調工作是為了人，不是為了口號。昭月佩服他勇敢又有耐心。以誠則在桌上找到一根連著音叉的細繩，牆上掛著銅鐘的仿製品。它雖然打不開時間之門，但以誠哼起老指揮的旋律時，竟微微共鳴。某種規律逐漸成形。

The conductor did not begin his lesson with music. He asked Yicheng to close his eyes and describe every sound outside the workshop. The boy listed footsteps, rain, an animal's cry, and a distant cart. Then he heard a second rhythm hidden beneath the first: the slight change in the rain whenever the wind passed the roof. “Sound is not only what you notice,” the conductor said. “Sometimes the clue is what disappears.” Yicheng remembered how static vanished before a radio station came into focus. The clock might behave in the same way, opening its passage only when one sound briefly canceled another.

老指揮的課不是從音樂開始。他要以誠閉眼，說出工作坊外所有聲音。以誠列出腳步、雨、動物叫聲、遠方車輪，然後聽見藏在雨裡的第二種節奏：風掠過屋頂時，滴水聲微微改變。「聲音不只是你聽見的，」指揮說，「有時線索是突然消失的聲音。」以誠想起收音機調到正確電臺前，雜音會短暫消失。銅鐘也許一樣，只有某個聲音短暫抵消另一個時才打開通道。

Zhaoyue practiced humming while Wenbo kept notes. Her first attempts were wildly inaccurate. The conductor corrected her kindly, explaining that perfect pitch was less important than listening to another person. He paired her with Yicheng and asked them to answer each other rather than sing together. Their notes traveled through the dark room like questions passed across a table. When Yicheng drifted too high, she brought him back; when she lost the rhythm, he waited. No official order could have created that small cooperation. It grew because each trusted the other to make room for an imperfect voice. By the end of the evening, the imitation clock had started to glow.

昭月跟著練哼唱，文博負責筆記。前幾次她唱得荒腔走板，老指揮溫和地糾正，說聽見別人的聲音比音準完美更重要。他讓昭月和以誠輪流回應，而不是同聲唱。音符像桌上傳遞的問題穿過暗室。以誠唱高了，昭月帶他回來；昭月亂了拍子，以誠便等她。沒有官令能創造這樣小小的合作，只有雙方願意為不完美的聲音留空間。夜晚結束時，仿製銅鐘開始發光。

That night, Zhaoyue and Yicheng compared what each era expected from them. She was supposed to obey family rules; he was supposed to find steady labor and say little about the war. Neither wish was unreasonable, yet both felt small beside the mystery. “Psychology can explain fear,” Yicheng said, “but it does not always tell us what we should do with fear.” The conductor returned and placed two tuning forks on the table. One was marked 1895, the other 1945. When struck together, they made a note that seemed to hang in the air long after the room fell silent.

夜裡，昭月和以誠談起各自年代對他們的期待。 她被要求守家規，他被期待找穩定的工作、少提戰爭。這些要求並非完全不合理，但在謎團面前又顯得渺小。「心理學可以解釋恐懼，」以誠說，「卻不一定教我們如何與恐懼相處。」老指揮再次出現，在桌上放下兩支音叉。一支刻著1895，一支刻著1945。兩支一起敲擊，房間安靜後，那音符卻似乎仍在空氣中迴盪。
