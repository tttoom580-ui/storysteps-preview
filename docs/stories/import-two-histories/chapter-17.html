記憶的代價

The Price of Remembering

Returning to 1945, they called a small reunion in the radio shop. Wenbo had remained in 1895, but his voice came through the clock whenever the note was repeated. Madam Luo, Mei, Shen, and Liang joined the conversation. The group had adequate evidence to demonstrate the mechanism's danger; what they lacked was an agreeable solution. Closing it would protect the present but prevent anyone from giving future assistance to those still trapped. Keeping it open might preserve more records, yet also expose people to further tragedy. Zhaoyue listened carefully, reluctant to choose between harms that felt equally real.

回到1945年後，他們在收音機店召開小型重聚。 文博仍在1895年，但旋律重複時，銅鐘可以傳來他的聲音。羅館員、阿梅、沈仲和阿良都加入討論。大家有足夠證據證明機制危險，缺的是能讓眾人同意的解方。關閉會保護現在，卻阻止他們繼續幫助被困的人；開著也許能救更多紀錄，卻可能再造悲劇。昭月認真聽著，不願從兩種真實的傷害裡硬選一個。

A memorial ceremony was taking place down the street for those lost during the war. Yicheng stood among families holding faded portraits. An old woman wore bright lipstick, the only color against her dark clothes. She told him that maturity did not mean pretending sorrow was gone. It meant learning to live without demanding that grief become neat. Her words helped Yicheng overcome a quarrel with his father, who feared every new risk. His father had polished the old radio before the ceremony and secretly repaired the tuning fork. “I cannot stop you,” he said, “but I can make sure your tools work.”

街上正在為戰時亡者舉行紀念儀式。 以誠站在手抱泛黃照片的家屬中。一位老婦人擦著鮮豔口紅，是深色衣服裡唯一明亮的顏色。她說，成熟不是假裝悲傷已過去，而是學會帶著悲傷生活，不強迫它變得整齊。這番話讓以誠與父親和解。父親擔心每個新危險，卻早已把舊收音機擦亮，還偷偷修好音叉。「我不能攔你，」他說，「但至少能讓你的工具正常。」

Madam Luo discovered a sheet of statistics showing how many names had disappeared after each activation of the second bell. The count rose sharply whenever someone used the clock for personal advantage. A clerk had resigned rather than alter the numbers; his report was hidden in the margin of a children’s story. Zhaoyue admired the clerk's wit. She also saw the price of his quiet sacrifice: years of suspicion and poverty. The group agreed that the system should be repaired, not used again in its present form. They would first rescue the remaining records, then ask every witness to decide what part of their testimony could be made public.

羅館員找到一張統計表，記錄第二聲鐘每次啟動後有多少姓名消失。 當有人為私人利益使用銅鐘，消失數便急升。一名書記員寧可辭職也不改數字，並把報告藏進童書頁邊。昭月佩服他的機智，也看見默默犧牲的代價：多年猜疑與貧窮。眾人同意，不能再照原樣使用系統。應先救回剩餘紀錄，再由每位見證者決定能公開多少證詞。

The archivist's note revealed that several crucial documents had been carried elsewhere during an earlier evacuation. Their owners had been told that the papers would be safe, but nobody had recorded the new address. Madam Luo recognized the pattern. “People often use the repetition of a promise as proof that the promise was kept,” she said. “A promise is not a receipt.” Yicheng suggested asking former volunteers where the boxes went. Zhaoyue insisted that they approach them without accusation. In each age people made hurried choices with limited knowledge, and a mistake was not automatically a crime. The group agreed to gather accounts before drawing conclusions.

檔案員的便條顯示，一些關鍵文件在較早的撤離行動中被送到別處。主人被告知紙張會安全，卻沒人寫下新地址。羅館員認出這種模式：「人們常把承諾重複很多遍，當成承諾已實現的證明。但承諾不是收據。」以誠提議找當年的志工問箱子去了哪裡，昭月堅持別一見面就指控。每個年代的人都可能在資訊不足時匆忙決定，錯誤不等於犯罪。大家同意先收集說法再下結論。

Later, Yicheng and Zhaoyue walked beside the dark river. Across the water, lights marked houses where ordinary families were preparing dinner. He wanted to ask whether she could stay in 1945, but the words felt selfish. She wanted to ask whether he might live in 1895, yet knew she would be asking him to abandon his parents. Instead they spoke about work. He described teaching children to repair simple circuits. She imagined a travelling collection of village stories. They discovered that neither dream required the other to become a stranger. For now, they let that modest comfort be enough.

後來兩人沿著黑暗河岸散步。水對岸亮著家家戶戶的燈，人們正準備晚餐。以誠想問她能否留在1945年，卻覺得自私。她也想問他可否住在1895年，卻知道那等於要他丟下父母。於是兩人談工作：他想教孩子修簡單電路，她想建立流動的村落故事收藏。他們發現，兩個夢都不需要對方變成陌生人。此刻，這份小小的安慰已經足夠。

Yicheng tried to send a warning by fax to an office he had heard of in a technical magazine, then remembered that the local network could not support the machine. The mistake made everyone laugh for the first time in days. They wrote ordinary letters instead, making copies for several trustworthy addresses. As the envelopes dried, Zhaoyue looked at the portraits from the memorial and imagined the lives behind them. “Even if we forget the sound of a person's voice,” she said, “we must not let someone else decide that the person never lived.” No one disagreed.

以誠一度想依技術雜誌介紹，用傳真機送警告到某個辦公室，隨即想起本地根本沒有可用的網路。 大家好幾天來第一次笑了。他們改用普通書信，寄給數個可信任的地址。等信封晾乾時，昭月望著紀念儀式的照片，想像背後的一生。「即使我們忘記一個人的聲音，」她說，「也不能讓別人擅自決定那人從未活過。」沒有人反對。
