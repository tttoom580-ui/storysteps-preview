山裡沒有墓碑的人

The Names in the Mountain

雙時代之鐘 · 第 3 / 25 章 · 3000–4000 · 英中對照

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

Zhaoyue returned to 1895 just as the lamps in the theatre went dark. Wenbo was waiting behind a barrier of wooden chairs. “You were gone for one minute,” he said. “Where did you go?” She could only blink. The answer sounded impossible, even to herself. Then she noticed the notebook Yicheng had placed in her pocket. It smelled of mild soap and carried a page of unfamiliar handwriting. She wanted to evaluate it carefully, yet the urgent need in Wenbo's face made her stop. A family from the mountainous outskirts had arrived with news of another missing relative. There would be no time for private mysteries.

昭月回到1895年時，戲院的油燈恰好熄滅。 文博躲在椅子排成的障礙後等她。「妳只消失了一分鐘，去了哪裡？」他問。昭月只能眨眼，因為答案連自己都難以相信。她這才注意到口袋裡有以誠放入的筆記本，帶著淡淡肥皂味和陌生筆跡。她本想仔細判讀，卻看見文博焦急的神情。山區邊緣來了一家人，帶著親人失蹤的消息，此刻沒有時間研究私人的謎團。

They walked uphill beneath trees heavy with rain. An old philosopher who taught local children had been beaten after protesting unfair treatment. His injuries were grave, although his voice remained steady. “A ruler may order reform,” he told them, “but reform means nothing when people neglect their neighbors.” The man's granddaughter gripped a twig so tightly that it snapped. She spoke of revenge. Zhaoyue felt the same sharp impulse, but remembered the terrible city she had glimpsed in the other time. Brutal acts could produce more brutal acts; anger alone could not tell them where that chain would end.

他們冒雨上山。 一位教孩子讀書的老人，因抗議不公平待遇而遭毆打。傷勢嚴重，說話卻依然平穩。他告訴兩人：「統治者可以下令改革，但若大家忽視鄰人，改革便沒有意義。」老人的孫女緊握一根小樹枝，直到它折斷，口中說著復仇。昭月同樣憤怒，卻想起另一個年代裡見到的殘破城市。殘酷會引來更多殘酷，而憤怒不能告訴人們這條鎖鏈會在哪裡停止。

Wenbo discovered a narrow cave used to hide records of households. An accidental fall of rocks had sealed its entrance. As he pulled away the stones, dust began to choke them both. Inside lay a box containing a short musical composition and a list of names. Beside several entries, the same symbol appeared: a bird with two tails. Zhaoyue recognized the clockmaker's mark. The document also recorded something stranger than a missing-person report. A traveler with remarkable intelligence had arrived from a future year, then disappeared after asking too many questions. The family had protected the stranger, but nobody remembered where he went.

文博找到一座狹窄山洞，據說有人把戶籍紀錄藏在裡面。 一場意外落石封住了洞口；搬開石頭時，兩人被灰塵嗆得直咳。洞裡有一個箱子，裡面裝著一份簡短樂譜與一張姓名名單。幾個名字旁都畫著同一個記號：有兩條尾巴的鳥。昭月認出那是鐘匠的標誌。但文件記載的不只是失蹤人口：一名異常聰慧的旅人曾從未來某一年到來，因問了太多問題而失蹤。當地人曾保護他，卻沒人記得他最後去了哪裡。

The mountain families did not share one opinion about the changing authorities. One elderly man hoped new rules would finally protect merchants from unfair taxes. A young mother worried that any sudden decision might separate her children from their father. Neither was foolish. When Zhaoyue listened, she discovered how easily her own certainty could become a barrier. She had wanted to identify heroes and villains in a single afternoon, yet the village carried many smaller histories that did not fit a simple answer. The local teacher asked her to write each statement without deciding in advance which one deserved to survive. She promised to try.

山區家庭對政權變動並沒有相同看法。年長男子希望新規則終於能保護商人免受不公平稅負；年輕母親則怕突然的決定把孩子與父親分開。兩者都不愚蠢。昭月傾聽時才發現，自己的過分確定也會形成障礙。她原想一個下午就分出英雄與惡人，但村裡每段小歷史都無法塞進簡單答案。老教師要她先照實寫下每句證詞，別事先決定誰值得被保存。她答應試試看。

At dusk, Wenbo showed her the tree under which villagers had buried letters during an earlier conflict. The ground was wet, and his fingers shook as he uncovered a little tin box. Inside were messages that no longer had living recipients. Zhaoyue thought they might be useless until she saw a name written twice in different hands. It was a name from the cave list. Even someone who could not be found might have left a pattern across other people's lives. She drew the tree and its position on a map so she could locate it again. That small act felt less dramatic than revenge, and far more responsible.

黃昏時，文博帶她看一棵樹。過去發生衝突時，村民曾在樹下埋信。土地很濕，文博挖出小鐵盒時雙手發抖。裡面是收件人已不在人世的信。昭月起初覺得沒有用了，直到看見同一個名字被不同的人寫了兩次，正是洞穴名單上的名字。即使人已不知去向，他仍可能在別人生命裡留下軌跡。她畫下樹與地圖位置，以便日後找回。這舉動不如復仇戲劇化，卻更負責任。

At the foot of the hill, the granddaughter bowed with sincere gratitude. Zhaoyue felt unworthy of it. She had brought back papers, not people. Wenbo asked whether she believed the papers would offend powerful officials. “Yes,” she replied. “And their tendency to silence questions is exactly why we must keep asking them.” Still, she urged him not to publish the names until they could protect the families involved. She had learned one lesson from the boy of 1945: information might be true and still be dangerous in the wrong hands. When she opened his notebook that night, she found a sentence he had not written: The first witness has been found. The second is already among the forgotten.

山腳下，老人的孫女真誠道謝。 昭月卻覺得受之有愧，她帶回的是紙，不是人。文博問，這些紀錄會不會冒犯有權勢的官員。「會，」昭月回答，「但他們總想使提問的人閉嘴，這正是我們更該繼續追問的理由。」不過她催促文博，在保護當事家屬前先別公開姓名。1945年的男孩讓她明白，真相即使是真的，落到錯的人手裡也可能很危險。當晚她翻開以誠的筆記本，裡面竟多出一句不是他寫的話：「第一名見證者已找到。第二名就在被遺忘的人們之中。」
