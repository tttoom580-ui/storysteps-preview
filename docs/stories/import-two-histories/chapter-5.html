兩扇門的戲院

The Theatre of Two Doors

雙時代之鐘 · 第 5 / 25 章 · 3000–4000 · 英中對照

Zhaoyue's grandmother had adopted an orphan years before Zhaoyue was born. The boy grew into a travelling actor, and eventually vanished while working for a government agency that collected records from local communities. Her grandmother cherished his memory, but avoided discussing his unusual behavior before he disappeared. He had spoken to empty rooms, hidden a primitive pocket device beneath his pillow, and claimed that an influential group wished to control what future generations remembered. As a child, Zhaoyue believed he was a wizard. Now she suspected he had understood the clock long before she did.

昭月出生前，祖母曾收養一名孤兒。 孩子長成巡迴演員，後來替一個向地方收集紀錄的官方機構做事，最終失蹤。祖母珍惜他的回憶，卻不願談他失蹤前的奇怪行為：他會對空房間說話，把一個原始的小裝置藏在枕頭底下，還說某個有影響力的團體想控制後人記得什麼。小時候昭月以為他是巫師，如今卻懷疑他早就懂得銅鐘的秘密。

She searched the old room and found a photograph surrounded by paper decoration. The man in it wore a ridiculous hat and stood beside a robber who had once been arrested near the market. Both looked terrified, though neither was restrained. Behind them, the theatre scenery showed two painted doors, one marked 1895 and the other 1945. On the back of the photograph was written: The fundamental problem is not travel. It is choice. Zhaoyue carried the photograph to the stage, where a strange interaction began. The painted doors opened into two real streets, each filled with the sounds of its own year.

她搜索舊房間，找到一張周圍貼著紙飾的照片。 照片裡的男人戴著滑稽帽子，旁邊站著一名曾在市場附近被捕的強盜。兩人都顯得恐懼，卻沒有被束縛。背後的舞臺布景畫著兩扇門，分別寫著1895和1945。照片背面有一句話：「根本問題不在穿越，而在選擇。」昭月拿著照片上臺，奇異的互動開始了：兩扇畫出來的門竟通往真實街道，各自傳來所屬年代的聲音。

Across the second doorway, Yicheng stared at her. For the first time they could speak without shouting through a wall. He told her that public reception of the clockmaker's work had once been enthusiastic, but that a private congress of businessmen later condemned it. The outcome was a sealed theatre, missing witnesses, and people taught to distrust their own memories. Zhaoyue felt horrified. “Why would anyone discourage us from remembering?” she asked. “Because memory gives people a conscience,” he replied. “And a conscience can make a powerful person uncomfortable.” He reached out, but his hand passed through the doorway like smoke.

第二扇門的另一端，以誠正盯著她。 兩人終於不必隔牆喊話。他說，鐘匠的作品一開始受到熱烈歡迎，後來卻被一群私下集會的商人譴責。結果是戲院被封、見證者失蹤，人們還被教導要懷疑自己的記憶。昭月感到毛骨悚然。「為什麼有人不希望我們記得？」她問。「因為記憶會喚醒良知，」以誠回答，「而良知可能讓有權勢的人不舒服。」他伸出手，手掌卻像煙霧一樣穿過門。

For years Zhaoyue had wondered whether her missing uncle left because he did not love the family. Her grandmother never answered directly. She would polish his cup, place it back on the shelf, and ask Zhaoyue to help prepare dinner. The silence had once made the girl angry. Now she wondered whether it had protected the family from questions no one could safely answer. She felt horrified at how many possibilities one disappearance contained. Still, she refused to describe the grandmother's silence as shameful. A person might lack the strength to speak today and find courage tomorrow.

多年來昭月一直想，養叔叔是不是不愛家人才離開。祖母從不直接回答，只擦亮他的杯子、放回架上，再叫昭月幫忙煮飯。以前這樣的沉默讓她生氣；如今她懷疑那是否保護家人不受危險追問。她驚恐地發現，單是一次失蹤就有那麼多可能原因。但她不再把祖母的沉默說成可恥。一個人今天也許沒有力氣說話，明天卻可能找到勇氣。

Yicheng noticed her uncertainty and did not rush to comfort her with promises. He had heard too many false assurances in his own time. Instead, he pointed to a simple difference between the two painted doors: one had been repaired repeatedly, while the other was still covered by its original paint. “If you can tell where a repair was made,” he said, “you can ask why someone made it.” They examined the frame and found scratches beneath the newer paint. The marks formed a route toward the theatre's locked storehouse. For a moment Zhaoyue laughed at the absurdity: even a door in a painting was keeping a record.

以誠注意到她不安，沒有急著用承諾安慰她。他在自己的年代已聽過太多虛假的保證。相反地，他指出兩扇畫門的差別：一扇反覆修補，另一扇仍是最初的漆。「若知道哪裡修過，」他說，「就能問為什麼要修。」兩人查看門框，在新漆下面找到刮痕，竟組成通往戲院上鎖倉庫的路線。昭月一時笑出來，連畫裡的門都在替歷史留下紀錄。

A voice behind Zhaoyue ordered her to step away. The theatre owner had returned, accompanied by a visitor wearing a silver ring. The visitor spoke of stability and prosperity while his fingers tightened around a weapon. The owner looked ashamed but did not move. Zhaoyue recognized the visitor's face from the photograph. She wanted to run; instead she asked the man what he had done to her adopted uncle. Her question filled the theatre with a cold terror. Then the copper clock rang one sharp note, and Yicheng crossed from 1945 into 1895. He fell onto the wooden stage at her feet. The doors vanished, leaving them together in a century that could now arrest them both.

昭月身後有人命令她退開。 戲院老闆帶著一名戴銀戒指的訪客回來。那人一邊談安定與繁榮，一邊把手握緊在武器上。老闆滿臉羞愧，卻不敢動。昭月認出照片裡就是這張臉。她想逃，卻反而問對方把養叔叔怎麼了。問題讓整間戲院陷入冰冷的恐懼。銅鐘突然敲出尖銳的一聲，以誠竟從1945年跨進1895年，摔在她腳邊的木舞臺上。兩扇門隨即消失，留他們困在同一個世紀，也一同面對可能的追捕。
