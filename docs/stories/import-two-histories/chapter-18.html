第二聲鐘之夜

The Night of the Second Bell

The archivist finally revealed himself in the theatre's upper balcony. His name was Xu, a former colleague of Yicheng's father and a man admired for his thoughtful lectures on history. He had used a gigantic stack of forged documents to convince officials that certain families never existed. Xu claimed divine authority from the clock, as if access to many years made him more than human. “You cannot understand what it takes to protect a city,” he told Yicheng. “Some memories must be removed.” The boy answered that no regulation could turn a lie into truth by repeating it enough times.

檔案員許某終於在戲院樓上包廂現身。 他是以誠父親的前同事，常以深思熟慮的歷史講座受人敬重。他靠一大疊偽造文件說服官員，相信某些家庭根本不存在。他宣稱銅鐘賦予自己神聖權威，彷彿能看許多年就高人一等。「你不懂保護城市需要什麼，」他對以誠說，「有些記憶就該刪除。」以誠回答，規定不能因謊言重複夠多次，就把它變成真相。

Xu carried a battery-powered lamp and a radio transmitter. He ordered an assistant to fetch a key from beneath a landmark statue near the river. While the assistant ran, Zhaoyue noticed that Xu chewed the end of his pencil exactly as Huang had done in 1895. Their resemblance was no accident. The two men had spent years exchanging instructions. A dance troupe rehearsing ballet in the next hall suddenly lost its music when Xu interrupted the power. Without the familiar rhythm, the performers stumbled. “You see?” Xu said. “People need one person to direct them.” Zhaoyue shook her head. “They need to hear one another.”

許某帶著電池燈與無線電發報器，命助手去河邊地標雕像下取鑰匙。 助手離開時，昭月注意到許某咬鉛筆的方式，竟與1895年的黃某一模一樣。兩人多年來互傳指令，這不是巧合。隔壁廳有芭蕾舞團排練，許某切斷電力，音樂突然消失，舞者因失去熟悉節奏而踉蹌。「看吧，」許某說，「大家需要一個人指揮。」昭月搖頭：「他們需要的是彼此聽見。」

To delay Xu, Yicheng asked about the identification numbers on the forged pages. His questions were dry, almost grammatical: Why did one name change its spelling halfway through a report? Why did a year appear in a form not invented until later? Xu grew irritated. His arguments began to exhaust him. Zhaoyue used the distraction to open a cabinet containing the original register. Inside lay proof that several erased families had survived the theatre fire. She felt an admirable urge to shout with victory, but remained quiet. The truth could not yet help anyone if Xu destroyed it before they left.

以誠用偽造文件上的身分編號拖延許某。 問題乏味得像文法練習：為何同一個人的名字在報告中突然換拼法？為何某個年份採用了更晚才有的格式？許某越來越惱怒，辯解讓他精疲力竭。昭月趁機打開櫃子，取出原始登記簿，證明好幾個被刪掉的家庭其實在戲院火災中倖存。她想高聲歡呼，卻忍住了。只要許某還有機會毀掉原件，真相就還無法救人。

Before the confrontation, Yicheng's father gave him a lesson in the grammar of honest disagreement. “When you say a man has lied,” he explained, “you must know exactly which statement you mean. Otherwise, your anger may become the only thing people hear.” Yicheng repeated the advice to Zhaoyue. She recognized the same caution her father had taught her about printing names. Their motivation was not to win an argument but to prevent further harm. They checked their documents again, marking statements supported by multiple accounts and setting aside the rest. Only then did they enter the theatre.

對峙前，以誠父親教他「誠實爭論的文法」：「你說某人撒謊，就必須明確指出是哪一句；否則別人只會聽見你的怒氣。」以誠把這個建議告訴昭月，她認出那和父親教自己印姓名時的謹慎相通。他們的動機不是贏辯論，而是避免更多傷害。兩人再次查文件，標出有多份證詞支持的內容，其餘先放旁邊，然後才走進戲院。

Xu's status as a respected public figure had once protected him from questions. He believed that anyone doubting his records was challenging the city itself. But several people in the audience had known him as a helpful teacher before he began to hide documents. Their disappointment was deeper than simple hatred. They wanted an explanation that would make their earlier trust understandable. When Xu raised his voice, Zhaoyue saw their faces tighten. She realized that exposing wrongdoing was only a beginning. The harder task would be helping a community learn how to trust evidence again without becoming unable to trust people at all.

許某備受敬重的地位，曾使他免於質疑。他相信誰懷疑他的紀錄，就是挑戰整座城市。然而觀眾裡有人記得他從前是熱心老師，直到開始藏文件。那份失望比單純的恨更深，他們想知道從前的信任為何會被辜負。許某提高音量時，昭月看見那些人的臉緊繃。她明白揭發錯誤只是起點，更難的是讓社區重新相信證據，同時不至於完全不敢再相信任何人。

Xu pressed the bell's switch. Instead of a second note, the theatre filled with a reflection of hundreds of voices. Faces appeared around the walls, asking to be remembered, not worshipped. For one instant Xu looked like a man waking from a nightmare. Nevertheless, he reached for the lever again. Yicheng struck the repaired tuning fork; Zhaoyue matched its note with her own voice. The clock hesitated. The portraits trembled but stayed visible. In the silence afterward, Xu whispered, “How can a machine choose against its master?” Yicheng answered, “It didn't. We chose not to obey you.”

許某按下鐘的開關。 第二聲沒響，劇院卻充滿數百個聲音的回音。牆上浮現出許多臉，他們只求被記得，不求被崇拜。那一瞬間許某像從惡夢驚醒，但仍伸手去扳拉桿。以誠敲響修好的音叉，昭月以自己的聲音配上音高。銅鐘停住，肖像顫動卻沒有消失。寂靜中，許某低語：「機器怎麼能背叛主人？」以誠回答：「不是機器選了我們，是我們決定不服從你。」
