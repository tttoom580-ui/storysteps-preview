The Chorus of Ordinary People

The final performance would take place simultaneously in both years. Zhaoyue prepared the 1895 stage while Yicheng organized the 1945 hall. Their plan consisted of three parts: a reading of verified names, a chorus based on the clockmaker's tune, and a public explanation of how the records had been altered. The project required concentration and creativity, not weapons or arms. Some volunteers were disgusted by Huang's lies and wanted to exaggerate his crimes. Zhaoyue refused. “Our objective is to prove what happened,” she said. “If we invent one detail, we give people a reason to doubt the rest.”

最後的演出將在兩個年份同時舉行。 昭月準備1895年的舞臺，以誠安排1945年的禮堂。計畫分三部分：朗讀核實姓名、合唱鐘匠的旋律，以及公開解釋紀錄如何被篡改。這需要專注與創意，不需要武器。有些志工厭惡黃家的謊言，想誇大罪行。昭月拒絕：「我們的目標是證明發生過什麼；只要編造一個細節，就讓人有理由懷疑其他證據。」

The rehearsal was a dynamic mess. A vegetarian cook brought soup, but half the choir forgot to eat. An idle stagehand tried to imitate the conductor, moving his arms with more enthusiasm than logic. Children laughed; adults looked embarrassed. Yet the noise helped strangers begin to talk. A worker from the print shop described a workplace accident that had once been erased from a report. Another volunteer offered a practical remedy: every statement should be copied and checked by someone who was not related to the speaker. Soon numerous small tasks replaced one enormous and frightening mystery.

彩排熱鬧得一團亂。 一名素食廚師送來湯，合唱團卻有一半忘記吃。一位閒著的舞臺工人模仿指揮，雙臂揮得熱情，動作卻沒什麼邏輯。孩子大笑，大人有點尷尬，但吵鬧反而讓陌生人開始交談。印刷店工人說起一樁曾被紀錄抹掉的職災。另一名志工提出實際補救方式：每份說法都要由非親屬的人核對。許多小任務，逐漸取代原本巨大又嚇人的謎團。

At sunset, a flower began to bloom beside the clock, though it had grown for years without opening. Liang called it a sign, but Zhaoyue preferred to think of the blossom as a simple reminder that life continued. She faced a singular fear: after the performance, the clock might close, and Yicheng might return permanently to 1945. Neither spoke about the possibility. They practiced the first note together, adjusting until their voices met without forcing one to follow the other. “Whatever happens,” Yicheng said, “I don't regret meeting you.” Zhaoyue smiled. “That sounds like farewell.” He did not answer.

夕陽西下，銅鐘旁一朵多年不開的花忽然綻放。 阿良說是徵兆，昭月卻寧可把它當成生命繼續的提醒。她面對一種獨特的恐懼：演出結束後銅鐘可能關閉，以誠也許永遠回到1945年。兩人都不願談。他們一起練第一個音，直到聲音自然契合，誰也不必勉強追隨對方。「不管結果如何，」以誠說，「我不後悔遇見妳。」昭月微笑：「聽起來很像道別。」他沒有回答。

The rehearsal succeeded largely because no single person tried to control it. A singer who could not read music stood beside one who had studied for years, and both learned to follow the same simple signals. Wenbo wrote the names in large letters so elderly people could see them from the back rows. A carpenter repaired the seats without being asked. Zhaoyue realized that a gathering became powerful when many people could contribute something small without waiting to be noticed. No one would remember every helper, but the archive could at least record that the work had belonged to more than one hero.

彩排之所以能成功，很大程度是因為沒有人試著獨自控制。看不懂樂譜的歌者站在多年受訓者身旁，兩人都學著跟同樣的簡單手勢。文博把姓名寫得很大，好讓坐後排的老人看見。木匠不等請求就修好椅子。昭月明白，當很多人都能貢獻一點而不必等待被看見，聚會便有力量。沒有人會記得每個幫手，但至少檔案可以留下，這份工作不只屬於一個英雄。

Yicheng visited the first surviving witness just before the performance. The woman had been invited to speak, yet she worried that her memories would sound too ordinary. He told her about the child who had preserved a wooden toy. “Your story doesn't have to be grand,” he said. “It only has to be yours.” She showed him a list of people who had shared meals during the hardest months of the war. The names were written on scraps of wrapping paper. Some entries were incomplete, and she apologized for the gaps. Yicheng assured her that an unfinished list was still worth preserving, provided they did not pretend it was complete.

演出前，以誠去拜訪第一位仍在世的見證者。老婦人受邀發言，卻怕記憶太平凡。他說起保存小木鳥的孩子：「故事不必宏大，只要是妳的。」她拿出一份戰時最艱難月份裡曾分享食物者的名單，寫在包裝紙碎片上。有的紀錄不完整，她為空白道歉。以誠告訴她，只要不假裝完整，未寫完的名單也值得保存。

The first audience gathered. Several people offered testimony; others came only to listen. The most moving moment came when the child of an erased messenger brought a small wooden toy. He had kept it even after adults insisted his father never existed. The toy's paint was worn, but its shape resembled the bird with two tails. Zhaoyue accepted it without demanding a story. She understood now that rejection could be loud or silent, and that the simplest refusal to forget could outlast an official decree. Above the stage, the copper hand reached midnight. Across fifty years, the same note began to rise.

第一批觀眾入場了。 有人願意作證，有人只想聽。最動人的一刻，是一名失蹤信差的孩子帶來小木玩具。即使大人堅稱父親從未存在，他仍保存著它。玩具的漆已磨損，形狀卻像雙尾鳥。昭月收下，沒有逼他說故事。她如今明白，拒絕可以大聲，也可以安靜；最簡單的「我不忘記」也可能比官令更長久。舞臺上方，銅鐘分針到達午夜。相隔五十年，同一個音開始升起。
