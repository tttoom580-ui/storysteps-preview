寄給明天的兩張明信片

Two Postcards for Tomorrow

寄給明天的兩張明信片 · 第 25 / 25 章 · 1000–2000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

The final public day began with a chart of the project's results. An did not aim to win a prize; she wanted ordinary visitors to understand the choices behind each number. The chart showed delivered, returned, and waiting cards, but no private names. Her niece came with a curious expression and asked why a large company had not paid for a bigger celebration. An explained that money was useful only when its rules fit their promise. The girl nodded, then pointed to a spelling mistake on the public sign. An thanked her and fixed it before anyone arrived.

最後的公開活動以一張成果圖表揭開序幕。安的目標不是贏得獎品，而是讓普通訪客了解每個數字背後的選擇。圖表列出已送達、已退回與等待中的卡片，沒有任何私人姓名。她的姪女好奇地問，為什麼沒有讓大公司出錢辦更大的慶祝。安解釋，金錢只有在條件符合承諾時才有用。女孩點頭，接著指出公開看板上的拼字錯誤。安謝謝她，在訪客抵達前把字改好。

A classical music group played softly as people entered. The event looked simple, yet it had taken months of work. A young man grew angry when he learned that the team would not deliver a card across the national border. Chen explained that Slow Post had no license for international mail. He offered the correct public post office address instead. Another visitor asked why the table for damaged cards was separate from the garbage bin. An answered that damaged private writing still belonged to its owner. Justice did not mean treating an unwanted letter like rubbish without asking.

古典音樂小組輕聲演奏，訪客陸續進場。活動外觀簡單，背後卻花了數月準備。一名年輕男子得知團隊不能代送跨越國境的卡片後生氣了。澄解釋慢遞沒有國際郵務許可，改提供正確的公共郵局資訊。另一位訪客問，為什麼受損卡片桌要和垃圾桶分開。安回答，即使私人書寫受損，所有權仍屬原主。正義不代表可以不問就把不想要的信當垃圾丟掉。

An had expected a rapid line of visitors, but the day began slowly. A volunteer worried that the quiet appearance would seem like failure. An shook her head. The aim was never to master a crowd or make it obey an exciting plan. It was to offer a service people could enter or leave freely. She felt no anger toward visitors who chose not to write. A good event could be gentle, with room for questions and a few long pauses. Chen added an extra chair beside the table, plus a large-print copy of the rules. The first guest sat down and began to write.

安原以為訪客會迅速排隊，活動卻緩慢開始。一名志工擔心安靜的外觀會顯得失敗。安搖頭。目標從來不是掌控人群，或讓大家服從刺激的計畫，而是提供能自由加入與離開的服務。對於選擇不寫信的訪客，她沒有怒氣。好的活動可以溫柔，容得下提問與長時間停頓。澄在桌旁加一張椅子，另外放上大字版規則。第一名來賓坐下，開始書寫。

Mr. Wu, the old musician, returned with a fresh postcard. He had written it to his future self after receiving the first one. The gardener came too, carrying a leaf pressed between two pages. Both chose to keep their new cards until the dates they had written. A student who had mastered the delivery rules taught the next volunteer group. An watched with pride. The service would continue under the library's care, with public reviews and a clear way for people to withdraw consent. She and Chen could leave without leaving a broken promise behind.

老音樂家吳先生帶著新明信片回來了。讀過第一張後，他又寫一張給未來的自己。園丁也來了，帶著夾在兩頁紙間的葉子。兩人都選擇把新卡留到自己寫定的日期。一名已熟練掌握投遞規則的學生，正在教下一批志工。安自豪地看著。服務將由圖書館接手，定期公開檢討，也保留清楚的撤回同意方式。她與澄可以離開，而不留下破碎的承諾。

At sunset the two walked back to the blue box. Chen asked if An wanted to write one last card for tomorrow. She agreed. On hers she wrote, “I hope I keep asking before I choose for someone else.” He wrote, “I hope I never stop learning how to listen.” They sealed the cards and marked a date one year later. Then An turned to him. “I cannot promise every day will be easy,” she said, “but I want to try the next part of life with you.” Chen answered, “So do I.” Their first kiss was gentle and chosen, with no audience and no announcement.

夕陽下，兩人走回藍色郵筒。澄問安願不願意寫最後一張給明天的卡。她答應了，寫道：「希望我在替別人選擇以前，永遠記得先問。」澄寫的是：「希望我永遠不停止學習傾聽。」他們封好卡片，標上明年同一天的日期。安轉身對他說：「我不能保證每天都輕鬆，但我想和你一起試著走向下一段人生。」澄回答：「我也是。」他們輕柔地初吻，出於雙方意願，沒有觀眾，也沒有公告。

Before sunset the librarian gave the team a small certificate of thanks. An accepted it for everyone, but she asked the volunteers to stand with her in the photograph. The blue box had begun with forgotten cards and uncertain rules; now its next leaders were ready. An felt proud without needing to keep control. Chen whispered that they could still visit the project as ordinary guests. She laughed and said that sounded perfect. Their private cards could wait until next year. They had no need to open the future early, and no reason to be afraid of it simply because it remained unknown.

日落前，圖書館員頒給團隊一張小小的感謝狀。安代表大家收下，卻請所有志工一起站進照片。藍色郵筒從被遺忘的卡片與不明確的規則開始，如今下一任負責人已經準備好了。安感到驕傲，卻不再需要掌控一切。澄低聲說，他們以後仍能以一般訪客身分來看看。安笑說這聽起來太好了。兩人的私人卡片可以等到明年。他們無須提早打開未來，也不必只因它尚未揭曉就害怕。

Afterward they returned the keys to the librarian, who checked the final records. Nothing had been hidden for a beautiful ending. Some letters would never be opened, some would be returned, and some would bring joy on an ordinary morning. An understood that this was enough. She had not solved every life, and neither had Chen. They had only made a small place where people could speak, wait, say no, and choose again. The blue box stood quietly beneath the tree. Tomorrow would bring new stories, but none would need to be taken from their owners.

之後，他們把鑰匙交回圖書館員，由對方核對最後紀錄。為了漂亮結局，沒有人藏起問題。有些信永遠不會打開，有些會退回，也有些會在平凡早晨帶來喜悅。安明白這已足夠。她沒有替所有人解決人生，澄也沒有。他們只是創造一個小地方，讓人們能說話、等待、拒絕，再重新選擇。藍色郵筒靜靜立在樹下。明天會有新故事，但不需要從任何主人手中奪走。
