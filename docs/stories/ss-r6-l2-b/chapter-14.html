A Debate About Stories

寄給明天的兩張明信片 · 第 14 / 25 章 · 1000–2000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

The student council held a debate about the future of Slow Post. Some members wanted the team to produce a public collection of its best letters. Others said private messages should stay beyond the reach of any audience. An listened to every conversation and wrote down the difference between permission to deliver and permission to publish. A football game outside sent cheers through the windows. Chen passed her a note with one clear detail: even a person who agrees today may wish to change the decision tomorrow. That thought became the center of her essay for the council.

學生會針對慢遞未來舉行辯論。有些成員希望團隊出版最佳書信合集；另一些人認為私人訊息應遠離觀眾。安聽完每段談話，寫下「同意投遞」與「同意發表」的差別。窗外足球比賽的歡呼聲傳進來。澄遞給她一張便條，寫著一個重要細節：今天同意的人，明天也可能改變決定。這成為安向學生會提交文章的核心。

A young artist from the southern town brought a tale about a girl who wore a mask in order to speak freely. He asked whether a story with changed names would be suitable for publication. An said names were only one part of privacy. A place, a date, or a unique detail could still point to a real person. He thought about that while adjusting the belt around his waist. “I never meant to hurt anyone,” he said. “I know,” she answered. “That is why we should check before the book comes out.”

一位來自南方城鎮的年輕畫家帶來故事：女孩為了自由說話而戴上面具。他問改過名字的故事是否適合出版。安說姓名只是隱私的一部分；地點、日期或獨特細節仍可能讓人認出當事者。畫家調整腰際的皮帶，沉思了一會。「我從沒想傷害誰，」他說。「我知道，」安回答。「所以在書出版之前，我們更該先檢查。」

The council chair said the public guide was due before the end of term. An had only three days to finish the text, so she asked two volunteers to check it with her. The computer room was closed because of an electric problem, and they worked with paper instead. Chen read each rule aloud while An marked confusing sentences. A first-year student asked whether there was a difference between refusing a letter and refusing a friendship. An answered that a person could care about someone and still not want contact at a particular time. The student nodded, relieved by the answer.

學生會主席表示，公開指南必須在學期結束前交出。安只剩三天完成文字，便請兩位志工一起檢查。電腦室因電力問題關閉，他們改用紙筆工作。澄逐條朗讀規則，安把容易誤解的句子標出來。一名大一學生問，拒絕一封信與拒絕友誼有沒有不同。安回答，一個人可以關心對方，卻在某個時間不願聯絡。學生聽完點頭，神情輕鬆了些。

An prepared a summary of the debate for students who had not been able to attend. She used clear examples invented for the purpose, rather than quoting someone's real letter. Two volunteers checked the examples to make sure they were easy to understand. Chen helped place the summary on the university website. The public could now see why the council had chosen a guide instead of a book. An knew that a fair decision was stronger when people could understand the reasons behind it.

安替沒能出席的學生整理一份辯論摘要。她使用專為說明而虛構的例子，沒有引用任何真實私人信件。兩位志工檢查例子是否容易理解。澄協助把摘要放上大學網站。公眾現在能知道學生會為何選擇指南，而不是書信集。安明白，當人們理解決定背後的理由，公平的決策才更有力量。

A stamp collector offered a box of old envelopes to the council. One envelope carried a note about a wound that had changed its writer's life. Chen reminded the room that emotional weight could be heavier than paper. A council member argued that the public had a right to seek history. An replied that people also had a right to decide what part of their own lives became public. They could teach students to write new tales and invite volunteers to share freely. They did not need to take stories from those who had given only permission to deliver them.

一位集郵者向學生會提供一盒舊信封，其中一封提到改變寄件人人生的傷口。澄提醒大家，情感的重量可能比紙張還沉。一名議員主張公眾有權追尋歷史。安回應，人們也有權決定自己生命的哪部分公開。他們可以教學生創作新故事，也能邀請志工自願分享，無須拿走那些只同意投遞者的故事。

After the vote, An and Chen met by the river as planned. They bought warm drinks and found a bench away from the road. Chen asked if she wanted to talk about the guide. She said not yet. She wanted to hear about his favorite book and how he had chosen his field of study. He told her about a teacher who had once helped him when he was shy. An listened, then shared a memory of her own first school project. Their conversation moved slowly. There was no race to reach a clever ending, only the pleasure of getting to know each other.

投票後，安和澄依約在河邊碰面。他們買了熱飲，找一張遠離道路的長椅坐下。澄問她想不想談指南，她說先不要。她想聽他最喜歡的書，以及為何選擇現在的科系。澄說起一位在他害羞時曾幫助他的老師。安認真聽，也分享自己第一次做學校專題的記憶。他們的對話很慢，沒有比賽誰先說出精彩結尾，只有慢慢認識彼此的快樂。

The council voted for a public guide rather than a book of private letters. An was relieved, though she knew the decision was not the ending of every problem. On Sunday she met Chen at the river walk. They talked about the debate, then left work behind for an hour. They watched boats and shared a small bag of nuts. An did not call it a date until Chen asked whether she wanted to. She laughed. “Yes, I think I do.” He answered, “Then I am glad I asked.” She liked how simple the truth sounded beside the water.

學生會投票決定出版公開操作指南，不出版私人書信合集。安鬆了口氣，卻知道這不是所有問題的結局。星期天，她和澄在河邊步道見面。他們先聊了辯論，接著有一小時不談工作。他們看著船隻，分享一小袋堅果。安原本沒把這稱為約會，直到澄問她是否願意這麼稱呼。她笑了。「我想是的。」他回答：「那我很高興自己問了。」河水邊，真話聽起來如此簡單，令她喜歡。
