沒有公開的照片

The Photograph That Stayed Private

寄給明天的兩張明信片 · 第 9 / 25 章 · 1000–2000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

The school paper wanted to write an article about Slow Post. Its young writer asked An to provide a painting, a puppy photograph, and a few personal stories. She also asked for a chapter from an old notebook, hoping to make the article shine. An felt the daily pressure of publicity. If they refused, would the project fail? Chen pointed to a legal notice on the wall. “A person can give us a card without giving us the right to publish it.” An called the writer back and offered an interview about their system instead of private letters.

校報想替慢遞寫一篇文章。年輕記者請安提供一幅畫、一張小狗照片與幾個私人故事，還希望拿到舊筆記本的一個章節，讓文章更出色。安感受到每天宣傳工作的壓力：如果拒絕，計畫會不會失敗？澄指著牆上的法律告示說：「一個人交給我們卡片，不代表也給了我們發表的權利。」安回電給記者，改提議接受關於制度運作的訪問，而不是提供私信。

A shopkeeper near the university offered to print simple cards for free. He showed them a historical picture of his street and a child's drawing done with a blue crayon. He had used paste and a small nail to hang them beside his counter. An asked for permission before copying the drawing. The shopkeeper agreed but wanted the child's family to approve as well. His careful answer pleased her. It was proof that people outside the team could understand the idea. The writer from the school paper listened and began to see a different section for her story: ordinary people making thoughtful choices.

大學附近的店主願意免費印製簡單卡片。他拿出街道的歷史照片，以及一張孩子用藍色蠟筆畫的圖。他曾用漿糊和小釘子把它們掛在櫃台旁。安在複製圖畫前先詢問許可。店主同意，但希望孩子的家人也批准。這樣謹慎的回答讓安很高興，證明團隊外的人也能理解這項理念。校報記者在旁邊聽著，開始替文章想到新的段落：平凡的人如何做出體貼的選擇。

The school writer returned to check the final article. An said she could use photographs of the empty room, the volunteer guide, and the paper box, unless any picture showed private addresses by mistake. The writer carefully checked each frame. She had expected the students to be difficult, but now she understood their concern. She told An she had once published a classmate's drawing without asking and had regretted it. An did not turn the confession into a public lesson. She simply thanked the writer for taking permission seriously this time.

校報記者回來檢查文章最後版本。安說她可以使用空房間、志工指引與紙箱的照片，但若畫面不小心拍到私人地址，就不能用。記者仔細檢查每個鏡頭。她原以為學生們難合作，如今理解了他們的顧慮。她告訴安，自己曾未經同意發表同學畫作，事後很後悔。安沒有把這段坦白變成公開教材，只謝謝記者這次認真看待同意。

A student editor later asked whether photographs of volunteers needed permission too. An answered yes; helping at an event did not make a person's face public property. The editor nodded and brought a simple consent form for the team to review. Chen checked that it included a way to say no without losing a volunteer position. The new rule made the photographers slow down, but they took better pictures because people in them were comfortable with being seen.

一位學生編輯後來問，志工照片是否也需要徵得許可。安說需要；參與活動並不代表一個人的臉成了公共財產。編輯點頭，帶來簡易同意書供團隊審閱。澄確認表單允許志工拒絕拍照，而不因此失去工作位置。新規則讓攝影師慢下來，但因入鏡的人都自在，拍出的照片反而更好。

An's partner on the project still worried about losing readers. Chen suggested one addition: invite volunteers to describe their own work in public, without naming any receiver. They could talk about carrying boxes, cleaning the room, and learning to ask difficult questions. No one needed to reveal another person's fear to make a good story. The team met a student who had once made a worse mistake by posting a letter without asking. He had apologized and removed the image. An told him he could help explain why the rule mattered. He agreed, though it was not easy.

安的專案搭檔仍擔心文章失去讀者。澄建議新增一個做法：邀請志工公開描述自己的工作，但不提收件人的名字。他們可以說搬箱子、打掃房間，以及學會提出難題的經驗。沒有人需要暴露別人的恐懼，才能讓故事精彩。小組遇見一名曾犯下更嚴重錯誤的學生：他未經同意就張貼別人的信。他已道歉並撤下圖片。安請他幫忙說明規則的重要性。他答應了，雖然這並不容易。

When the paper was printed, An brought a copy to the shopkeeper who had offered the cards. He read slowly, smiling at the description of the blank box. “This article gives people a way to understand your work,” he said. “It does not make anyone into an object.” He taped the article beside the door, away from the child's drawing. Chen suggested bringing a copy to the librarian as well, because she had first trusted them with the old blue box. An agreed. Good work was easier to continue when people knew who had helped make it possible.

校報印好後，安拿一份給提供卡片的店主。他慢慢讀，看到空盒子的描寫時笑了。「這篇文章讓人了解你們做什麼，」他說，「卻沒有把任何人當成物品。」他把文章貼在門邊，與孩子的畫隔開。澄建議也送一份給圖書館員，因為是她最先信任他們，交出舊藍色郵筒。安同意了。當人們知道這件事由誰協助完成，好工作便更容易持續下去。

The article appeared on Friday. It included a photo of the empty blue box, not of any private card. An read it three times. The writer had not made the project sound perfect; she had described a small effort that might succeed only if its people stayed careful. A first-year student cut out the article and placed it on her desk. She said it helped her understand why saying no could be a kind act. An felt proud, but she knew the work was not done. That evening she thanked Chen for letting her decide how to speak to the press. He answered, “You did not need my permission.”

文章星期五刊出，配圖是空的藍色郵筒，而非任何私人卡片。安讀了三遍。記者沒有把計畫寫得十全十美，而是描述一項小小的努力，能否成功取決於成員是否持續謹慎。一名大一學生把文章剪下來放在桌上，說這讓她明白拒絕有時也是善意。安很驕傲，也知道工作還沒結束。那天晚上她感謝澄讓她自行決定如何接受採訪。他回答：「妳本來就不需要我的允許。」
