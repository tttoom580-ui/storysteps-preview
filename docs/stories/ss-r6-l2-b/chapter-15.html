不是密碼的鑰匙

The Key That Was Not a Password

寄給明天的兩張明信片 · 第 15 / 25 章 · 1000–2000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

A new volunteer suggested using a shared password for every card file. Chen said no. “An easy password is not fair to the people who trust us.” He proposed separate access for each task, with a record of who opened a file. An asked him to explain the phrase in simple words for everyone. The team was working in a room with a high ceiling near the old castle-shaped clock tower. A student cheered when she found the missing workbook for the volunteer class. The discovery seemed small, but it helped them teach the new rules clearly.

新志工提議替所有卡片檔案共用一組密碼。澄反對。「太容易的密碼，對信任我們的人不公平。」他建議依不同工作分配權限，並記下誰開啟過檔案。安請他用簡單的話向所有人解釋。團隊位在一間天花板很高的教室，附近有座像城堡的舊鐘塔。一名學生找到志工課遺失的練習本，高興地歡呼。這發現雖小，卻幫助他們清楚教導新規則。

At lunch, a nearby shop gave them slices of mango and cold pork sandwiches from its refrigerator. The owner had heard that a local patient with cancer wanted to send a message but could not travel. An suggested asking the patient first whether a family member could carry the card. She did not allow anyone to discuss the person's health in public. Another volunteer brought a saw to repair a broken chair, but the manager told him to work outside the reading room. The sound would disturb people, and the task was not part of mail service.

午餐時，附近店家送來芒果切片和從冰箱取出的冷豬肉三明治。店主聽說一名罹癌病人想寄信，卻無法外出。安建議先詢問病人，是否願意由家屬代送。她沒有讓任何人公開討論病情。另一名志工帶鋸子想修一張壞椅子，管理員卻請他到閱覽室外工作。噪音會打擾他人，而且修椅子並非郵務工作的一部分。

When the new volunteers passed their safety review, An wanted to cheer, but Chen raised a hand and reminded her that the library was still open. They celebrated outside with quiet laughter instead. A student said the rules felt less frightening after practice. Another asked whether an error would mean being removed from the team. An explained that honest mistakes should be recorded and corrected, while deliberate misuse of private information would require a stronger response. The volunteers agreed. They wanted a place where they could learn without hiding trouble, and where visitors could trust that mistakes would not be covered up.

新志工通過安全複習時，安很想歡呼，澄卻舉手提醒圖書館仍開放。他們改在戶外安靜地笑著慶祝。一名學生說實際演練後，規則不再那麼可怕。另一人問犯錯是否就會被趕走。安解釋，誠實的錯誤應被記錄與改正；若故意濫用私人資訊，就需要更嚴格處理。大家都同意。他們希望這是能學習而不必掩飾問題的地方，也讓訪客相信錯誤不會被偷偷蓋掉。

The librarian joined the meeting near the end and asked whether the project could work without An and Chen. They showed her the training guide, the keys kept by the office, and the way volunteers could report mistakes. She asked a new member to explain the rules in her own words. The student did so clearly. An felt a sudden warmth. What began as two students opening an abandoned box was becoming a shared service with people ready to care for it after they left.

會議快結束時，圖書館員加入，詢問沒有安與澄時計畫能否運作。他們展示訓練指南、由辦公室保管的鑰匙，以及志工回報錯誤的方法。她請一位新社員用自己的話說明規則，學生說得很清楚。安心裡突然一暖。原本只是兩名學生打開被遺忘的郵筒，如今逐漸變成有更多人願意接手的共同服務。

The fifth weekly meeting brought a negative report and a useful one. There had been two late deliveries, but altogether the team had completed twenty agreed requests and returned every rejected card. An insisted that equal care mattered more than a perfect number. She read the facts in a calm voice, then let the volunteers suggest changes. Someone proposed a shorter walking lane for rainy days. Another offered to teach simple computer skills. Chen added that a clear record of mistakes would protect both visitors and students. The team voted for the changes.

第五次週會帶來壞消息，也帶來有用的結果。團隊有兩件投遞延誤，但總計已完成二十件同意收件的要求，所有被拒收的卡片也都退回。安堅持，平等的照顧比漂亮的數字重要。她平靜地念出事實，再讓志工提出修改。有人建議雨天改走較短的巷道，另一人願意教授基本電腦技能。澄補充，清楚記錄錯誤能保護訪客與學生。團隊投票通過這些變更。

Before leaving, An found a note written for the project by the elderly musician. It contained no private history, only a sentence of thanks to everyone who had helped him listen to himself. She asked whether it was meant to be public, and the sender had marked yes. For the first time, An placed a real message on the display wall without worry. It had been offered freely, with a clear choice. Chen stood beside her while she read it. They were both quiet, and the quiet felt like a small celebration of what they had learned.

離開前，安找到老音樂家寫給計畫的便條。上面沒有私密往事，只有一句謝謝每個幫助他重新傾聽自己的人。安確認它是否可公開，寄件人已清楚勾選同意。她第一次毫無擔心地把真實訊息貼到展示牆上。那是自由提供、經過明確選擇的文字。澄站在她身邊，看她讀完。兩人都很安靜，而那份寂靜就像對所學一切的小小慶祝。

When the meeting ended, Chen asked whether An wanted to tell the team about their walk. She said they did not owe the group an answer. “Our work belongs here,” she said, touching the table. “Our private choices belong to us.” He agreed immediately. They walked through the quiet lane toward the tram stop. An carried the weekly report and thought about how much had changed since the first blue box. The project was no longer just hers or Chen's. It belonged to people who could use it, question it, and leave it freely.

會議結束後，澄問安是否想向團隊提起兩人的散步。安說他們不欠小組任何交代。「我們的工作屬於這裡，」她碰著桌面說。「私下的選擇屬於我們自己。」澄立刻同意。他們穿過安靜小巷，走向電車站。安抱著週報，想起最初那只藍色郵筒以來的改變。如今計畫不再只屬於她或澄，而是屬於能使用、質疑，也能自由離開它的人。
