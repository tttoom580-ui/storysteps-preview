藍色郵筒

The Blue Box

寄給明天的兩張明信片 · 第 1 / 25 章 · 1000–2000 · 英中對照

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

The blue box stood beside the university café, under a tree that lost its leaves every autumn. An had passed it for three years without reading its sign. Now, at twenty-three, she was in charge of a small class project about written messages. She opened the box and found seventeen postcards, each dated for the future. Chen, a twenty-five-year-old student who worked in the library, came to help. He had a plain notebook, a calm voice, and the kind of careful personality that made people trust him before he said much. An did not share that feeling yet. She liked quick answers; he wanted rules first.

藍色郵筒立在大學咖啡館旁，每到秋天，頭頂的樹便會落葉。安走過這裡三年，從未仔細讀過郵筒上的告示。如今二十三歲的她負責一項關於書面訊息的課堂計畫。她打開郵筒，發現十七張寫給未來的明信片。二十五歲、在圖書館工作的學生澄前來幫忙。他帶著樸素的筆記本，說話沉穩，個性謹慎，常讓人還沒聽他說多少話就願意信任。安卻還沒有這樣的感覺。她喜歡迅速得到答案，他則堅持先訂好規則。

The first card belonged to an old gardener with a white beard. He had drawn a tiny leaf beside a picture of his daughter. On the back he asked one favor: when the date came, please send the card only if his daughter still wanted it. Another postcard mentioned a chemical leak near the river. An wondered whether it was evidence of a crime, but Chen shook his head. “A story is not a report,” he said. “We cannot relate a person's memory to a real accusation without checking.” They agreed to divide the cards into private and public groups, without reading the private messages aloud.

第一張卡片是一位留著白鬍子的老園丁寫的。他在女兒的畫像旁畫了一片小小的葉子，並拜託收件人一件事：日期到了，只有女兒仍願意收信時才寄出。另一張明信片提到河邊曾經發生化學物質外洩。安猜想那會不會是犯罪證據，澄卻搖頭。「回憶不等於報告，」他說。「未查證之前，我們不能把某個人的記憶和真實指控連在一起。」兩人決定把卡片分成私人與公開兩組，不朗讀私人的內容。

Before they moved the cards, Chen made a space for questions. An asked why the blue box had been left alone for so long. The librarian explained that the old project had ended when its teacher retired. Nobody had been appointed to decide what should happen to the remaining mail. An looked at the dates, which spread across several years, and understood the danger of a simple answer. A card might still be welcome, or it might touch a life that had changed completely. She made a list of people to ask for guidance, beginning with the librarian and the school's privacy officer. She did not write down the private words themselves.

搬動卡片之前，澄先留出討論時間。安問為什麼藍色郵筒會被忽略這麼久。圖書館員解釋，舊計畫在指導教師退休時結束，之後沒有任何人受指派決定剩下的郵件該怎麼處理。安看著跨越好幾年的日期，理解到簡單回答可能帶來危險。卡片或許仍受歡迎，也可能觸動一個早已完全改變的人生。她列出需要請教的對象，從圖書館員和學校隱私承辦人開始，卻沒有抄下私人文字。

At noon, An's roommate arrived in pajamas, carrying two slices of watermelon left from a breakfast party. She wore rubber shoes because the floor near the old mail room was wet. “Can I help?” she asked. An gave her a dry cloth, but Chen asked everyone to keep the letters on a clean table. The temperature in the room rose when the sun reached the window. Some old stamps had begun to curl. They decided to weigh each bundle before moving it, so nobody would lose a card by mistake. The work was slower than An wanted. It was also safer.

中午，安的室友穿著睡衣走來，手上拿著早餐聚會剩下的兩片西瓜。因為舊郵務室附近的地板潮濕，她穿了橡膠鞋。「我可以幫忙嗎？」她問。安遞給她乾布，澄則請所有人把信件放在乾淨的桌面上。陽光照到窗戶時，室溫逐漸上升，一些老郵票開始捲曲。他們決定搬動之前先秤每一疊卡片的重量，以免不小心弄丟。工作比安想的慢，卻也更加安全。

A student from the business department came to ask whether the project would pay a city tax. Chen did not pretend to know. He wrote the question down and offered to check the rule with the school office. “There is no shame in saying you do not know,” he told An. She almost answered with a joke, then stopped. Such honesty was strange to her after a year of noisy group projects. Outside, the gardener returned with an empty envelope. He did not ask to see his daughter's card. Instead, he said, “If she says no, please let no be enough.” An looked at the blue box differently after that.

一位商學院學生來問，計畫是否需要繳市府的稅。澄沒有裝懂，而是記下問題，說會向學校辦公室確認規定。「不知道就說不知道，沒什麼丟臉的，」他告訴安。她差點開玩笑回嘴，卻停住了。經過一年吵鬧的分組作業，這樣的坦白令她覺得陌生。窗外，園丁拿著空信封回來。他沒有要求看女兒的卡片，只說：「如果她拒絕，就請把拒絕當成答案。」從那一刻起，安再看那只藍色郵筒，眼光已經不同。

Chen brought a little box of blank sleeves from the library desk. They used soft pencils to label the outside, never writing on the original envelopes. An thought of the gardener's request and decided to put each person's choice at the center of their plan. She also made space for a card to stay where it was. Not every story needed to move on the day someone discovered it. When her roommate asked whether this work was exciting, An said it was quieter than she had imagined. But the feeling of holding another person's trust, carefully and without a claim to it, was stronger than she expected.

澄從圖書館櫃檯拿來一小盒空白保護套。他們用鉛筆標示外層，絕不在原信封上寫字。安想到園丁的請求，決定把每個人的選擇放在計畫中心。她也為那些應該原地保留的卡片安排位置。不是每個故事都必須在被發現的當天繼續前進。室友問這工作是否刺激，安說比想像中安靜。然而，謹慎守住他人的信任，又不主張占有它，帶來的感受比她預期更強烈。

That evening they made the first rule on a page in Chen's notebook: a postcard may be delivered only to someone who agrees to receive it. The second rule was just as important. People could ask the team to return or destroy an undelivered card. The third rule said that nobody could post a photograph of a card without permission. An added a fourth line in large handwriting: “We are not here to collect secrets.” Chen smiled at the sentence, then asked her if she would lead the project with him. It was not an invitation to a date. Still, An carried the question home as carefully as a letter.

那天晚上，他們在澄的筆記本上寫下第一條規則：明信片只能送給同意收件的人。第二條同樣重要：寄件人可以要求取回或銷毀尚未送達的卡片。第三條則禁止未經允許拍照公開信件。安用大字補上一行：「我們不是來收集別人的秘密。」澄看了笑起來，接著問她願不願意和他共同主持這項計畫。那不是約會邀請。可是安仍像珍惜一封信那樣，小心地把這句話帶回家。
