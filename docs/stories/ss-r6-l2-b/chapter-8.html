長桌上的故事

The Long Table

寄給明天的兩張明信片 · 第 8 / 25 章 · 1000–2000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

The student dining hall offered the team one long table for a Saturday event. An expected an average gathering, perhaps ten people. Instead, families arrived with stories about childhood dinners, summer barbecue days, and the first time they learned to use chopsticks. A retired cook brought a heavy load of old recipe cards. He did not command the volunteers to display them. He simply asked if they could help him choose which ones were safe to share. Chen placed clean paper on a shelf and offered a gentle smile. An took notes about the source of each story.

學生餐廳借給小組一張長桌，舉辦週六活動。安原以為只會有普通規模的聚會，大約十個人。沒想到許多家庭帶著童年晚餐、夏日烤肉，以及初學使用筷子的回憶前來。一位退休廚師搬來一大疊老食譜卡。他沒有命令志工全數展示，只問他們能否協助挑選適合分享的內容。澄在書架上鋪好乾淨紙張，溫和地微笑；安記下每個故事的來源。

One recipe was written by a woman who had once worked as a servant in a large house. She had saved enough money to open her own small kitchen. Another card carried a picture of a flat stone that marked the first place she sold food. “Does that mean her fate was decided there?” a child asked. An shook her head. “It means she made a choice there. A story can move forward after one important day.” The child's mother nodded. The woman in the story had built a business by her own means, not through anyone else's permission.

有張食譜出自一位曾在大宅工作、擔任幫傭的女士。她存夠錢後開了自己的小廚房。另一張卡片畫著一塊平石，那是她第一次賣食物的地方。「這是不是表示她的命運就在那裡決定了？」一個孩子問。安搖頭。「這表示她在那裡做了一個選擇。故事可以在重要的一天之後繼續向前。」孩子的母親點點頭。故事中的女人靠自己的方法建立事業，不是靠別人的允許。

After the table opened, a young chef began teaching children to hold a chopstick without pressing too hard. He brought a second chopstick and showed them how the pair moved together. A grandfather watching nearby said the lesson reminded him of his first meal in the city. The chef laughed and offered to teach him too. An liked the small exchange. In reality, most guests had not come to talk about old letters. They had come to spend a little time with others. The cards simply gave them a gentle reason to begin a conversation.

長桌開放後，一位年輕廚師教孩子拿筷子，不要握得太緊。他再拿來第二根筷子，示範兩根如何一起移動。旁邊觀看的祖父說，這讓他想起初到城市的第一餐。廚師笑著說，也可以教他。安很喜歡這個小交流。事實上，大部分訪客並不是特地來談舊信，而是想和別人相處一會。卡片只是給大家一個溫和的理由，開啟對話。

Before everyone left, the retired cook offered An a recipe written on a fresh card. She asked whether he wanted it placed on the public wall or kept as a private gift. He chose the wall and signed his name himself. Chen put the card in a clean frame, and the children gathered to read it. For one afternoon, the dining hall had become a place where food and letters brought people together without taking away anyone's right to be quiet.

訪客離開前，退休廚師送給安一張新寫的食譜卡。她問他想放在公開牆上，還是當成私人禮物。他選擇公開，並親自署名。澄把卡片放入乾淨的框裡，孩子們聚過來閱讀。那個下午，餐廳成了食物與信件讓人們相聚的地方，卻不因此奪走任何人安靜的權利。

A student tried to stick all the old cards to a wall, but Chen stopped him before any glue touched the paper. A visitor had already seen a family photograph disappear from a past show, and she was worried. The team explained that they would use copies only with clear permission. A small sign on the table read, “Whatever you share, you may later ask us to remove.” An watched people read that line. It gave the event a different meaning. They were not collecting a perfect picture of the past; they were offering people a chance to choose what remained visible.

一名學生想把老卡片全黏上牆，澄在膠水碰到紙之前阻止了他。有位訪客過去曾在展覽中弄丟家族照片，因此十分擔心。小組解釋，只有取得明確同意才會使用複製品。桌上的小標語寫著：「無論分享什麼，日後都可以要求撤下。」安看著大家閱讀那行字。活動因此有了不同的意義。他們不是在收集完美的過去圖像，而是讓人們選擇什麼可以被看見。

A woman offered to leave a recipe card in the public box. She read it aloud first and asked that her home address be removed. Chen helped her cross it out on a new copy without touching the original. An asked whether she wanted to sign her name. The woman chose only her first name. Two children came back later to say they had tried the recipe with their father, and it had tasted wonderful. The team recorded the public feedback, not the family's private details. For An, that was a better result than any large exhibition of old letters could have given them.

一位女士願把食譜卡留在公開箱中。她先朗讀一次，要求移除住家地址。澄協助她在新複本上遮掉地址，沒有動到原件。安問她想不想署名，女士只選擇留下名字。後來兩個孩子回來說，他們和爸爸照著食譜做了菜，非常好吃。團隊記下公開回饋，沒有收集那個家庭的私人細節。對安而言，這比任何大型舊信展示都更有意義。

At the end of the day, An asked the guests whom they wanted to thank. Some named parents or teachers. A woman thanked the neighbor who taught her to cook after she moved to the city. Chen wrote no names unless the speaker agreed. The table was nearly empty when An found a blank card left by a child. On it were five words: “Please keep the table here.” That was not a grand plan, but it was enough to make her smile. Chen suggested asking the dining hall for another Saturday, and she happily agreed.

活動結束時，安問來賓想感謝誰。有人說父母或老師；一位女士感謝初到城市時教她做菜的鄰居。除非說話者同意，澄一個名字也不記。長桌快收乾淨時，安找到孩子留下的空白卡片，上面寫著：「請把這張桌子留在這裡。」那不是宏大的計畫，卻足以讓她微笑。澄建議再向餐廳借一個星期六，她高興地答應了。
