沒有名字的地址

An Address Without a Name

寄給明天的兩張明信片 · 第 7 / 25 章 · 1000–2000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

One postcard had an address but no name. The paper was old, and a blue line ran across the bottom. An wanted an expert to study the stamp, yet Chen gave different advice: first ask the sender's family whether the card should be opened at all. The actual address was near a shop that sold fine china and old glass. The owner said the building had changed hands three times. She offered a magnifying lens so they could read the date, but warned them not to guess the origin of the message from a faded mark alone.

一張明信片有地址，卻沒有收件人姓名。紙張已舊，底部有一道藍線。安想請專家研究郵票，澄卻提出不同建議：先問寄件人的家屬，是否同意任何人打開卡片。實際地址在一家販售瓷器與老玻璃器皿的店附近。店主說那棟樓已三度易主。她借給他們放大鏡讀日期，也提醒別只憑模糊印記就猜測訊息的來源。

A friendly listener at the shop heard their question and told them about her brother. He had been a prisoner many years ago, and the family had saved his letters. She spoke without shame. “None of those words belong to strangers,” she said. An realized how easily curiosity could take the role of kindness. She thanked the woman for the story but did not ask to see the letters. Chen made a short form for families who wanted to help identify an owner. It asked for permission and contact details, not for personal secrets.

店裡一位親切的聽眾聽到他們的問題，談起自己的哥哥。他多年前曾是囚犯，家人一直保存他的書信。她說得坦然。「那些話一句都不屬於陌生人，」她說。安察覺好奇心多容易冒充善意。她謝過對方分享故事，卻沒要求看信。澄替願意協助確認所有者的家屬製作簡短表單，只詢問同意與聯絡方式，不索取私密內容。

The shop owner asked how the team would sort postcards when two people had the same family name. Chen explained that they never guessed from a name alone. They checked dates and safe contact information, then asked the right person directly. The woman looked relieved. Her brother had once been confused with a stranger who shared his name, and the mistake had caused trouble for years. An promised that they would never send a card because a guess happened to sound likely. A wrong delivery could be difficult to undo, especially when an old story involved feelings that had changed.

店主問，如果兩個人同姓，小組要如何分類明信片。澄解釋，他們絕不只憑姓名猜測，而是核對日期與安全的聯絡資訊，再直接詢問正確當事者。女士鬆了口氣。她哥哥曾被誤認為另一個同名陌生人，這個錯誤帶來多年的麻煩。安承諾不會因為一個猜測聽起來很合理就寄出卡片。錯誤投遞有時很難挽回，尤其當舊故事牽涉早已改變的感情。

The woman at the shop also gave them a useful suggestion: put a blank space on every contact form for the person to choose a safe time to answer. An liked the idea because a correct address did not mean someone was always ready to talk. They tested the change with volunteers, then asked the librarian to approve it. The blue box had begun to teach them not only where a message belonged, but also when it should be allowed to arrive.

店裡的女士還提出一個好建議：每張聯絡表都留一格，讓當事人選擇安全且方便回覆的時間。安喜歡這個想法，因為地址正確不代表對方隨時都準備好談話。他們先請志工測試修改，再交由圖書館員核准。藍色郵筒開始教會他們的不只是訊息該往哪裡去，還有何時才適合抵達。

By midnight they were still sorting a box of loose covers. An's roommate brought toast and teased Chen about the way he checked every line. “You beat everyone at being careful,” she said. A naughty cat jumped onto the table and knocked down a pen. From under the books came a small necklace tied to another postcard. The tag held a date but no name. Instead of putting it in the public display, An placed it in a separate bag. She was learning that the whole story might never be theirs to tell.

到了午夜，他們還在整理一箱鬆散的封套。安的室友帶來吐司，取笑澄逐行核對的模樣。「論謹慎沒有人比得過你，」她說。一隻調皮的貓跳上桌，碰落一枝筆。書本下露出一條綁在另一張卡片上的小項鍊，標籤有日期卻沒有姓名。安沒有把它放進公開展示，而是另袋保存。她逐漸明白，整段故事也許從來就不屬於他們講述。

At the end of the visit, Chen put two identical blank cards on the table. One had an address filled in, and the other did not. He asked An which one was easier to deliver. She answered that neither could be delivered without permission. He laughed and said she had learned the lesson so well that he could no longer tease her with it. An put the blank cards away. She wanted to ask why he had once written to himself, but she remembered his earlier answer. For the first time she found that choosing not to ask could bring her closer to someone.

拜訪結束時，澄把兩張一模一樣的空白卡放在桌上，一張填了地址，另一張沒有。他問安哪一張比較容易送達。安回答，如果沒有取得同意，兩張都不能寄。他笑說她把這一課學得太好，已經無法再拿它逗她。安收起空白卡。她想問澄從前為何寫信給自己，但想起他先前的回答。她第一次發現，選擇不追問有時反而能更靠近一個人。

The next morning a family member called. She confirmed that the card belonged to her late aunt, but she wanted it returned unopened. An did not try to change her mind. She wrote the request in the log and prepared the envelope. Chen looked pleased. “You asked the right question,” he said. “Not what is written there, but who may decide.” An answered that she had only learned it from several people, including him. She liked the pleasant silence that followed. Neither of them needed a clever line to fill it.

隔天早上，一名家屬打來電話，確認卡片屬於已故的姑媽，但希望原封不動退還。安沒有試圖說服她改變心意，只在紀錄本寫下要求並準備信封。澄看起來很高興。「妳問對了問題，」他說。「不是上面寫什麼，而是誰有權決定。」安回答，自己是從許多人身上學會的，也包括他。接著出現的舒適沉默讓她很喜歡。兩人不需要用聰明話填滿它。
