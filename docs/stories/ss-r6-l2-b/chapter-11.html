沒有署名的信

The Letter with No Author

寄給明天的兩張明信片 · 第 11 / 25 章 · 1000–2000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

A narrow envelope arrived without an author's name. The return address led to a waiter at a restaurant near the forest park. He looked surprised when An asked about the card. “I wrote to my sister before her wedding,” he said, “but I never sent it.” A crow called above the roof while they spoke. The man measured the old envelope with a ruler and pointed to a mark less than one centimeter wide. “That is my mark,” he said. Chen raised an eyebrow. The detail made sense, yet it did not prove who could now receive the letter.

一個狹窄的信封沒有署名。回郵地址通往森林公園附近餐廳的一位服務生。安詢問卡片時，他看起來很驚訝。「我在妹妹婚禮前寫給她，卻一直沒寄。」交談時，屋頂上方傳來烏鴉叫聲。男子拿尺量舊信封，指出一個不到一公分的記號。「這是我留下的，」他說。澄挑起眉毛。細節說得通，卻仍不足以證明現在誰能收信。

The man said the letter included a puzzle they used to play as children. He could answer it, but his sister's choice mattered more. “We had a disagreement after the wedding,” he admitted. “I was rude, and I do not want a delivery to become another demand.” An thanked him for saying that. The restaurant manager offered to pay a fee to speed the matter up. Chen refused. There was no paid service, nor a special path for people with money. They would ask the sister only whether she wanted contact, not why the family had fought.

男子說信裡有他們小時候常玩的謎題，他能答出來，但妹妹的選擇更重要。「婚禮之後我們起過爭執，」他承認。「我說話很沒禮貌，不想讓送信變成另一種要求。」安謝謝他願意說明。餐廳經理提議付費加快流程，澄拒絕了。這不是收費服務，也沒有有錢人專用通道。他們只會問妹妹是否願意接觸，不會追問家人為何吵架。

A guard at the restaurant entrance knew the brother and sister from years before. He offered to explain why they had stopped speaking, but An declined. She did not want her team to become a judge of an old family disagreement. The guard seemed surprised. He had thought the young volunteers were searching for dramatic stories. An told him they were only trying to return a letter with care. He shook her hand and said he wished more people understood that difference. Chen thanked him for his time, and they went back to the campus with the envelope still sealed.

餐廳入口的警衛多年前就認識那對兄妹。他主動提出解釋兩人為何不再說話，安卻婉拒。她不希望小組變成舊家庭爭執的法官。警衛有些驚訝，原以為年輕志工在尋找戲劇化的故事。安告訴他們只是想謹慎地退回信件。他和她握手，說希望更多人理解這個差別。澄謝過他的時間，帶著仍封好的信封回校園。

The waiter wrote one final message to his sister, but chose to keep it in his own drawer. An told him that was a complete choice, not a failure to finish the project. He thanked her with a small bow and returned to his work. On the way out, Chen bought a paper bag of bread from the restaurant counter. He and An shared it on a bench. They talked about how much courage it sometimes took to leave a message unsent, especially when a person wished very much to be heard.

服務生又寫了一封給妹妹的信，最後卻選擇留在自己的抽屜裡。安告訴他，這是完整的選擇，不是計畫失敗。他微微鞠躬道謝，回去工作。離開時，澄從餐廳櫃檯買了一紙袋麵包。兩人在長椅上分享，談起有時把信留著不寄，需要多大的勇氣，尤其當一個人真的非常渴望被聽見。

Back on campus, a skilled drama student invited An and Chen to help with a short stage reading about letters. An liked the idea but was careful about the relationship between fiction and private stories. They could write new words; they could not copy a family's pain. A pale light shone through the rehearsal room window. A priest from a nearby community center stopped by to ask if elderly residents could send cards as well. An explained the rules. He listened, then said a good welcome should always include the freedom to leave.

回到校園，一位演技純熟的戲劇系學生邀請安與澄協助一場書信短劇朗讀。安喜歡這個想法，卻小心區分虛構與私人經驗。他們可以寫新台詞，不能抄用某個家庭的痛苦。蒼白的光從排練室窗邊照進來。附近社區中心的牧師順道問，長者是否也可以寄卡片。安解釋規則，他聽完說，真正的歡迎應包含隨時離開的自由。

That evening the theater student asked whether the letter's story could be turned into a play with a different ending. An asked him to invent a new family and a new problem instead. He sat with a notebook for an hour, then returned with a scene about two friends who disagree over a garden gate. The scene was funny and gentle, and it belonged to the writer who had made it. The rehearsal group laughed in relief. They had been afraid that respecting privacy meant there would be no stories left. An told them imagination was not the enemy of honesty.

那天晚上，戲劇系學生問能否把信裡的故事改寫成不同結局的戲。安請他發明新的家庭和新的問題。學生抱著筆記本坐了一小時，帶回一場關於兩位朋友爭論花園大門的戲。那一幕既好笑又溫柔，屬於真正創作它的人。排練組鬆了口氣，笑成一片。他們原以為尊重隱私就沒有故事能說。安告訴他們，想像力並不是誠實的敵人。

The sister replied two days later. She thanked the team but did not wish to receive the letter. An returned it unopened to its writer. He looked sad, yet he said the choice was fair. On the walk home, Chen asked An what she would have done if the sister had changed her mind later. “We would ask again, at a time she chose,” An answered. He smiled. Their project had taught her something that was useful outside any classroom: a closed door was not always an invitation to knock harder.

兩天後，妹妹回覆了。她感謝小組，但不願收信。安將未拆封的信退還寄件人。他雖然難過，仍說這個選擇很公平。回家路上，澄問安，如果妹妹之後改變心意，她會怎麼做。「等她自己選擇時間，再詢問，」安回答。澄笑了。這個計畫教會她課堂之外的重要道理：一扇關上的門，不一定是要人更用力地敲。
