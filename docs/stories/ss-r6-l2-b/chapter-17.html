一直關著的抽屜

The Drawer That Stayed Closed

寄給明天的兩張明信片 · 第 17 / 25 章 · 1000–2000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

A local teacher asked Slow Post to help with a village letter exchange. Her students were of different ages, but many had alike interests in stories and drawing. An liked the idea because education did not depend on one grand building. Chen asked a basic question: who would manage the letters when the student team graduated? The teacher offered to take that role, but she wanted training first. Together they created a small project sheet. It explained consent, return requests, and how to store cards in a wooden drawer with a clear label.

一位在地老師邀請慢遞協助村莊的書信交換。她的學生年齡不同，卻有許多相似興趣，例如故事與繪畫。安很喜歡這個點子，因為教育並不仰賴宏偉建築。澄提出基本問題：當大學生畢業，誰來管理信件？老師願意接手，但希望先受訓。於是他們共同製作一張專案說明紙，解釋收件同意、退回申請，以及如何把卡片存放在貼有明確標籤的木抽屜裡。

A student brought a sample card and asked if the team could correct its spelling. An said they could offer help but must not change a private message without the writer's choice. Another asked for a quiz about safe delivery. Chen made three questions, each with one correct answer, and the children enjoyed playing. During the break, someone opened a bottle of soda. A parent mentioned wine from the village festival, but the teacher reminded the children that the meeting was for writing, not drinking. They returned to the lesson with warm smiles.

一名學生帶來卡片樣本，問團隊能否改正拼字。安說他們可以協助，但不能未經寄件人同意就改動私人文字。另一名孩子想玩安全投遞的小測驗。澄設計三題，每題一個正確答案，孩子們玩得很開心。休息時，有人打開一瓶汽水。家長提到村莊節慶的葡萄酒，老師提醒孩子這場聚會是為了寫信，不是喝酒。大家笑著回到課程。

The first village lesson ended with a small shock. A child had placed a public card in the private drawer by mistake. The teacher noticed it before anyone read the wrong message. Instead of scolding the child in front of the class, she asked him to help correct the labels. He chose two colors that could be recognized easily and drew a picture on each. An liked the idea but asked whether students who could not see colors would understand the system. The class added words and raised marks as well. The mistake had led to a better design because the teacher let the children take part in fixing it.

第一堂村莊課以一個小小的震驚收尾：有孩子誤把公開卡片放進私人抽屜。老師在有人錯讀訊息前發現了，沒有當眾責罵孩子，而是請他協助修正標籤。男孩選了兩種容易辨認的顏色，各畫一個圖案。安喜歡這點子，但問看不清顏色的學生能否理解。全班又加上文字與凸起標記。因為老師讓孩子參與修正，錯誤反而帶來更好的設計。

During the next class, a student asked whether he could send a letter in a language that none of the volunteers understood. An answered that he certainly could. The team did not need to read the message to deliver it safely. They only needed the date, a safe contact method, and clear permission from the people involved. The student looked pleased. He had wanted to write to his grandmother in the language they spoke at home, and now he knew he would not have to translate his private words for strangers.

下一堂課，有學生問能否使用志工都看不懂的語言寫信。安回答當然可以。團隊不需要讀懂內容也能安全送件，只需知道日期、安全聯絡方式，以及相關當事人的明確同意。學生很高興。他原本想用與祖母在家說的語言寫信，如今知道不必為陌生人翻譯私人心事。

The hardest lesson came when a boy tried to open a drawer full of other people's cards. “Suppose your friend put a letter there,” Chen said. “Would you read it because you could?” The boy shook his head. An showed him the division between public and private shelves. A wide channel on the wooden box carried returned mail to the correct folder. No special skill was needed to understand the rule. After a little practice, the boy closed the drawer himself and asked permission before touching the next card.

最難的一課發生在男孩試圖打開裝滿他人卡片的抽屜時。「假設你的朋友把信放在這裡，」澄說，「你會因為開得了就讀它嗎？」男孩搖頭。安向他展示公開與私人層架的區分。木箱上有一道寬槽，讓退回信件進入正確資料夾。理解規則不需要特殊技能。練習一會兒後，男孩自己關上抽屜，碰下一張卡之前也先問了許可。

The train home was crowded, and An had to stand beside the door. Chen asked whether she wanted his seat. She said no, because he had carried the boxes all morning. They held the same rail and watched the lights of small houses appear across the fields. An thought of the children making their own rules. She hoped the project could continue in places she would never visit. Chen said that hope was reasonable if they trained people well and accepted that other groups might change the plan. She smiled. The blue box had taught them to share not only stories, but also responsibility.

回程火車擁擠，安只能站在門邊。澄問她要不要坐他的位子。她說不用，因為他一整個上午都在搬箱子。兩人扶著同一根欄杆，看田野另一邊的小屋燈光逐一亮起。安想到孩子們正在制定自己的規則，盼望計畫能延續到她永遠不會走訪的地方。澄說，只要訓練充分，也願意讓其他團隊修改方法，這個願望便合理。安笑了。藍色郵筒教會他們分享的不只是故事，還有責任。

The teacher thanked them for showing that a simple rule could carry respect. On the train home, An told Chen she had once believed leadership meant doing the most work herself. Now she felt proud when someone else could do it safely. He agreed. “A good project should not fall apart when its first leaders leave,” he said. She rested her head on the window and watched the countryside move past. The idea made her both happy and a little sad. They were already planning for a day when their own part of Slow Post would end.

老師謝謝他們示範簡單規則也能傳達尊重。回程火車上，安告訴澄，自己以前以為領導就是親手做最多事，如今看見別人也能安全完成工作，她反而更驕傲。他同意。「好的計畫，不該在第一任負責人離開後就瓦解，」他說。安靠著窗，看鄉野風景掠過。這個想法既讓她開心，也有點傷感。他們已開始替自己終有一天離開慢遞做準備。
