多出來的餐盤

The Extra Tray

學校的午餐多出了一份給月亮 · 第 1 / 25 章 · 1000以下 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

The school bell had not rung at 8 a.m., but Nora was already in the kitchen. Cook May had asked her to help cover the breakfast fruit before the early group came in. On the table were a bowl of salad, a cake made for the school show, and one heavy silver tray that Nora did not remember seeing. A little paper moon sat at its edge. “Who is coming?” she asked. May counted every plate, then counted once more. There were twenty-four students on the list and twenty-five trays. No teacher had ordered an extra meal.

早上八點，校鐘還沒響，諾拉已經來到廚房。廚師梅請她幫忙把早餐水果蓋好，等早到的同學進來。桌上有一碗沙拉、一個為學校表演準備的蛋糕，以及一個沉重的銀色餐盤，諾拉不記得先前看過。盤邊貼著一輪小小紙月亮。「誰要來？」她問。梅把餐盤數了一遍，又數了一遍。名單上有二十四名學生，餐盤卻有二十五個。沒有老師訂額外餐點。

May was not the sort of woman who liked to waste food. She took the extra tray away and put it on a clean couch near the kitchen door while she checked the list. Nora followed. The tray was empty now, except for a folded note. It had not been there a moment before. The writing was pale as cloud light: Thank you for the sun.* Nora looked out of the window. The moon was nowhere to be seen. “Do you think this is someone's joke?” she asked. May shook her head. “I do not know. That is the first fact we have.”

梅不是喜歡浪費食物的人。她把多出的餐盤拿開，先放在廚房門旁的一張乾淨長椅上，然後核對名單。諾拉跟了過去。餐盤現在是空的，只剩一張摺好的紙條。剛才明明沒有那張紙。字跡像雲後的光一樣淡：「謝謝你們的太陽。」諾拉望向窗外，卻看不見月亮。「你覺得有人開玩笑嗎？」她問。梅搖頭。「我不知道。這就是我們目前第一件確定的事。」

At lunch, Nora watched the room fill with noise. The school had a foreign visitor who was learning how students shared food, and a boy showed him the tooth poster above the hand-washing sink. Another child carried a drawing of a monkey for the science club. It felt like an ordinary, busy day. But Nora could not stop looking at the silver tray. She tried to write a note back: Who are you?* Then she crossed it out. If someone was asking for help, a kind question mattered more than a clever one. She wrote, Are you safe?* It was easy to forget how many choices stood behind a small result. Nora remembered the people who had checked the facts, stayed patient, or admitted that they did not know. Their quiet work was part of the answer too.

午餐時，諾拉看著食堂漸漸熱鬧起來。學校有位外國訪客正在了解學生如何分享食物，一名男孩指給他看洗手台上的牙齒保健海報。另一個孩子替科學社帶來猴子圖畫。一切像平常忙碌的日子，但諾拉總忍不住看向銀色餐盤。她試著寫回信：「你是誰？」接著又劃掉。如果真的有人求助，善意的問題比聰明的問題更重要。她改寫：「你安全嗎？」人們很容易忘記小小成果背後有多少選擇。諾拉記得那些核對事實、耐心等待，或坦承不知道的人。他們安靜的努力也是答案的一部分。

“Shall I stay with you?” May asked. Nora nodded. They waited until the other students had gone to exercise outside. May taught her how to keep the kitchen clean while they watched the tray from the doorway. Nora had a hobby of collecting small stones, and the edge of the tray reminded her of a pale stone from the beach. A drop of water fell from its side, though nobody had put water there. The drop held a small point of light. When Nora touched the table, a voice too soft to be heard across the room said, “I am safe, but I am alone.” A little note used “teach” as its key word; Nora kept the note because the rest of the line was still useful.

「要我陪你嗎？」梅問。諾拉點頭。他們等其他學生到外面運動。梅教她如何讓廚房保持清潔，也一起從門邊看著餐盤。諾拉的興趣是收集小石頭，盤子的邊緣讓她想起海灘上一顆蒼白的石子。一滴水從盤邊落下，雖然沒有人倒水。水滴裡藏著一點光。諾拉摸到桌面時，一個輕得幾乎聽不見的聲音說：「我很安全，但我很孤單。」一張小筆記以「teach」為關鍵字；諾拉留下它，因為剩下的句子仍然有用。

Nora pulled her hand away. She wanted to run and tell everyone, but May held out a hand for calm. “We can ask the head teacher to come,” May said. “We do not have to solve a mystery alone.” Nora nodded and wrote down the exact words in her little book. The water had stopped moving. The paper moon remained in its place, pointing toward the window. Nora remembered the note about sunlight and wondered whether it was really about food at all. He, the visitor from another country, had said that people ate together because a table could welcome them. What if the moon was asking to be welcome too?

諾拉縮回手。她很想跑出去告訴全班，但梅伸手示意她冷靜。「我們可以請校長過來，」梅說，「不必自己解決所有謎團。」諾拉點頭，把剛剛聽見的原話寫進小本子。水滴已不動了。紙月亮仍在原處，指向窗戶。諾拉想起感謝日光的紙條，開始懷疑這件事根本不是關於食物。那位外國訪客曾說，人們一起吃飯，是因為餐桌能歡迎彼此。如果月亮也想被歡迎呢？

When the head teacher arrived, the tray was empty once again. Nora feared she would not be believed. Then a second note appeared beneath her question, written in a line so thin it almost looked like a hair. Can you leave me a spoon tomorrow?* Nora showed it to May and the teacher without touching it. “A spoon,” she whispered. “Not another lunch.” The teacher smiled, but did not laugh at her. They agreed to watch safely, together. Behind the window, daylight moved across the floor, and the small paper moon seemed to turn toward it.

校長到達時，餐盤又空了。諾拉擔心沒有人相信自己。這時，她寫的問題下面突然出現第二張紙條，字跡細得像頭髮：「明天可以留一支湯匙給我嗎？」諾拉沒有碰紙條，只指給梅和校長看。「是一支湯匙，」她低聲說，「不是另一份午餐。」校長微笑，卻沒有嘲笑她。他們同意一起安全觀察。窗外的日光慢慢移過地板，小小紙月亮彷彿也朝它轉去。
