The Wrong Name

第一次心動，是一則傳錯的訊息 · 第 1 / 25 章 · 1000以下 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

Nineteen-year-old Mina sent a message saying I like you to the wrong contact on her phone. A text message could be read again and again, yet it did not show the whole face of the person who wrote it. Mina was learning that words needed context, and feelings needed time. Leo did not answer every silence with another message. Sometimes he waited until they could speak at the library table, with books between them and no audience to please. The quiet gave Mina space to ask what she really felt. She was not ready to name it all at once, and for once that was perfectly all right. A tiny bug crossed the dry edge of the window. The camera on the counter stayed off until permission was given. One fact mattered: the group had to check before judging. Leo offered a friendly smile instead of a quick judgment. Mina wondered what a small choice today might mean for the future.

十九歲的米娜把「我喜歡你」傳到了手機裡錯誤的聯絡人。 一則訊息能反覆閱讀，卻不能呈現寫信人的完整模樣。米娜學到言語需要脈絡，感情也需要時間。里歐沒有用更多訊息填滿每次沉默，有時等到兩人能坐在圖書館桌旁、書本隔在中間，也不用取悅觀眾時才談。這份安靜讓米娜有空間問自己真正的心意。她還不急著一次說清，而這樣也完全沒關係。 一隻小蟲爬過窗邊乾燥的角落。櫃檯上的相機保持關閉，等待取得拍攝同意。有一件事實很重要：大家必須查證後再判斷。里歐給了個親切的微笑，沒有急著評斷。米娜想知道今天的小選擇會如何影響未來。

The message reached Leo, a quiet student from her library club, not Ethan from the drama club. The information was small enough to overlook. Mina nearly moved on, but Leo wanted to know what it meant for the people involved. They talked through two possibilities, then checked which parts could actually be supported. In the campus, other things were happening at the same time; it would have been easy to mix them together. 'Let's keep these separate,' Mina said. One careful question led to another, and the situation became clearer without anyone needing to raise a voice or make a dramatic accusation. A glass of juice waited at the side of the table. Nobody wanted a lie to take the place of a clear answer. The middle of the page was left blank for a new idea. A little arrow on the map pointed north. Mina read the notice by the door before making a plan.

訊息送到圖書館社的安靜學生里歐，而不是戲劇社的伊森。 這項資訊很小，幾乎會被忽略。米娜正想往下走，里歐卻想知道它對相關的人代表什麼。他們討論兩種可能，再確認哪些有證據支持。校園同時還發生其他事，很容易混淆。「先把它們分開。」米娜說。仔細的一問接著一問，不必大聲說話或戲劇性指控，情況也漸漸清楚。 桌子一側放著一杯果汁。沒有人希望謊言取代清楚的答案。頁面的中間留白，準備寫下新點子。地圖上的小箭頭指向北方。米娜先讀過門旁公告，才開始做計畫。

Mina apologized immediately and said Leo did not have to answer. The next step sounded small when Mina said it aloud. That was a good sign. A clear action was easier to try than a large promise that no one understood. Leo repeated the plan to make sure both had heard it in the same way. When one part sounded unfair, they changed the wording. The plan left space for a person to ask a question or take a break. Mina noticed a quiet sense of relief. The problem remained real, but it no longer needed to be faced through shouting, rushing, or guessing about another person's feelings. The other chair stayed free for anyone who needed it. A picture of a pet sleeping safely hung near the entrance. Someone mentioned a picnic planned for the next free afternoon. "We can reach that corner if we move carefully," Mina said. A red mark showed the part of the page still unfinished.

米娜立刻道歉，也說里歐不必回覆。 米娜把下一步說出口時，聽起來只是一件小事。這反而是好現象。明確行動比大家聽不懂的大承諾更容易嘗試。里歐重複計畫，確認兩人理解相同。有一部分聽來不公平，他們便改了說法。計畫也讓人能提問和休息。米娜感到些許輕鬆。問題依然存在，卻不必靠吵鬧、急躁或猜測別人的心思來面對。 另一張椅子留給有需要的人。入口附近掛著寵物安心睡覺的圖片。有人提到下一個空閒下午的野餐計畫。「只要小心移動，我們就能到那個角落。」米娜說。紅色記號標出頁面尚未完成的部分。

Leo replied that mistakes happen and promised not to share the message. A small mistake had brought them to the same table. What happened there was no longer an accident. Mina had chosen to ask questions, and Leo had chosen to answer without taking away her freedom. They did not agree about everything. Sometimes their tastes in books or plans for a weekend were completely different. That difference made their conversations more interesting, not less. When they said goodbye, Mina did not need to invent a perfect line. The simple words they had chosen felt true enough. A small star sticker marked the page they wanted to keep. They checked the first step and then moved to the second. "This is what we can check," Mina explained. "Let us use the notes we already have," Leo suggested. A woman near the door asked a quiet question.

里歐說誰都會犯錯，並承諾不轉傳訊息。 一個小錯誤把兩人帶到同一張桌旁，但之後發生的事已不再是意外。米娜選擇提問，里歐選擇回答，也沒有奪走她的自由。他們並非每件事都同意，有時喜歡的書或週末計畫完全不同。差異讓對話更有趣，而不是更無趣。道別時，米娜不必想出完美台詞。自己選擇的簡單話語，就已足夠真實。 他們想保存的那一頁貼著小星星貼紙。他們查好第一步，然後進入第二步。「這就是我們能查證的部分。」米娜說明。「先用手邊已有的筆記吧。」里歐提議。門邊一位女士輕聲提出問題。

After the busy part ended, Mina and Leo found a quiet minute. There were still things they wanted to say, yet neither felt a need to win the conversation. Mina asked what had been most difficult. Leo answered honestly and then asked the same question back. They were surprised to hear two different answers. That did not make either one wrong. The talk helped them see that working together did not mean thinking exactly alike. It meant taking those differences seriously enough to make a plan both could understand.

忙碌告一段落後，米娜和里歐有了片刻安靜。他們還有話想說，卻不必在對話中爭贏。米娜問最困難的是哪部分。里歐誠實回答，也反問同一個問題。兩人的答案竟然不同，卻不代表誰錯了。這場談話讓他們理解，合作不必想法完全一致，而是認真看待差異，做出雙方都能理解的計畫。

As the lights outside grew softer, Mina remembered the feeling at the start of the chapter's trouble. It had seemed larger before anyone could name it. Now it had a shape, a few facts, and a next step. That did not make it harmless, but it made it possible to face. Leo had helped without taking the whole choice away. Mina hoped to remember that balance. It was a kind of strength that did not make a loud sound, and it might be needed again very soon.

窗外的光漸漸柔和，米娜想起麻煩剛開始時的感受。那時在還沒說清楚前，事情看起來特別巨大。如今它有了形狀、幾項事實和下一步。問題不一定變得無害，卻能面對了。里歐提供幫助，卻沒有奪走米娜的選擇權。米娜想記住這種平衡。這份力量不需要大聲，也許很快又會派上用場。
