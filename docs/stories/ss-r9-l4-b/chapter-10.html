The Correction in Large Type

1994，榕灣週報的最後一頁 · 第 10 / 25 章 · 3000–4000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

After the false nuclear story, people began sending the weekly rumors of every kind. One claimed that an explosion had broken windows near an ancient tomb outside town. Another said a surgeon had forbidden visitors to enter the museum because a pregnant woman had been injured. Caiping asked for dates, names, and records before printing either report. The museum director explained that workers had installed a new display wall, making a loud noise when an old shelf fell. Nobody had been hurt. A first-aid worker had applied a bandage to a cut finger in an unrelated accident the previous week. Caiping wrote an apology to readers who had been frightened by incomplete information and promised to illustrate the difference between verified incidents and unconfirmed stories.

核廢料傳聞澄清後，民眾開始把各種消息寄到週報。一則說鎮外古墓附近發生爆炸，震碎窗戶；另一則說外科醫師禁止參觀博物館，因為有孕婦受傷。采蘋先索取日期、姓名與紀錄，再決定是否刊登。館長解釋工人正在安裝新展示牆，舊架子倒下時發出巨響，沒有人受傷。急救員前一週確曾替另一場小意外中割傷手指的人貼上繃帶。采蘋向被不完整消息嚇到的讀者道歉，也承諾用清楚示意解釋「已查證事件」與「未經證實傳聞」的差別。

The tomb itself stood beneath a hill once occupied by early settlers. A historian explained that old burial sites were records of civilization, not simply attractions to decorate a tourist map. The museum had a critical decision to make: it needed safer storage, yet construction near the graves required care. A wealthy donor offered a bargain. He would fund the work if the newspaper congratulated him on the front page and promised not to question the project. Caiping refused the condition, but she was willing to report his donation accurately. The director thanked her for treating the site seriously. No journalist should forbid improvements merely to preserve a dramatic story, he said, yet no donor should be allowed to control the record of what changed.

古墓位在一座早期移居者曾居住的山丘下。歷史學家說，舊墓地是文明紀錄，不只是觀光地圖上的裝飾。博物館正面對重要決定：儲藏空間需要改善，但墓旁施工必須謹慎。富裕捐款者提出交易：他願意出資，條件是週報要在頭版恭賀他，並承諾不質疑工程。采蘋拒絕這個條件，卻願意如實報導捐款。館長感謝她認真對待古蹟。他說，記者不應只為保留戲劇性故事就禁止改善，但捐款者也不能控制變動如何被記錄。

A student visiting from a university showed Caiping a printout from a new website maintained by the school's history department. Most people in Banyan Bay had never seen a website, and there was no computer in the newspaper office that could connect to it. The student warned that an electronic page could contain errors just as a printed one could. The department's article described a proposed resolution to protect the graves and allow limited repairs. Its language was cautious, mentioning that the settlement had changed after a long period of local revolution and reform. Caiping asked the historian to check the dates against physical records. She did not want a strange new technology to become a substitute for evidence simply because it looked modern.

一名大學生拿來大學歷史系新設網站的列印資料。榕灣多數居民從沒看過網站，報社也沒有能連線的電腦。學生提醒，電子頁面和印刷頁面一樣可能出錯。系上的文章描述一項保護墓地、允許有限修繕的決議草案，措辭審慎，提到聚落曾在漫長的地方革命與改革後改變。采蘋請歷史學家以紙本實物紀錄核對日期。她不願只因技術看起來新，就把它當成證據的替代品。

At the hospital, the surgeon mentioned in the rumor was treating patients as usual. With permission, she explained that she had advised one pregnant visitor to avoid a steep path, not ordered the entire museum closed. Her statement was short but clear. Meanwhile, the shopkeeper who had first spread the story came to offer a public apology. He was suspicious of the museum's donor, but admitted that suspicion was not proof of an accident. Caiping accepted his statement and asked him to help distribute the correction notice where the rumor had appeared. People who had shared a frightening claim could also help repair its damage. This did not excuse careless speech; it gave those responsible a useful next step rather than a place to hide.

在醫院，傳聞中的外科醫師正照常看診。獲得同意後，她解釋自己只是建議一名孕婦不要走陡峭山路，並沒有下令關閉整座博物館。說明簡短卻明確。同時，最先傳話的店主到報社公開道歉。他仍懷疑博物館捐款者的動機，但承認懷疑不是事故證據。采蘋接受聲明，並請他在先前傳播消息的地方協助分發更正。曾散播恐慌的人，也能參與修復傷害。這不表示輕率說話可以被原諒，而是給責任者一個有用的下一步。

The next issue carried a full-page explanation with a timeline, a drawing of the fallen shelf, and the hospital's permitted statement. Shiming worried that a mere correction would never sell as well as a disaster. Caiping replied that their purpose was not to bargain with fear. It was to give readers information they could use without passing on a false alarm. To her surprise, several families bought extra copies for elderly neighbors who did not come to town often. The paper was still far from prosperous, and its bills remained serious. But the correction had done something no advertisement could guarantee: it let readers compare the original rumor with the evidence that replaced it. For the first time, Caiping felt that the weekly's mistakes might become part of its value, if they were never hidden.

下一期用整頁刊出時間線、倒下的架子圖，與獲准引用的醫院聲明。世明擔心單純更正永遠賣不過災難消息。采蘋回答，報社不是要拿恐懼討價還價，而是給讀者可使用、又不會繼續散播假警報的資訊。令她意外的是，有些家庭多買幾份，送給不常進城的年長鄰居。週報仍遠稱不上繁榮，帳單也依舊沉重。但更正做到了廣告無法保證的事：讓讀者能把原本的傳聞與新證據放在一起比較。采蘋第一次覺得，只要不隱藏，週報的錯誤也可能成為它的價值之一。
