沒有鑰匙的檔案櫃

The Cabinet Without a Key

1994，榕灣週報的最後一頁 · 第 12 / 25 章 · 3000–4000 · 英中對照 · 校訂中

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

The oldest archive cabinet in the weekly stood beside a wooden bin of rejected printing plates. One afternoon its lock was broken. A silver bracelet and a bundle of letters were missing from a drawer used to store reader donations. Xiaoyu immediately called the intruder a burglar, but Caiping reminded her that they did not know who had entered. The police agent who came to inspect the room found no definite sign of forced entry through the back door. Someone might have used a spare key, or the lock might have failed. The destruction was real; the identity of the person responsible was not. Shiming looked devastated. He had protected those papers through floods, inflation, and years of hardship, and he believed the missing bundle held the story of the weekly's earliest readers.

週報最老的檔案櫃放在一箱淘汰鉛版旁。某天下午，櫃鎖被弄壞，一條銀手鍊和一束信件從讀者捐贈抽屜中不見了。小雨立刻說闖入者是竊賊，采蘋提醒她還不知道誰進來過。到場查看的警員在後門找不到明確撬門痕跡。也許有人用備用鑰匙，也可能只是鎖壞了。破壞確實發生，責任者身分卻還未知。世明十分難過。他在洪水、通貨膨脹與艱難歲月中保護這些紙張，相信失去的那束信記錄著週報最初的讀者。

Among the letters was a collection about a tribal community that had lived in the hills before the modern town was founded. A visiting cultural researcher warned that these were not objects the newspaper could simply claim to own because they had been stored in its office. Some accounts had been shared for a particular purpose, and their descendants might wish to review them before publication. Caiping invited representatives chosen by the community to examine the remaining records. She asked them what the paper could legally and respectfully retain. One older woman said the weekly had once been a faithful messenger for her family but had also printed a name without permission. "A newspaper can remember us," she said, "and still get us wrong." Her comment changed the way Caiping understood the theft.

信件中有一批談到山區部族社群的資料；那些社群在現代城鎮成立前就住在此地。來訪的文化研究者提醒，資料寄存在報社，不代表報社便能自稱擁有。部分故事是為特定用途分享的，後代可能希望在公開前重新檢視。采蘋請社群自行推選代表查看剩餘紀錄，詢問週報可以合法、也尊重當事人地保留什麼。一名長者說，週報曾忠實替家人傳話，卻也曾未經同意印出姓名。「報紙可以記得我們，卻還是會寫錯我們。」她說。這句話改變了采蘋對失竊事件的理解。

On the second day a volunteer confessed that she had carried the letters out for drying after noticing dampness in the cabinet. She had meant to return them, but a relative had taken the silver bracelet to a jeweler for repair without asking. This did not make the broken lock acceptable. It did, however, explain how objects had left the building without a planned theft. Caiping asked every volunteer to commit to a clear procedure: every borrowed item would have a dated form, a named keeper, and written permission from its owner. The volunteer said she had acted to save the papers. Caiping believed her, but replied that kindness without a record could endanger the very things it hoped to protect. The papers came back on Tuesday, except for one letter whose corner had torn during transport.

第二天，一名志工承認，她發現櫃內潮濕後，把信件帶出去晾乾，原本打算歸還；家人卻未經同意把銀手鍊拿去珠寶店修理。這不表示弄壞櫃鎖可以接受，但也解釋了物品如何在沒有預謀偷竊的情況下離開。采蘋要求每位志工承諾遵守明確程序：借出的每件物品都要有日期、保管人與所有者的書面同意。志工說自己只是想救資料。采蘋相信她，仍回答：沒有紀錄的好意，可能危及原本想保護的東西。信件星期二歸還，只有一封在搬運時撕破了角。

The torn letter described the tragic loss of a fishing boat long ago. Caiping thought of turning it into a memorial feature, but the descendant who had donated it asked her to wait. Her grandfather had spoken about the accident only in private, and his fame among fishermen did not make every family sorrow public. The editor agreed. She would pursue permission before publication, rather than treat the archive as a mine of dramatic material. Meanwhile, a carpenter offered a durable replacement cabinet with separate compartments and a sign-in sheet. It would occupy valuable floor space, so Shiming asked whether the paper could afford it. Caiping checked the budget and suggested sharing the cost with the public library, which also needed dry storage.

撕破角的信提到很久以前一艘漁船遇難的悲劇。采蘋想做追憶專題，但捐贈信件的後代請她先等等。祖父生前只在私下談過意外，漁民間的名聲不代表所有家族悲傷都能公開。編輯同意先取得許可再報導，不把檔案當成戲劇素材的礦藏。另一方面，木工願意製作耐用的新櫃，設獨立隔間和借出紀錄表。它會占去珍貴地面空間，世明問報社是否負擔得起。采蘋核對預算，提議和同樣需要乾燥儲藏空間的公共圖書館共同分擔。

By the end of the week the archive had emerged from disorder with more careful rules, but Caiping knew an administrative solution was not enough. She visited the older woman's community again and asked what should happen to letters whose authors could no longer be contacted. The answer was not immediate. Some families wanted the paper to hold copies, others preferred the library, and others wished to retain the originals themselves. Caiping agreed to keep those options open. Her father found this slow process exhausting. She told him that an era of care could not begin by assuming every past decision was correct. The weekly printed an account of the damaged cabinet, the returned items, and the new rules, while leaving private contents out of the story.

一週結束時，檔案室已從混亂中建立更細緻的規則，但采蘋知道行政程序還不夠。她再次拜訪長者所在的社群，詢問作者已無法聯絡的信件該如何處理。答案無法立即定案。有些家庭願由報社保管副本，有些偏好圖書館，也有人希望自己留下正本。采蘋同意保留各種選擇。父親覺得過程緩慢又費神。她說，新的照護時代不能從假定過去所有決定都正確開始。週報報導了損壞的檔案櫃、歸還物品與新規則，卻沒有公開私人信件內容。
