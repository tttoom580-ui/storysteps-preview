空轉的印刷機

The Empty Press

1994，榕灣週報的最後一頁 · 第 1 / 25 章 · 3000–4000 · 英中對照

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

In March 1994, Wu Caiping returned to Banyan Bay with a suitcase, a broken umbrella, and one letter she had not answered. Her father had written that the weekly newspaper would close after its next issue. Their family lived in a small cottage behind the printing room, where a flat cushion protected his aching back from a hard chair. Outside, a fierce wind sent pages rolling along the road. Caiping caught one and recognized a photograph she had taken at sixteen. The picture showed market sellers, fishermen, and children from different families. That diversity had once been the paper's strength. Now the front page carried nothing but an advertisement for a property company. It did not convey what the town was living through, and nobody could tell her who had approved the change.

一九九四年三月，吳采蘋提著行李箱、壞掉的雨傘，以及一封始終沒回覆的信回到榕灣。父親在信中說，地方週報再出一期就要停刊了。全家住在印刷間後方的小屋，父親坐的硬椅子上放著扁坐墊，以減輕背痛。屋外強風把紙頁吹得沿街翻滾。采蘋撿起一張，認出那是自己十六歲時拍的照片：市場攤販、漁民，還有來自不同家庭的孩子。這份多樣性原本是週報的力量。如今頭版只剩一家地產公司的廣告，沒有傳達鎮民真正面對的生活，也沒人說得清是誰同意換版的。

Her father, Wu Shiming, offered an immediate defense of the decision. The sponsor had paid for paper, ink, and electricity; without it, there would be no issue at all. Caiping knew his argument was not foolish. The family owed six months of rent on the printing equipment, and her mother's hands shook after long shifts setting type. Her younger cousin Xiaoyu tried to flatter her: a genius editor from the capital could solve everything before Friday. Caiping heard her mother's short giggle from the doorway. "A genius who cannot add the wages is useless," her mother said. They began with a reference folder containing every bill and receipt. Caiping was pessimistic about the numbers, but being pessimistic did not mean surrendering to an arrangement she had not examined.

父親吳世明立刻為換版辯護：贊助商付了紙張、油墨與電費，沒有這筆錢，這期報紙根本出不了。采蘋知道這並非毫無道理。家裡欠了六個月的印刷設備租金，母親長時間排字後，雙手也會發抖。表妹小雨想奉承她，說從省城回來的天才編輯一定能在星期五前解決一切。母親在門邊輕笑：「不會算工資的天才一點用也沒有。」他們先打開一個參考資料夾，裡面存著所有帳單與收據。采蘋對數字相當悲觀，但悲觀不代表要接受一項尚未查清的安排。

At noon the sponsor's representative arrived with a visual presentation made from colored charts. He promised tremendous growth if the newspaper abandoned dull local stories and reported only festivals, sports, and attractive scenery. He even pointed to a planned parachute demonstration above the beach and a charity workout organized by visiting athletes. Those events could be real news, Caiping said, but readers also needed the bus timetable, school notices, and reports on the damaged riverbank. The representative replied that the town possessed enough troubles without printing them. Caiping asked whether his contract allowed him to remove unfavorable reports. He showed her a page that did exactly that. It called editorial silence a condition of financial support.

中午，贊助商代表帶著彩色圖表做成的視覺簡報來訪。他保證，只要週報放棄沉悶的地方消息，改報節慶、運動和美景，就會迎來驚人成長。他還指出海灘將有跳傘表演，外地運動員也要舉辦慈善體能活動。采蘋說，這些確實可以是新聞，但讀者同樣需要公車時刻、學校公告，以及河岸毀損的報導。代表回答，這座鎮的麻煩已經夠多，不必再印出來。采蘋問合約是否容許他刪除負面新聞；他拿出的條款正好允許，還把編輯沉默稱作財務支持的條件。

The argument brought a witness to the door: Mrs. Luo, a retired teacher who had come to collect an announcement about a missing school instrument. She had watched the representative take the newspaper's old river map off the wall. When Caiping asked why, he said it was merely decoration. Mrs. Luo answered that the map showed the last safe crossing after heavy rain. Removing it without preserving a copy would be a violation of the town's public record rules, which the weekly had promised to follow. She did not threaten anyone. She simply asked for a receipt identifying the person who had taken it. The representative returned the map. For the first time that day, Caiping saw a small action that might protect the paper better than a grand speech.

爭論時，退休教師羅太太來到門口，正要領回一則尋找學校樂器的公告，也成了現場見證人。她看見代表從牆上取下舊河道圖。采蘋問起原因，他說那只是裝飾。羅太太指出，那張圖標示了大雨後最後一處安全通道；不留副本就拿走，違反週報承諾遵守的地方公共紀錄規則。她沒有威脅任何人，只要求取走者開收據、留下姓名。代表於是把地圖放回。采蘋第一次發現，一個小小的行動可能比宏大的演說更能守住報紙。

That evening her father spoke about the spiritual meaning of a paper that had carried birth announcements and death notices for twenty-nine years. Caiping listened, then told him that memories could not pay a printer or repair a broken machine. She had left the city after publishing an inaccurate report; she knew how an editor's certainty could harm a family. She would not claim moral superiority merely because she had returned home. Instead, she proposed one trial issue, paid for by small subscriptions and clearly marked advertisements. Every correction would appear where readers could find it, not in a hidden corner. Her father stared at the empty press and said that one issue was not enough to save a business. "No," she said. "It is enough to find out whether we deserve another."

那晚，父親談起這張報紙二十九年來刊登出生喜訊與訃聞的精神意義。采蘋聽完才說，回憶付不了印刷工資，也修不好機器。她當年離開城市報社，就是因為刊出過一篇不準確的報導；她知道編輯的自信也可能傷害別人。她不會只因返鄉就宣稱自己比較高尚。她提議先試印一期，以小額訂閱和清楚標示的廣告支付成本，所有更正都放在讀者容易看見的位置。父親望著空轉的機器，說一期救不了生意。「沒錯，」她回答，「但足以知道我們值不值得再出下一期。」

Before sleeping, Caiping wrote five rules on the back of a used proof sheet. Reporters would name their sources when permission was given, distinguish observation from rumor, show corrections, count every paid hour, and let a subject refuse an interview. Xiaoyu suggested adding a sixth: nobody should confuse a newspaper with a family treasure that must be protected at any price. Caiping agreed. In the city she had learned that a paper could look brave while hiding its own mistakes. In Banyan Bay she would try something harder: making the method visible even when the result was embarrassing. She placed the rules beside the press, under the old photograph. Tomorrow they would ask readers what questions still needed answers, rather than announce what readers were supposed to believe.

睡前，采蘋在用過的校樣背面寫下五條規則：取得同意才公開消息來源、區分觀察與傳聞、公開更正、計算每一小時的有薪工作，以及讓受訪者有權拒訪。小雨提議再加一條：不要把報紙當成不惜代價也要守住的家傳寶物。采蘋同意。她在城市學到，報紙表面上可以很勇敢，卻悄悄藏起自己的錯誤。回到榕灣，她想做更難的事：即使結果難堪，也讓查證的方法被看見。她把規則放在印刷機旁、老照片下方。明天，他們要問讀者還有哪些問題需要回答，而不是先告訴讀者應該相信什麼。
