A Name in the Wrong Place

1994，榕灣週報的最後一頁 · 第 5 / 25 章 · 3000–4000 · 英中對照

用互動版查詞、朗讀與收藏

A spring festival filled the harbor with banners, music, and the sharp smell of salt. The youth orchestra would perform at noon, and a canoe race would begin when the tide turned favorable. Xiaoyu carried a portable recorder to collect short interviews. She also brought a small device for enlarging photographs in the darkroom, hoping to enlarge a clean image of the winning team. Caiping had warned her not to abandon her notes in the excitement. Names mattered as much as the finish line. A local teenage paddler, Chen Lifen, explained that her team trained before school because the public allowance for equipment covered only part of their costs. She spoke with an imaginative clarity that made Xiaoyu write down every word.

春日慶典讓港口掛滿旗幟、響起音樂，空氣中帶著濃烈鹹味。青年管弦樂團中午演出，獨木舟競賽則要等潮水轉好才開始。小雨帶著可攜式錄音機採訪短訪，也帶上暗房放大照片用的裝置，希望把優勝隊伍的照片放大得清楚。采蘋提醒她不要因興奮而丟下筆記；名字跟終點一樣重要。當地少年划手陳麗芬說，隊伍得在上學前練習，因為公共器材補助只夠負擔部分費用。她說話充滿想像力又清楚，小雨把每句話都記下來。

That night Xiaoyu made an adjustment to the page layout but failed to adjust the caption beneath the photograph. The picture of Lifen appeared with the name of another paddler, a boy whose father was a well-known festival sponsor. By morning the paper had made two distinct mistakes: the winner was not identified, and the wrong child had been credited. Lifen's coach raised an objection, while her mother came to the office with visible anxiety. The sponsor's secretary proposed a quick solution: leave the page alone, because the boy's fame might help the paper's prosperity. Caiping refused. The photograph belonged to Lifen's achievement, not to an advertising arrangement. She called both families and offered a correction that would be as large and clear as the original caption.

當晚，小雨調整了版面，卻忘記同步調整照片下方的說明。麗芬的照片配上另一名划手的名字；那男孩的父親是知名的慶典贊助商。隔天早上，報紙造成兩種不同錯誤：真正的優勝者沒有被寫出，另一個孩子卻得到不屬於自己的榮譽。麗芬的教練提出異議，母親也滿臉焦慮地來到報社。贊助商祕書提議別改，因為男孩的名氣可能有助於報紙繁榮。采蘋拒絕了。照片記錄的是麗芬的成就，不是廣告交易。她聯絡兩個家庭，提出與原本說明同樣醒目的更正。

The boy's grandmother surprised them. She said that her grandson had never wanted the false title and that keeping it would only cause more impact when the truth emerged. The boy himself arrived carrying the race list. "Please don't make me an idol for a thing I didn't do," he said. Xiaoyu began to protest that she had worked all night, but Caiping stopped her. Hard work could explain a mistake; it could not erase it. The grandmother offered a familiar idiom about a boat that must be steered while the current is changing. Caiping smiled, then asked her to repeat the final syllable because the local pronunciation was unfamiliar. The newspaper would not print a traditional saying inaccurately just to sound wise.

男孩的祖母卻讓所有人意外。她說孫子從沒想要這個假的頭銜；若繼續保留，真相出現時傷害反而更大。男孩自己也帶著賽事名單來了：「拜託不要因為我沒做過的事，把我當偶像。」小雨剛想抗議自己熬夜工作，采蘋就讓她停下。辛苦能解釋錯誤，不能抹去錯誤。祖母引用當地方言的成語，意思是水流變動時仍要掌穩船舵。采蘋笑了，卻請她重複最後一個音節，因為自己不熟悉當地發音。週報不能為了看起來有學問，就把傳統說法印錯。

When the correction went up at the office window, a few adults gathered to protest the sponsor's influence over youth competitions. The sponsor insisted that nobody had ordered the mistake. Caiping said his statement would be printed, but so would the production record showing where the caption had changed. No one was allowed to seize that record or hide it. The error was Xiaoyu's, not proof of a secret plot; nevertheless, the secretary's suggestion to leave the false credit standing was a legitimate subject for scrutiny. The correction named the true paddler, apologized to the other child, and explained how captions would be checked before printing. Lifen asked for one additional line about her whole team. Caiping agreed after confirming the names of every member.

更正貼在報社櫥窗後，有些大人聚集抗議贊助商對青少年比賽的影響。贊助商強調沒人指示報社犯錯。采蘋說，這項說明可以刊出，但記錄照片說明更動過程的製作單也要公開，不許任何人拿走或藏起。錯誤確實出自小雨，不能據此斷言有陰謀；然而祕書主張保留錯誤功勞，仍值得審視。更正寫出真正划手、向另一位孩子道歉，也解釋往後如何核對照片說明。麗芬希望再加上一行介紹整個隊伍；采蘋確認全員姓名後答應了。

At supper Xiaoyu admitted she had imagined that writing for a newspaper meant chasing extraordinary events. She had not expected the hardest part to be reading a name twice. Caiping told her that journalism also required ordinary care, and that the people in a photograph were not decorations. Her mother had listened silently, worrying that each correction would make the weekly appear weak. But when Lifen's team stopped by with a thank-you note, she understood that an honest correction might build trust instead of destroying it. Caiping placed the note in a separate drawer, not among the payment receipts. Gratitude was welcome, but it was not income. The newspaper would need customers willing to pay for accuracy, even when accuracy required an extra page of ink.

晚餐時，小雨承認，她曾以為報社工作就是追逐非凡事件，沒想到最困難的竟是把名字讀兩遍。采蘋告訴她，新聞工作也需要平凡的細心，照片裡的人不是裝飾品。母親一直默默聽著，擔心每次更正都讓週報顯得軟弱。然而，麗芬全隊送來感謝字條時，她明白誠實更正可能建立信任，而不一定摧毀信任。采蘋把字條收在另一個抽屜，不和付款收據放在一起。感謝令人高興，卻不是收入。週報仍得找到願意為準確內容付費的讀者，即使那代表多花一整頁的油墨。
