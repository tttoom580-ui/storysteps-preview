石頭與船

The Stone and the Ship

1994，榕灣週報的最後一頁 · 第 2 / 25 章 · 3000–4000 · 英中對照

The first reader meeting brought seven people, two broken umbrellas, and a question Caiping had not expected: why had the closed paper mill been described as empty land? Mr. Gao, who had worked there for thirty years, brought a photograph of a school exhibit. On one table a child had built a model of an atom; beside it was a drawing of an atomic clock borrowed from a science magazine. Other children had arranged fossil samples beside stones from the old coastal fort. Their teacher wanted a story showing the combination of science lessons and local history. The sponsor's map, however, labeled the mill and the fort as unused property. Gao's gaze remained fixed on the blank squares. "A landscape does not become empty because its workers lose their jobs," he said.

第一次讀者會議只來了七人、帶來兩把破雨傘，還問了采蘋一個沒料到的問題：為什麼報紙把關閉的造紙廠稱作空地？在那裡工作三十年的高先生帶來一張學校展覽照片。桌上有學生製作的原子模型，旁邊是從科學雜誌臨摹的原子鐘圖。其他孩子把化石標本和舊海防堡壘旁的石頭排在一起。老師希望寫一篇結合科學課與地方史的報導。然而贊助商的地圖把造紙廠與堡壘標成未使用土地。高先生緊盯著那些空白方格：「工人失業了，不代表這片土地就從來沒人生活過。」

Caiping assigned Xiaoyu to find the mill's original records. She warned that a delicate claim needed more than one witness and could not be guaranteed by an impressive old photograph. The extent of the site's contamination was also unknown. A former employee, Mrs. Tran, explained that immigration had brought her family to the bay fifteen years earlier. Her memories of the mill were intimate: a locker painted yellow, a lunch bell, a neighbor who taught her written Chinese. She did not want those details made public without consent. The room fell quiet, except for the low murmur of the ceiling fan. Caiping promised that the story would retain the workers' own words where they wished to be quoted, and would not turn their private lives into an exhibit.

采蘋派小雨去找造紙廠原始紀錄，並提醒她：細緻的主張不能只靠一名證人，再厲害的舊照片也無法保證正確。廠地污染的程度也還不清楚。前員工陳太太說，十五年前家人因移民來到榕灣。她對造紙廠的回憶很私密：黃色置物櫃、午餐鐘聲，以及教她寫中文字的鄰居。沒有同意，她不想公開這些細節。房裡只剩吊扇的低聲嗡鳴。采蘋答應只在工人願意被引用時保留他們原來的話，不會把私生活變成展覽品。

On Tuesday a messenger from the harbor delivered shipping lists covering the mill's final decade. The records described each voyage that brought wood pulp across the water. Some shipments were ordinary; others had missing labels. The lists could show what arrived, but not whether any dangerous material remained in the ground. Caiping took them to a retired engineer, who refused to guess. He suggested contacting a university laboratory and checking the old drainage plans. A productive interview, he said, was not necessarily one that produced a dramatic answer. It was one that revealed what must still be measured. Caiping revised the article's title from "Poison Beneath Our Feet" to "What We Know About the Closed Mill." She knew the second headline was less exciting, but it matched the evidence.

星期二，港口信差送來造紙廠最後十年的運貨清單。紀錄列出每趟運送木漿的航程，有些貨物很普通，另一些卻缺少標籤。清單能證明有哪些物品運抵，卻無法證明地下是否仍有危險物質。采蘋把資料交給退休工程師，他拒絕猜測，建議聯絡大學實驗室並查核舊排水圖。他說，有成效的訪談不一定要得到驚人答案，能指出下一步該測量什麼，也很有價值。采蘋把標題從「毒物埋在腳下」改為「已知的廢棄造紙廠資料」。第二個標題不那麼刺激，卻更符合證據。

Mrs. Tran later asked why the editor cared so much about an abandoned building. Caiping said the issue was not the building alone. The newspaper's words might affect whether old workers could ask for a fair review of their health records. They might also cause unnecessary psychological fear if a rumor was printed as fact. Neither effect could be erased with a small apology. Mrs. Tran considered this and offered one public memory: the mill whistle had once guided fishing boats home through fog. She wanted her grandson to know that useful work had happened there, even if the mill never reopened. She allowed Caiping to print that sentence and nothing about her family. The editor wrote the permission on paper, dated it, and let Mrs. Tran keep a copy.

後來，陳太太問采蘋為何如此在意一棟廢棄建物。采蘋說問題不只是建築本身。報紙文字可能影響老工人能否要求公平檢查健康紀錄；若把傳聞當事實，也可能造成沒有必要的心理恐懼。任何一種影響都不是角落裡的小小道歉能消除的。陳太太想了想，願意公開一段記憶：當年的造紙廠汽笛曾在濃霧中引導漁船回家。她希望孫子知道，這裡曾有值得記得的工作，即使造紙廠再也不開。她只允許印這句話，不公開家人細節。采蘋把同意記在紙上，寫下日期，讓她保留副本。

That evening the printing room seemed smaller than before. Her father wanted a clear answer about the mill because the paper had only six pages, while Xiaoyu wanted to keep every moving story. Caiping said an editor must decide both what to print and what to leave uncertain. They placed the laboratory questions in a box at the end of the article and invited qualified readers to reply by letter. The project was modest, but it had a shape: historical records on one side, current evidence on the other, and explicit gaps between them. When Shiming read the draft, he said it sounded like an unfinished conversation. "It is," Caiping replied. "That is why the next issue might matter." The press began its slow mechanical beat after midnight, carrying their first careful questions into town.

那晚，印刷間似乎比從前更小。父親因為報紙只有六頁，希望造紙廠報導能給出明確答案；小雨則想把每段動人故事全留下。采蘋說，編輯既要決定印什麼，也得決定哪些事必須保留未知。他們把實驗室問題放在文章末尾的小框裡，邀請有專業知識的讀者來信。計畫雖小，結構卻清楚：一邊是歷史紀錄，一邊是當前證據，中間明白標示尚未查清之處。世明讀完草稿，說這像一場沒說完的對話。「的確，」采蘋回答，「所以下一期才可能有價值。」午夜過後，印刷機緩緩響起，把那些審慎的問題送往小鎮。
