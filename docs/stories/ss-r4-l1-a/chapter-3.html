海灘邊的路

The Road by the Beach

On Wednesday, Ada learned about the road from the station to the beach. Mr. Lee said the road was far from town, but the walk was safe in good weather. A little bird sat on a sign near the bridge. A cow stood on the other side of a fence. Ada cleaned the dust from her shoes before entering the first house. The owner had been born in this town and had lived there for nearly every year of her life. She wanted Ada to leave the mail on a small table, not by the open door.

星期三，艾達學會了從車站到海灘的路。李先生說那裡離鎮上遠，但天氣好時走路安全。一隻小鳥停在橋旁的路牌上，圍欄另一側站著一頭牛。艾達進第一間屋前，先把鞋上的灰塵擦乾淨。屋主出生在這個鎮，幾乎一生都住在這裡。她希望艾達把信放在小桌上，不要放在敞開的門旁。

At the next bend, a strong wind began to blow. Mr. Lee asked Ada to pull her hat down and follow the white line by the side of the road. “Do not go near the water,” he said. They crossed the bridge slowly. Ada watched the water move into the river below. Nothing bad happened, but she understood why he had shown her the safe path first. At the far end, they met a man who asked about a letter for his daughter. Ada checked his name and the house number before handing it over.

到下一個彎道時，強風吹了起來。李先生要艾達拉低帽子，跟著道路一側的白線走。「不要靠近水邊。」他說。兩人慢慢過橋。艾達看見水流進橋下的河裡。沒有發生壞事，但她明白李先生為何先教安全路線。橋的遠端，一名男子詢問寄給女兒的信。艾達核對姓名和門牌後，才把信交給他。

Mara was waiting at the public map board by the station. She had followed an old walking route and found only a field. “It may be the wrong place,” she said. Ada did not tell her to be cheerful. Instead, she listened. Mara said her mother had drawn waves beside the words Willow Road. “Maybe the drawing meant the river, or maybe it meant the sea,” she said. Mr. Lee showed them where the river had once met the beach. The map was old, so he asked them to check the date before making a new guess.

瑪拉在車站旁的公開地圖板等著。她循著一條舊步道走，卻只找到一片田地。「可能找錯地方了。」她說。艾達沒有叫她打起精神，只是聽她說。瑪拉說，母親曾在「柳樹路」旁畫上波浪。「也許畫的是河，也許是海。」她說。李先生指出河流從前通往海灘的位置。地圖很舊，他請兩人先看日期，再作推測。

They went to a small cafe for water and a rest. There were children there, drawing maps of their own street. One girl had put a blue line across a school garden and called it a sea. Mara smiled at her. “A drawing can tell a story,” she said, “but we must ask what the person meant.” Ada wrote that sentence in her notebook. She thought it was a good rule for her work as well. A name, a number, and a date could mean more than a pretty picture on an old envelope.

他們到小咖啡館喝水休息。幾個孩子正在畫自己街道的地圖。一個女孩在校園花圃上畫了條藍線，說那是海。瑪拉對她微笑。「圖畫會說故事，」她說，「但我們仍要問畫的人想表達什麼。」艾達把這句話記進筆記。她覺得這也適用於郵務工作。姓名、號碼與日期，有時比舊信封上的漂亮圖案更重要。

Before leaving, Ada helped an older man find the right road to the station. The man had stopped at a sign that looked new, yet the name on it was old. She did not simply point and walk away. She went with him to the next crossing, where the route was clear. That small act took only a few minutes. When they said goodbye, the man asked if the post office would always be open. Ada answered, “I hope so, but I do not make that choice alone.” It was the first time she heard that question.

離開前，艾達幫一位老人找去車站的正確道路。老人停在一面新路牌前，但上面寫的是舊地名。艾達沒有只指個方向就走，而是陪他到下一個清楚的路口。這件事只花幾分鐘。道別時，老人問郵局是不是會一直開著。艾達說：「我希望會，但不是我一個人能決定。」這是她第一次聽到這樣的問題。

A shop worker came out with a clean cloth and offered to wipe the wet table by the road. Ada thanked her and asked where to put a letter that was too large for the little box. The woman showed her a dry place inside the shop. “Did anything happen to the box in the last rain?” Ada asked. The woman said no, but one customer had left one shoe there by mistake. Ada smiled. She checked the box and wrote a note about its size. The road had taught her one more practical thing about keeping mail dry.

一名店員拿著乾淨抹布出來，幫忙擦路旁潮濕的桌子。艾達道謝，問太大的信該放哪裡。店員指著店內乾燥處。「上次下雨時，箱子有發生什麼事嗎？」艾達問。店員說沒有，只是一位客人誤放了一隻鞋在旁邊。艾達笑了，檢查箱子，再記下它的尺寸。這條路又教會她一件保持郵件乾燥的實用小事。

That night she looked again at her route book. There were houses with new numbers, roads with old names, and people who needed more than a quick delivery. She made three clear notes for tomorrow. First, check the public dates on the maps. Second, leave private letters closed. Third, give a person time to ask a question. She could not solve every problem on the same day. Still, the road had brought her closer to the people who lived beside it, and she felt ready to go again.

那天晚上，她再翻開路線簿。鎮上有換了號碼的房子、有仍用舊名的道路，也有需要的不只是快速送信的人。她為明天寫了三條清楚的提醒：第一，核對公開地圖的日期；第二，不打開私人信件；第三，讓對方有時間提出問題。她不可能一天解決所有問題。然而這條路讓她更認識住在沿途的人，她願意再走一次。
