藍色的門

The Blue Door

The weather turned cold, and Ada found a blue car beside the office with a flat tire. A nurse stood near it wearing warm pants and holding a bag. “I need to be at the temple before noon,” she said. “I cook for the older people there on Fridays.” Ada could not fix the car herself, but she could call the repair shop. Mr. Lee asked whether the woman needed a safe ride first. A driver from the next street offered to help. The nurse thanked them, then checked that her bag of food was still under the car roof.

天氣轉冷，艾達發現郵局旁有輛藍色汽車爆胎。一位穿著保暖長褲的護理師站在旁邊，手提袋子。「我中午前得去寺廟，」她說，「星期五我會為那裡的長者煮飯。」艾達不會自己修車，但可以打電話給修車行。李先生先問護理師是否需要安全的接送。隔壁街的司機願意幫忙。護理師向他們道謝，又確認食物袋仍在車棚下。

Inside the post office, a fan made a soft sound near the window. The office secretary had sent a letter with a thousand questions, or so it seemed to Ada. In fact there were only six. The first sentence asked how many people came to the office after lunch. Another asked how often workers went to houses that were far from town. Ada opened her dictionary for one word she did not know. Mr. Lee smiled. “Maybe this letter looks hard,” he said, “but we can answer what we know. We do not need to guess the past.”

郵局裡，窗邊的電風扇輕輕作響。辦公室秘書寄來一封看似有上千個問題的信，至少艾達覺得如此。實際只有六題。第一句問午餐後有多少人到郵局，另一題問工作人員多久去一次偏遠住家。艾達翻字典查不懂的詞。李先生笑了。「這封信也許看起來很難，」他說，「但我們可以回答知道的事，不必猜以前的數字。」

Mara spent the morning at the library and came back with a public photograph of a tree beside a blue door. “I think this is the old house,” she said. Ada asked where the photograph came from. Mara showed the book and the date. It was from twenty years ago. The old street name was there, but the house number was not. “We are probably looking at the right road,” Ada said, “but we still cannot be certain of the house.” Mara nodded. She put the photograph in a clean pocket, away from the rain.

瑪拉早上待在圖書館，回來時帶著一張公開照片：樹旁有扇藍門。「我想這就是舊房子。」她說。艾達問照片來自哪裡。瑪拉拿出書和日期。那是二十年前的照片，有舊街名，卻沒有門牌。「我們大概找對路了，」艾達說，「但還不能確定房子。」瑪拉點頭，把照片放進乾淨袋子，避免淋雨。

At lunch the nurse returned to thank the driver. She told Ada that some older people could not come to town when the weather was bad. They needed medicine and letters, but they could not always walk under the cold rain. Ada asked if the nurse could write a short note about the public service problem without naming patients. “I can,” she said. “I will only speak about the work, not the people.” The note would help Ada show the city office why afternoon hours could matter in a small town.

午餐時，護理師回來向司機道謝。她告訴艾達，天氣差時，有些長者無法進城。他們需要藥品與信件，卻不能總在冷雨中走路。艾達問護理師能否寫一段不提病人姓名的說明，談公共服務遇到的問題。「可以，」她說，「我只談工作，不談個人。」這段文字能幫艾達向城市辦公室說明，小鎮下午營業時間為何重要。

In the evening, Ada took a walk by the big tree on her own street. The leaves were moving, and a cat slept under a bench. She thought about the blue door in Mara's photograph. A color could help them ask better questions, but it could not prove where a person once lived. She thought about the nurse as well. Helping her find a ride had been simple; writing a good report would take more care. Ada went home and made a table with dates, requests, and the work done. She left every private name out.

傍晚，艾達走到自己街上的大樹旁。樹葉搖動，一隻貓睡在長椅下。她想著瑪拉照片中的藍門。顏色能幫她們提出更好的問題，卻不能證明一個人從前住在哪裡。她也想到護理師。替她找車很簡單，寫好報告則更需要細心。艾達回家，畫出一張包含日期、需求與完成工作的表格，把私人姓名全部省略。

The office secretary called near the end of the day. She had found a public list of old road names, but it did not include house owners. Ada thanked her and asked where the list could be seen. The secretary said it was on the town board, and she gave the opening hours. Ada wrote them down for Mara. She did not ask the woman to make a special search through private records. The simple answer was enough for the next step. When Mara came back, Ada handed her the information and let her choose when to visit.

快下班時，辦公室秘書打電話來，說找到一份公開舊街名表，但裡面沒有屋主資訊。艾達道謝，詢問在哪裡能查看。秘書說資料在鎮上公告板，並告訴她開放時間。艾達替瑪拉記下，沒有要求秘書另外查私人紀錄。這個簡單答案已足夠作為下一步。瑪拉回來後，艾達把資訊交給她，讓她自己決定何時去看。

The next morning she would show the table to Mr. Lee. She was ready to be wrong about a number and correct it, but she did not want to be careless with people's lives. The office was small, and the city might see only a few rows on a page. Ada wanted each row to tell the truth. Mara's red envelope waited in the drawer where she had chosen to keep it. Both women were looking for something from the past, but they could still do good work in the present.

隔天早上，她要把表格拿給李先生看。她願意承認數字錯誤並修改，卻不願隨意對待別人的生活。郵局很小，城市辦公室可能只看到紙上的幾行資料。艾達希望每行都真實。瑪拉的紅信封放在她親自選定的抽屜裡。兩名女子都在尋找與過去有關的東西，但她們仍能在當下做好有意義的工作。
