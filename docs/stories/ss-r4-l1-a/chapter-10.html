Words We Can Hear

鈴聲過後的每一封信 · 第 10 / 25 章 · 1000以下 · 英中對照 · 校訂完成

An apple fell between two trees near the town center. Ada picked it up just as a boy ran past with a ball. He was playing with his brother, who was blind. The older brother knew the way by the sound of his feet and the line of a low wall. The boy wanted to kick the ball near the wall, but Ada asked him to move the game. “Your brother has the right to walk here safely,” she said. They agreed to play on the open grass instead. The older brother thanked her in a warm voice.

一顆蘋果從鎮中心兩棵樹中間掉下。艾達正彎腰撿起，一個男孩抱著球跑過。他和失明的哥哥一起玩。哥哥靠腳步聲和矮牆的位置認路。弟弟想在牆邊踢球，艾達請他們把遊戲移開。「哥哥有權安全地走這裡。」她說。兩兄弟同意到開闊草地玩。哥哥用溫暖的聲音向她道謝。

The brothers asked about the letters they could not read in the usual way. Ada showed them the library's public service list on her phone. It included a simple way to hear text with help from a teacher. “Do you love reading?” she asked. The older brother smiled. “I love stories. I do not love being asked if I can read at all.” Ada said she was sorry. She had asked the wrong question. He told her that he often used the internet, but some old letters had to be read by a person he trusted.

兄弟問起無法用平常方式閱讀的信件。艾達用手機給他們看圖書館的公開服務表，上面有一個簡單的方法，可以在老師協助下聽到文字。「你喜歡閱讀嗎？」她問。哥哥笑了。「我喜歡故事，但我不喜歡別人問我到底能不能讀。」艾達道歉，知道問題問得不好。他說自己常上網，但有些舊信需要由他信任的人念給他聽。

Mara met the brothers at the library. She used a pencil to draw a map with large marks. The older brother said he could not use the picture, so Mara stopped and asked what would help. He wanted a short spoken list of turns. They spent half an hour making one together. They did not need a net around the walking path or a very costly machine. They needed to listen and change the plan. The brother said, “This way is simple, and I can choose my road.” Ada wrote that in her work notes.

瑪拉在圖書館見到兄弟。她用鉛筆畫了一張標記很大的地圖。哥哥說他看不到圖，瑪拉便停下來問怎樣才有幫助。他想要一份簡短的口述轉彎指引。他們花半小時一起做出來。他們不需要在步道周圍架網，也不需要昂貴機器；只要傾聽並調整計畫。哥哥說：「這方法簡單，我能自己選路。」艾達把話記進工作筆記。

In the afternoon, a cold drink with ice was waiting on the office table. Ada drank half of it while looking over the city letter. It said that the town's mail office might lose its afternoon hours next year. The office needed to show what work it did and how local people used it. There was no order to close it yet. Mr. Lee drew a line under the word “might.” “We must not tell the town that it is already closed,” he said. “But we should not hide this notice either.”

下午，郵局桌上放了一杯加冰的冷飲。艾達一邊喝了半杯，一邊閱讀城市通知。通知寫著，鎮郵局明年可能失去下午的營業時段。郵局需要說明提供哪些工作，以及居民如何使用。上面還沒有關閉的命令。李先生在「可能」一詞下畫線。「不能告訴大家郵局已經關了，」他說，「但也不應隱瞞這張通知。」

They made a plan for a public meeting. Ada would read the notice in plain words, and the town would be able to ask questions. The older brother from the park offered to help. “If you put your answer online, please also make a way for people to hear it,” he said. Ada agreed. Mara offered to help with a short spoken message, but Ada told her that public work should not take all her time. Mara laughed. “I know. I am here to find my mother's road, not to become a second postman.”

他們計畫召開公開說明會。艾達會用簡單的話讀通知，鎮民可以提問。公園遇見的哥哥也願意幫忙。「如果答案放到網路上，請也讓人有辦法聽到內容。」他說。艾達同意。瑪拉願意協助錄製簡短訊息，但艾達提醒她，公共工作不應占滿她的時間。瑪拉笑說：「我知道。我是來找媽媽的路，不是來當第二個郵差。」

In the late afternoon, a ball hit the fence near the library. The boys stopped the game and checked whether the older brother was all right. He was still sitting safely on his bench. Ada said they had done the right thing by stopping. “If you are not sure, ask,” she told them. The younger boy agreed to keep the play on the grass. The older brother said he liked being part of the talk, not only the person everyone talked about. Ada remembered that when she wrote the public meeting plan. Every person needed a voice of their own.

下午晚些時候，一顆球撞上圖書館旁的圍欄。男孩們停下遊戲，確認哥哥是否安全。他仍穩穩坐在長椅上。艾達說他們停下來做得對。「不確定就開口問。」她告訴孩子。弟弟答應把遊戲留在草地。哥哥說他喜歡自己參與討論，而不只是被大家談論的人。艾達在寫公開會議計畫時，記住了這件事。每個人都需要自己的聲音。

At home, Ada put the notice beside her work book. It was the first clear sign of the hard choice ahead. She could spend every night worrying, or she could take simple steps with the people who used the office. She chose the second way. The brothers had shown her something important that morning: one service did not fit everyone in the same way. A town office had to listen to each person and make room for different needs. Ada did not feel ready to win a big fight, but she felt ready to begin a fair talk.

回家後，艾達把通知放在工作簿旁。這是前方艱難選擇的第一個明確訊號。她可以夜夜擔心，也可以和真正使用郵局的人一起採取簡單行動。她選擇後者。早上那對兄弟讓她看到重要的事：同一項服務不會以同一方式適合每個人。小鎮郵局必須聽見個別需求，為不同的人留下空間。艾達還沒準備好贏得一場大爭論，但已準備好展開公平的對話。
